Tony Quinn and the EDUCO System, SATURDAY MAGAZINE NAIROBI Sat March 01, 2003

Tony Quinn

Saturday March 01, 2003   SATURDAY MAGAZINE DAILY NATION NAIROBI KENYA

Tony Quinn and the EDUCO System

‘Educo’ is a Latin word that means ‘to change from within’. The English word ‘education’ stems from it, and points to the original intent, perhaps, of those who first sought to educate; they wanted to help humans develop their intelligence, rather than cram their minds full of stuff, in the name of education.

This is the name that Tony Quinn has chosen to call his system of thought. Through it, he aims to teach people to draw out the latent power inherent in their brains, to help them achieve their life, career, relationship and health goals. EDUCO is founded on the premise that the average person uses only 10 per cent of their total brain capacity. If we were to use, say, 10 per cent more, Tony says, imagine what sort of results we would achieve. Even a 20 per cent increase, according to him, would be considered genius. He wants to teach you to use all 100 per cent of it.

He goes on to say that the person using all of their brain is capable of programming oneself and one’s life to achieve specific targets by ‘photographing’ them. We are, he says, a product of the programmes our environment has put into our heads; low self-esteem, health issues, career and financial under-achievement, botched relationships – all of these things are dictated by the beliefs we hold in our minds, that we are either not capable of achieving these goals, or cannot make appropriate change to the positive.

He also believes that a mind-set free of these negative programmes is a mind of its own, separate from the body that carries it. He calls this mind the ‘Self’ or ‘Force’. It is a force of pure energy, channelled towards achieving the great, if it is unfettered by the negative thoughts that the ‘Me’ part of ourselves imprisons it in. The ‘Self’ can only be achieved by a brain that is in full use; the person who is in the ‘Self’ is doing what Tony calls ‘unconscious attention’. When the person is focused on unconscious attention, positive, life-enriching goals can be attained, simply by being ‘photographed’ by the ‘Self’, and the ‘Self’ (not the ‘Me’, or the conscious part that we are all aware of) working towards achieving it, with seemingly no effort on their part. All you have to do to achieve this state, he says, is to be still and ‘relax’ into life and let it flow through you.

For those of you who need further proof, consider this; Tony Quinn, who was once a body-builder and a butcher, is now a multi-millionaire living in the tax-free Bahamas, travelling all over the world holding seminars on EDUCO in only the world’s sunniest holiday spots, such as Egypt, Monte Carlo and the Bahamas Islands.

Understandably, Quinn has been the target of enormous criticism, mostly from Irish papers that claim that he is a cult leader exercising hypnotic control over the minds of his ‘subjects’. He has been accused of extorting millions of pounds from innocent people; a two week, sun-drenched EDUCO seminar will set you back £15,000, not including air-fare. It is also recommended that followers of his system attend at least one seminar a year.

He has also been accused of running pyramid schemes, now illegal in Ireland; agents who aggressively sell his seminar to others will get £1,000 per person who attends through them, and £2,000 per person who attends through the first person they recommended it to.

Still, the results of the EDUCO system cannot be denied, and Milda Farrell is just one example. Boxer Steve Collins, another EDUCO success story, managed to beat Chris Eubank, unbeaten in 63 bouts, after a hypnotherapy session with Quinn. And Bob Delmonteque, and 81-year-old man who should, by all definitions, have the stooped, sagging body of man his age, looks as fit and musclar as a man less than half his age. Hoax or not, the results of Quinn’s EDUCO system are there for the whole world to see.

Milda’s surgery

Imagine having pain-free surgery while fully conscious. This is exactly what happened to Milda Farrell 10 years ago. She tells her story below.

By Wayua Muli

Pics by Fredrick Omondi

Milda Farrell has an incredible story to tell, about how she once had pain-free surgery to remove a lump in her breast performed on her – without the benefit of anaesthetic. She is, understandably, one of only a small clutch of people to have ever had this sort of operation.

You would be forgiven for asking what sort of newfangled medical technology she was being used a guinea pig for. Underwater surgery, perhaps, like they do with women in labour? Or maybe it was some sort of procedure that eliminates the need for knives and swabs, like laser surgery. According to Milda, there was nothing ‘new’ about it. It was the traditional, scalpel-and-forceps surgery, except that she applied herself towards the effort of removing all feeling of pain. She credits the success of this procedure to the use of a new mind technique designed by New Age guru Tony Quinn, with whom she first came into contact about 20 years ago when she was a nurse in her home town of Cork, in Ireland.

Milda, who is currently in Kenya for a series of one-day seminars, was searching for a good gym in Dublin that a colleague of hers could go to when the gym instructor mentioned that a new yoga class that was starting up would be perfect for her. She describes her state of mind at the time. “My brother had just died in a drowning accident, trying to save someone else,” she says. It had been a year since his death, but she suffered from panic attacks almost every night, and severe stress, as a result of it. “My personal relationships were all ruined because I had trouble relating to other people. I was going through a difficult stage. Yoga, which had never been formally practised in Ireland, was a relatively new phenomenon for many Dubliners, and Milda was keen to give it a go.

Tony Quinn had just launched the programme to pass on his theory of positive living, that involved tapping into the ‘Self’ that he said was composed of pure energy. By tapping into this source, one would have the power to heal oneself and others, and to live in good health by letting life’s natural forces flow through oneself. “I remember being totally hit by (what he was saying),” Milda remembers. “He said that your life force had the ability to change others positively, and I made a decision that I wanted to affect others in that way.” Not only did she emerge from the yoga classes a new person, but she also learnt how to utilise what she had learnt to help the patients she was dealing with feel less pain and heal faster. “I felt completely healed after those sessions,” Milda says. “I realised what my purpose in life was.”

Eventually, Tony Quinn picked her to become a ‘healer’, using his system. “He could read you, and see what your purpose in life was meant to be,” says Milda. “I suppose it was natural with me because I was already a nurse. Now all I had to do was apply what he had taught me to my work.”

After a few one-on-one sessions with Tony Quinn, Milda had learnt how to use the ‘source’ of energy to heal people. She could now apply it to her work. “Of course, there might have been some uproar if many people knew and thought that I was doing something strange but then I just did it and no one ever knew what I did.” Most of her work involved relaxation, and easing pain in her patients. For example, she would get women in labour to relax, so that they would feel less pain. “It was completely natural,” Milda says. No one ever noticed that she was doing anything. But she still could not come out into the open with it. “It’s very difficult in the structured environment of a hospital,” she says, “because some people might not understand.”

The pressure was on for Milda to quit working at the hospital and to start her own treatment centre, where patients could benefit from the full effects of Tony Quinn’s system. “I didn’t want to quit,” she says, “because of the security issue.” She had a very good job as a nurse’s sister, a job that it would have been considered insane to quit. “People tend to stay as sisters until they retire. It’s not a job you leave.” But after a while, she decided to take the plunge. “It took some courage, although when I did it, I knew deep down inside that it was going to work.”

Milda never thought she would ever face the day when she would have to ‘heal herself’ of a serious health problem, but that day came, in 1994, when she discovered a lump in her breast. “Many people have asked me why I did not heal myself, and when I think it’s because of the chain of events. Things had to happen the way they did in order for everything to work out.”

Milda had not been in touch with Tony Quinn in the last year, so he did not find out from her that she had a lump in her breast. However, just before she was scheduled to go into surgery, he called her. “I remember it all being very sudden. The doctors were worried about the lump and they wanted it removed as soon as possible. So I was diagnosed on a Friday and by Monday I was in hospital, waiting for the operation.” So it was a surprise that he managed to find out and call her, when only three of her closest friends knew. By this time, Tony had moved on from just teaching yoga techniques, to the whole EDUCO system of thought (see sidebar). The average human being uses less than 10 per cent of his total brain. EDUCO is the name he has given his method of teaching people to use more than just that 10 per cent to achieve financial, health, career and relationship goals. In this particular case, he was going to help Milda use her brain to stop herself feeling pain. This was not the first time that Tony Quinn had done this experiment; he had helped a lady through a gynaecological operation – without anaesthetic.

“Tony was looking for someone with a breast lump and when he heard that I had one, it all came together,” says Milda. He had found a doctor willing to perform major surgery on a patient who wasn’t under anaesthetic, but the conditions were that Tony could only do this on a patient he knew, who approved of the procedure beforehand, because of the fear of litigation. “It all just came together,” says Milda. “I had a breast lump, Tony knew me, and my doctor was the same doctor who was willing to work with him.”

A documentary of the operation was made for one of Ireland’s most popular chat shows, ‘The Late, Late Show’. Snippets of it are now on the EDUCO introduction video. In it, you see four people undergoing this sort of operation; one of them is a man on whom leg surgery is being performed. As the doctors cut and slice, he smiles broadly at the camera, simultaneously singing a song and conducting a conversation about what it feels like to be operated on without anaesthetic, with Tony.

In another, a lady, who is wide awake during a gynaecological operation, can’t wait to get back on her feet immediately after the operation and is seen dancing a little jig to prove just how comfortable she is.

And then there’s Milda, a growth about three inches being taken out of her breast while she smiles and nods at something that Tony Quinn is whispering in her ear. “I could feel everything they were doing to me,” she describes, “but I didn’t feel any pain. I felt them yanking the tumour out – it was quite adherent – and the scalpel cutting into my skin, but all painless.” She also says that sensation was heightened while she was in this state, but not the sensation of pain. How did Tony get her to this point?

“The idea is to get you connected to this source of energy that flows through you. It’s called unconscious attention,” she says. “You tap into this source of energy and you don’t feel the pain.” Was it hypnosis? “You could call it hypnosis to simplify it,” she says, “but it goes beyond that. It is going beyond yourself into this place where you are part of a universal energy force.” Tony Quinn is always present during the operations, working on delivering his own brand of ‘anaesthetic’ to the patient, by getting them to the point where they feel no pain.

Milda had had two tumours before, and all of them had been removed while she was under anaesthetic. “You can’t eat for a whole day before the anaesthetic, and after that you feel horrible. Then you can’t eat after the operation because you feel nauseated. After this last one, I felt happy and free, and in a good enough state to go home.” As a nurse, Milda also knew that patients who ‘went under’ during operations faced great risks. “There are always risks with anaesthetic, even now when technology is so advanced.”

While she was enthusiastic about an anaesthetia-free operation, Milda still had her doubts. “I remember telling Tony that I didn’t think I could do this, but then he told me that it would be okay, and I relaxed into it.”

Just like a normal operation, Tony has a pre-op procedure to determine whether the patient is fit to have an operation performed without anaesthetic. “He puts you into this relaxed state where you are in the (life force),” Milda says, “and tests to see if you still feel pain.” In the video, the viewer can see that this procedure involves clamping forceps onto a loose area of skin. “If the patient can feel this pain, then he will not risk going through with the operation.” Milda successfully underwent the pre-op test, and was soon on the operating table, having her lump removed. Once the stitches were put in, Milda got off the operating table, went into another room to dress up, did an interview with the press and half an hour later, went to a restaurant with some friends for lunch. “I felt wonderful!” she declares.

The next day, Milda was back at work. A week later, when the stitches were removed, doctors were amazed to discover that Milda had healed rather fast. And she still did not feel any pain. “It was a wonderful feeling,” Milda says. “I felt peaceful before the operation and even more after it. There was no pain at the site of the operation, and I was able to get back to work immediately.”

Milda has since been for the full EDUCO course, and is still a trained healer. She now runs a Tony Quinn health clinic in Cork, that sells his food supplements, and offers yoga and chi massage therapies. She has also taken to travelling around the world, preaching the EDUCO gospel.

Milda has had no serious health problems since then; thankfully, her tumours were never cancerous and have not returned to haunt her. She is at the peak of her health, and her business and personal relationships are now back on track. Milda now has peace of mind, and fulfilling life, thanks to the lessons Tony Quinn taught her.

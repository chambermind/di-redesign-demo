Judi Bloomfield’s testimony

Magnificat Meal Movement

Judi Bloomfield’s testimony:

I visited Australia and had a very pleasant stay with Judi and Phil. They were lovely people who were very concerned about what had happened to Helidon and to its people. Here is the testimony of my host. They are great Australians and hopefully they will see the truth triumph yet.

I first met Debra the first time she attended Mass in St. Joseph’s church at Helidon. Phil and I sat behind her towards the back of the church on the left hand side. I thought at first that this woman was quite attractive and really well dressed (she had a ‘gown’ on – and jewellery) I wasn’t sure who she was until she turned for the ‘sign of peace’. Then, with the white rosary beads round her neck, and the smile that didn’t reach her eyes, I knew who she was. At that instant, I sensed a mutual awareness and, on my part at least, a wariness.

As time progressed and the Parish tried to give this new religious group the benefit of the doubt, I was often bewildered and worried at the magnetism Debra seemed to project when she spoke. People would draw closer and hang on every word. “There, but for the grace of God, go I” – and thanks be to God for a small run in with a new age cult; and to the Presentation Sisters in Dorrigo for their instructions re wearing of rosary beads (they are for prayer purpose only, to be worn at the waist or carried in the pocket, never to be worn around the neck for adornment). I was able to stand back and observe, with much sadness, the power that this woman could influence over people, and refuse many an invitation to visit- be it only for singing and afternoon tea!

My opinion of the whole situation was that with every inch given, she wanted to take the mile.

At prayer group in Gatton one night, we split into smaller groups; Debra and a young girl was in my group. We were to pray; I began by singing the Hail Mary, Debra suggested we sing in tongues. The young girl said she couldn’t, and so did I. Debra said “It’s easy, just start making a noise; it’s just practice”. Between that, and the ‘prophecies’ she gave, I guess I was blessed, with a scepticism – which was to bring upon me much ill feeling and sometimes danger from many of her followers, and Debra herself.

I was witness to much abhorrent behaviour, quite vocal abuse at people in the church foyer; disobedience, rudeness and abuse to a very gifted and special man, our Parish Priest. A priest who both cherished and honoured his calling and position as a priest.  It was great testament to him, that to people who eventually were able to walk away from this movement, he was the first they went to.

I was saddened the day I saw Debra hitch the skirt of her ‘gown’ and run across the road to put a young man, who was already being held by two of her men, in a headlock and force him to the ground. Saddened because, in my opinion, this was the woman who had so many people convinced she was their passport to healing and heaven. So many people caught in a mind control, who didn’t know they weren’t thinking for themselves, living life away from mainstream society. It seemed that Debra ruled, amid fear and retribution. Any not for Debra were deemed a threat; demonized, stay away from them!  I didn’t understand why members avoided eye contact with me, turned their heads, even crossed the street; it was not until speaking to people after they had left the movement, that I learned that Debra had told them that I was a witch.

Followers became more fanatical to her commands. I noticed this, more prominently, during the times they were sent to disrupt the services in St. Joseph’s Church.

Some have swerved cars at me, laughing and jeering.

The change in people after they are able to walk away is great. Their whole personality is much more relaxed and friendly. Though they are still afraid. The fear and the deprogramming is a big hurdle in their lives, as is the huge step for them to take to get back into mainstream church and everyday life. It has taken an enormous amount of courage, I am very proud of them and to have them as friends.

Much is needed to help these people. Many meetings and support get-togethers have been held in our Parish and Diocese.

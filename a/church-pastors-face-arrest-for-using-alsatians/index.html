Church pastors face arrest for using Alsatians to keep the receiver out

Irish Mail on Sunday JUNE 9 2013

Pdf of article:

Irish Mail on Sunday 9th June 2013 Page 2

By Michael O’Farrell
INVESTIGATIONS EDITOR


THE pastors of one of Ireland’s most high-profile evangelical churches face arrest after  Alsatians were used to prevent receivers seizing their premises in Firhouse in Dublin.

Gardai were yesterday served with a High Court ‘attachment order’, which was
handed down on Friday after the court heard testimony that staff at the church had
been preventing receivers entering the property including using German a
shepherd dogs.

The church, the Victory Christian Fellowship, is run by Pastor Brendan Hade and
his wife Sheila, and owes €18m to Bank of Scotland Ireland. An Irish Mail on
Sunday investigation has established that Mr Hade and his church have earned
millions from a series of businesses – including €16m paid by the State for housing
asylum-seekers. Mr Hade now faces imprisonment over his refusal to cooperate with receivers from Grant Thornton. His wife, their two sons and another man are also named
as trustess affected by the court order. They have been ordered to appear before the
court on Wednesday. The Victory Christian Fellowship is registered as a religious
charity and as a result does not have to publish its accounts.

This week Mr Hade declined to comment when approached. Mike Garde, the Director of the
Dialogue Ireland Trust, a group concerned with protecting people from cult organisations, said he had been receiving requests for help from members of Victory Christian Fellowship
for several years.

Mr Garde spoke of cases where people ‘had come for help having paid over significant amounts of money. The receivers were granted a injunctions against anyone preventing access, but  reported in courton Friday that security staff recorded a trustee telling a congregation the repossession was ‘not legal in the name of Jesus’. They also said a rota had
been organised for a sit-in. The receivers sought an order for the attachment and committal of the church trustees to prison, saying that. while such a step was ‘draconian’, there was no other way to get them to come to court. Judge Mary Laffoy said the receivers had been given ‘the run around’ while attempting to serve the trustees. She ordered that the trustees must
attend court this Wednesday or face arrest.

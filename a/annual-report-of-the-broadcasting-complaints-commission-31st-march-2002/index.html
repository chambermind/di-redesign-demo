ANNUAL REPORT OF THE BROADCASTING COMPLAINTS COMMISSION 31st March 2002

Mormonism

The Director of Dialogue Ireland had a complaint about him on Live Line brought by Mr. Joseph J. Peters a member of the “The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints” (LDS) or colloquially known as the Mormons.

Complaint made by           Mr. Joseph J. Peters

 

Station : RTE           Programme : ‘Liveline’       Date :   25th / 26th September 2000

Summary of Complaint:

Mr. Heney complained about the programme ‘Liveline’.  The topic discussed related to concern expressed by a caller regarding college age youth moving to cities and being targeted by religious cults.

He further stated:-

  • “What followed was a blatant attack on minority religions in Ireland orchestrated by a series of phone calls many of which contained alarming misrepresentations that were not corrected by Joe Duffy or Mike Garde.
  • One caller to the show told how he was invited by a friend out to Tallaght.  His friend and others were dressed in black and proceeded to lacerate themselves with knives and drink blood.  After this call Joe asks his ‘expert’ (Mike Garde) – are there any other movements that we should be worried about?  The expert then mentions the Mormons on the top of his list, followed by the Jehovahs Witnesses.  The caller merely reported a rare incident, but Joe Duffy by careless questioning elevated them to a ‘movement’.  This positioning of minority religions is unfair, inaccurate and is a far cry from reasoned debate which should be the hallmark of a national broadcaster.
  • Mike Garde knows that the name of the Mormon Church is “The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints”.  On 26th September, he dropped the ‘Jesus Christ’ from the title.  He failed on every occasion to correct inaccuracies made by callers often inadvertently.
  • The impression over the two days was that the Mormon Church is secretive and wants to lure young people away from their families.”

Mr. Peters has been a member of the Mormon Church for seventeen years.  The Church is established in 167 countries.  The church goes in the front door in every country and meets the officials and seeks approval.  In Ireland, they have registered buildings in Dublin, Cork and Limerick and are open to the public.

Mr. Peters feels the programme has done a disservice to him and his family.

 

Station’s Response

RTÉ stated that all its programming upholds the rights of people to hold whatever religious beliefs they wish and to be able to express freely those beliefs.

RTÉ regrets that the programmes caused such distress to Mr Peters.  Mr Peters’ religious views and the Church he belongs to are entitled to respect and fair and balanced coverage of issues that relate to them.  The ‘Liveline’ programmes transmitted on the 25th and 26th September attempted to deal with the very real concerns of listeners who had contacted the programme with their experiences of members of their families whom they felt were being indoctrinated and brain-washed by religious cults.  In order to provide some expertise to answer and respond to listeners’ accounts, Mr Mike Garde was invited onto the programme.  His credentials were clearly given to listeners in an abbreviated form in the programme on the 25th and in a fuller form in the programme on the 26th.  This included information about how Mr Garde’s organisation ‘Dialogue Ireland’ was funded and which groups supported it.

RTÉ went on to describe various ‘phone calls from members of the public giving accounts of family members’ experiences with religious groups.  One caller to the programme ‘Eamon’ described himself as a Mormon for twenty years.  This caller was given ample opportunity to give his views of the Mormon Church and corrected some inaccuracies in Mr. Garde’s account of the Mormon Church.  It is RTÉ’s view that this caller afforded the Mormon church the opportunity to set the record straight from their viewpoint.

RTÉ believes the programme was balanced and fair about a most difficult topic.

Decision of the Commission:

The Broadcasting Complaints Commission rejected the complaint made by Mr. Peters.  The Commission was of the view that RTÉ did not infringe Section 18 of the Broadcasting Authority Act, 1960, as amended.

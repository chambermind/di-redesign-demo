Controversial Tuam religious movement’s conference cancelled

by Claire Gallagher

Ireland’s leading religious watchdog has claimed that media attention has forced a Tuam religious group to cancel a major conference in Dublin this week.

The controversial King of Kings Foundation has cancelled a two-day women’s event entitled “King’s Daughters of Ireland International Women’s Conference” at the prestigious Berkeley Court Hotel in Dublin, which was scheduled to take place on July 28 and 29.

The religious movement previously known as the Tuam Christian Fellowship came to the attention of the media over the past number of weeks, as former members accused the leadership of intimidation, spiritual abuse and greed.

The movement came to Dialogue Ireland’s (the religious watchdog) Mike Garde’s attention most recently when he was contacted by Harcourt Square Garda Station, Dublin regarding an anonymous fax which suggested that Mr. Garde was investigating the event.

Prior to this he was aware of the group’s existence as he was asked to provide pastoral care in Tuam for previous members who has been hurt by the grouping.

When he tried to enter into dialogue with the leadership of the KoK to express the concerns of ex-members and the individuals and families affected by this group, all his efforts he claimed met with failure.

The leadership simply refused to meet with him, he said.  Mr. Garde then felt it necessary to bring the KoK to the public’s attention while doing all possible to protect the identity of former members.

Mr. Garde told The Tuam Herald that even at this late stage he was willing to enter talks with the church’s leadership.

While the conference continues to be advertised on the KoK’s website, the hotel confirmed that the conference has been cancelled.  Mr. Garde said he believed media attention forced the conference to close.

The conference was to be addressed by one of the leaders of the Hillsong Church in Australia – a Church that has also been at the centre of media attention down under, despite its claim of 15,000 members and a number of television broadcasts and CDs under its belt.

There was no one available at the KoK office to comment on why the conference would not be going ahead when contacted by The Tuam Herald on Tuesday.

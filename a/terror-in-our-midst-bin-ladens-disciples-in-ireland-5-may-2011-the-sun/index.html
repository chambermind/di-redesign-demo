TERROR IN OUR MIDST Bin Laden’s disciples in Ireland 5 May 2011 The Sun

Islamism

THE AL TRIANGLE; BULLET FOR THE BEAST: TERROR IN OUR MIDST Bin Laden’s disciples in Ireland

By JOANNE McELGUNN, Crime Editor BARRY MORAN and MYLES McENTEE, Political Correspondent;

5 May 2011 The Sun

© 2011 Times Newspapers Ltd. All rights reserved

THESE are Ireland’s top al-Qaeda suspects — in hiding and wanted by agencies all over the world.

Ibrahim Buwisir, Shafiq Ben Mohamed Ben Mohamed Al-Ayadi and Hamid Aich are believed to be key cogs in the al-Qaeda wheel and were driven underground even before the death of Osama Bin Laden.

A high-ranking garda source has warned frontline terrorists use Ireland as a base to “rest and recuperate” after carrying out attacks for Bin Laden in Afghanistan — with the Special Detective Unit monitoring up to SIXTY here. Our source explained: “Ireland was used as a base in which operatives could rest and regain their strength after frontline fighting in Afghanistan for Bin Laden. They would be secretly shipped here to recuperate for future offensives. It may sound a bit extreme, but it has happened.”

Irish citizen Buwisir — named as a key member of an al-Qaeda cell in Ireland — has gone missing from his home in Dublin. Neighbours told how it was over a year since they’d seen the charity worker — who shares a home with his wife in the south of the city.

One said: “He doesn’t mix or socialise and keeps himself to himself. Neither he nor his wife have been around in a year and half.”

The front garden of the house in a quiet Ballinteer was overgrown yesterday, and post was piled up behind the front door.

The Libyan, 49, who has lived here since 1982, has always firmly denied UN claims he was a “close associate” of slain Osama Bin Laden.

Quizzed by the Special Branch along with three other men in the wake of the 9/11 terror attacks, he has always stressed he had no links to the international terror group.

The dad of four, who has been under intense security surveillance and had his home searched a number of times, insisted: “I am innocent, but I am being harassed for political reasons.

“They pick on me because I’m outspoken, because I don’t agree with American foreign policy, because I want to do something for the poor and needy.”

Short, balding, and bearded with wire-rimmed glasses — the one-time executive member of the Islamic Foundation of Ireland headed up the Irish branch of the Islamic Relief Agency, which shut down in 2002.

But in 2009, shortly before he vanished, the UN Security Council added Buwisir’s name to a list of people associated with al-Qaeda and other groups, including the Libyan Islamic Fighting Group and Egypt’s Islamic Jihad.

It accused him of helping them with funding and media relations.

And it claimed Buwisir “directed a European al-Qaeda cell that provided support for operations in Europe and LIFG camps in the 1990s, and was actively involved in providing funding and material assistance to both groups during that period”.

It went on: “He has been deeply involved in promoting al-Qaeda and associated groups through the media as well as distributing their pamphlets and other publications.” Five years earlier, he was also listed by the US Treasury as having directed a European al-Qaeda cell that provided logistical support.

It accused him and and four other people of funnelling money to Bin Laden via Sudan.

Their assets were frozen and American citizens were banned from doing business with them.

And in 2008, on foot of a Libyan legal warrant, Interpol issued its highest level “red notice” arrest warrant which would allow for his detention if he tried to leave the country. Cops here are also on the lookout for another major suspect they fear may have returned to lie-low here after Bin Laden’s death.

Algerian engineer Hamid Aich went on the run after he was suspected of helping to plot the failed millennium attack on LA Airport.

The 45-year-old moved from Canada to Dublin in 1999 with his Japanese wife and was questioned along with Buwisir about fund raising for Islamic terror groups before finally leaving the country in 2001.

He was living in Tallaght. Buwisir said he knew Aich only because they prayed at the same mosque but said they were not friends and he did not know him well.

Tunisian Shafiq Ben Mohamed Ben Mohamed Al-Ayadi, 48, has been put on special notice by Interpol and the UN Security Council.

Believed to be living in Dublin since August 2009, Interpol say he is closely associated with Saudi charity the Al-Haramain Islamic Foundation.

The US Department of the Treasury claims the organisation had “direct links” to Bin Laden.

Although all charges were dropped by a federal judge, it is now banned by the UN Security Council.

He was listed by the UN on 17 October 2001 as being associated with terror groups.

Al-Ayadi went to Afghanistan in the early 1990s to get paramilitary training, and then travelled to Sudan with others to meet Bin Laden.

They agreed to train recruits for Bin Laden in Tunisia.

Tunisian authorities issued an arrest warrant for him in 1994.

Al-Ayadi, was sentenced in his absence by a Military Tribunal of Tunis, Tunisia, to ten years’ jail in 1995.

Mother of pupil at Burke’s former school pleads for State to remove ‘disruptive’ character

Ali Bracken

The mother of a first-year student attending Wilson’s Hospital School said it as “horrendous” that former teacher Enoch Burke is still presenting at the school and called for the State to spearhead his removal over parents’ concerns.

The mother of a first-year student attending Wilson’s Hospital School said it as “horrendous” that former teacher Enoch Burke is still presenting at the school and called for the State to spearhead his removal over parents’ concerns.

Aneta Safiak’s son Jonatan (13) started attending Wilson’s Hospital School in Westmeath on Thursday, August 24. Last Monday, as the new term officially began, the sacked teacher arrived and took up position in a corridor at the school.

In July, Mr Justice Alexander Owens made an order restraining Burke from trespassing on the premises of the school.

Ms Safiak said that, as a parent of a child at the school, she was “worried” about Enoch Burke’s ongoing protest.

“The summer holidays clearly didn’t bring him any wisdom. He needs to be removed. What are we waiting for? How many years is this going to be allowed to go on?

“I feel as though he is laughing at us all, particularly the gardaí and the judiciary. He clearly wants to be a martyr. I think it’s bigger than a school issue, it’s a failure of the legal system.”

The mother-of-three added that she “wants someone to step in and take control” of the situation.

“I think it’s horrendous that it’s still happening. He should be removed. I understand that gardaí cannot bring him to the station every day. Can the State not intervene in another way?

“It’s very disruptive to the children and staff. But the school has done as much as it can do. The reality in that there is a strange man who keeps showing up and doesn’t plan to leave the space.”

In March of this year, High Court judge Mr Justice Brian O’Moore described Burke’s continuing disobedience of a court order as “conscious”, “ceaseless”, “egregious” and “deliberate”.

He noted that the evidence of the school was that Burke had breached the injunction “at the first opportunity” and had breached it every day since the fines were imposed on January 26, except for days when the school was officially closed or he was in court in relation to ongoing litigation.

According to school principal Frank Milling, Burke’s presence was causing “considerable disruption and unease” for staff, students, parents and the wider school community and was “inimical to the proper functioning of the school”.

This was rejected by Burke who said he had support from “various quarters”.

​As a parent, Ms Safiak said it was impossible not to be concerned by his ongoing presence at the school.

“Jonatan is there and I have to trust that he is safe. I am very happy with the school. But at the same time, I’m worried.

“I worry that this situation has the potential to escalate. I’m also angry at the injustice of it. I think most parents feel this way. We want this sorted out, to come to an end.”

The Polish woman explained that she saw Burke when she first visited the school with her son in March ahead of him becoming a pupil.

“I saw him outside a back door of the school. I assumed he would be a long-gone memory by the time my son was starting at the school. But he isn’t.

“Jonatan is aware of the situation. We have discussed it. When we saw him in March, Jonatan jokingly said to me: ‘Don’t say anything to him, mummy,’ because he knows I would want to. This is a situation that is of worry to every parent in the school I imagine.

“Arresting him and sending him to prison didn’t work. Fining him has not worked, since he doesn’t seem to be employed at the moment. So something else has to be figured out. Because this cannot continue, for the sake of the children.”

Jonatan Safiak has been diagnosed with a genetic disorder known as ataxia telangiectasia (AT). His mother praised Wilson’s Hospital School for facilitating her son, who needs the use of a wheelchair.

Last Monday, Burke returned to the school that sacked him in what is seen as a pledge to continue his protest at his dismissal.

A statement from a Burke family spokesperson last week said he “will not endorse an ideology which he as a Christian disagrees with, and which will have serious repercussions for young people”.

Attempts were made to contact Burke and Wilson’s Hospital School for comment.

Burke’s dispute with the school in Multyfarnham, Co Westmeath, had its roots in a request by former principal Niamh McShane, that teachers call a transgender child by a new name and by their preferred pronoun.

However, Burke, who is an evangelical Christian, objected and publicly challenged the principal’s request at a chapel service and after a school dinner.

These events prompted the principal to compile a report for the board of management, which ultimately decided to put Burke on paid administrative leave on August 22 last year.

Despite being suspended, the teacher continued to show up “for work”, even when the school secured a court order restraining him from doing so.

Even after being arrested for trespass in January, Burke continued to turn up at the school, prompting the High Court to issue him with a daily €700 fine for every day he remained in contempt of court by turning up at the school. His daily fines now total €148,400.

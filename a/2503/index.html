Belize’s Fair Share of Oil Revenue

Tony Quinn

Belize’s Fair Share of Oil Revenue

http://www.guardian.bz/component/content/article/54-highlight/1289-belizes-fair-share-of-oil-revenue

Written by Administrator

Thursday, 28 January 2010 00:00

BNE’s oil tanks at Spanish Lookout BNE’s oil tanks at Spanish Lookout Immediately after assuming office in February of 2008, the United Democratic Party government’s first priority area was to address deficiencies that existed within the petroleum department. In speaking on Wave’s Morning Show on Wednesday January 27, Director of the Geology and Petroleum Department Andre Cho explained that since that time the Department has moved in great strides to increase capacity. He explained that now the Department is well on its way to becoming properly staffed to address the needs of the oil industry. There has been addition to the technical staff as well as geology staff and training has been done to get those involved in the Department up to speed with the petroleum industry.

On the revenue and expenditure aspect of the industry, Cho says, that in July of last year the government secured the services of a petroleum accountant. There is now a move to get an assistant to the accountant. Their role will be to ensure that the country gets its fair share of oil revenues.  The accountant, says Cho, is being tasked with auditing BNE’s operations from it started production in 2006. The work of the auditor has already begun and along with the Income Tax Department they are looking into the revenues and expenditures of the company.

Cho comments that it will not be until that exercise is completed that an exact figure as to government’s take on the oil industry can be properly determined. He adds that while an exact figure of the revenue that government gets from the oil industry cannot be had he says that there are some figures that are available. Most notably was the revelation by Cho that from the beginning of oil production in Belize up to the 3rd quarter of 2009, government has secured $40,124,376 in revenue as royalty that is collected from the production of oil. That is representative of a 7 ½ percent take from the gross revenue of BNE. He explained to the Guardian that since production of oil began BNE reports that cumulative production up to January 25 of 2010, 4,840,495.13 barrels have been produced. Gross revenue, he adds from November 2005 up to the end of the 3rd quarter of 2009 as is reported by BNE, is in the region of US$296,002,684. A 10% working interest that government secures stood at $6,154,232 from 2006 up to 2008. The production share which is 1.5% of net petroleum produced stood at $668,842 from 2006 up to 2008. The figures which were not available were for the 40 percent take that is claimed as tax on income which is administered by the Income Tax Department. That 40% makes up the largest amount of money that is taken in by government from the oil industry.

While the revenue aspect of the oil industry is the area that attracts most attention, Cho disclosed that BNE’s exploration license will come to an end. Currently, he says the oil company invests significant amounts of money from production back into exploration; however that arrangement will come to an end in January of next year. This, he says, will see millions of dollars that are being used for oil exploration remain as revenue for sharing and taxation.

Presently, the contract area for BNE covers land blocks from Calla Creek moving east to Cotton Tree Village and North to Rancho Dolores. The Spanish Lookout and Never Delay fields fall within the exploration block of BNE. When BNE’s exploration license expires, Cho says they will have to relinquish the exploration block and remain with the producing fields. He added, however, that the company, or anyone for that matter, can reapply for the license to explore in the same block.

Turning over to the apparent controversy that has sprung up as a result of announcements that were made by BNE in an Irish newspaper, Cho explained that there is some level of misunderstanding as to the declaration of the field as being commercial. He says that indications that the Never Delay wells had commercial value came as early in 2008 when BNE submitted a notification that it had potential to become such. The well was then subjected to tests and another well was dug, and by November 4 of last year it was confirmed that indeed the wells were of commercial quantities. While that was the find itself, there are requirements that need to be met before a formal declaration is made of the wells being commercial.

Cho says that a development plan has to be submitted and an Environmental Impact Assessment has to be made. That is currently being reviewed by the Department of the Environment which will take it up with the National Environmental Appraisal Committee. The Never Delay oil field cannot begin to produce oil commercially until these requirements are met. Additionally, before the field is declared commercial a field boundary needs to be established. In the development program, Cho says, that there will be 15 wells drilled with connecting pipelines to a central facility.

While there has been some controversy and much talk about the oil industry recently, Cho maintains that the Geology and Petroleum Department maintains an “open door policy” where anyone who wishes to get information on the industry can freely do so by visiting the Department’s offices.

Early response of DI trust to events in Core Fri 13 Feb 2004

Abuse by Church and Irish institutionsEvangelicalism

Fri 13 Feb 2004

Dear xxxxxxx,(CORE Trustee)

I prayed about it, and decided to talk to Willi or xxxx. I got to talk to xxxx and she told me that Willie has been in professional counselling for over two years, so I am not sure of the sequence of events now. I offered to talk to them, but the overwhelming impression I picked up was how did I find out and that publicity should not follow.

Primarily this is a failure of Willi in relation to his fellow believers in Core, but it is also going to have implications for the whole Evangelical witness in Ireland. Now there is no comparison to what happened in the Catholic Church in relation to the sexual abuse of minors, but it could prove to be the first salvo in moving beyond the Catholic community in relation to publicity. From the Tuam situation, Tralee, Cork and Limerick I can tell you Evangelicals also have issues to attend to.

I would like to reflect a bit further on my conversation with you on Friday and give you some ideas that should be of use to you as a Trustee. Also I am going to pass this onto Bishop xxxxx xxxx a fellow trustee, xxxxx xxxx, xxxxx xxxx and xxxxx xxxx on my committee who already know about this so that we can find the right way forward. I am quite happy for them to pass this on to archbishop Neill, but I will not.

xxxxx xxxx Church of Ireland, and when Core began in August 1993 we were at the first service and felt it was going to be an important Evangelical contribution not just to the Church of Ireland, but to other Churches as well. I saw it as empowering people from other parishes to come and be trained and then go back to make a contribution back home. In reality the Church of Ireland was merely a flag of convenience, like Liberia is for tankers. Many parishes lost their only youth, and many pastoral issues I observed ignored the C of I set up. Also I was asked to be a contact for CMS, but it was clear that CORE would do its own thing in places like Nepal rather than work with Church agencies. Also the Vineyard approach which is similar to general Pentecostal structures puts a lot of emphasis on leadership imposed from the top without reference to the body of believers. Here one remembers the disasters with the Apostolic Church in the early ’90s. Also it is 10 years since the Toronto Blessing this summer coming and we will have to evaluate whether this was a factor in the ethical collapse, or a positive factor in the growth of Core. I am in the school of those who believe it was the equivalent of Spiritual BSE or mad cow disease. Others were richly blessed and deepened their faith, and others who have reflected on it theologically feel it was from God.

Core of course preceded Toronto, and in my view lost direction after it. The Alpha course was a definite gift to the Church, but for the rest history and fruit will have to be the judge.

I am an Anabaptist and I would be very much into congregational democracy, though I have no problem with connectional structures, which are well developed, in the Church of Ireland. So even though we signed the membership covenant I felt from the start that Willi had constructed a leadership system that gave him absolute control of who was in leadership, the executive, and in the end the Trustees.

This would manifest itself in rules like you had to be part of a small group, and often he would not be part of it himself. Many committed people were simply terminated and left in a totally devastated position for years to come.

When the decision to move to St Catherine’s was made, we as members were not consulted about the process. I remember a meeting in his house where we were told about the decision and told he had the vision, and if we did not like it to get of the ship. All we were required to do was to come up with imaginative ways to raise the funds for it.

He surrounded himself with people of his choosing and there was no congregational dimension to decision making. So when I began to talk to people who were hurting as a result of this I could see that my future in all of this was not to try to challenge this model which naturally I rejected, but I saw that others were willing to buy into. We decided for our spiritual health to move on.

Now I would say that part of the problem is that this system was not C of I either and the Trust church system also tended to favour Willi.

Coming to the issue of homosexuality. First I should say that no one should be in any way be challenged because of their sexual orientation. The issue here is not homosexuality, but inappropriate sexual contact. After I left it was brought to my attention that Willi had had an inappropriate affair with a person in the music group. At that time xxxxx xxxx was the C of I rep on Dialogue Ireland. We both went to see Archbishop Empey who said, he would have the issue looked into by the archdeacons. Note: Though we had heard rumours about an affair, what we in fact raised with the Archbishop was the issue of inappropriate leadership. I do believe the Archbishop said he would raise this though but his successor found no reference to it in his files.

All I know after that was that Willi was elevated to being a minor canon.

My understanding is that the leadership covered this up at the time. That is the same leadership who this time has taken over since Willie resigned. Is it credible that they can function with authority when they are in their position through him and the structures he put in place? So even if you decapitate him from leadership what you still have in place is a very dangerous structure from the perspective of healing in the congregation. New systems of accountability need to be put in place, so that the leadership does not continue with this flawed model. Also I would be of the opinion that you can’t disconnect the sexuality issue from the power issue. Basically Willi was a power unto himself and like Dedalus believed he could fly. He was not governed by the norms of other believers. Also last year when xxxxx xxxx was in court as a result of the Amethyst issue it brought to mind that he was a person who as a young believer was thrust into leadership beyond his spiritual competence, a possible factor in his undoing.  There will be people who will feel totally wrecked by what happened. Anger, depression and from an earlier era loss of faith. Pastoral resources need to be put in place FROM outside. This can’t be solved from inside.

I know a lot of people are afraid of this getting into the public domain. All is being done to stop it getting out. There are the xxxxx xxxx who do not need the grief of Willi being outed. He was making radical demands for people to follow Jesus, and must be prepared to face the public in not living up to these ideals himself.

I do not believe we should make this issue public, but I am of the opinion CORE must prepare for it becoming public.

So though I grieve for the loss of Willi at present and the waste of his great gifts, I feel the most important people to consider are those he abused his power with and those left reeling from recent events in Core and those who are discarded in the night.  Do feel free to continue the dialogue with me, and for the present we must pray that Christ might be glorified in this mess. My insights may be wrong, but hopefully they will help in the process of healing.

Yours in Christ

Mike

At this time DI Trust was a Christian organisation but soon after this we decided to become an organisation which was not faith based so as to not have any conflict in regard to our mission related to cultism.

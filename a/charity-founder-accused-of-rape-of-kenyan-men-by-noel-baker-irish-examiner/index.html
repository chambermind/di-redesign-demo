Charity founder accused of rape of Kenyan men By Noel Baker Irish Examiner

Icross Charity

By Noel Baker Irish Examiner

Tuesday, April 20, 2010

http://www.irishexaminer.com/ireland/charity-founder-accused-of-rape-of-kenyan-men-117674.html#ixzz0ldyO8rtL

SEXUAL allegations, including those of rape, have been made against the Irish head of a charity working in Kenya. Five Kenyan men have made sworn statements containing the allegations against Michael Meegan, the founder and head of the ICROSS (International Community for the Relief of Starvation) charity, some of which date back to 1986. The allegations include claims of rape and attempted rape, sexual exploitation, making unwanted sexual advances and that he caned men.

The allegations, all of which have been rejected by Mr Meegan, came to light after the president of the High Court yesterday threw out an injunction brought by Mr Meegan against a Sunday newspaper. Mr Justice Nicholas Kearns ruled the Mail on Sunday could publish a story based on the allegations, in the first case of its kind to come before the High Court since the introduction of the new Defamation Act. The statements include one man alleging he was raped by Mr Meegan in 1986.
It alleges: “He raped me that night. I didn’t shout but I cried. I cried a lot. Even now I have problems with sex, problems I do not even discuss with my wife.” At a hearing yesterday, senior counsel for Mr Meegan, Morgan Shelley, argued the allegations were false, defamatory and that there were credibility issues regarding the men who made the affidavits containing the allegations. He said the Kenyan courts were also dealing with the matter, in view of Mr Meegan’s claims that the allegations could constitute criminal libel. Mr Shelley added the allegations could affect Mr Meegan’s right to privacy and to make a living and that it was arguable whether some of the allegations made the standard of sexual abuse or sexual assault. Senior counsel for Associated Newspaper Michael Cush said the statements on which the story was based were recently given and were “broadly corroborative” with other allegations of sexually inappropriate behaviour. He said there was “a broad body of evidence” which he also described as “significant”, and that any future defence in any possible libel trial would be the defence of truth. Mr Justice Kearns said he had to look at the competing rights of the citizen to the protection of their good name and the newspaper’s right of expression, and that it was a “discretionary matter for the court”.
He refused the application for the interlocutory injunction and said he was satisfied the respondents had a reasonable chance of succeeding in any subsequent libel action.

Last month it emerged that ICROSS Ireland, now functioning separately from ICROSS Kenya, had repaid €97,000 in Irish government aid after an internal audit found it could not be traced. ICROSS in Kenya has criticised the running of the Irish operation and rejected allegations of impropriety. Mr Meegan is a former Rehab/ESB International Person of the Year and ICROSS has in the past received high-profile support from celebrities such as the Corrs and model Rosanna Davison. In recent years the Elton John AIDS Foundation and US Aid stopped funding the organisation, while others have voiced concerns over whether aid was reaching its intended targets.
The organisation has denied those allegations. The editor of the Irish Mail on Sunday, Sebastian Hamilton, said yesterday’s High Court decision was “a great day for press freedom”.

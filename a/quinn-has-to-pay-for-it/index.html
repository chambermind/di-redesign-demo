New Year New Decade

January is generally a time for fresh starts, but people often need help to turn a New Year resolution into a lasting improvement

NEW Year resolutions are almost as inevitable as the New Year itself, and why not? After all, 2009 may go down as the worst twelve months in living Irish history, so if we can banish some bad habits along with the rest of the sorry year, then it makes

sense to use the symbolism of a fresh start in a fresh decade to help us on our journey.

Of course, Janus, after whom January is named, knows all about fresh starts. He was the Roman god of doorways and new beginnings, blessed with two faces, one looking forward and one looking back. For millennia he has been able to see the futures of our attempts to better ourselves, but he has also been able to look back upon some of our failures during the previous year. Because it is all too easy to lose sight of our goals once the initial ardour of a New Year becomes nothing more than the drudgery of the daily grind. This is why special care and attention needs to be paid to every New Year resolution. It will only work if it becomes an ongoing process, rather than being a one-off promise to ourselves. This is true whether we want to get fitter and healthier, whether we want to give up some of our vices, or whether we want to accomplish something which heretofore had seemed out of reach. It goes without saying that goals must be achievable if they are to survive into February and beyond; and each individual will know what, for them, is possible, even if they are going to have to push themselves to make it happen. But it is also advisable to get some help, if that is what is required to make an ambition a reality. You may need help from professionals to give up a bad habit. You may need more than just encouragement to become fitter and healthier. Because the more help that we have, the more likely we are to succeed in making 2010 a better year for ourselves.

NEW Year resolutions

January is generally a time for fresh starts, but people often need help to turn a New Year resolution into a lasting improvement

With the festivities over, diet and health are uppermost on all our minds.

JANUARY can be a time when people about themselves, and feel they want to tackle weightloss, detox and look after their general health,” says Caroline McDonagh, Holistic Clinical Nutritionist, and nutritional advisor to the Tony Quinn health stores.

Weight loss is about taking a holistic approach to the body, says McDonagh, who is also a trained psychotherapist. Following a thorough assessment with clients which includes looking at any medical issues, diet, age, weight, lifestyle and personal issues, McDonagh provides customized practical menu plans for clients, focusing on foods to include and those to avoid. Most clients have two visits with McDonagh, although others may prefer more regular sessions. “Some people really benefit from the  accountability and encouragement offered in our follow-up sessions. We also offer a follow-up service by phone for clients living outside the Dublin area.” “Sometimes it’s a matter of a client being low in essential nutrients, such as Vitamin B, which could make them more prone to stress, irritability and tiredness,” adds McDonagh. Caroline McDonagh is now offering clients free-radicals testing, a brand new service, the first of its kind in Ireland. “Free radicals underlie a lot of modern-day illnesses, and can accelerate the aging process. They can increase in the body when people have a poorly balanced diet, or smoke and drink excessively,” explains McDonagh.

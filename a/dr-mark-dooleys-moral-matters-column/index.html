DR MARK DOOLEY’S ‘MORAL MATTERS’ COLUMN

Abuse by Church and Irish institutions

DR MARK DOOLEY’S ‘MORAL MATTERS’ COLUMN

IRISH DAILY MAIL

NOVEMBER 30, 2009

“The Priests who Prove Evil is Real”

People don’t tend to use the word ‘evil’ anymore.  Most moral philosophers dismiss it as a relic of theological superstition, while psychologists explain it in terms derived from the science of psychiatry.  Wickedness, they say, stems from an unconscious disorder which only time on the couch can cure.

I have never entirely bought into that hypothesis.  True, there is certainly a place in our society for psychiatry and psychoanalysis.  But sometimes they are simply not enough to remedy a particular type of moral disorder, one that responds to no other name but evil.

Put simply, I do not believe that the actions of those shamed by the Murphy Report can be explained in psychiatric terms.  They were evil, pure and simple.  For that is the only way to analyse the deeds of those who would cunningly debase another human being for the sole purpose of self-gratification.

Other explanations can be found in the case of teenage sexual deviance, or in that of the sex addict who pleads for salvation from his sickness.  But no such rationalisations can be advanced when it comes to a vicar of Christ.  He has, after all, years of theological and moral training behind him.  Hence he knows, more so than most, why morality shuns those who squander a child’s sanctity.

Now I realise there were many that entered the priesthood who were unsuited to its call.  These were young men, many of whom were forced by their families to join a seminary straight from school.  They had no experience of life, let alone a mature sexuality.

Still, there can be no mitigating justification for those who proceeded to vent their frustrations on the vulnerable.  Yes, maybe the entire culture of Irish seminaries was once corrupt.  Even so, the vast majority of Irish priests did not sell their souls to Satan by becoming paedophiles.

I know dozens of priests from that generation who would rather die than abuse a child.  For them, the actions of their depraved brethren are those of the Anti-Christ.  And no excuse, other than the presence of evil, can they provide for what occurred.

In the context I am using it, evil does not mean possession.  Rather, it connotes the process by which one human being strives to extinguish the selfhood of his victim.  As I said last week, sexual virtue relies upon seeing the other as a person whose consent must be earned.  This means acknowledging his freedom to give his body as a gift, or to refuse consent when he chooses.

The reason we consider the abuse of children by adults so abominable, is because the perpetrator consciously snuffs out the child’s autonomy.  What should be gifted by consent, he takes by force, thereby devaluing his victim’s most precious possession.  In so doing, he drags the child into the world of animals and objects, a place where the light of true human sovereignty never shines.

Evil is, in other words, an attempt to render indistinguishable a person and his flesh.  Hence we decry murder as evil because it literally does just that: it empties the body of consciousness, thus reducing it to a motionless cadaver from which all semblance of personality has flown.

What the priests named in the Murphy Report did was somewhat similar.  Despite their moral preparation, they coldly looked upon the young with the eyes of evil.  Indeed, they could not have considered those poor children as human beings, for that would have forced them to see freedom and sanctity where they noticed only flesh.

I suspect that is why, for many of them, the burden of conscience and the weight of sin never took its toll.  Instead, they moved from child to child with the haste of a hunter in pursuit of wild prey.  And all the while they continued to administer the sacraments, a fact that merely serves to highlight the extent of their hideous hypocrisy.

Christianity urges us to forgive our enemies.  And yet, who can blame the victims of such terrifying torture when they refuse to absolve their abusers?  For only they know what it is like to have one’s spirit smothered by wicked men for whom morality never mattered.

Yes, there is only one name for that condition and that is evil.  And there is nothing that any psychoanalyst or psychiatrist can do to overcome it.  In the end only divine mercy can serve that purpose, by which time, of course, it is already too late for the wounded.

Dr. Mark Dooley

Fergus Ryan Submitted on 2013/06/06 at 9:05 pm

Mike, quite apart from the prosperity issue you raise here isn’t it is somewhat misleading to say that all evangelicals are ‘anti-religion’. This only makes sense if the word ‘religion’ is used in a completely pejorative sense, to mean (merely) ‘man-made’ forms or perhaps even some kind of affirmation of only the non-denominational expressions of Christianity.

Many of the most committed evangelical Anglicans have been ‘high church’, and of course there is a biblical principle of what constitutes ‘true religion and undefiled’. Evangelicalism is pan-denominational, and in that sense perhaps one of the most successful global ‘ecumenical’ movements.

Evangelicalism is defined by four things, a high view of the authority of scripture as being in essence ‘from above’, the centrality of the sacrifice of the cross as the only basis for salvation, the need for personal regeneration, and the mandate for world evangelisation. Other distinctives you refer to in this wide community are marks of its several and diverse parts. It would be unfair to define as ‘evangelical’ any distinctives beyond that four above, held by everyone fro Wesley to Whitfield, Welby to NT Wright. I often wonder whether it is appropriate for DI website to list orthodox ‘evangelicalism’ amongst the movements under scrutiny in the right column, but not, say, theological liberalism which often denies the orthodox of the creeds, regarding them, with Schleiermacher, as ‘the human affections set forth in speech’. Liberalism (painting here with a broad brush) shares with Enlightenment scepticism a low view of scripture (‘from below’), a general denial of the miraculous, a deemphasis on spiritual rebirth, and so on. And, anyway, Mike, I have always understood you to hold evangelical convictions in the traditional definition of Bebbington of Stott. Just a little note to be cautious about defining evangelicalism as all one thing. Anyway, I know you know that.

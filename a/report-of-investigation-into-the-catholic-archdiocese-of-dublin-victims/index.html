Survivors want gardaí and clergy prosecuted

Thursday, 26 November 2009 17:57

Survivors of clerical abuse demanded tonight that senior clergy and gardaí who allowed known priests to prey on defenceless children be brought to justice.

Victims said those in power who protected the Catholic Church ahead of youngsters are as guilty as the priests who carried out the sickening sex assaults.

They also called for the probe into how allegations of abuse were dealt with by the Catholic hierarchy in Dublin to be widened to investigate every Archdiocese across Ireland.

Andrew Madden, who first spoke out 10 years, said the publication was a shocking indictment on the Catholic Church in Dublin.

‘Those who turn a blind eye to these offences are as much a part of the problem as those who actually commit them,’ said Mr Madden.

‘Looking the other way causes more children to be sexually abused.’

Maeve Lewis, of support group One In Four, called for a criminal investigation into the religious figures, gardai and civil servants who shielded clerics.

‘I would consider people, like Archbishop Connell, is as guilty as the priest who actually sexually abused the children,’ she said.

‘A criminal investigation should be launched against all of these people.

Survivors slammed how clerics continued to abuse after their innocent lives were ripped apart as young children.

They also fear Auxiliary Bishops criticised by the Commission for covering up scandals may have carried the ethos with them when they were appointed as Archbishops elsewhere.

Marie Collins, who was abused by Father Edmondus almost 50 years ago, said she had no trust in the institution.

‘The Church should be put in the hands of the State,’ she said.

‘The Church should not be policing itself. They have shown that they cannot be trusted.’

John Kelly, from Irish Survivors of Child Abuse (SOCA), demanded Pope Benedict XVI apologise to the Irish people.

‘We want to see prosecuted everybody involved, whether they be priests, Bishops, Cardinals, or senior policemen, who were in this collusion and cover-up,’ said the survivor of institutional abuse.

‘The Pontiff also needs to come to Ireland not only to apologise to the victims of abuse, but to the nation of Ireland, for what happened and to reform the Irish Church root and branch.’

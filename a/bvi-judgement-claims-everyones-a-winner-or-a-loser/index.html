BVI JUDGEMENT CLAIMS – EVERYONE’S A WINNER? OR A LOSER?

Tony Quinn

There have been comments on the DI commentary on the BVI Judgment – why don’t you point out this, or that?  Why do you focus on this point, not that point? Here are some answers:

  • The Judgment is 24 pages long and covers a multitude of points.
  • The two sides, basically Sheila McCaffrey who took the case and Susan Morrice the first-named defendant and the person who seems to be putting out all the public statements on behalf of the defendants, are both making statements about the Judgement and naturally, focusing on the points that favour them.
  • At DI we are not interested in taking sides but are very interested in the Judgment because of the facts which emerged about the behaviour of people influenced by a cult leader, and the behaviour of the cult leader behind closed doors which is very different from the public statements.
  • We have also, not through any doing or wish of our own, become embroiled in the next legal action between Susan Morrice et al and a disgruntled former colleague in INE – a case taking place in Denver, Colorado, in which the DI Director, Mike Garde, has been “deposed” (a US legal term for giving evidence in person prior to a trial) by Susan Morrice’s legal team. (More on this in future posts.)

See full judgement here:   Judgment July 16, 2012-3

http://www.eccourts.org/judgments/decisions/2012/HC/stkitts/SMLifeVenturesvSusanMorriceetal.pdf


So the DI comments have focused on the points that are important, in order of importance to our interests – the opinions of an experienced Judge who had no former knowledge or opinion of Quinn, Morrice et al of the facts presented to him, and on the behaviour of the parties involved.

The Judge did, in fact, dismiss many of the complaints that Sheila McCaffrey brought to the Court. He described these as

“a bewildering assortment of twenty-two heads of relief, including a claim for damages under each of the known economic torts [a tort is a wrong done to you for which you claim compensation in Court].” [Clause 16]

He seems to have concluded that gaining compensation for this long list of wrongs done to her was not her primary goal

“what SM Life [Sheila McCaffrey’s company, in the name of which she brought the case] really wants is an order that it be bought out either by INE itself or by Ms Morrice and the second Defendant (Mr. Quinn) on unfair prejudice grounds. Only if that relief is not to be granted, does it seek  a dissolution.” [Clause 16]

So throughout the case, the Judge was analyzing the information presented to see if Sheila McCaffrey had suffered “unfair prejudice” and therefore deserved to be given money for her shares and allowed to walk away. And here the cult issue emerges – Sheila was caught in a difficulty in which many people who have become disillusioned with cults and left find themselves trapped. While they were believers they said and did things which they now no longer agree with, but from the point of view of the law, they said and did these things and so they are responsible. [Clause 97]

The Judge took a view of the case as a whole, and in his opinion the people who had suffered “unfair prejudice” were the Class B shareholders. Sheila McCaffrey had sold them the shares under the company’s 2002 Operating Agreement, which gave them clear rights, and then many of their rights were removed by the 2007 Operating Agreement, which the Judge declared was not valid.

These were some of the wrongs the Class B shareholders had suffered in this opinion

  • 64,000 shares were “gifted” to Tony Quinn in exchange for no apparent consideration of value to the company
  • Tony Quinn was illegally appointed as a Director
  • Sheila McCaffrey was illegally removed as a Director
  • The 2002 Operating Agreement was illegally set aside and flouted
  • The shareholders received no Dividend
  • Only one, or at most two, Annual General Meetings were held in nine years instead of every year as set out in the 2002 Operating Agreement [at an Annual General Meeting, the Directors are supposed to present the audited Accounts of the company and to answer detailed questions from shareholders on the accounts and the running of the company – this does not appear to have happened even at the AGM(s) that did occur]
  • Morrice and Quinn seem to have treated the company’s money as their personal property and spent massive sums on themselves

So the essential point of the Judgment is that the running of the company and the treatment of the Class B shareholders, by Morrice and Quinn is a disgrace, and in Clause 91 the Judge gives it as his Opinion that it is so bad that he would be willing to consider giving an order to dissolve the company. But the only people who have suffered the unfair prejudice that would allow him to make this order on their behalf are the Class B shareholders.

In some countries, the law has begun to allow for the fact that people can be “unduly” influenced by cult leaders and this should be taken into account in legal cases about what they did under this influence. Given her time again, Sheila McCaffrey would obviously not “gift” shares to Quinn, or introduce him as a “Director” to the company, or go along with “replacing” the true Operating Agreement behind the backs of the Class B shareholders, removing many of their rights. [Clause 97]

This article in the Sunday Times from April 2006 shows Sheila McCaffrey under that influence.*

ST Sheila on oil

She has clearly broken with Quinn but because she has not explicitly stated this and introduced this defence in proposing her case before Justice Bannister all he could do was deal with the commercial/ legal issues. Obviously it is easy with hindsight to make these points, Sheila was in a very difficult position whatever strategy she adopted.

Dialogue Ireland campaigns for undue influence to be recognised in law. But at present it is not so recognised in many countries, and Sheila McCaffrey has suffered the consequence. Because of her actions while she believed things she no longer believes (that Tony Quinn was a worthy teacher and leader), she did not have a case for unfair prejudice, and so had to list other “wrongs” [torts] that she felt she had suffered. At Dialogue Ireland, because these “torts” were not listed in the Judgment, we do not know what they were, but the Judge felt they were insufficiently evidenced and substantial to consider [Clause 99], and cut to the main point, which was the unfair prejudice suffered by the Class B shareholders.

However, the opportunity to claim for unfair prejudice is still available for the Class B shareholders.

The Sunday Times – Ireland

The Sunday Times   April 16, 2006

Quinn has left trail of broken families

IN MY experience, successful entrepreneurs and millionaires tend to turn to Tony Quinn to resolve issues (Quinn’s mind games given credit for oil strike in Belize, News, last week). Instead of getting a handle on the issues, they often lose direction and focus in their own area of competence. Quinn is the only person to benefit. I have become aware of this through the families of some, who come seeking help, or from employees who see the effects of mental manipulation. Some people, who have spent up to €100,000 on Quinn’s courses, go on to invest in this oil exploration company because they believe he is the source of its success. I would suggest that the business and entrepreneurial skills these people had were undermined rather than enhanced by what Quinn’s courses have to offer. Shares can rise and fall, but seminars that leave a trail of broken families, marriages and debt need to be addressed. It is time Ireland followed the example of Belgium and France and enacted laws to bring these practices to an end.

Mike Garde

Director, Dialogue Ireland Trust, Dublin

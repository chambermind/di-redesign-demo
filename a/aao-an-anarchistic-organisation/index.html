13 April 1978

AAO – An Anarchistic Organisation?

Action Analytic Organisation

By Flemming Falkenberg Olsen

On the fringes of our subject, new religious movements, stands AAO, Action Analytic Organisation, which was founded 8 years ago. It is not a religious movement according to its own self-understanding.

Ideologically the movement is linked to the psyko-analyst Wilhelm Reich and the philosopher Otto Mühl, of which the latter has played and still plays a important role in the movement. AAO is a wellknown phenomenon in Germany and Austria, the main center is Friederichshof, near the Neusiedlersee, south of Vienna. In Denmark a group is established in Holbæk, in Sweden an attempt is being made to start a group in Stockholm.

Our main question to this movement is: What kind of leadership does it have? And how is the leadership-concept linked to the life in the movement? – First of all: the use of the word movement instead of organisation could be symptomatic. AAO as external structure does not exist any longer – there is no organisatorical system keeping the different groups together. So we must look for other factors, that might have a sustaining function.

The movement exists as a cooperation between different groups, generally of a left-wing political view, but dissatisfied with the actual form or praxis of the socialist/marxist parties.

1. “The marxist left is lacking a positive goal. They don’t know what the end of alienation would signify in ,t positive sense, at least as far as the individual is concerned. They don’t have the slightest idea of emotional or mental depths of a “concrete utopia”. And this is the main reason for their failure.” (1)

One might say as a kind of slogan: While marxist talk about the theory-praxis relation, AA do something about this relation. (And marxists do not like this sentence).

Starting from a socialist/marxist point of view, one always talks about “communis”. – In ‘society’ we must have common this and common that, it is said. Common what? AA then asks – and the answer is as follows:
1.         common life
2.         common free sexuality
3.         common property
4.         common economi
5.         common clothes, food and residence
6.         common children
7.         common Selbstdarstellung (Self-expression)
8.         common conscious mental work and – spreading
9.         common administration, organisation and conscious mental structure*
10.       common ecological identity. (2)
* this point of course is rather unclear at the moment

These are the “ten Commandments” in AA, or “principles” as the members prefer to name them. Some of them are clear and understandable even to an outsider, but most of them need some kind of explanation, especially with regard to the problems of authority or non-authority. With this in mind I will divide my explanation into four parts: (1)a) common free sexuality, b) common children, (2) the relation between common free sexuality and common property, (3) the relation between Self-expression, conscious mental work and economi, (4) conclusion.

This does not follow the official presentation of AA, but has emerged with more or less clarity in reading and especially in discussing the material from the movement with members.

(1) a: common free sexuality

One of the most outstanding features in AA is free sexuality. Perhaps it is also one of the most misunderstood features in AA. Personally I believe this says as much about our society as about AA. Free sexuality does NOT mean that you’re fucking everyone within a square mile. Neither would it be 8 – 10 people lying together – or anything one might imagine. And here there is an important key to understanding AA. When you have a free attitude to your sexuality, you are not trying to imagine so much, but you are trying to do what you really want to do. And here AA has a message. It is not sadism or sexual perversion the movement want to express. According to their understanding free sexuality means you not having one partner, but 2 – 3 perhaps more. And this is not a secret, but an accepted custom in the group, in which you live. It is within the “family-group” you have free sexuality. It means you are not dominated by your one and only partner.

Positively the movement works with hatred, jealousy, friendship and physical love in an open way. The members are aware that society as such is dominated by men and that this often influences the relation between a man and a woman.

Negatively, there must be rather serious adjustment difficulties because of the group pressure. E.g. a woman “who hasn’t slept with anyone for some days, becomes a group problem. According to the community’s understanding of human lewdness and its suppression by the nuclear family, every member of the group is looked upon as a sick if he/she has no lust.” (3)

b: common children

When there is no nuclear family structure, the children then has to be common in some way. According to ‘the biological rule of procedure’ there is always a mother. In AA a mother is off duty for two years in order to take care of the child. Another woman can replace her, but this is very unusual as far as we know. The biological father is always known and he can have and normally has a special relation to the child.

But the child itself chooses other grown-ups as “educationalists”, so just as there is more than one “couple-connection” so there is more than one father and mother. (my own designations)

(2) relation between free sexuality and common property

As already mentioned AA-members are often left-wing marxists or socialists. Therefore the movement started with common property in general, thinking in terms of clothes, food, residence, economi etc. Every kind of material was common – and the members found another kind of property i.e. a man is owned by his wife, a woman by her husband. This caused frustration because it was understood as a new variant to ‘the missing link’ between theory and praxis. So they broke up the couples and replaced them by several bi-lateral relations, “couple-connections“.

You could say that free sexuality as understood in AA is the natural consequense of common property. This is one of “the emotional or mental depths of a “concrete utopia”.

(3) relation between self-expression, conscious mental work and economics

AA stands for action analysis: i.e. only in your doings or misdoings is it possible for others to analyse your person.

An analysis in AA means to clarify the reasons for your doings – (or misdoings).

This rather vague term leads to one of the features in AA: Selbstdarstellung

Selbstdarstellung (or self-presentation) is an artistic game of consciously unmasking oneself. The self-presenting person steps into the middle of the group and begins (with no programme or censorship) trying to follow his inner impulses and to express them in movements, words and dance. Once he has found his own, emotional) rhythm he experiences that his expression continues by itself until he runs into the next barrier of anxiety and embarassment But it is just at this 5 s point that things become interesting because it is the permanent avoidance of these barriers which robs human life of its creative strength and beauty. Where anxiety arises there are generally internalised taboos at work and beyond these taboos lie the real possibilities of life which are to be integrated.

This transformation of neurotically imprisoned energies into creative self-processes appears to be inherent in our organism as a real possibility. And whoever has experienced this himself has an idea of what “free emotionality” is. It is an overpowering catharsis for body and mind. (4)

Selbstdarstellung (SF) in the group is the very factor that constitutes the movement. The need for contact and communication is fulfilled by SF. The single member becomes the center of attention as long as he or she wants to. Often the self-expressionist remembers situations from childhood, being left alone, problems with sisters and brothers etc. And it is by this emotional acceptance of the past, that the therapeutic part of SF starts functioning.

Later on the SF is used to describe general situations and at last SF becomes a part of the realisation of the world’s creation. (5)

The content of a SF normally has a double aspect: a. the person and b. his or her sexuality. Starting with the therapeutic function of SF you present yourself, your worries and anxiety. Next you present what your sexual problems stands for – what you are and what you could be. When you have overcome this barrier, the next one is to do the same in general situations.

(4) conclusion

Of course each leader can and should be changed at any time, if the groups find it necessary. A system of rotation is to be developed in which everyone is eventually to take over leadership functions. The highest goal and prerequisite of a true democracy is the creation of maxi-mal transparency of all social contexts and decisions. In the model therefore much time will be needed for meetings, information, education and common planning.

And important point in each social concept is the social composition of the local community. Prerequisitites for participation do not involve titel and status, but motivation and capacity for social integration and cooperation. Experience will show if additional conditions of an economic nature are necessary (6)

This could be the conclusion. In fact it is the conclusion, but not the entire conclusion. Starting once again with the critique against the marxist left, it lacks a positive goal. There are no ideas about the emotional or mental depths of a concrete utopia. Members of the movement have not seen the “capitalistic property” of the couples, that brings about the transformation of neurotically imprisoned energies into creative self processes. This catharsis of mind and body, the therapy of one’s own sexuality and the common free sexuality, is the real master and leader of the movement, the life-energy. The leaders (if that is the right concept) are the persons, who have the capacity to express their per-son and sexuality in the best way in the grouplife and the SF. As a girl from Norway Wencke Mühleisen (7) told me when Opera Extase was in Arhus:

The leadership is emotional. It is the intention to reveal the unconscious structures. The leader is accepted by the group in the SF and the life of the group. It could be said that there is a kind of positive authorithy. The leader is the one who plays a positive role in a group.

In this system there is no specific status or title given to the leader, and nobody passes an examination, sitting at a table. But on the other hand I feel strongly the real possibility of a suppressive factor in SF. The way to “salvation” is going through breakdowns in order to transform neurotically imprisoned energies. Now, is this true when some members have breakdowns because of the leader? Of course not, because who is the one who allows the member to go further, to take the next step? – the leader. And when members are not strong enough (do not have capacity or motivation) to take a further step, who are responsible for solving the problem – and how? During discussions and more SF, where the leaders express their personality, sexuality and problems better than the “victim” – and therefore continue as leaders.

Another limit is the local community. I believe the “ten Commandments” to be a possible life-style for small groups with strong members. On a larger scale, as is admitted in the movement itself, these principles would Lead to chaos. Therefore I have doubts about the new point in the movement: common research for an alternative social model. See note (2)

The AA movement is in its outer structure an-archistic, non-religious, but has in its inner structure both a certain leadership-concept and a certain religious-spiritual life.

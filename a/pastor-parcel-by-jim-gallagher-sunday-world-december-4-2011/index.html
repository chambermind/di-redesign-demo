Victory (Faith Movement or Prosperity Gospel) Archive Abundant Life

Dialogue Ireland continues to be concerned about the apparent contradictions in the view that Pastor Hade had no knowledge of the ethical issues arising from the collapse of the Abundant Life Christian Church. We will publish further information on this subject. There is a lack of transparency and the people who were involved with Abundant Life seem to be unwilling to face these issues. We note that when the King of King’s Church in Tuam had major problems many people sought refuge in Abundant Life. When you are part of a large group you can put your head in the sand and just gloss over the ethical issues which would be raised by any normal person not least someone espousing the values of the gospel. Here it is our view many will actually believe that going from hotel to hotel is an exciting adventure and not an escape from the real world where people are responsible for their behaviour. No the cultist attitudes and tendencies mean that the person is subsumed under the group processes which dissolves their critical faculties. DONORS TOLD THE MORE THEY GAVE THE MORE GOD WOULD GIVE BACK

EXCLUSIVE, EXCLUSIVE

A CONTROVERSIAL church run by two money-loving pastors and exposed by the Sunday World has closed.

Abundant Life left its Galway premises this week after we revealed how American pastors Kevin and Heather Sanford were moving to Texas leaving behind massive debts. The church is expected to be wound up this month.

Rolex-wearing Kevin and wife Heather, who sports a € 10,000 diamond ring, gave their final service last Sunday. The Sunday World revealed how the couple, who bought a Spanish villa with the proceeds of their church, owed a previous Galway landlord € 80,000.

Followers

 The High Court ordered Kevin Sanford to pay up to € 160,000 in rent, fines and legal costs back in March. But instead of paying up, the couple, who demanded ten per cent of their followers’ earnings, announced they were quitting the country after being “told by God” to move to Texas. The Sunday World can reveal today one of the Dublin pastors who hopes to take over the Sanfords’ remaining 100 worshippers – there were 250 at its peak – is also being sued over money. Brendan and Sheila Hade were named by the Sanfords as their successors last month before the Sunday World exposé of Abundant Life. The Hades run the controversial Victory centre in Firhouse, Dublin, another church which teaches the prosperity gospel and which takes one- tenth of its followers’ incomes. Even though Brendan Hade was a director of Abundant Life between 2001 and 2003, he is now desperate to distance himself from the disgraced church. He plans to call his new Galway church Victory West. He refused to renew Abundant Life’s lease in Briarhill Business Park insisting his church find a new home. From today he will meet his new congregation in a Salthill hotel until a permanent home is found.

Auditorium

The Hades built a fabulous 1,000 seat auditorium in Dublin complete with restaurant, Starbucks café, TV studio, crèche and snooker hall. The 71-year-old minister is currently being sued in the High Court by three businessmen who claim they were ripped off by the pastor. They declined to speak this week. Hade has been involved in several controversial businesses over the years. He and his wife were shareholder directors in a technology company called Global Mobile Vision (GMV) which was launched in 2004 but went into voluntary liquidation the following year leaving creditors € 1.46 million out of pocket. Employees were owed € 160,000 in wages and the Revenue € 150,633. In 2005 independent TD Finian McGrath told the Dail that employees had approached him with allegations of exploitation, intimidation, breach of rights, dismissals, failure to pay staff, threats and sexual harassment. He said he was told of incidents where female members of staff were asked to give managers “hugs”. GMV was set up by another pastor couple, David and Esther Crownborn, friends of the Hades, who fled to Florida after the company collapsed. The Hades’ Victory church was also at one time one of the country’s biggest providers of asylum-seeker accommodation.

In 2004 it received € 2.5 million in State funding to house 240 refugees. It ran three centres: Kilmarnock House, Killiney, Co Dublin; Kilmacud House, Stillorgan, Co Dublin; and Milverton House, Carlow. Kilmarnock House, which accommodated 125 people, was closed temporarily in 2004 for health and safety issues.Victory lost its lucrative government contracts for Kilmacud House and Milverton House in 2006.

Electrician Brendan Hade began his church in 1989 with meetings in his Rathfarnham, Dublin, home and it rapidly expanded. One former follower said: “Hade is a good preacher and people believe what he says. But his message is all about giving. The more you give, the more God will give you back. “You can pay in cash or by credit card or standing order. I have seen people putting in huge amounts.

“Only certain people in the church have access to the books, while in most Christian churches the finances are very open. People can see what comes in and how it is used but not in Victory, which is run as a trust. “It’s a charity so they pay no tax.” The Victory church has played host to American TV evangelist Benny Hinn who people complained charged € 1,000 to pray and lay his hands on worshippers to “cure” them.

A spokeswoman for Brendan Hade said: “Victory is not taking over Abundant Life. Victory is opening a new Church in Galway and until we start we will not know how many people will attend.” Meanwhile, the Sunday World has spoken to more victims of Abundant Life and the disappearing Sanfords. One former follower, who donated up to € 30,000 to the church, said last night: “They were obsessed with money. Every sermon began with a long lecture about the need to give. The more you gave, the more blessings you would get from God.”

Brainwashed

Another former follower told how she was brainwashed by Heather Sanford, who even told her what to wear and who to talk to. “They preyed on vulnerable people.

My fear is that some of the women she has surrounded herself with, and who do all her work, will follow her to Texas,” said the ex-member. One ex-member said: “There is nothing Christian about them. Heather turns everyone into clones of herself.

“The men in the church have been emasculated. It is all about women and is so unhealthy. They are not encouraged to date.”

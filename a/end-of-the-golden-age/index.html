11 October 2010

End of the golden age

Scientology

This Bauhaus-inspired retreat was one couple’s dream home. But now it’s time to move on, writes Siobhán Maguire.

When Stacy Brooks and Bob Minton arrived in Ireland in January 2008, they realised immediately that they would retire here. The American couple had been living in Geneva, Switzerland, where their home was a hotel suite. When they struggled to find their perfect property, Minton suggested they fly to Dublin.

“Bob had already been to Ireland and thought the west was one of the most beautiful places he had seen,” says Brooks. “I had never been here before but as soon as we landed in Dublin, even looking out of the aeroplane, I had a good feeling about the country. “The plan was to take a road trip around Ireland so we could see as much of the country as we could. Instead, we rented a car and drove straight to Ashford Castle, fell in love with the area and never went any further.” The couple rented a cottage in Cong Co Galway, while they looked for a property to buy. After months of searching, they came across a holiday home in nearby Clonbur. Sitting on the banks of Lough Corrib, at Golden Bay, the house, of a Bauhaus-inspired design, ticked all their requirements. It sat on six acres of woodland, had access to the lake and was secluded without being isolated — the fishing towns of Clonbur and Cong are a short drive away. “In June 2008, we decided that this was the place that we wanted to live in,” says Brooks, who is from Atlanta. “Bob fell in love with it immediately. You feel as if there’s not a soul around, yet you’re only five minutes from the village of Cong. You can’t see the house from the road, either. You have to come up a long drive- way and then you suddenly come upon it.”

Brooks and Minton paid €1.3m for the property and spent a further €1.2m on renovations. A five-month facelift turned the house into a contemporary light-filled home with an open-plan living area, bespoke furnishings and even an art gallery. Sadly, tragedy struck earlier this year when Minton, a multi-millionaire credited with spending more than $10m (€7.3m) fighting the Church of Scientology, died suddenly at the age of 63. The retired banker, who suffered heart failure in January, had been healthy and active. For Brooks, 58, a writer, the loss of her companion of 12 years, was devastating. Now, nine months after his death, Brooks has decided to sell the home that the couple restored together. This was our dream home,” she says. “I love this place — he loved this place — but it’s not the two of us any more, it’s just me and I have to try to start a new life.”

The house is on the market for offers over €950,000, substantially less than the couple paid for it and less than the amount they spent on it. It has 5,000sqft of living space — a 1,500-sq-ft guest apartment was added—and landscaped gardens. The house was built in 1985, designed by a Dutch architect of the Bauhaus school, and built for a Dutch family who used the property as a holiday home. Although the building was structurally sound, Brooks and Minton wanted to put their stamp on the place. “When we bought it, Bob said we’d have to do a little painting to fix it up,” says Brooks. But the couple took on much more. “When we got the house, there were about 5,000 trees on close to seven acres,” says Brooks. “They were planted so close together that no light could come through. Many of the trees were unhealthy and it made the place feel very dark. “So we hired a local landscaper, Martin Flattery, who came in and cleared about 3,500 trees and in their place planted 9,000 plants. He reworked the grounds, creating beautiful rock gardens around the house and planting Japanese maples, dwarf rhododendrons, azaleas and ornamental grasses. He put new patios around the house and we even have Spear na Lugh, a 9ft-tall sculpture, in the garden.”

Inside, Brooks initially thought the house felt like a fortress. “I think it was built more as an urban dwelling than as a place out in the country. It had a brick wall when you drove up, so you couldn’t see the house, and this wall blocked out most of the views of the lake.” The couple tore down the brickwork and put up a new entrance made of glass. Inside, with the wall gone, light started to fill up the rooms. But Brooks and Minton wanted to go further: opening up the entire living space to create an open-plan design that incorporated the home’s original curved walls and the new flow of natural light. Again, they relied on a local tradesman, Peter Wall, and his staff to oversee the building work. Joanne Smyth, an interior designer from Galway, helped source furnishings. “The house has an open-plan centre, which is used for the dining room, living room and the den,” says Brooks. “We wanted an all-in-one room that opens up into the kitchen. We took down all the walls shrouding the house and put skylights into the big room, opening up the place completely to the light. “On either side are two wings: the master bedroom wing and a guest wing with three bedrooms. Then we added a separate apartment, which has its own entrance, which I use as my studio where I can paint.”

The open-plan design offers up plenty of different views: the woods, the gardens, the mountains and lakes. The house is set on the isthmus between Lough Corrib and LoughMask, two popular fishing lakes. “Even when you’re inside, you almost feel like you’re on the outside because the views are so visible,” says Brooks. “When we first bought the house, there were doors everywhere and the house felt shut in. I felt like the space was crying out to be opened up. We removed pine wood that covered part of the ceiling of the big room and that is now one big expanse.” The property has one final quirky feature: its own art gallery. “That was my idea,” says Brooks. “By the time we were nearly done with the renovation, I felt the curved walls, oak floors and open spaces resembled an art gallery and were crying out for some beautiful artwork.” Paintings were sourced from Stronach Gallery in Connemara, while Brooks, who had bought a loom, set about making wall hangings. Sculptures are also dotted around the house.

The renovation took five months but in December 2008, one week before Christmas, Brooks and Minton finally moved in. “It was the greatest Christmas ever,” she says. “I managed to get my Christmas tree up and we were finally living in our dream home.

“We have two dogs and three cats — Lizzy, our golden retriever puppy came that first Christmas—and it was a time for having friends over and taking long walks in the grounds of Ashford Castle with our dogs. My 81-year-old mother has even visited. This house is happiest when it’s full of people and entertaining.”

Brooks now plans to return to America, where she hopes to train as a yoga teacher. But giving up her lakeside retreat, or the communities of Clonbur and Cong, where Bob is fondly remembered, will not be easy. “I have made many good friends here so, even if I leave, I will always come back and stay in the area for a few months each year,” she says. “This house has so many happy memories for me. It deserves to have a happy couple or family living here.”

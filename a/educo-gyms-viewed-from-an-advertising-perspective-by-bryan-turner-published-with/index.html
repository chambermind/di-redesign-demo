Educo gyms viewed from an advertising perspective by Bryan Turner published with permission.

Tony Quinn

Mike Garde the Director of Dialogue Ireland received this paper from a former Scientologist who felt this study would be a good cross reference showing the elements of cultism found in the Tony Quinn organisation or its Educo subsidiary

INTRODUCTION

 

In this assignment I will be analysing an advertisement from the Kildare Post on Tuesday the 5th of October 2010. The advert is for Educogym, I will attempt to dissect both the subliminal meanings within the text and also the more obvious ones. I intend to carry this out in informed and factual fashion. Although I will be expressing my own opinions on the text it is my intention to base these not only on my own experiences but also on previously proven facts.

I chose the advert specifically because it was the first one to catch my eye as having something interesting to work with. The areas we will be looking closely at are

  • Product,
  • Target Audience,
  • Cost,
  • Colour,
  • Words,
  • Publication,
  • Context,
  • Denotation/Connotation,
  • Lighting,
  • Popular Culture,
  • Genre,
  • Institution/Agency,
  • Construction,
  • Shot/Composition,
  • Effectiveness.

I will attempt to delve into each area as in-depth as I can and derive as much information from this advert as possible.

Although producing notes is required there will not be many of these as I will be using a memetic approach to writing the report as this appears to work best for me, as opposed to taking notes and planning what is to be written I generally just start writing and allow the work to take its own course. I then edit any information that needs it. As notes I will alternatively be providing Newspaper clippings and any research materials used in the process of formulating an opinion.

 

PRODUCT

Educogym is a franchise gym company that are set up in many locations worldwide. They have a commercial presence in England, America, Ireland, Northern Ireland and even South Africa. The company claim to succeed where other gyms, Exercise programmes and diets fail. The system they use consists of several key elements

  • Goal setting and concentration;               This is designed to help you stay focussed while                           exercising
  • Personalised exercise;                                Focussed exercise programmes based on the client’s needs
  • Personal nutrition;                                                          Deciding between low carb low fat diets Etc
  • One on one consultation;                                                 Checking progress every six to eight weeks
  • Continuous updates;                                                                 Changing routines based on progress
  • Supervised Training;                                                                                      To help performance
  • Group dynamic;                                       Small groups designed to create positive atmosphere
  • Appointment sessions;                                                            Schedule helps maintain regular visits
  • Certified instructors;                                             Strained in nutrition exercise and mind technology
  • Specialised Equipment;                With new machine it is possible to train for 20 mins straight

To quote the Educogym website verbatim;

“The Educogym® atmosphere is like nothing you’ve experienced before. The focused approach and invigorating exercise produces a world class environment for success. The highly trained certified staff has been taught how to create the most conducive atmosphere that is both exciting and result orientated. The groups are kept small to ensure a level of supervision for maximum results. This gives you the benefits of personal training plus the advantage of the group dynamic for a fraction of the price. Your trainer will personalize your exercise and nutrition program and schedule a one to one consultation on a continuous basis. The small club environment is also friendly and welcoming to beginners.”

Even in the very initial stages of viewing the advertisement, several alarm bells went off with regard to integrity of the company I will go into detail on these issues later. However I do understand that advertising companies are renowned for using shady techniques when advertising so, as it may have been possible that this was not a specific idea from Educogym I decided to do some research into the company itself to see if my hunches were correct. As I delved in to the company I discovered the man behind this operation is none other than Tony Quinn the renowned “guru” and life coach.

Tony has been around the scene for quite some time now and appears to have been tapping into Neurolinguistic Programming techniques, which is now widely regarded among many scientific Idealists such as Michael Shermer of skeptic.Org as something of a pseudoscience. Many such “life coaches” use the training of the techniques in Neurolinguistic Programming (NLP) to amount masses of cash by preying on weak people with the offer of the perfect life through the power of “positive thought” and Tony is no different it would seem with many stories of his escapades popping up around the pages of the internet.

One such story (enclosed) from the October 11 Sunday World tells the story of a free recruitment seminar held in Naas where Tony is trying to sell his courses for extortionate prices with what would appear to be staged actors and stories about his healing massage techniques.

In short Tony Quinn the man behind Educogym would appear to be nothing short of a conman and a seller of false hopes to people who are easily drawn in.

PUBLICATION

 

“The Kildare Post is delivered to homes and businesses in the Kildare area, and is available in Newsagents, Convenience Stores, Shopping Centers, Hotels, Bars, Restaurants and more. The Kildare Post has the highest potential readership in the Kildare area, and offers a range of options for advertising to this growing county”

 

This advertisement was published in the Kildare post on Tuesday the 5th of October 2010. The Kildare post is a local newspaper which is handed out free In county Kildare. According to their website www.kildarepost.com

The Kildare Post is a river media newspaper production. It is a partnership newspaper between The Irish News and media entrepreneurs Padraig O’Dwyer and Tim Collins. The newspapers intention is to appeal to all ages but they believe to have a core demographic of 20 to 50 year old people. Its main focus is on women as it is believed they are the key drivers of household expenditure. The company does this in a bid to make this as profitable as possible to investors.

In terms of the advert we are dealing with, The Kildare Post was good choice to try and gain the interest of a large amount of people as the publication itself is not only free but also delivered to most households in the area, many people are more likely to have a copy and get to see the advert.

Section 1 – Construction

 

In this section I will attempt to understand the construction of the advertisement, from the formal codes of construction (mise-en-scene) down to the technical information of how and why the shots were taken. Which type of angles, shot type and lighting was used. I will also be looking at the body language and non verbal communication aspects of the pictures

Codes of composition.

 

The layout of the advertisement would be considered to be of symmetrical guidelines whereby the two main pieces of information are to be seen as equally important we have the picture on one side and the narrative on the other, both pieces of information take up roughly the same amount of space and are calling for equal amounts of attention. There is no background and no foreground as such everything appears to have an almost bullet point format which is in line with the aggressive formula used in the advertisement. The overall set up is in a portrait fashion which narrows our field of vision and gives the advert a sense of urgency and importance. To some extent we can consider the pictures themselves as props which enhance the benefits of what is on offer.

With regard to the static /dynamic composition of the article we can say with some certainty that it is a static composition as all the angles are either vertical or horizontal and everything seems to be in strict block format. If for some reason the format in the article had been at obscure angles or had a more freestyle loose composition to it we could regard it as being of dynamic nature.

N.V.C. body language

 

Within the imagery we are shown a set of before and after shots. If we look closely at the pictures we can start to see a pattern forming, most notably each of the before pictures are taken in particularly unflattering positions with slouched shoulders, hands down by the side and relaxed muscle being a strong continual format. Not only are these positions designed to allow the more fatty areas be more enhanced they also convey a submissive or unhappy persona. This is a common point raised in body language books such as PEOPLEWATCHING by Desmond Morris, UNDERSTAND BODY LANGUAGE by Gordon Wainright and Richard Thompson and BODY LANGUAGE (for dummies) by Elizabeth Kuhnke.

When we look at all of the after pictures taken we can see that every single picture is taken with hands on the hips, which draws attention to the gaps between the elbows and torso, shoulders back which elongates the muscles in the body and even in one case a facial makeover with the addition of makeup adding to the overall positive outcome look. In terms of body language again where these poses are concerned shoulders up and head straight demonstrate the positive outlook of a strong confident person. The smile which is on each after face is also indicative of this.

Technical codes

Shot size; The camera shot that is taken is a close up and appears to be cropped in order to negate the background completely. The effect of this shot ties in with the advertisements theme of being aggressive and in your face. The effect works perfect for the advert as any background information may take your attention away from the image which would not be a desired effect.

Camera angle; the camera angles used in each of these pictures is taken head on as though you are standing with the person practically in your personal space. It makes the add feel very immediate and also cleverly ties in with the overall theme.

Lighting; The lighting of the photo shoot is what is known as high key. This refers to the brilliance of the picture and the brightness. The individual pictures used in the advertisement were taken with quite a high element of brightness as though a spotlight was trained on the individual for the photo shoot.

Contrast; In terms of contrast this would be described as high contrast it has a high range of tones and gives a darker element to the areas with dark coloring and the brighter areas are accentuated by a more brilliant shade of white.

Focus; The type of focus used for these pictures is single focus, whereby the focus is on one single stationary object and it is the centre of attention, in this case it is the model being used.

Section 2 – audience

 

In this section I will attempt to understand the target audience and how they may be perceived by the company in charge of the advertising campaign.

In the demographic/psychographic area I will examine at a base level the attributes of the people this advert is made for as well as some of the deeper probing categorizations and assumptions.

In the positioning section I will examine how the company wishes you to feel when the advertisement is viewed by their target audience. Also I will attempt to decipher if the advert is hard sell which is more of a forceful approach which establishes the brilliance of a product. Or soft sell which merely implies that the product is available.

In the section modes of address I will try to understand whether or not the advert is carried out in the basis of a question, a statement, a command, as news or with emotional appeal.

In the section pop culture I will attempt to connect the advert to our current trends and analyze how it fits in with today’s society.

Finally in effectiveness I will relay how effective I feel the advertisement was on its target audience and also how it would be perceived by people who are not within the desired demographic.

Target audience – Demographic/Psychographic

We can learn a great deal about the companies target audience by paying attention to both the pictures and the way the text is laid out and also where the advertisement was placed. The demographic and psychographic of the people this advert is aimed at become very clear when we do this. Firstly the words Diet and Particularly around the middleare designed to catch the attention of someone who is trying to lose weight e

ven if they just happen to be just turning the pages these words will call out to the persons subconscious mind. These words are not only in red but they are the biggest words on the page. Secondly we see the words more results we can fairly reasonably assume here that this is going to appeal to perhaps people who have tried a few exercise and/or diet programs in the past and have not had the desired effect. Thirdly less time and great motivation which is designed to appeal maybe to the people hoping for the quick fix, which may also have limited patience and motivation and would possibly be weak prey for such an advertisement.

We can also learn something more about the target audience demographic by looking at the publication that the company decided to run the advert in. As discussed earlier in this text we are to understand that The Kildare Post has a target audience of people between the ages of 20 and 50 with a higher percentage to be preferably female, Also we know that the paper is free this would make it available to pretty much anyone from lower class all the way up to the top of the food chain.

When we look at the pictures in the advert, our information from the publication demographic is backed up again as we have 2/1 majority of female models with what seems like an early 20s to 50s age group with a token male model  as if to say it’s ok for guys too. Based on this information it is fairly reasonable to assume that;

  • DEMOGRAPHIC

Fairly broad spectrum here we are talking predominantly female with an age group of 20 – 50. Living in or near Kildare with any status from lower class up to upper class

  • PSYCOGRAPHIC

People looking to lose weight who have maybe tried a few gyms and diets and have maybe not had the motivation to keep it all going. They may be the type of person who is looking for a quick fix. One point on a personal level here that I would like to add is that the psychographic we are dealing with here is also the type of person who may sign up for a one year contract to a gym, change their mind a few months into the year and then be stuck with a bill for the rest of the year as is common practice among companies like this one as once you are signed up for a direct debit with a company like this it can be very hard to get out the contract. I find this quite disturbing.

 Target audience – Positioning

It is fairly clear from viewing this advertisement that it is a hard sell advert as it tends to boast the abilities of what the product can achieve, in addition to this the article boldly claims that “never before has a program produced such high results” therefore attempting to position itself above other gyms in the mind of the consumer. The advert I feel tries to position the audience into a state of guilt and perhaps even jealousy as it subconsciously conveys a message of both they can do it why can’t you mixed with a degree of look how good these people look now.

This advert implies that we could all look better and do a little more

 

Target audience – modes of address

The mode of address that is used here is an aggressively pushy domineering technique. It demands that you “don’t diet until you contact us” this is also backed up by the fact that the actors in the pictures are staring directly out at the page seemingly as if talking directly to us. In massive writing we also have LOSE WEIGHT which when put in context of the sentence it is in seems harmless however the fact that it is in bigger writing on its own independent line and even in a different color than the rest of the sentence it pretty much comes across as an order.

To some extent the advert could be seen as culturally specific for example if you are already a fit person who exercises regularly you may well look at this advert and laugh about the indescrepencies that you could easily spot from simply having a base level understanding of health and fitness. However if you are a little unfit the advert may act as a little reminder that perhaps you have not been doing enough leaving you with a feeling of self pity and guilt.

 Target audience – Pop culture

With regard to pop culture and how this advertisement connects to current trends I believe we can take an abstract view of the society we live in. Take for instance walking into a book shop today we are bombarded with a million and one self help books designed to promote “the best you possible” There seems also to be a rise in so called gurus who will happily help lead you in the way your life’s course for a fee of course. It would appear the single best currency generator at the moment is people’s desire and need to be seen as an individual or as a strong confident person. Our desire to be viewed as an individual of worth these days seems to have less of a requirement for depth of character. It seems to have more to do with people’s initial reaction to how you look, what you own, and other such material attributes. This could be due in part the rise of the so called “bling” mentality that is conveyed today in popular music. This could also have a lot to do with the fact that we now have an entire generation of people who have been brought up in the “good times” of the Celtic tiger who may have no clue what it is like to want for things. As this is the generation that most new products are marketed to this will have memetic effect as the evolution of culture takes these ideals and mixes them with the ways that are already in place. The result of this I believe is that we currently have a large amount of people who are desperately unhappy because they do not in their head match up to these supposed guidelines. This advertisement is a good indicator therefore of the times we live in today and of the willingness of our society to be made feel inadequate.

 Target audience – Effectiveness

In terms of effectiveness, I would have to say that I believe this advert is very effective. The target audience that this is being aimed at on a psychographic level is quite likely to be easily influenced by an advertisement of this nature. I would imagine that the thought that would come to mind when looking at this article would be “finally something that won’t take too much effort”.

I would have guessed just by looking at the advert that a few people have been taken in by it and a quick search of www.boards.ie using Educogym as a key word proves the very same point. It also proves the effectiveness of their advertising campaign as being fairly successful.

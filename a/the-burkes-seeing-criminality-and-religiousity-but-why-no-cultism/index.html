The Burkes: Seeing criminality and religiousity, but why not cultism?

ChristianEnoch BurkeThe Burke familyUncategorized

Enoch Burke is approaching a record-breaking prison sentence (almost 600 days at the time of writing) for contempt of court in Ireland and shows no sign of obeying the orders against him. The public’s perception of this as a case of religious freedom seems to be beginning to change; however, the cultism dimension at the family level continues to be missed.

Enoch Burke spent another Christmas in Mountjoy Prison, Dublin, for refusing to comply with a court order against him trespassing on the grounds of Wilson’s Hospital School, an injunction that’s been in place (and consistently ignored) since 2022.

Burke outside Wilson’s Hospital School (Source RTE)

Supporters of Burke’s still believe that he’s in prison for refusing to use correct pronouns when addressing a transgender student, and he’s the victim of an increasingly “woke” Irish society.

Even X/Tesla/Space X CEO Elon Musk waded into the fray and reshared a post containing false information about the case, and etho-nationalist British activist Tommy Robinson, who was recently released from prison in the UK after serving seven months of an 18-month sentence for contempt of court: “Irish teacher Enoch Burke has been dragged off to prison again, his ‘crime’? Refusing to abide by gender bending ideology” on X in November of this year.

As Burke is back inside prison again for the fourth time, some of his most ardent supporters— those who may believe his initial dismissal from the school was unjust— are beginning to recognise that Burke’s subsequent behaviour in the years since is the source of all his problems.

However, the Dialogue Ireland Burke Family Facebook Group is constantly under attack from a dedicated cultist support of 23,000 members from Enoch Burke support groups. Only about three comments have been able to address our request that you should only comment if you oppose the Burkes.

Overall, commentators seem unable to explain what they are seeing in front of them. Is it an issue concerning criminality, or is it an issue to be filed under “problematic religiousity”? A touch of both, maybe?

Nicola Tallant’s new Crime World podcast gets to the heart of the legal nature of the Burkes’ case (after a few opening minutes of Christmas holiday chat): the “contempt of court,” Enoch’s obstruction of justice, his (and his family’s) undermining and ignoring of the court’s authority and its orders.

Tallant, who was once very much aware of the issue of cultism and criminality, and many other people reporting on the case, seem to be overlooking one crucial element: Aspects of cultism and cultist behaviours within the Burke family.

In the opinion of Dialogue Ireland, the Burke family is demonstrating behaviours, as well as dynamics, that are hallmarks of what is seen in “cultist” relationships and contexts:

  • An insular, closed group resistant to outside influence and information.
  • Recognising no authority other than their own.
  • Encouraging members to behave in ways that disadvantage them.
  • Us vs them mentality.

Analysing the Burkes through the lens of cultism, as well as potential radicalisation at the family level, may be the key to understanding what’s driving the Burkes.

It seems like a few people on the Internet (mainly Reddit) are making the right connections

Cult expert Janja Lalich, in her 2006 book Take Back Your Life: Recovering from Cults and Abusive Relationships, writes about how family units can become like, or display similar characteristics, group “cults”, with the parent of the family (whether that be male or female) fulfilling the criteria for a “cult leader”:

In a cultic system the boundaries of knowledge are shut tight and reinforced through resocialization processes, the use of ideology, and the institutionalization of social controls. The goal of this profound worldview shift is the reconstruction of personality. The ultimate aim is to get the devotee to identify with the “socializing agent”—the cult leader, the patriarch or matriarch of the cult, or the controlling and abusive partner, as the case may be.

Family matriarch, Martina Burke, is an experienced teacher of over thirty years, who homeschooled all ten of her children, many of whom went on to excel academically in multiple fields. While this environment allowed for each member to succeed academically and professionally, ideologically, it would have provided the correct conditions for indoctrination.

Lalich comments on the ‘self-sealing’ nature of groups that are displaying cultist tendencies, and how the ideology can become so entrenched in these contexts that it does not allow for contradictory information to penetrate it or undermine it:

A self-sealing system is one that is closed in on itself, allowing no consideration of disconfirming evidence or alternative points of view. In the extreme, a self-sealed group is exclusive, while its belief system is all-inclusive, in the sense that it provides answers to everything.

The Burkes appear to be an insular family group, acting in unison, ideologically in complete accord, and resistant—or acting hostile towards—influence and information outside of their own belief system.

They are often mischaracterised as a conservative “evangelical” family, but seem to have no affiliation with any church; therefore, they subscribe to a belief system of their own, one that mother, Martina, is said to “fight like a dog” for.

The Executive Director of the Evangelical Alliance Ireland, Nick Park, recently criticized Enoch’s “not very Christian” behavior as well as his refusal to follow court orders.

The Burkes have a long history of refusing to play ball, recognising no authority other than their own.

In 2023, Ammi Burke (a solicitor by trade) and her mother, Martina Burke’s courtroom behaviour in the hearing of Ammi’s unfair dismissal from Arthur Cox Law Firm, resulting in the case being dismissed.

Speaking to the Irish Examiner, Ms Justice Marguerite Bolger described the court’s reaction ot Emmi’s conduct as “…horrified that any litigant, and in particular a qualified solicitor, would conduct themselves in this manner before the court.”

The Burkes responded by telling the judge, “God will judge you.”

And it’s not just judges that are on the Burkes’ list of people they’re up against: politicians supporting gay rights, other Christians, university societies, and chief medical officers, the Burkes seem to be taking on, or have taken on, anybody who crosses them professionally, or ideologically, demonstrating a strong “us versus them” mentality:

‘Cults differ from other groups with religious, philosophical, or cultural beliefs and norms. One major difference is that cults impose an us-versus-them mentality that is characterized by isolationism, elitism, secrecy, and a fear of outsiders that can border on paranoia. This places a heavy burden on children interacting with the “evil” outside world’ Lalich writes, ‘The group has a polarized, us-versus-them mentality, which may cause conflict with the wider society. The leader is not accountable to any higher authorities (unlike, for example, teachers, military commanders, or ministers, priests, monks, and rabbis of mainstream religious denominations).’

A history of public outbursts and proactive campaigns against LGBTQ+ causes has led to the Burkes being compared to the infamous, funeral-picketing “GOD HATES FAGS” Westboro Baptist Church. (In 2023, the head of the Westboro Baptist Church, Fred Phelps Jr, publicly criticised Enoch’s non-compliance with the courts, showing the Burkes are on a level of cultism beyond any other.)

Whether they’re being forcibly removed from courtrooms, finding themselves in trouble for assaulting female gardai, or engaging in other acts of self (or family) sabotage, the Burkes are consistently behaving in ways that disadvantage individual members, but seem to further the ideological objectives of the group.

Burkes being removed from court (Source: RTE)

They seem to welcome controversy, and with the media circus that ensues, the opportunity to reinforce the notion that they are being persecuted for their religious beliefs. A “narrative” that they’ve systematically tried to curate with things like paid Facebook ads.

Burkes being removed from court (Source: RTE)

There’s currently no end in sight or solution on the horizon.

Burke has begun the year by seeking an injunction against members of the disciplinary board that initially dismissed him from his position at Wilson’s Hospital School. As of January 8, the disciplinary appeals body has decided to postpone its review of Burke’s case.

With Burke in jail (again) and the costs to the state increasing, there is no sign that Burke is changing his approach anytime soon, leaving the situation stuck in an endless cycle of courtrooms and prison cells.

Dialogue Ireland’s Mike Garde has suggested that a course of ‘deradicalisation”‘ whilst in prison is what’s required to break the patterns of behaviour. With Enoch temporarily separated from his family’s influence, it would be an ideal time to engage and have Burke critically examine the situation.

Garde has offered to meet with Burke in Mountjoy Prison in an attempt to begin a process of deradicalisation, but his letters have gone unanswered.

However, any exits from cultist situations are a painful and difficult process for former members. People growing up in families that could be described as ‘cultist’ have a particularly tough time, writes Lanja Lalich:

People leaving family cults also have a unique departure experience. When a person leaves a larger cult and most one-on-one cults, often she will have a fractured community to return to: some family, old friends, and so on. In contrast, someone leaving a family cult is leaving behind everyone and everything they knew, similar to the situation of someone born or raised in a cult.

On the 15th of January, Enoch Burke was released from prison to prepare his case against the Disciplinary Appeals Panel (DAP). Again, the release comes with the condition that he would abide by the court order and would not trespass on his former place of work.

Unsurprisingly, Enoch Burke had this to say:

“If I am released from this court today, I will be at my workplace tomorrow,” he said.

“If I am released from prison, my duty is to be at my workplace.”

No prizes for guessing what happened next.

Burke returns to Wilson’s Hospital School (Source: RTE)

As of today, (16/01/26), he appears in court again, with a clear sound of a prison door closing behind him.

Here we go yet again.

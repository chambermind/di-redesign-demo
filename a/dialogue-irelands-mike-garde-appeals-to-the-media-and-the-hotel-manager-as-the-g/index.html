On Deaf Ears: Dialogue Ireland’s Mike Garde Emails to Media and Hotel Management About Week-Long Scientology Exhibition Go Unanswered

CCHRCultismScientology

An exhibition featuring a collection of works detailing the “atrocities” committed by psychiatry found a temporary home at Dublin’s Gresham Hotel. The public may see this as a bit of “dark tourism” coming to the city, but only if the organisation behind the exhibition wasn’t Scientology, which has its own long history of physical and mental abuse. It’s obviously a recruitment drive, but why is the media not reporting this?

IMPORTANT: AS OF 14/04/2026, NEITHER THE GRESHAM HOTEL NOR ANY MEDIA OUTLET HAS RESPONDED TO ANY OF THE CONCERNS MIKE GARDE HAS RAISED.

From the 21st to the 27th of February, the Gresham Hotel in Dublin, Ireland, hosted Scientology’s anti-psychiatry exhibition, Psychiatry: An Industry of Death, which continues founder L. Ron Hubbard’s war on the field of psychiatry and positions Scientology as the remedy to all modern mental illness.

The Citizens Commission on Human Rights (CCHR), the organisation behind the exhibition (and one of Scientology’s many fronts), states that its purpose is to “eradicate abuses committed under the guise of mental health and enact patient and consumer protections“, abuses that range from the atrocities committed by the Nazis in the Second World War, right up until the misdiagnosis of modern mental illnesses.

Scientology’s CCHR logo

The itinerant exhibition, which also has a permanent home at the CCHR’s headquarters in Hollywood, and shares its name with a 2006 documentary (with an underwhelming 4.7 score on IMDb), in the words of the CCHR, is “an eye-opening, in-person exhibition that dives deep into the controversial side of psychiatry. Explore compelling stories, powerful visuals, and facts that challenge the mainstream narrative. Whether you’re curious or passionate about mental health topics, this event is a must-see. Don’t miss the chance to experience it firsthand and get a fresh perspective!”

The exhibition is the site of, and a contributing cause to, BBC journalist John Sweeney’s highly publicised 2007 screaming fit at Scientology spokesperson Tommy Davis. According to Sweeney, he had been subjected to Scientology harassment and intimidation techniques throughout the filming of the documentary. Still, it was their attempt to reframe the Holocaust that was the final straw:

Scientologists want “the global obliteration” of psychiatrists, who they say were to blame for the rise of Nazi Germany. To prove their point, they showed me hideous images of people having needles stuffed in their eyeballs, of patients undergoing electric shocks and having their brains operated on.

Sickening, nasty and wholly unconvincing – modern psychiatry, for all its faults, is not Nazi and to press the point in the way that Scientology does devalues the horror of the Holocaust.

Ironically or not, it was in the “Mind Control” section of the exhibition that I lost it.

Scientology’s issue with psychiatry began in the early 1950s, when L. Ron Hubbard’s “new science”—Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health—was catching the public’s attention as well as raising eyebrows of professionals in the scientific communities. In his 1951 review for the American Journal of Orthopsychiatry, American psychiatrist Richard L. Jenkins wrote:


Dianetics impresses the reviewer as the unacknowledged offspring of a union of important elements drawn from Christian Science with other elements drawn from the lunatic fringe of dynamic psychiatry. Perhaps we should be humble to see some of the characteristics of our field so clearly portrayed with the bold lines of caricature-the authoritative dogmatism, the exaggerated claims of scientific character, the substitution of personal testimonial for scientific evidence, the schematic oversimplification in accounting for all problems in terms of early experience unavailable to consciousness, the invariable pat solution in the release of these experiences, here called “engrams,” the lofty certainty displayed toward the skeptic.

Dianetics (source: Wikimedia Commons)

During Scientology’s infancy, Hubbard viewed medicine and psychiatry as rival disciplines. However, as Scientology would eventually move from a “science” to a “religion, mainstream psychiatry’s initial reception of Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health was never forgotten by Hubbard, and provided him and his burgeoning religion with one of its first major enemies. According to Spiegel International:

In a 1969 article, “Today’s Terrorism,” published in a Scientology journal, Hubbard claimed that “the psychiatrist and his front groups operate straight out of the terrorist textbooks. The Mafia looks like a convention of Sunday school teachers compared to these terrorist groups.” The psychiatrist, Hubbard went on, “kidnaps, tortures and murders without any slightest police interference or action by western security forces.” Later, Hubbard wrote that, in society, “there’s only one remedy for crime — get rid of the psychs! They are causing it!”..

Exhibition visitor warning (source: Wikimedia Commons)


Mental health professionals internationally are concerned about the exhibition’s use of scare tactics, and see its core message that mental illness and addiction are misdiagnosed and do not require medication as dangerous, especially when it’s being spread in communities where drug addiction is rife.


“It could arguably be construed as irresponsible to frame psychiatrists as a non-resource for people experiencing difficulty with mental illness and/or addiction,” said Jonny Morris, senior research director at B.C.’s Canadian Mental Health Association. “They are a critical resource.”


Concerned about Scientology—with its long, well-documented history of abusing current and former members, as well as viciously going after its detractors—targeting Ireland’s population, especially those who may be suffering from mental health issues, susceptible to undue influence, and therefore vulnerable to recruitment, Dialogue Ireland’s Mike Garde wrote the following email to the Gresham Hotel’s management:

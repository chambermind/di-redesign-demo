The Origins of Tony Quinn at Templeogue House

Tony Quinn

Tony Quinn, Sexual assault, Templeogue, patterns of cultism buried for 40 years. This building was the base for Quinn’s activities in the early Seventies.  The original building, known as Templeogue House, became a residence for the Columban Fathers and was known as St. Columban’s.

Quinn rented this building from Cramptoms the builders for nearly 2 years from mid-1973 after the Columban Fathers vacated the building.  This was Quinn’s base when he first appeared in a string of highly critical expose articles by newspapers in the Seventies.

Quinn crammed more than 50 rent-paying devotees into squalid living conditions.  As the building itself became more and more dilapidated Cramptoms were glad to see the back of Quinn and his circus.  The building was eventually given a major refurbishment and part of it was demolished.  The building was the operations centre from which Quinn built up his businesses, including Yoga classes, health foods, etc.  This was no ordinary business enterprise but was built on a completely cultist basis where Quinn’s word was law, as a self-proclaimed messiah, and exploitation and oppression by Quinn was the norm.  Unpaid labour and extremely poor working conditions were suffered by devotees who believed in the “spiritual” vision propounded by Quinn.  For many female members of the Yoga group the exploitation extended to severe sexual exploitation usually justified as being important for their “spiritual progress”.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2010/01/12/tony-quinn-is-sued-for-‘sex-assault’-by-nicola-tallant-sunday-world/

Many young people were ruthlessly exploited, lost years off their lives, suffered financially and emotionally and when Quinn was forced to move about 30 of the group moved with him to a smaller commune in 5 Church St. Howth where the pattern of exploitation and oppression continued.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2009/09/07/hicks-tower-malahide-co-dublin/

When the spiritual vision failed to materialise, Quinn turned on his devotees and mutated his enterprise into a “success” system of self-help with options of “spiritual” benefits for those interested in them.  Quinn also dabbled in hypnotherapy and NLP courses to get “qualifications” for cover for his activities.  As a dyed-in-the wool occultist he continues to practice his occult methods, not just the nicey-nice bland “spiritual” blarney about “Song of Life”, etc, behind the scenes and proclaims his warped “messiahship” to core believers.

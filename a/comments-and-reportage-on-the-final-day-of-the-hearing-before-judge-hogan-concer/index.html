Comments and Reportage on the final day of the hearing before Judge Hogan concerning Depositions

Tony Quinn

Counsel for Mr Garde, Séamus Ó Tuathail SC outlines the reasons Mr Garde should not be deposed.

JUDGMENT HAS been reserved in a case which the High Court has heard raises “key issues” concerning journalists’ protection of their sources. However, Justice Hogan hopes to give his decision next Tuesday 18, allowing appeals to go ahead.

The High Court has been asked to make orders compelling Sunday World journalist Nicola Tallant and Mike Garde, the Director of Dialogue Ireland, a charity trust which helps victims of cults, to give evidence in Ireland which will be used in an action involving businessman Tony Quinn in Denver, Colorado. Both are opposed to the application.

The US action arises from the sale of shares in International Natural Energy (INE) LLC, a company in which Mr Quinn is a shareholder.

Former INE director Jean Cornec has sued INE, its chairwoman Susan Morrice and the firm’s directors and affiliates as he claims he has not been paid in full for a $15 million sale of his shares in the firm to Ms Morrice.

The claims are denied and, in a counterclaim, the defendants allege Mr Cornec breached their contract of sale by engaging in wrongful conduct, including a campaign of disparagement designed to damage the reputation of the defendants, including Mr Quinn. Mr Cornec denies the allegations. As part of the proceedings, due to be heard in Denver in January, lawyers for the defendants in the US case secured orders from a court in Denver requesting the Irish High Court to grant orders that Ms Tallant and Mr Garde be deposed as witnesses in the case.

Counsel for Mr Garde, Séamus Ó Tuathail SC, said his client’s right to receive and impart information should be protected.

Again should his client be deposed Séamus Ó Tuathail said,  “there would be a chilling effect which could put people off dealing with Dialogue Ireland. Séamus Ó Tuathail indicated that Garde because of his expertise in the area of cultism had chronicled the activities of Quinn for decades and having to reveal confidential information would undermine his ability to perform his function as Director of the Dialogue Ireland Trust”

These include assisting and accompanying those people heavily affected by groups that have removed their critical thinking and they feel that they can’t speak as doing so may have terrible effects on them. They are not members of a society but purely instruments in the hands of the cult leader. Many have been reduced to penury. These contacts must remain absolutely confidential and what they speak about must remain protected. So privilege is claimed in regard to the client adviser relationship.

Séamus Ó Tuathail stressed that he would rely on the submissions already made by Brian O’Moore SC in regard to journalistic privilege.

He stressed that Dialogue Ireland had a minor journalistic function.  Fundamentally they do publish some commentary but their main function is to act as the most extensive archive in Western Europe in regard to cultism. They are really in the business of republishing other peoples material, and trying to preserve articles which are a short period become hidden behind a pay wall or totally disappear. The Sunday world has a limited archive and most of its material is lost by the Monday after publication. Closer inspection of their web site suggests that it has limited coverage of material.

(Also the DI archive is like a library and it is one of the main ways people recover from their involvement with a cult. Their problem is not generally a psychiatric one, but a deficit of information. Once they find out the truth they are freed. It is similar to being in a state like North Korea, the truth is censored. So unlike commercial traditional print media, the more hits DI get the less revenue they obtain. Their whole rationale is educative and charitable. So the view that they are a threat to the traditional media is misplaced.)

The defendants claim Mr Garde and Ms Tallant have evidence that is relevant to their counter claim. Yesterday, after the conclusion of submissions by all parties, Mr Justice Gerard Hogan said he was reserving his decision to later this month.

Gary Davis of the Data Protection Commission indicated in 2011 when DI was given two related requests for data for both Quinn and Morrice that whereas the print media is covered by privilege in regard to the 1988 Act, currently electronic media is not. However, it was clear as Justice Hogan clarified it is not oppressive to seek for one’s personal data what was clear though was that the purpose of the exercise in 2011 as emails inadvertently sent to DI indicated was to gain information about our clients. The Commission denied this right and the documents were redacted to emphasise this.

In a submission, Brian O’Moore SC, for Ms Tallant, said the evidence being sought was not necessary for the Denver case. He said information was being sought for the purpose of “beefing up” separate defamation proceedings brought by Mr Quinn arising from articles Ms Tallant had written.

Counsel for the Susan Morrice, Jim O’Callaghan SC, again started his summary by producing a new file and this had to be challenged.

Willi Stewart: Controversy following former C. of I. cleric’s resignation from English parish

Abuse by Church and Irish institutions

CHURCH OF IRELAND GAZETTE  Home News FRIDAY 23 NOVEMBER 2012 – 5

Controversy following former C. of I. cleric’s resignation from English parish

PDF:

willie stewart 1

The Revd Will Stewart, formerly of the Church of Ireland’s CORE Church in Dublin until 2004,

resigned at the beginning of this month as rector of the Chet Valley Benefice in Norfolk.

The Bishop of Thetford, the Rt Revd Alan Winton, said that there had been a campaign focused on “a matter” from Mr Stewart’s past. This concerned a sexual offence against a man aged 20, which had given rise to his resigning from CORE Church.

In a Dublin court, Mr Stewart pleaded guilty to the offence but, the judge ruling in accordance with probation law provisions, no criminal record ensued.

When information about Mr Stewart was posted on the Internet, the Diocese of Norwich consulted Norfolk police who advised that the offence had not been against a child or vulnerable adult and that, therefore, the matter was to be regarded “as a diocesan responsibility to manage in accordance with its normal procedures”.

In a statement, Bishop Winton said that while a number of parishioners had been supportive of Mr Stewart and his family, the rector had increasingly felt The Revd Willi Stewart that “under the present circumstances” he was no longer able to continue his ministry. The Bishop added: “In appointing William Stewart, the Diocese followed the normal procedure of seeking assurance from the Church of Ireland that he was a priest in good standing, and a CRB [Criminal Records Bureau] check was made and returned clear.”

Last week, the since retired Archbishop of Dublin, Dr John Neill, said that, whilst extending pastoral support to Mr Stewart and his family when he resigned from CORE Church, he “consistently refused to give him clearance to apply for any further ministry in Ireland or overseas”.

Bishop Alan Winton

Nonetheless, some time after leaving CORE, Mr Stewart was issued with a licence by the Bishop of Cashel and Ossory; the Rt Revd Michael Burrows.

In a statement supplied to the Gazette, Bishop Burrows said:

“The Revd William Stewart was permitted to exercise a limited and local ministry in the Diocese of Cashel and Ossory in a non-stipendiary capacity from 2008 to 2011, after having obtained extensive and appropriate professional advice following the conclusion of the court case in Dublin in 2005.

“When his move to England took place last year, there was full disclosure to the Norwich diocesan authorities of the nature of his ministry here and in particular in respect of the full facts relating to the court case.

“Nothing was, as far as I am concerned, concealed or unacknowledged. This naturally included the outcome of his vetting prior to departure by the Irish police.”

Asked to confirm, whether or not he had sought advice from the previous Bishop under whom Mr Stewart had served, namely the then Archbishop of Dublin, Bishop Burrows told us: “I sought extensive and appropriate professional advice concerning Mr Stewart, and with his [Mr Stewart’s] co-operation. I naturally sought other informed pastoral advice as well, and full details concerning this have to remain confidential.

“Having taken extensive pastoral and professional advice into account, I had to make a decision concerning the appropriateness of permitting a limited, local and mentored ministry in the context of the lapse of time since the completion of the court case.”

Invitation to Round table with Director of EU Fundamental Rights Agency, Friday 16th November at 9.15am.

We would like to invite you to a round table meeting with the Director of the EU Fundamental Rights Agency (FRA), Morten Kjaerum, next Friday 16th November.

The Director is visiting Ireland to meet with Government departments and other institutions, ahead of Ireland’s EU Presidency Jan-June 2013.

FRA attaches great importance to its partnerships and engagement with civil society in each member state and at EU level. The meeting will provide an opportunity for civil society organisations to find out more about FRA’s work and current initiatives and to brief Morten on issues of concern for you in the fundamental rights area.

Where:           EU Commission, Molesworth St., Dublin 2

When:             Friday 16th November at 9.15 am – 10.30am

Proposal for meeting on November 16 with FRA Director, Morten Kjaerum.

Dialogue Ireland frames its work in assisting the victims of cults within a human rights context:

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/about/

We are members of the FRA as is our European wide body Fecris. Through our representative Friedrich Griess we have been outlining issues connected to cultism  at the meetings of the FRA.

http://fra.europa.eu/fraWebsite/research/publications/publications_per_year/2012/pub-annual-report-2012_en.htm

He has already submitted reports on the following aspects of the report above:

•           Chapter 4: The rights of the child and protection of children was based on the report made by Redd Barna, Norway, see http://griess.st1.at/DOCS-45355-v2-Report_Regardless_of_Faith.pdf

•           Chapter 5: Equality and non-discrimination (with special emphasis on the abuse of religious freedom to violate other human rights) – was that cults abuse the freedom of religion and violate other human rights, see http://griess.st1.at/gsk/fecris/osce/Griess.pdf  and http://griess.st1.at/gsk/fecris/Charter.htm

 http://www.fecris.org/

We are concerned that the Irish State has not put any protections in place to assist the victims of cultism*

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/02/04/dail-question-addressed-to-the-minister-for-justice-and-equality-mr-shatter-by-deputy-paschal-donohoe/

This leaves the citizen very isolated and vulnerable to religious groups who use freedom of religion as a means to deny some particular human right.

Ireland has just passed a Referendum on Children which enhances the Rights of Children. Take a serious case where because of the beliefs of the Jehovah’s Witnesses a child needs a blood transfusion. This has happened on a number of occasions over the years.  Before yesterday the case could be taken straight to a High Court judge. Whereas, the Referendum has increased the overall protection of Children it has increased the need to legislate specifically in a number of areas where the High Court would have intervened before.

Since then and not connected to the Referendum but still exposing the legislative deficit in this country is the tragic death of Savita Halappanavar.

 Old

Article 42.5                                                  

In exceptional cases, where the parents for physical or moral reasons fail in their duty towards their children, the State as guardian of the common good, by appropriate

means shall endeavour to supply the place of the parents, but always with due regard for the natural and imprescriptible rights

New

Article 42A.2.1°

In exceptional cases, where the parents, regardless of their marital status, fail in their duty towards their children to such extent that the safety or welfare of any of their

children is likely to be prejudicially affected, the State as guardian of the common good shall, by proportionate means as provided by law, endeavour to supply the place of the parents, but always with due regard for the natural and imprescriptible rights of the child.

The problem is that there are no laws in place and as a result children will be vulnerable to harm. This is an example of where there is confusion in regard to the rights of a religious body and the human rights of their members.

Also we are a lot clearer about the damage done to children and the violation of their rights in the sexual sphere by adults. We are not quite as clear about the idea of vulnerable adults who are subject to undue influence resulting from some ideological, or religious conditioning.

Some groups will allow their own internal code like say Canon Law subvert the criminal law. We are concerned about the loss of freedom and in some cases the impoverishment of those who have lost their critical ability through cult involvement is not understood or responded to by the Irish State. We are aware of persons who have been very badly damaged by their involvement with such groups.

An unintended consequence of a legal action we were involved with recently was that a High Court judge protected the work of Dialogue Ireland in educating the public.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/09/21/final-version-cornec-v-morrice-ors-judgment-by-hogan-j/

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/09/23/the-importance-of-justice-hogans-ruling-for-the-work-of-dialogue-ireland/

It is important to assist victims that this type of protection is given by the constitution.

So we welcome your visit to Dublin and hope it will further the on going work we need to engage with to assist those affected.

*Whenever we use the words ‘cult’, ‘cultism’ or ‘cultist’ we are referring solely to the phenomenon where troubling levels of undue psychological influence may exist. This phenomenon can occur in almost any group or organisation.

 

Mike Garde Director Dialogue Ireland November, 11, 2012

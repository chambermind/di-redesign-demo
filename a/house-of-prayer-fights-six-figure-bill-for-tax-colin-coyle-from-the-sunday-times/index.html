House of Prayer fights six-figure bill for tax Colin Coyle From The Sunday Times Feb 14, 2010

Conferences & CommentariesHouse of Prayer

The House of Prayer, a retreat established by Christina Gallagher, a self-styled Marian visionary, is appealing against the removal of its charitable tax status in an effort to avoid a six-figure bill.

http://www.timesonline.co.uk/tol/news/world/ireland/article7026389.ece

The Revenue Commissioners revoked the Co Mayo retreat’s special tax status in 2008. This followed revelations that Gallagher had “amassed great wealth” from donors, some of whom claimed that their contributions had been used to fund the former housewife’s lifestyle.

Newly filed accounts for the House of Prayer say that if the Revenue Commissioners’ decision is not overturned in respect of the years 2002 and 2003, there could be a potential liability of about €125,000, before interest and penalties.

The Revenue said last week that “a body may be liable for tax for a period when charitable tax exemption was in place, but subsequently withdrawn, in circumstances where terms and conditions had not been fulfilled”.

The loss of the House of Prayer’s charitable status does not affect the company’s tax bill in more recent years, as it has made an operating loss. Last year it lost €177,536, while in 2008 losses totalled €97,110.

While the centre received €289,913 in donations last year and sold €186,058 worth of “religious objects”, its expenses rose. Wages and salaries amounted to €267,783, while it also had legal fees of €56,087 and paid out consultancy fees of €11,979.

General expenses amounted to €30,134, while repairs to property owned by the centre cost €31,161. Profit from a monthly lottery at the converted convent on Achill Sound also fell; it raised €9,442 last year, compared with €86,417 in 2008.

Many of the House of Prayer’s donations are now believed to come from outside Ireland, including America, where Gallagher operates six other similar prayer centres.

The self-proclaimed mystic, who claims to have apocalyptic visions, was forced to return about €250,000 to two donors last year after they initiated legal action.

The operation of the centre, established in 1993, was investigated by gardai after complaints by former devotees, but the director of public prosecutions decided against taking legal proceedings.

Michael Neary, the archbishop of Tuam, has said that it has “no ecclesiastical approval” and banned priests from saying mass in the former convent. A former employee is suing the House of Prayer, claiming that he was denied medical attention after falling from a ladder there in 2008. Anthony Tierney, now living in England, alleges that he was locked in a room for a weekend.

Other former devotees claim they were coerced into donating money to Gallagher.

Gallagher’s spiritual adviser, Father McGinnity, held fundraising events in nursing homes where the audience was encouraged to donate cash to the House of Prayer to avoid an “apocalypse”.

Although Gallagher has no recognisable source of income, she is the registered owner of a house in Newport, Co Mayo, and joint owner of a property in Knockmore, also in Mayo. She bought a €1m property outside Ballina in 2006. She is not listed as a director of the retreat.

In a country which does not understand what cultism is, and where the following Response to a parliamentary question was given by the Minister of Justice it is clear we have still a long journey to travel to assist the survivors and victims of cults:

* To ask the Minister for Justice and Equality if his attention has been drawn to the danger posed by the development of cults within Ireland; if he has a working group or plan in relation to this issue; and if he will make a statement on the matter.

– Paschal Donohoe TD Dublin Central

REPLY.
The Deputy will appreciate that the word ‘cult’ is a chameleon-hued word that may gets its colours in any given context from the subjective views of the speaker, and that there are no clear, specific, manageable, and universally accepted criteria for identifying cults.

Also in our state where there has not been the development of a civic society, we do not find doctors, lawyers or the media who understand the phenomenon of cultism. Generally you will hear comments like it is a question of free choice and Article 44 protects the Freedom of Religion. There little realisation that the issue is not freedom of religion but the abuse of human rights and severe conditioning which reduce intelligent people to state of total dependency. Comments which are equally common are. “If they joined that group they must be a bit thick, they deserve it.” They are adults etc.

In this book published at the end of 2009 Jim Gallagher has illuminated the dark recesses of the House of Prayer. During the period he wrote the book, he was vilified and because what he wrote was in the Sunday World it was assumed it was lies.
However, the Sunday World has been one of the only newspapers to take on cultist groups and what is more they have great photos which are worth a thousand words.

Also folk Catholics who are totally unaware of the teaching of the Catholic Church were easy prey to the manipulations of Christina Gallagher. Also many were persuaded by the tortured soul Fr McGinnity whose experience of being cast out of the centre of the Church because he raised issues of abuse at Maynooth led him to endorse Gallagher. They were co dependent. In Achill he was a big fish in a small pond, in his parish he is a small fish in a big pond!
The authorities in Armagh for what ever reason refuse to exercise good authority and suspend him for involving himself in the House of Prayer which is not a recognised Catholic entity.

Jim is not remotely interested in theology, which is his strength. Once people try to engage on the level of these visions and Fr McGinnity’s learning rationality goes out the window, hence so many unlettered and folk Catholics lost their way. As her cohort is elderly they are not able to be reached by the overwhelming evidence this book represents and the archive we hold on our web and blog. It is generally the younger generation who are trying to reach these people who are nearly beyond the reach of normal dialogue.

The significance of the narrative of this book goes beyond the House of Prayer. It is illustrative of the grooming, gradual loss of ones ability to think critically and the attendant tendency to give away lots of cash and ones life to the Guru, leader who is behind the group. There are different theological or ideological emphases but we find the same attitudes in Buddhist, Scientology, Tony Quinn, Self Help or so called visionaries.

So let me stress the importance of this book to all struggling to understand what has happened to them or a member of their family. The fact that the House of Prayer has an Irish flavour will help to recognise the same traits in the groups you are struggling with.

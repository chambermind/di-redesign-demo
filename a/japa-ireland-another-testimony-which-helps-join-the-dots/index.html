Japa YogaTherapy

What is Japa Meditation?

Japa yoga is an ancient practice involving the repetition of a mantra. In the word mantra, “man” means “mind” and “tra” means “to protect”. Mantra is a way of protecting and watching your mind.  When mantra is initiated by a teacher and pronounced with love, vigour and devotion, it will unleash the true power of a person. Mantra strengthens the mind and can change the direction of one’s life. Depending on the nature of the practitioner it can bring blessings and enrichment.

This spiritual practice requires combining the watching of one’s mind, with personal development and the learning of different philosophies like Vedanta. Vedanta is an ancient philosophy that speaks about the oneness of existence, harmony of religions and the divinity of the soul. Vedanta requires patience, is full of life recipes and contains amazing truths.

How Japa came to Ireland

My name is Dennis Curran and I am a healer and Japa meditation teacher. I have been practicing Japa yoga since 1998. The word Japa originates from Sanskrit, one of the most ancient languages on earth.  “Ja” means to change life and death cycles and “pa” means to cleanse sin.

In February 1998 I met my teacher who inspired me to attain my spiritual practice. That combined with the ancient philosophy of Vedanta has changed my life.

My teacher taught me mantras and introduced me to Vedanta. “Veda” means knowledge and “anta” an end. It means to use your knowledge to the right end. I was moved by the power of sound. Mantras work on body, mind and soul. Mantras gave me space within myself and a clarity of mind I had never experienced before. The journey I began then was amazing and wonderful. I connected with my soul and learnt how to control my mind. Soon, my wife and children followed in my footsteps and we started to experience a union with each other that was blessed with the presence of Spirit. God started to make a lot more sense.

Testimony 2

My introduction to Japa Ireland was due to a lady I had known and trusted for some years. We met by chance and, after I confided in her that I was going through a bit of a ‘midlife crisis’, she strongly urged me to join her meditation group.

She told me that it was the best thing ever, that it had totally transformed her life.

I felt it was worth giving it a go and so I went along.

I found it a bit strange. Everyone in the group wore a scarf or white piece of cloth over their heads and the ‘leader’ talked for about 30 minutes, welcoming contributions from those who sat around the circle, before we began some ancient – I think Indian – chants.

I had done silent meditation before and this was definitely strange, but I gave her and the group the benefit of the doubt and began to return every week.

I soon began to notice that she never let a week go by with mentioning her ‘Master’, who was based in Kildare. She kept telling me I had to meet him and that he was the most amazing man she had ever met. I have to admit that I became very uncomfortable with the “hero worshipping” of the leaders of the group in Ireland.

Not one week would go by when our leader didn’t mention how brilliant he was. His name was Dennis Curran and eventually he came to visit our little group. It was made clear to us that this was a massive honour for all of us.

He used his ‘psychic’ powers to describe people in the room that night and the experience left me a little cold.

I had always thought that meditation was supposed to be empowering, instead I felt that my teacher was really under the influence of Mr Curran – who she always referred to as her “Master”. Even that term made me feel uncomfortable.

As the weeks went by, and I became more and more comfortable with the group, the issue of the Japa Ireland trips to India would surface more and more.

My teacher would press me almost every week, to see when I would go to India with the group. She said this was just the life-changing experience I needed to sort out my “midlife crisis!”

When I discovered that the trip cost in the region of €4,000, I was shocked. I lived away from Ireland for a long time, and I knew full well that this was an extortionate price given the cost of living in India.

My discomfort rose and yet I stayed with the group for the best part of a year. I’d find myself on a strange kind of a high after the repetitive chanting, but then I’d slip into a low during the week, and then feel a sense of elation when I’d return to the group the following week.

It was when one of the women in my group returned from one of the Indian trips that I began to become alarmed. She seemed very ‘down’ after her encounter with Shashey Dubey, the Indian ‘guru’ who was supposed to transform all of our lives.

She also described sharing a room with four or five other women in low-budget hotels, which shocked me given the cost of these trips to India.

It was thanks to another chance encounter that I managed to make my escape from this Japa Ireland group.

I had misgivings or suspicions for about ten weeks and was really uncomfortable with the ‘hero worshipping’ of the ‘Masters’.

Then, out of the blue, a friend of mine met somebody at a wedding. By complete chance the subject of Japa Ireland came up. When they spoke, the woman told my friend she had heard allegations of sexual abuse or inappropriate encounters on the Indian trips.

My friend rang me the day after the wedding because she knew I’d been having misgivings about the group for a while.

She told me about the allegations of sexual abuse in India and suddenly it made sense that the lady in my group was so down and unwilling to talk about her experiences after she returned home.

I already felt that the people in the group were giving their power away to the group leaders and I was very uncomfortable with the way the Japa ‘community’ seemed to close in on itself. They used to organise events where those who left were bad-mouthed or ostracised.

I raised concerns about what I’d heard from my friend with my Japa ‘teacher’ and he immediately shut down. He refused to answer my concerns.

I left the group immediately after that. I have to say that I did not witness inappropriate behaviour during meditation sessions in Ireland, but the way in which the group just closed ranks after I raised concerns really bothered me.

Looking back now, I’m kind of shocked at myself for staying with this group for so long. Some good people got themselves involved with Japa Ireland and, like me, I think they hadn’t a clue what was really involved. I am so glad I got away from the group, as I have heard far more serious allegations since then.

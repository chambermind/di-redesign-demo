Why is the Dalai Lama anywhere near Sogyal?

RIGPATibetan Buddhism = Lamaism

 Are we missing something here in DI? What is the Dalai Lama doing on a throne near this guy Sogyal?  Have we lost the power of critical thought?

http://www.viewmagazine.org/index.php/articles/tibet/147-lerab-ling-temple-inauguration-sogyal-rinpoches-address.html

Sogyal Rinpoche’s address at the inauguration of the Lerab Ling temple by His Holiness the Dalai Lama.

 “Our aspiration for this temple is that it will be a centre of learning and contemplation that will play an active part in the wider spiritual and cultural life of Europe.”

 

Your Holiness, it is the greatest honour and blessing to welcome you here today. People all over the world know and love you as a great spiritual leader, a spokesman for peace, and a champion of human values. Your message of compassion has touched the hearts of millions of men and women, for whom you are a source of hope and courage in this troubled world of ours. You have awakened an awareness of Tibet, and Tibet’s unique spiritual culture, all over the globe; and if, over the last twenty years, Buddhism has won greater respect and acknowledgement in the world, we know that this is largely because of your leadership, and your example.

Your Holiness, you have always been the guiding light and constant inspiration for all of our work in Rigpa. As you know, over the last thirty years, we have offered the teachings of Buddha in a way that is authentic, open-hearted and relevant to today’s world. At the same time, we have tried to be of service to your vision, to the cause of Tibet, and to Tibet’s great spiritual and cultural heritage. Whatever we have achieved, all of it is thanks to you and your support; and this is also true of this temple, which has only been possible because of your blessing.

We have built this temple in order to provide a truly authentic example of the spiritual culture of Tibet. Your Holiness, when you were here in the year 2000, you said that our task at Lerab Ling was to make the Buddhist culture of Tibet known in an authentic manner. So, we have created this temple to be a testimony to the richness and uniqueness of Tibetan art and culture. We believe it will serve as a source of education about Tibetan culture here in Europe for many generations to come.

 Our aspiration for this temple is that it will be a centre of learning and contemplation that will play an active part in the wider spiritual and cultural life of Europe

In this way, we see this temple as a gift from Tibet and her great spiritual heritage, to the great nation of France, which is a country that has always shown a heartfelt affection for Tibet and interest in its Buddhist tradition—with the great pioneers like Alexandra David-Néel.

 At the same time, we think of this temple as a tribute to the Tibetan people, who have undergone so much in order to hand the Buddhist teachings down to the present day, and who are unable to practise them freely in their homeland. We know that by maintaining Tibet’s Buddhist tradition here authentically in the West, we can play a part in preserving Tibetan culture and keeping its unique traditions alive.

We have also built this temple as a home for the teachings of Buddha. We could say that even more important than the outer temple is the inner temple—the people who come to study here, and practise and experience for themselves the truth of the teachings. In the years ahead, these men and women—like those currently on our three-year retreat—will take the wisdom and compassion they have learned, and put them into action in many areas of society and life. In a very real way, they are being trained to become ‘servants of peace’, so they can serve Your Holiness’s work and your vision.

Our aspiration for this temple is that it will be a centre of learning and contemplation that will play an active part in the wider spiritual and cultural life of Europe, and that it will explore how the teachings of Buddha can contribute to the welfare of society here in France and in this region. We also intend that the temple will be open to all the different religions, and that it will be a meeting place for different disciplines and traditions—a kind of spiritual university. This is why we call it the Institute of Wisdom and Compassion.

This temple contains many holy images and relics from Tibet. Their blessings will radiate peace and harmony to the land and people of this region, throughout the whole of France, to Europe and beyond. And we hope that, one day, Lerab Ling itself will become a place of pilgrimage.

I am very moved to think of the profound significance of this gathering here. Your Holiness, you are the heart of Tibet and its great Buddhist tradition. By coming here today, you are bringing with you the life force and blessing of that tradition.

Here in this temple, so many Buddhist countries and heritages are represented. This Buddha, modelled on the sacred image of Buddha at Bodhgaya in India, where Buddha was enlightened, was made in Burma. The one thousand Buddhas are copies of a statue from the ancient Indian monastery of Nalanda; and they were created in Nepal and Thailand. From Sri Lanka, we have a cutting from the original Bodhi tree under which Buddha attained enlightenment.

This Tibetan temple, decorated by Tibetan and Bhutanese artists, has been built on the soil of France; and now it is filled with people from many different countries and every corner of the world. All of this seems to confirm the importance of the spiritual teachings, like the message of Buddha, and their power to bring the wisdom, love and compassion that the world so urgently needs today. And here, beneath the compassionate gaze of this Buddha, it seems to speak of our universal responsibility to one another, and of the oneness of all humanity.

I would like to request Your Holiness to please pray from the depth of your heart, and direct your blessings so that Lerab Ling truly serves as an authentic representation of Tibetan culture, a place where the teachings of Buddha flourish and are transmitted faithfully, and a place that produces true spiritual practitioners who can make a difference in the world. May Lerab Ling become a place of enlightenment, and may the teachings last long into the future, for the benefit of the world and Tibet.

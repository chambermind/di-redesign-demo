Scam of God

Victory (Faith Movement or Prosperity Gospel) Archive Abundant Life

Civil action over €225k investment – by Jim Gallagher, Sunday World, 9th July, 2013

THE disgraced pastors of the collapsed Victory Christian Church have been accused of ripping off three businessmen to the tune of €225,000. Brendan Hade and Gerry Byrne face a civil action by the men, who are demanding their life savings back. The alleged victims, who told their story to the Sunday World, but who did not want to be named, said they were promised three times their investment within 18 months. However, within weeks of handing over their cheques the project had gone bust. The Dublin men, one of whom is about to lose his home, said the pastors collected the money knowing the scheme was insolvent.

Debts

Months after persuading the men to invest in Global Mobile Vision (GMV), Gerry Byrne (66), bought a fabulous villa with its own swimming pool on Portugal’s Silver Coast. Amazingly, one of the men, unaware his own money had been lost, was with Byrne when he put down the deposit. Byrne’s son has an apartment nearby. Gerry Byrne, Brendan Hade and pastor wife Sheila are currently in a court battle with receivers for the Bank of Scotland, who have taken over their three Dublin church premises because of €18 million debts. The Sunday World revealed the full story last week of how the Hades had run the Victory Christian Fellowship into the ground while amassing a staggering portfolio of properties.

Today we can reveal that fellow pastor Byrne and wife Marion also own numerous properties, including their current home at 11 Woodstown Road, Knocklyon and an apartment near the city centre, in Francis Street. Records show Gerry Byrne also had a show apartment in Salthill, Galway. The three Dublin men involved in the court action against Gerry Byrne and Brendan Hade agreed to talk to us after reading our shocking exposé last Sunday. Two of them put €50,000 each into GMV, a business providing data services to mobile phones which was being pushed by the pastors in 2005.The third man put in €125,000. Brendan and Sheila Hade, along with Byrne, were shareholder directors in the project with two British pastors called David and Esther Crownborn.

The company went into voluntary liquidation in June 2005 owing €1.4 million, including €160,000 in wages and €150,000 to Revenue. The Crownborns disappeared to Florida where they set up another church. The three men were horrified to discover they were not listed among the creditors. “We want to know what happened to our money,” said one of the men, a church member who worked for Byrne and Victory. The alleged victim said he never knew GMV collapsed and was still being fooled the following year. “Then in March 2006 [nine months after GMV had ceased treading] I bought the paper and saw a story about a GMV creditors meeting at the Harcourt Hotel.

Gone

“I asked Gerry and he said, ‘oh no, your money did not go into GMV, it went into another company Crownborn set up in America’.” In September 2006 his world collapsed when Byrne told him: “We lost all our money. Everything is gone.” The man said he owed the bank a total of €85,000 and Is now in danger of losing his home. A second man, a businessman who was not a church member, said he was persuaded to invest in GMV by Brendan Hade at a meeting in the Red Cow hotel. “He guaranteed me the return. I paid in February 2005 when effectively I believe they were collecting money to save themselves.” The third man put in €50,000 but later added another €75,000 under pressure. The three men began a civil action against Brendan Hade and Byrne, but it has dragged on for years as the defendants keep changing solicitors. Meanwhile, Gerry Byrne’s villa in Sao Martinho do Porto can be booked for £950 a week.

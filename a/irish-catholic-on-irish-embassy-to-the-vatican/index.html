Early hopes for Pope Francis

Pope Francis has a lot of challenges, but also a lot of goodwill writes Michael Kelly

Michael Kelly

http://www.irishcatholic.ie/20130321/opinion/early-hopes-for-pope-francis-S31739.html

Thursday, 21 March 2013

5:00 AM GMT

Pope Francis arrives at the altar to celebrate his inaugural Mass in St. Peter’s Square at the Vatican March 19. Photo: CNS

I was in Rome for the election of Pope Francis. Much has been written about the new Pontiff since his election last week. Most commentators are in agreement about one thing: his profound humility. But how could it be otherwise for a Latin American Jesuit? Since the moment of his election, he has set a different tone. But then, Popes tend to shape the role in a very different way while retaining the essential elements of the Petrine ministry.

Roman Pontiff Emeritus Benedict XVI deserves huge respect and credit for his courageous decision to resign the papacy. In doing so, he has lifted a burden of office from all of his successors. Of course, no one wants a situation where Popes resign on a whim. But the humility of Benedict to stand down when he felt unable to continue, laid down a marker of simplicity that Pope Francis is, and will continue, to build on.

The Argentinian Pontiff has been careful to reference Benedict in several of his public addresses to date. Leading the crowd in St Peter’s Square in prayer for his predecessor was a poignant touch. It also asserts that while there will be change, there is also continuity in the papal office.

Francis’ election has been broadly welcomed in Ireland. But, you can’t please everyone as Pope, and he certainly won’t. I was surprised, for example, to see a commentator writing in a newspaper about the election and pointing out that the new Pope has what was described as a “hard line” on abortion. Does it come as a surprise that the Pope upholds the Church’s teaching on the sanctity of all human life?

Pope Francis combines a fidelity to the Church’s teaching on key issues with a lively love for the poor and the vulnerable. He has many challenges as Pope: there are issues of governance in the Vatican that need to be addressed. There is also a need for realistic structures of dialogue to bring the voices of laypeople in to discussions within the Church.

Inspire

Above all, however, Pope Francis will have to preach the Gospel message of hope in an often hopeless and jaded world. He will have to inspire Catholics around the world, particularly in Europe, to embrace the new evangelisation with renewed vigour. He will have to inspire pastoral zeal among priests, bishops and religious that will give new hope to many in consecrated and religious life who are feeling quite lost. It goes without saying that he will have to continue the Church’s vital work of ensuring that there are robust child safeguarding procedures in place in every corner of the Catholic world.

People will also look to Pope Francis as a global leader who can prick the conscience of politicians and policymakers.

It was good to see President Michael D. Higgins and Mrs Higgins in the Vatican for the inauguration representing the State. They were accompanied by Minister for Finance Michael Noonan representing the Government.

Relations between Ireland and the Holy See have diminished considerably. Unfortunately, President Higgins was one of the few high-profile guests at the Mass who does not have an Embassy to the Holy See. The election of Pope Francis is an ideal opportunity for the Fine Gael-Labour coalition Government to re-visit the decision to downgrade relations with the Holy See. World powers such as the United States, Russia, France and Britain (to name but a few) understand the vital role that a permanent embassy to the Holy See plays. A Latin American Pope, the first non-European Pope in over a millennium, adds to the importance of the Vatican as a global listening post. Ireland’s overseas aid priorities often run hand-in-hand with the priorities of the Holy See in international affairs. Where Irish Aid resources are deployed in the developing world, it is often the case that the only institution present living with the poor and the vulnerable is the Church. Closing our Embassy to the Holy See has cut off these vital links.

Resources

Soon, Ireland’s Presidency of the European Council will come to an end freeing up personnel and resources. Very few people believe that the Vatican embassy was closed for economic reasons. However, if Tánaiste Eamon Gilmore still maintains that cost is his only concern, the conclusion of the Presidency will give him ample resources at his disposal to prioritise a fresh start in relations with the Vatican.

Extremes

Historian Diarmuid Ferriter has written a book on the history of the Pioneer Total Abstinence Movement (PTAA). Perceptively, he titled the book A Nation of Extremes. Undoubtedly in the past, Ireland’s relationship with the Holy See was characterised by subservience.

The way to remedy that, however, is not a radical break as Mr Gilmore has done. The remedy is to work hard and build warm relations based on mutual trust and respect. In the person of Archbishop Charles Brown, Apostolic Nuncio to Ireland, the Vatican has shown good faith in trying to rebuild such relations. The Government should now seize the opportunity and do the same.

 Reply by Mike Garde Director of Dialogue Ireland Trust

Though I agree that the issue of the Embassy is important I agree with the decision of the government to close it down. I likely would disagree with their reasoning, but I would see it as actually going in the very direction this Pope is taking namely disconnecting the Church from the Vatican Empire. Which Empire? Not a major power but the use of the power of Caesar in its relations with the world is counter to the upside down values Francis is trying to lead to.

What values? Those of resisting the temptations Jesus was offered.

Money, power and the avoidance of the cross.

The plan:

1. Surrender the Vatican state and just be the Bishop of Rome in Italy.

2. Donate the Vatican State to the world and have the Church have its HQ there, but also make it a kind of World Peace Centre. Allow it to be a World Heritage Centre. Let the Bishop of Rome live like the Archbishop of Buenos Aries.

3. Ireland has a Catholic Church and the Irish Government can communicate its views through its leaders here. Abolish the Papal Nuncios as State Representatives and have them only as Apostolic representatives of the Church.

4. Focus on a way to move women from being hand maidens to equals in the Church. Examine the ideology of patriarchy which has removed them from the script. To quote the real Mary, “He has put down the mighty from their seats and the rich he has sent empty away.”

DI Editorial on amnesia in regard to those that supported Meegan

Icross Charity

SUPPORT: Dr Ronan Conroy refuses to condemn his friend

MeeganMay 2 Mail on Sunday

NGO leader Dr Vincent Kenny, whose sworn statement helped lift the lid on the Meegan scandal, said last night: ‘There are lots of people in high places who are aware of these allegations and yet they continue to support him and in doing so I feel they are validating his work.’ Dr Ronan Conroy of the Royal College of Surgeons, one of Meegan’s most dedicated followers and closest friends, said: ‘I’m a piano player and I’m struck by something said by a rather waspish music critic Charles Rosen. He said you should judge a pianist by the best they have ever done. Sometimes we want to judge people on the worst they have ever done. ‘As a scientist I can have my own personal opinions but we have to limit my judgment to what the evidence is.’

Here we find that a person- Dr Vincent Kenny that has tried to get to the truth concerning Meegan stating, “There are lots of people in high places who are aware of these allegations and yet they continue to support him and in doing so I feel they are validating his work.”

We are not in the business of outing people who uncritically supported Meegan but the least that the public deserves from them is a willingness to now step forward and say we got it wrong. Some are in the political sphere, some comment on it, and others are celebrities. Surely the fact that the High Court has adjudicated on this case means that these supporters should at least stand up and be counted. They are willing to make all kinds of statements about human rights and about the current failure of our bankers, political system and the terrible abuse of children in Ireland. Do they not get it? Do they not read what happened to workers and young people in Kenya?

Let us take the example of Dr Ronan Conroy of the Royal College of Surgeons, one of Meegan’s most dedicated followers and closest friends reported in the Mail On Sunday.

“‘I’m a piano player and I’m struck by something said by a rather waspish music critic Charles Rosen. He said you should judge a pianist by the best they have ever done. Sometimes we want to judge people on the worst they have ever done.”

Is this an appropriate comment from a person who works in the field of Medical Science?  What kind of message is this sending out? His final comment is bizarre, ‘As a scientist I can have my own personal opinions but we have to limit my judgment to what the evidence is.’ I would have thought that as a friend of Meegan’s, rather than as a Scientist he would say something like, “I have empathy for the fall of my friend. I believe he was wrong in what he did, but I feel for him as his whole world has collapsed.” His statement saying he limits his judgement to what the evidence is not a very scientific observation. He has the evidence if he will look at it, namely the massive body of evidence in the High Court Case.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2010/05/01/archive-following-the-mail-groups-success-in-the-courts-april-19-2010/

Meegan the evidence suggests is not going to face the music, rather he is now going to move to Cambodia and has already begun to assemble his new vehicle so he can continue to play his piano. It is likely to be a very discordant recital and hopefully the ghosts of Pol Pot are not waiting near Angkor Wat

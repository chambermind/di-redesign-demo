23 December 1978

The Book Of Religions

Books

Translation of an article in the magazine “Crux” (teenage magazine) 10/78, from Swedish

By Lennart Lundberg

“The book of Religions”, published by Esselte is a new “book of facts” for the upper departments in comprehensive schools and came on the market in January 1978. Attractively layed out and with a rich selection of illustrations the book is based partly on a new idea. Representatives for different re­ligious convictions, through the interview method, describe how they experience their religion. So far a good idea!

But the publishers delegated the chief editorial responsi­bility to Bertil Persson. He is in partnership with several of the leaders for the new religious movements and actively works to spread their propaganda. Now they have succeeded in working their way into the schools and on their own terms! The Children of God, The Scientology Church, and the Swedish Mission Covenant Church have all been alloted the same amount of space.

Indignant Authors

Several of the book’s authors are dissatisfied with the way Bertil Persson and Kerstin Lagström have used their editorial responsibility. One of them, Walter Mann, from Lidingö, hasn’t written anything in the book! “I was very surprised to see my name among the authors” was his comment to CRUX. I made some suggestions regarding some parts of the book, but nothing I wrote has been published. Olof Pettersson, Professor from Lund, remarked that his chapter on Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism and Judaism were unrecognizable in the published edition. He added that Bertil Persson himself had written the sections dealing with Nordic and Samic religion, and that in these section there were many points he could not support.

Many Factual Errors

The chapter on church history is poor and contains several factual errors. For example: “Luther allowed the Bible to be translated” (p.38). He translated it himself! But what is most disturbing are all the little mistakes, says Walter Mann.

Henry VII broke with the Pope and declared himself Head of the Church of England when the Pope refused to give his blessing to Henry’s divorce and re-marriage” (p. 41). It is a question of a completely different person, namely Henry VIII. There is hardly a page without this type of serious error.

Religious General Stores

The book is far too much of a religious general stores. There are no shades of meaning. The new spiritual movements are represented as being rather the same thing, says Erland Sund­ström. Neither he or any of the other authors were given the chance to read all of the proofs. They were only allowed to see what they themselves had written and thus had no chance to influence other parts of the book. On the other hand the authors are listed in such a way that the reader is led to believe that they had worked together on the book. Some of the authors had only revised what others had written and not even they had seen the final version before the book was pub­lished in January – except Bertil Persson, of course.

Dissatisfied Scrutinizers

The entire book is the work of Bertil Persson. He has used the work of other well-known authors as he has thought best. The book was chopped into short parts and sent to different experts for “scrutinizing” i.e. the scrutinizers points of view have been taken into account only when they have suited Persson. Several of the names on the three-page list of scru­tinizers are dissatisfied with the way in which their views have been dealt with. Jan Hjarpe from Lund who scrutinized the chapter of Islam asked that his name be excluded but the publishing house refused. He says that not all the changes be made have been carried out.

The three page list of scrutinizers appears to be much more impressive than in reality. Other dissatisfied scrutinizers are Gun Friedner (The Conflict in the Middle East) and Björn Orwald in the chapter on violence. Orwald appended material to the chapter in order to correct the errors.

Intentional Bias

He too is listed as a scrutinizer despite the fact that Bertil Persson had disregarded his remarks. Wilful deception is what the factual scrutinizer Hans Nystedt from Visby thinks. Hans Nystedt was appointed by the “Institute for the Scrutinization of Educational Aids” to scrutinize the Book of Religions and to judge the objectivity of the book. His judgement was to be the basis for the decision by the Educational Aids Commission of the Ministry of Education to approve the book for use in schools.

Hans Nystedt was not satisfied with the disposition. He thought that the chapter on violence and defence was intentionally negative towards defence. He recommended that the Educational Aids Commission waited with their decision to approve the book “pending a complete book”. The Educational Aids Commission approved the book without taking my remarks into consideration. It is a very poor book. All proportions are distorted. Religious cranks are allowed to advertise and market their product completely unchecked.

Poor Books Are Approved

One of the members of the Educational Aids Commission is John Ronnas, Head of the Swedish Radio’s Religious Dept. Does he think that the book meets the demand for objectivity? “I have difficulty in remembering. I can only verify that we have approved it”.

But it is “objectivity” that the Commission is to scrutinize: One reason that Ronnas has difficulty in remembering this can be that the Book of Religions, like other approved text‑books have wound up in Ronnas’ waste-paper basket. “We are often forced to approve books with several factual mistakes if these don’t distort the objective presentation”.

Persson Satisfied

The man behind the book says in an interview in the magazine “The Searcher” (Sokaren) no: 5/77: “It is very disturbing that incorrect information is spread in school, as it is in school that one assesses the fundamental values. Incorrect information about religious communities affects RE teachers, folk school teachers and preachers who in their turn later pass it on.’

But doesn’t the Book of Religions spread the same sort of information? “I can’t answer in detail. I don’t know everything about everything” says Bertil Persson.

I asked Persson about the factual inaccuracies concerning Luther and Henry VII (VIII). Persson blames the publishing house. But the chapter about the Children of God on p. 285 is written almost entirely by Persson himself. It states here, among other things: “One leads a very strict life. For example one refrains from every type of sexual relationship outside marriage and premaritial relations are forbidden”.

Compare this with the article in Crux no 9/77: “They (members of the Children of God) may love whom they wish, just as all other people the world over”, as stipulated in the MO Letter no: 598 “More of the Truth”. The description to be found in the Book of Religions is quite antique and does not correspond with reality. But Persson ducks the question again and blames the scrutinizer Karin Kvideland.

None Of The Scrutinizers Offended

The scrutinization has for the most part worked well. I have tried to express myself so that nobody need feel offended. He says that he is not aware of Jan Hjarpe’s wish to be ex‑cluded from the list of scrutinizers. How does he explain the disposition? One Page about “the Children of God”, one page about “the Scientology Church” and one page about “the Swedish Mission Covenant Church”, is this reasonable?

Yes, the background to the revival movement has already been outlined. A textbook today must be written so as to relate to the student; it must give scope for what the student will later confront. It can thus, just as well be “the Children of God”, “the Scientology Church” and “TM” as “the Swedish Mission Covenant Church”, “the Swedish Evangelical Mission” or “the Church of Sweden”, is Bertil Persson’s opinion, who claims to be Dr of Divinity of the People’s University of America”. (Doctors grades can be bought at many correspondance universities). Persson is a bishop, ordained in the”apostolic-episcopal” church by a former Methodist minister. He denies this for Crux. “A pre-fabricated story”, he says.

The Sects Celebrate The Publishing Of The Book

Bertil Persson is also deeply committed in the Society of Religious Forum. According to the regulations the Society works for religious liberty, to protect the interests of the smaller societies and to spread factual information and knowledge about religious phenonema.

Amongst other things the Society distributes pamphlets to clergymen and R.E. teachers. In actual fact the Society is purely a cover-organisation for the new religious sects.

Members of the Board include, among others, Jörgen Sandal, the Hare-Krishna leader, Fridehilde Bachle, former leader of the Tongil Family, Gun Lancai, a leader of the Scientology Church. Bertil Persson is responsible for the Society’s Science of Religion Dept.

In an invitation to the Society’s meeting in December 1977 we found the following: “Religious Forum celebrates the publishing of this book as unique and new in its kind”. It cannot be more explicit. Infiltration is a success.

The Publishers Re-Print

Lennart Berglund of the Esselte Publishing House is responsible for R.E. books. He tells us that so far the book has sold well, better than is usual during the first year. Approximately 7000 copies had been sold as per November. “The teachers are positive, but we have also received a number of negative reactions and reviews.”

According to other sources there has been a lot of strong criticism. The publishers have already decided to revise the book. Carl-Magnus Erlandsson who has written the study questions in the book and his teaching colleague Bengt Johansson have been given the task of revising the book before it had been on the market less than a year. This is quite normal says Lennart Berglund.

No Need To Be Objective

The book is not objectively tenable, but it doesn’t need to be either. We have given space to views which are not dealt with in former text-books. These have excelled themselves in the treatment of the traditional Christian churches. Do teachers know enough about the new spiritual movements such as the Children of God, to be able to see that the book reflects the different sect’s views of themselves. I have difficulty in believing that this is something to which the authors would agree.

Walter Mann says to CRUX that he hasn’t written a line in the book. But still he is listed as one of the authors. He worked on the adaption and disposition says Lennart Berglund. But there is not much left of his suggestions in the chapter dealing with ethics. We have used some of them to a certain extent says Lennart Berglund, who refers us to the publisher’s editor Kerstin Lagström. But she too is unable to say what Walter Mann has written. “A little, but I cannot say exactly what part of his material has been used”. Neither can Kerstin Lagström explain the factual errors concerning Luther and Henry VII (VIII). She tries to refer to the book as a collection of essays, but in the preface the book is presented as “a book of facts”!

No Commentary

Why did Esselte give Bertil Persson the main responsibility for the book? He had many opinions regarding educational aids, and is editor, for the R.E. Teacher’s magazine FLR­-news. We thought that his idea of giving different views a forum was the right way in the case of this book, says Lennart Berglund. But the publishers have decided to let two other people revise the book. In the autumn of 1980 the new edition will be available. Until this, the new religious sects can con­tinue to spread their false doctrine in primary schools with the approval of the Ministry of Education. Stop the Book of Religions! Demand other textbooks.

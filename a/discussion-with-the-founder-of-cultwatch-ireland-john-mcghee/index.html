14 September 2012

Discussion with the founder of Cultwatch-Ireland John McGhee

Cultism

About Cultwatch-Ireland

 Anti-cult activist, with the aim of exposing fraudulent cults, sects and movements posing as self-help and religious organisations.

Description

To help and advise families of those involved in such organisations, by educating them as to the tactics and abuses implemented therein. To act as a point of contact for those who have left, or who wish to leave the confines of such groups.

http://www.facebook.com/pages/Cultwatch-Ireland/379501365457049

 Q: MG

Generally there is no interest in the cult issue in Ireland. They get little publicity and it only if someone like Tom Cruise is in the news do the media address this issue?

A: JM

 That is it in a nutshell.

 Q: MG

 It receives little recognition so you feel protests focus the attention on these groups. Just those who were protesting about disabilities were able to crystallise the issue of the cuts?

 A: JM

 Those caught in the groups begin to realise their little world is being observed and cracks of light appear.

 Q: MG

 You believe that the issue is not about an individual cult, but the phenomenon of cultism. Just like the TV 3 documentary dealt with the House of Prayer, the Palmarians and Scientology, and mixed it all up. This was to show the groups differ in beliefs and ideas, but the way they work is very similar.

 A: JM

We need a multilateralist approach in regard to cults not a unilateralism as in a solo focus on one group. That is why I will not only be dealing with groups that affected me, but will reach out to assist the victims of all groups such as Scientology, Tony Quinn, JW’s etc. So we would invite and request people affected by all groups to assist on Sunday, we can discuss assisting you when your group needs to be focussed on

 Q: MG

Tell us a bit of your story?

A: JM

 Having spent a number of years as a member of the “church” of Scientology, making it out the other side with sanity intact and subsequently spending the last three years partaking in it’s undoing, both in Ireland and internationally, I can draw many parallels between it and various other quasi-religious and self-help/empowerment organisations. The most common denominator amongst these groups is money and the desire for it. Whether it is Scientology, Educo, UCKG (with whom I have experience), House of Prayer, and Jehovah’s Witnesses.  All of these seduce the seeker with initially workable and affordable paths, catching them at low ebb in their life is also paramount in them taking the bait. Once taken, they are made to feel welcome and part of the group due to the sickly sweet love-bombing bestowed upon them, like a new family with everyone on the same wavelength. With this new found feel good bubble they find themselves in, they start misattributing any subsequent happiness and success, to their respective group. At this point they can be coerced into anything, by their superiors, be it parting with large sums of money, to disconnecting with family and friends who don’t share their zeal for the path they’ve chosen. These groups DO operate on the premise that the more money you spend, the better your life will be, or the greater your seat in heaven. With Scientology steadily on a downward spiral into non-existence in Ireland and coupled with the fact that it’s paltry number of adherents (less than 50), indicates that it doesn’t top the poll with regard to families and individuals adversely affected by cults here, the time has come to highlight the remaining cults in operation. In the current economic climate, the morale of the people is at an all time low and this makes them susceptible to the predatory talons of such groups, in the quest for happiness and meaning in their lives.  The Groups I currently wish to focus on or highlight are Tony Quinn/ Educo  and Christina Gallagher’s House of Prayer.  Quinn because he has set up gyms and health food shops in many major towns across the country and poses a threat to those using them, insofar as it being a gateway to take his extortionately priced seminars and courses. These seminars can cost up to €80,000, they promise affluence and empowerment, but in reality they result in bankruptcy and despair, due to the remortgaging required to fund them. If a fitness instructor wishes to work in one of the Educo gyms, it is a prerequisite that he/she must do a series of Quinn’s courses, which run into thousands of euro. Therefore, they have to pay to work. He has amassed millions due to the exploitation of his staff who are very often delusional and revere him like a god.

Christina Gallagher’s House of Prayer, because she is preying on unsuspecting faithful Catholics, she has been able to brainwash a priest who was himself a victim of the hierarchy and use his good name to endorse her Marian visionary scam.  She continues to take advantage of the sick, elderly and vulnerable by promising them restored health and claiming to speak directly to the Virgin Mary. She has amassed millions by doing so.  Mrs Gallagher promotes, giving until it hurts, i.e, financially ensuring your place in the hereafter. All the while she is purchasing lavish homes in Ireland, the US and the U.K to name but a few. Her followers remortgage their homes, put themselves on the poverty line and sever relationships with their families to support her house of prayer, while she sits in a Jacuzzi. Meanwhile the misguided Fr McGinnity (á la Bishop Len Brennan-Father Ted) lends credibility.  Protests for these two Cults are planned.

 Q: MG

Why have you decided to do the protest at Quinn’s Seminar at DCU this Sunday coming?

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/09/04/john-mcghee-a-former-scientologist-and-anti-cult-campaigner-is-going-to-organise-a-protest-against-tq/

A: JM

Quinn has been on the go for 41 years and except for a few articles the public is asleep at the wheel. He is getting away with murder. He has managed to gain influence over a number of significant people like John Boyle of Boylesports, he got  given oil shares by people whose lights he switched off. He has the bulb and it is not going to be screwed in soon. I like what you wrote about this a few years before oil was found in Belize!

http://dialogueireland.org/dicontent/a2z/tonyquinn/index.html

I took heart from the two or three people who went after Quinn in 2009. My view is that a lot of people are expecting a big group. It just does not work that way. Aim to bring a dedicated small group and give strength to the resistance. A lot of the people in the Helix have to be there. They want a payout from the oil company so they have to be seen to be on fire. We will likely also go to the oil AGM, to make the point that the problem is not democracy in the company but Quinn’s shadow hangs over the whole enterprise. http://dialogueireland.org/dicontent/resources/video/tq%20protest.html

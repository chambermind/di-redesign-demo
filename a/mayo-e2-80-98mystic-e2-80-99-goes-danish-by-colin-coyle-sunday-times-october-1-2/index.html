Mayo ‘mystic’ goes Danish by Colin Coyle, Sunday Times October 1, 2010

Conferences & CommentariesHouse of Prayer

MAYO mystic who has been criticised for persuading the elderly to donate money to her House of Prayer is expanding into Scandinavia. Christina Gallagher is planning to set up Houses of Prayer in Denmark and Sweden. She will speak at public prayer meetings in both countries next week. The self-styled Marian visionary has told Scandinavians she has the support of priests in Ireland, including a “Fr Sean Brady”. She has set up Houses of Prayer, modelled on her Achill retreat, in America. Gallagher did not respond to queries last week about her new venture or requests to speak to Fr Sean Brady. There is no record of a Sean Brady in the Catholic Directory, apart from Cardinal Seán Brady, the Catholic Primate of All Ireland. But someone using that name has posted comments on religious discussion forums in Sweden saying he supports Gallagher “who has daily contact with our Blessed Virgin Mary”.

He has also asked for “new missionaries” eager to spread the word in Sweden to send him a private email message and emphasised that donations are welcome. Previously only Fr Gerard McGinnity, a parish priest in Co Louth, has been linked to raising funds for Gallagher’s House of Prayer. Gallagher will travel to Stockholm this week, staying for two nights before flying to Denmark. She is meeting followers for two hours in a city centre hotel in Stockholm, followed by a “group prayer session”, according to Swedish websites. Gallagher claims to have “a special message from Our Lady to the people of Sweden”, which she will reveal this week. She translated the website of the House of Prayer into Danish and is having it translated into Swedish. She is also looking for a “representative” to help her register the House of Prayer as a charity in Sweden. “The person would be responsible for the daily running of the prayer house and all the advertising,” she said. The House of Prayer lost its special tax status in Ireland in 2008, and is appealing against the decision. Much of its income now comes from America, where Gallagher operates five houses of prayer. She was forced to return about €250,000 to two donors in 2008 after legal action.

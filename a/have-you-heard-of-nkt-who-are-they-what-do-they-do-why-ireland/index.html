Have you heard of NKT? Who are they? What do they do? Why Ireland?

BuddhistCultismNew Kadampa Tradition - NKTTibetan Buddhism = Lamaism

Dialogue Ireland is happy to post this article as part of our series to assist the public in their understanding of Lamaism. This is group, the NKT is viewed as a cult by the followers of the Dalai Lama,

However, the NKT  have some pertinent views on the phenomenon of Lamaism which we see as the cultist form of TB. Many leaving comments on our site see the NKT as a big cult, but see Sogyal as purely an abuser whose behaviour can only be addressed within the Dharma. We see all of Lamaism as part of cultism and do not attach the cult label to any particular group as such. What we are saying is that it is not simply about cult like entities that appear to the mainstream as sectarian or cultist. Rather it is about these tendencies and attitudes that are found in the mainstream as well. By referencing this article to the Old Religion of Catholicism we get away from labeling and interrogate this issue.

 

MONK STUDYING IN NAMLING MONASTERY – EAST TIBET   © OLAF SCHUBERT

 I have become interested in your site and its emphasis on ‘dialogue’ as the appropriate approach to understanding Cultism in Ireland. I have a particular curiosity around NKT as will become clear as you read on.

A few introductory facts and thoughts –

As a retired businessman married to a former college lecturer, I am of an age that benefits from retrospective reflection on my own life experience mingled with that of my good wife!

I have been through a few decades of involvement in a rather ‘old’ Religious Movement, namely the Catholic Church; I read widely in, Classical and Modern History, English Literature, Theology, Psychology, Organisational Systems Analysis, Leadership Development.

I maintain a keen interest in how individuals learn to make meaning in their lives as they mature and indeed why some seem to become stuck at a certain stage on the way to maturity. It seems to me that many of us hit a point when belonging to a community of like-minded people is central and when taking risks to explore new ideas or experiences is too demanding or even scary.

What is it that blocks some of us from discovering our own innate capacity to think for ourselves and value our own human experience as opposed to sacrificing our autonomy to some outside authority – especially church/religious authority for the relevance of this piece?

And what are we to understand from the  plethora and variety  of ‘new’ organisations offering courses on “how to lessen stress”, “discover meaning”, “find peace /happiness”, “create wealth” etc., etc.? Katy Taylor credited her ‘born again’ Christianity with helping her in her career while some years ago, Stephen Collins, another boxing champion, was tutored and sustained to victory by Tony Quinn. On the one hand we have a scary spiraling suicide rate, on the other we have – using your term – Cultism and New Religious Movements attracting many.

The scrutiny given to some of these on Dialogue Ireland leads to some mind-blowing reading! I begin to wonder what Ireland have I been living in for almost 60 years.

I conclude that I am one of thousands of ‘innocents abroad’, allowing mention of Scientology,  Achill House of Prayer, Tony Quinn and so many more, to wash over me with a sort of benign “live & let live” attitude. And this, while I was busy delving into all sorts of aspects of human and social evolution /development, human behaviour, organisation and spirituality!!

As for NKT in particular, as I went into the sites where ‘survivors’ recounted personal experiences which amounted to abuse in one form or another – there is an experience relating to Tara Centre, Dublin at NKT World,

www.nktworld.org/leave.html

 I also studied the determined defence put up by official NKT sites refuting these experiences as lies and propaganda. This was uncannily like my own experience of listening to Catholic Church  figures desperately continuing to defend the indefensible history of abuse and cover-up of abuse in parishes and institutions throughout Ireland and globally.

I also searched out what seems to be a more objective description and contextualization of this form of Buddhism founded by Geshe Kelsang Gyatso – see Tibetan Buddhism in the West at

 www.info-buddhism.com/kadampatradition.html

 

MOUNT KAILASH   © OLAF SCHUBERT

This site situates NKT against the background of Tibetan Buddhism generally and particularly with reference to The Old Kadampa Tradition. It is interesting, to say the least to get this picture of where, when and how NKT originated with suggestions of revisionism in places.

What I am gleaning about NKT in particular, and many other ‘old’ and ‘new’ religious movements are that lots of people still need a system of belief in their lives.

This provides a very real and meaningful context of myth and ritual where a palpable sense of transcendence or the Divine becomes alive.

This is not a ‘superior attitude’ sort of statement judging those who sincerely follow a path through ‘mythical faith’ of any kind. Rather, it is an attempt to situate the phenomena which characterise the kind of non-hearing one can meet when one tries to engage directly with a staunch believer to question aspects of their particular ‘church’ practice and abuses.

Explorations into Meditation

It must be 9 / 10 years ago when an acquaintance invited me in to Tara Centre to explore meditation ‘Buddhist’ style as distinct from some earlier attempts within my then Catholic context. I was duly impressed with the welcome and sincerity of the teacher but did not persevere for too long. Then some time later, I bumped into the same acquaintance who had brought me to the classes and we had coffee and a long chat. Later on I found myself pondering on something I couldn’t quite put my finger on – my acquaintance was enthusiastic about his experience at Tara Centre and keen to encourage me back. It took me some time to figure out my underlying unease with our exchange – a subtle sense that this adult person had somehow handed his life over to the teachings of Geshe Kelsang Gyatso. It struck me that this individual had followed studies in science which demanded lots of rote learning as opposed to my background in humanities. To highlight that difference, I find that sometimes applied science can develop more set ways of thinking instead of drawing out one’s ability to learn through debate and critical analysis.

It was years again before I thought about any of this and then I began to spot leaflets advertising Meditation at Tara NKT Centre in all sorts of places around Centre Dublin – restaurants & pubs, cinemas, various notice boards – clearly there was a strong push to recruit people to join this movement. So, I continued to read what I could online and found two opposing streams – one, from NKT describing their movement, their leader and aims – among these is bringing ‘pure’ Buddhist teaching to the West, building Peace Temples in all major cities etc., and the other carrying some truly disturbing stories about the authenticity of the leader, the abuses suffered by former members, the emphasis on money, the controversy with Dalai Lama and other Buddhist traditions.

Gradually it dawned on me that I was discovering a sort of ‘parallel universe’ to the institution in which I had been reared and felt almost sickened at the thought of how some young, idealistic Irish people are being drawn into an exotic world which, like fundamentalist forms of Catholicism, subtly leads one to yield up the right and responsibility to think for oneself, to live in this wonderful & terrible world with as much courage and grace as one can muster.

Intelligence is key.

It seems to me that NKT gently draws people in to ‘learn meditation’, gradually encourages deeper engagement while charging for training fosters an attitude of total reliance on the founder guru, Geshe-la.

His teachings become the teachings and one of these teachings is that deviance from his teachings leads to danger of rebirth in Lower Forms.

In Catholic terms – outside the Catholic Church “there is no salvation”(central teaching until Vat. II and still found in the more fundamentalist sects – in other words if you do not belong to the church you will end up in Hell for all eternity).

Central teachings in NKT match Catholic teachings amazingly well!!

DO NOT QUESTION is the common sacrosanct rule – you may ask questions – for clarification and so that you may understand the teaching more in accordance with accepted teaching. But do not presume to use your innate critical intelligence to wonder about fundamental truths of the teaching.

Abstract ‘theological’ terminology –  resulting in control of mind – ‘group think’ and ‘group talk’ – as opposed to power of personal choice / responsibility; teachings consist of filling the mind with ideas about life, death, after-life with great sense of certainty that ‘this is how it is’ so anything other is seen as ‘negative mind’(NKT) or ‘heresy’ (Catholicism) and the virtuous path is about bringing one’s mind in line with the mind of GKG who is after all “the re-incarnation of Buddha” or in Catholicism “ the Church teaches” / and always knows best ‘/ what God wants through the Holy Spirit’!

Self-cherishing = selfishness, self-love: always put other  before self – no real exploration of “love your neighbour as you love yourself”; ‘self’ is an illusory concept in Buddhism generally – emphasis is always on “for the good of all sentient beings”

Acceptance of a system of ‘correction’ from teachers as appropriate in service of purification / virtue – leading to a culture of fear. “Formation of character” is the argument for this within Catholic seminaries and novitiates.

Vulnerable people need reliance on someone who ‘knows’ so finding a belief system which assures a person  of a safe way of understanding the shakiness of life and a clear path of practices which ensure ‘enlightenment’ or ‘salvation’ for afterlife or finding comfort in saints or deities.

Teachings are promulgated through ‘safe’ reading – in Catholicism suspect theologians are silenced; in NKT any books other than those written by GKG are removed from libraries at his Centres – in other words ‘blind faith’ is the norm.

Contradictory statements regarding the purity of the faith – on the one hand, Catholicism encourages much ecumenism and inter-faith dialogue but refuses to allow inter-communion at Eucharist; in NKT one hears that all religions are respected but GKG actually states that NKT ~offers “the only way to solve human problems”

One of the BIGGIES in both traditions: Catholicism claims to be the One, True Church claims that the Pope is the representative of God elected through the power of the Holy Spirit while Geshe is the re-incarnation of Buddha – and both are ‘INFALLIBLE’.

 The enshrining of celibacy as ‘higher’ in terms of developing spiritually; in Catholicism the law of celibacy for priests was established centuries after Jesus lived and taught – interestingly he did not insist on it for his first followers- it has created a 2-tier membership among Catholics and while attempts are made to side-step this issue, it still remains central as evidenced by a total refusal to discuss the possibility of relaxing a man-made rule. In NKT, the ordained are required to be celibate also and there is a clear deference to the ‘ordained’ over lay members. That’s the theory but evidence in both instances suggests that practice falls very short of this.

These are reflections for starters –what is the situation in Ireland as of now?

I do not set out to demonise either NKT or the Catholic Church – it is clear to me that much good is done through both. However, from my current understanding of the history and development of each, I sense that the good done has been more ‘in spite of’ and due to deep sincerity of practitioners in each rather than through the imperfect structures of each Organisation.

There is another whole line of exploration which could be developed in terms of Systems Analysis – where the dynamics of an organisation can be analysed in terms of POWER, ROLE, AUTHORITY /LEADERSHIP and how healthy /unhealthy that organisation can be seen to be for those who participate in it as well as for the place it holds in wider society. That’s another day’s work!

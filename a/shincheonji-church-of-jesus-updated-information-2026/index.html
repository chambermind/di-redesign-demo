Shincheonji Church of Jesus – Updated Information (2026)

ChristianShincheonji

Shincheonji’s (SCJ) ninety-five-year-old leader, Lee Man-hee, is in prison in Korea and awaiting trial for political interference. In response to his arrest, followers internationally are increasing their recruitment drives, moving their usual on-campus presence during the academic year to city centres. Dialogue Ireland has recently updated its overview of Shincheonji and made the report publicly available.

Since 2023, Dialogue Ireland has received several emails from families concerned about their relatives’ involvement with the Korean Christian group—Shincheonji (SCJ).

These concerns reported similar issues.

Sudden, but noticeable, changes in the family members’ behaviour as they began dedicating more of their time to—and lying about their involvement in—SCJ-related activities.

In cultism contexts, as groups demand an increasing amount of time and commitment, members inevitably begin spending less time with their usual friendship groups and family, eventually neglecting—or even abandoning—their academic and professional pursuits, and giving up their hobbies.

A former member interviewed by the Independent recalls SCJ’s increasing demands:



“The first weird thing they did was tell me not to tell my parents. My parents are Christian so I thought I would be able to tell them but the way they described it, they said it was too deep, and I should keep it between just us,” the Nottingham Trent University student explained.

Soon he felt the group had taken over his life. “I was cutting off my friends and family. No one in my life actually knew I was going to the sessions.”

He said there were many times when members would put pressure on him to make a choice between them and his friends, or ditch his university work so he could study their teachings.

Described as a ‘Christian doomsday cult‘ by the media for SCJ’s core belief that Biblical Revelation is imminent, leader Lee Man-hee is seen by his followers as the ‘promised pastor’ for the end times.

According to Shincheonji New Zealand’s website:


Man Hee Lee is the founder and Chairman of Shincheonji, and is the promised pastor according to the word of Revelation; having received the open scroll (Revelation 10) witnessing all the events of Revelation (Revelation 22:8) and being chosen by Jesus as his messenger, the Advocate, to testify to the churches about Revelation’s fulfilment (Revelation 22:16; Matthew 24:45).

The word ‘Chairman’ comes from a Korean biblical term meaning the head of God’s Assembly. 

Shincheonji leader Lee Man-hee in 2007 (Source: WikiCommons)

One of the major complaints levelled at SCJ is its deceptive recruitment methods.

Recruiters, known as ‘harvesters’ in SCJ terminology, will not disclose their affiliation; instead, they will use one of SCJ’s many aliases or front organisations, and recruitment drives are disguised as cultural activities, charity work, or non-denominational Bible discussions.

Harvesters will also use online dating apps and forums to find prospective recruits (known as ‘fruits‘).

SCJ has a negative view of other Christian denominations, but actively infiltrates other congregations to convert members and ‘flock steal‘, and has spent considerable time and effort in Africa encouraging churches there to change their ‘signboards to something that suits Shincheonji‘.

Christian churches and members of the public in SCJ’s home country of Korea regularly protest against SCJ and call on the government to ban it.

Anti-Shincheonji protests in South Korea in 2020 (Source: WikiCommons)

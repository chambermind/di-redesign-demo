Dublin Welfare Society Limited and their silence over the Clongriffin Mosque

Religion

DI does not address issues of belief, our only focus is on the influence that groups have in society. So the focus here is not on Islam but on the silence of the Dublin Welfare Society in regard to explaining to their fellow Muslims here in Ireland,  and overseas what they are trying to accomplish. Furthermore Irish society has for some months now been trying to find out who these potential neighbours are. We have already published more about this. Consequently this comes under the category of Religion. Below we have placed most of the planning application in the post without comment.

The group is anxious to make it known that the proposed amenities will be available not only to the Muslim population but also to families of other faiths – or no faith – with the intention of promoting harmonious relations. Funding for the development has been promised by 10 wealthy individuals as well as charitable organisations in the Middle East.

Most of the Dublin-based group promoting the Islamic Cultural Centre are professionals working in medicine, research and the universities. Once the centre has been developed, it will be capable of accommodating up to 5,000 people at any one time. The new campus will also include 147 houses and apartments.

Full Development Description

New development on lands at Panhandle Park, Clongriffin, Dublin 13 comprising (a) a 3-storey domed mosque and cultural centre (5573sqm) which includes main prayer hall, prayer rooms, meeting rooms, general purpose room, administrative offices, 1 ground floor 1-bed apartment, créche, bookshop, library, mortuary, ancillary accommodation and two minarets which is linked to: (b) a 2-storey conference centre (2849sqm) which includes reception foyer, 600-seat conference room, 130 seat restaurant, 200-seat single-storey banquet hall, kitchens and ancillary accommodation; (c) a 2 to 3-storey 16-classroom primary school and a 2 to 3-storey 12-classroom secondary school, each with its own administrative and ancillary accommodation. shared multi-use community hall, storage, ancillary accommodation and plant (total area 6809sqm), outdoor play space and ball courts; (d) a 2-storey fitness centre (1392sqm) which includes a 25m indoor swimming pool, gym, sauna, steam room, administrative and ancillary accommodation, plant; (e) a 3-storey block of 6 no. 2-bed apartments with balconies on the south and west elevations; and (f) a 4-storey block of 2 no. 1-bed apartments, 5 no. 2-bed apartments with balconies on the east and south elevations, and ground floor shop (231sqm). The development is accessed from Main Street, Park Avenue and Marshfield Avenue Clongriffin via the internal road network and includes 192 surface car parking spaces, 221 bicycle parking spaces, landscaped gardens and all associated and ancillary site works.

1)      It is considered that in overall terms the heights and appearance of proposed boundary walls are excessive and reduce the perception of an open accessible/permeable development. While it is recognised that heights at some locations may need to be maintained for aesthetic reasons in relation to certain features and elements within the development, there are other boundary locations where lower heights would be a more acceptable solution. Drawings should therefore be submitted showing amended boundary heights incorporating railings where appropriate and justification provided where it is regarded that full heights should be retained.

2)      The west facing balconies on the west side of Apartment Block B are only screened by a wall for part of one side and otherwise exposed. It would be desirable from an amenity point of view for these balconies to be at least semi recessed. Drawings should be submitted showing these balconies semi recessed.

3)      The applicant should give an indication of a phasing programme for the carrying out of the different elements of the proposed development.

4) The applicant is requested to respond to the following Roads Traffic Planning Division (RTPD) requirements A) The Roads Department consider due to the multiple site uses and mix of public and private parking that there may be insufficient parking for the proposed development having regard to the number of uses and potential vehicular trips to the site. The reference to use of the existing park and ride facilities at the Clongriffin Town Centre is noted. However it is considered that the demands for on-street parking, which could arise due to inadequate provision of on-site parking will result in serious over-spill parking as it will force visitors to seek parking elsewhere in the area, which is already experiencing a shortage of residential parking. In this regard the applicant is requested to submit • A brief summation of how overall parking demands are reconciled as development in the rest of the area comes on stream and reduces both Park and Ride and on street availability of public parking spaces. • details for provision of additional off street car parking including coach parking facilities. It is advised that these facilities may be provided off site. B) The applicant is requested to submit a revised comprehensive Traffic Impact Assessment for the proposed development, which clearly examines the impact of the proposed development on the surrounding traffic network. The TIA should have particular regard to the following: • analysis of the adjacent roundabout/junction, i.e. Hole in the Wall Roundabout and Malahide Road N32 Junction • Details of existing traffic volumes and capacity on the adjoining road networks. • Details on the number of daily trips to the development • Details on the number of trips to the development on Fridays • Details regarding how coaches shall be accommodated at the site. • Details on the numbers of staff expected to be employed at the centre. C) The proposed provision of approximately 45 public cycle spaces within the development appears inadequate having regard to the number of visitors to the various uses within the scheme. The applicant is requested to address this issue and to identify locations (not necessarily within the facility) where additional public cycle parking to adequately meet the needs of the development could be provided. D) The applicant is requested to provide more detailed information regarding arrangements for service and drop off at the site. Specifically, they are requested to clarify exactly how it is proposed to service the proposed restaurant and ground floor retail units. E) The applicant is required to clarify what provisions have been made with regard to access and pick-up/drop-off for the crèche element of the development and to clarify how it is envisaged this will work. F) The applicant’s reference to proposals for event management are noted. However, it is considered that insufficient detail has been submitted in this regard particularly having regard to item No. 4 A above. The applicant is requested to submit more detailed information regarding how it is proposed to manage the transportation issues relating specifically to special events.

4)      The applicant is requested to respond to the following Drainage Division requirements A) Revised drainage proposals in line with GDSDS requirements, together with a revised flood risk assessment in line with the OPW guidelines “The Planning System and Flood Risk Management” demonstrating that there will be no additional runoff from the new development to the Mayne River as a result of the development, and therefore that there is no increased risk of flooding to property downstream as a result of the development. B) Furthermore, proposals for the scheduling of the completion of the surface-water attenuation lake are to be submitted for agreement as part of the AI submission. All drainage infrastructural works shall be completed prior to commencement of the proposed development including the rectifying of snags and misconnections.

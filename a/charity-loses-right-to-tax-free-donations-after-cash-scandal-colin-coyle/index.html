Charity loses right to tax-free donations after cash scandal

Colin Coyle

The Sunday Times Irish edition

© 2010 Times Newspapers Ltd. All rights reserved

ICROSS, a charity fronted by Mike Meegan, a controversial aid worker, has had its charitable tax status removed by the Revenue Commissioners.

The organisation will now have to pay tax on future donations, and may also have to pay back tax on donations received or income generated during its beneficial tax status.

Revenue can revoke an organisation’s tax exemption if “terms and conditions of the status” have not been fulfilled. It was revealed earlier this year that the charity had to return ¤97,000 of public money to Irish Aid after an audit found that the cash could not be accounted for on the ground in Kenya. Meegan has also been accused of sexual assault and financial impropriety by former charity workers in the east African nation, though he denies both allegations.

Last week, attempts by some members of the charity to wind it up at an extraordinary general meeting failed. Instead a new board of directors, which was split between supporters of Meegan and opponents of the Kenya-based aid worker, was formed.

A statement from the charity, released last week, said the new board would meet soon to agree the appointment of a chairman. “The EGM further agreed that the board will evaluate all of the options open to it for the future of the charity,” it said.

Tim Bourke, a director of the charity in Ireland, asked the Commissioners of Charitable Donations and Bequests to take over the organisation’s remaining funds, including €266,000 in cash and as much again in bequests.

However, the commission has refused, saying that, as the charity is a registered company, it can be liquidated only by its directors.

The new board includes Dr Joe Barnes, the organisation’s 95-year-old co-founder, who remains supportive of Meegan.

Meegan has argued that all of the charity’s remaining funds should be released immediately to the organisation’s Kenyan wing, where the donations were originally intended to be spent.

Icross declined to comment last week on the loss of its charitable tax status. Revenue refused to comment on why the organisation had lost its charity tax designation.

Falun Gong’s musical Shen Yun under Federal Investigation.

Uncategorized

A 2024 New York Times investigation into the Falun Gong’s musical Shen Yun revealed harrowing tales of trafficking, forced labour, emotional and physical neglect and abuse of the musical’s pre-teen performers; now, the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and the State Department are conducting a federal investigation into the living conditions and treatment of Shen Yun’s young performers and now—potential visa fraud. 

It’s been a rough few years for Li Hongzhi’s Falun Gong (Falun Dafa).

Following their expulsion from mainland China and the suppression of Falun Gong activities in the country from 1999 onwards, things seemed to be going relatively well—for the first decade of the century, at least—for the group in exile.

In the time since the China crackdowns, leader and founder Li Hongzhi has a media empire, Epoch Media Group, his first book Falun Gong translated into thirty-seven languages (available for sale on their website), and an own anti-communism musical Shen Yun (with holdings of $266 million) travelling the world to tell international audiences about China before communism, and how bad it is compared to the inherently “good” Falun Gong.

For a good few years, everyone west of the Middle Kingdom seemed to be on the side of the “persecuted religious group.” However, a series of scandals from accusations of money laundering, running of deceptive political advertisements, and the trafficking and exploitation of minors is seriously tarnishing the group’s reputation and international image.

(Falun Dafa (法轮功) Booth; (2012) Credit: Wikicommons.)

In the wake of the 2024 investigation, this month’s CBS article Shen Yun Dance Group Faces Allegations of Abuse and Exploitation describes the civil complaints brought against the Shen Yun Performing Arts Inc.:

“The lawsuit alleges that the organization dictates dancers’ weight, and regularly deprives them of necessary medical care. “The Shen Yun Defendants dictate the Dancers’ eating habits and weight, and regularly deprive Dancers from necessary medical care,” the lawsuit states. It further claims that “when Dancers are injured due to their grueling rehearsal and performance schedule, they are forced to work through their injuries.”

(The full civil complaint is included in the article in PDF format)

Shen Yun is a huge source of income for the Falun Gong and, in their own words, is a challenge to the dominance of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) in China: “The Epoch Times has previously reported, Shen Yun faces relentless interference and sabotage attempts by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), which sees Shen Yun as a major threat to the Party’s ideological control.”

The heavily guarded 400-acre Dragon Springs compound in upstate New York acts as a kind of HQ for Falun Gong and is the home of the training school for the Shen Yun musical. According to news reports, inside Dragon Springs, Internet access is restricted, relationships are arranged, and medical treatment is avoided.

The Fei Tian Academy of the Arts, the entity responsible for training the young Shen Yun performers, claims they are “students” and, therefore, not eligible for payment, which starts at $12,000 a year for older performers (in their twenties).

According to the People’s World, these students live in an “atmosphere of fear [and] there are also threats that individual dancers would ‘go to hell’ for mistakes they make and there is a lack of proper medical care for on-the-job injuries.”

FALUN GONG

Created by Li Hongzhi in 1988, Falun Gong (Falun Dafa) is a cultivation practice with its roots in the traditional Chinese exercise practice of qigong. Falun Gong aims to help practitioners improve their mental, spiritual, and physical well-being through a series of low-impact physical movements, meditation, and breathing exercises.

However, the group’s pseudoscientific claims about its benefits, as well as the rising influence of Li Hongzhi in mainland China, saw tensions rising between the government and the group throughout the 1990s.

On the 25th of April 1999, things came to a head after 10,000 Falun Gong practitioners turned up at the government compound in Beijing to protest against the escalating negative press in state-owned media and request the release of prisoners detained just days earlier.

Falun Gong was immediately labelled an “evil cult” and outlawed by the CCP. During the subsequent crackdown, many Falun Gong left China, and those who remained and protested (or went into hiding with Falun Gong literature) were sent to re-education camps where stories of torture, forced labour, and organ harvesting emerged.

(Photo: Falun Gong demonstration outside the European Parliament on July 14th 2016 (Brussels). Falun Gong practitioners called MEPs to sign Written Declaration 0048/2016 on stopping organ harvesting from prisoners of conscience in China. Credit: Wikimedia Commons).

China has long faced international criticism over its unethical organ harvesting practices, where organs from accident victims and prisoners provide a lucrative trade in a country where voluntary organ donation is not common. (Falun Gong followers believe they are specifically targeted due to their superior organ function [augmented thanks to the teachings of Li Hongzhi]).

Amnesty International, human rights groups, and members of the European Parliament have all expressed concern about the treatment of religious minorities in China, including the treatment of Falun Gong practitioners in China.

Falun Gong protests internationally are a reminder of China’s heavy-handed dealing with domestic dissent; but what often gets overlooked or lost in the discussion are the problems within Falun Gong, which —in light of increasingly negative media reports—seem to increasingly bear all the hallmarks of an authoritarian cult.

THE EPOCH TIMES

For a group based around meditation and exercises to improve physical and mental well-being, they’ve found themselves embroiled in a number of unrelated scandals during the last five years.

In 2019, Facebook banned Epoch Times accounts for deceptive advertising in the lead-up to the US presidential election, and even more recently, its chief financial officer has been accused of being involved in a sophisticated £52 million money-laundering scheme to boost the media group’s revenue.

The Epoch Times has been accused of spreading fake news, climate change misinformation, and is regularly flagged by watchdog groups as a problematic news source of questionable reliability; furthermore, the paper’s strong anti-vaccine stance seems to align with their belief that modern medicine pales in comparison to the teachings of Li Hongzhi (practitioners should not get ill), and regularly overstates the medical efficacy of Falun Gong practice.

Epoch Media Times has been vocal in its defence.

In response to the media reports about the mistreatment of Shen Yun’s young performers, the paper has responded with lengthy articles mentioning how the CCP uses Western outlets to undermine Falun Gong: “The CCP is well-known for its efforts to spread disinformation in Western society through proxies, including foreign media and social media influencers.”

Some people have labelled the musical as nothing more than Falun Gong propaganda, or expressed concern about it being used for cult recruitment (on TikTok, especially), but according to the Shen Yun Performing Arts website, that would be Westerners allowing CCP ideology to infiltrate their judgement and have them unwittingly parroting the “party” line:

“In recent years, the Chinese Communist Party’s condemnatory rhetoric against Shen Yun has insinuated into Western criticisms, which portray the troupe’s effort to rejuvenate a spiritual culture in the face of authoritarian suppression as an insidious political and religious propaganda.” 

A DI contributor (and possible Manchurian Candidate) in their 2022 essay Falun Gong: Recruitment and Retention, explored Falun Gong’s beliefs and the effect these have on its practitioners:

“Falun Gong’s observable presence [public meditations and protests], persecuted status, and altruistic public image attract people seeking social connection or interested in building relationships focused around the activities of self-improvement or political action. The threat of an identifiable out-group enemy [The Chinese Communist Party] then solidifies allegiance and attachment to Falun Gong. 

Changes in follower beliefs begin to occur with increased synchronous activity and frequent exposure to the group’s “infallible” doctrine and leadership, forcing followers to adopt more conservative beliefs, which are more consonant with those of the authoritarian leader.

As beliefs change, so do behaviours, and some of these changes may go against the follower’s best interests. These include dedicating an increasing amount of unpaid time to Falun Gong activities, adopting conservative political and social beliefs, deferring decision-making to the leadership, avoiding medical care and medicine, and, in one extreme case, suicide or self-harm to defend the group.

The belief that Faun Gong is the only means of achieving physical, mental, and spiritual salvation creates dependency and this—combined with the guilt the followers feel that they are failing other members and their broader mission through inactivity or inadequate practice—could be one strong reason preventing people from leaving or reducing their attachment to Falun Gong.”

You can read the full essay here.

With an increasing number of former members coming forward with tales of manipulation and abuse, will the public’s perception of the “politically persecuted” and “harmless meditation practice” finally begin to change? According to Dialogue Ireland’s Mike Garde:

“We need to realise that people today seem caught between oppressive states. This forces them to see answers in what appear to be wholesome movements, which often turn out to be as oppressive as the governments these movements are running from, or up against.”

First and foremost, let’s hope there’s justice for the victims bravely speaking out against the problems within Shen Yun. And with that, there’s a chance the public will begin to approach the line: “Falun Gong: Good” with a little more scepticism from now on.

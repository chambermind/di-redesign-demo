ICROSS boss accused of sex by Masai workers

By SHEILA FLYNN, IRISH MAIL

Accusations: Meegan faces claims he assaulted Masai tribesmen over 25 years

The High Court yesterday heard the sensational story of how The Irish Mail on Sunday investigated Michael Meegan over allegations of sexual assault, inappropriate behaviour and misappropriation of funds.

In February, the MoS began looking into the affairs of Meegan, founder and director of the Kenyabased ICROSS, the International Community for Relief of Starving and Suffering.

As the court heard yesterday, it uncovered claims dating back almost 25 years – all denied by Meegan on affidavit.

According to affidavits filed by the newspaper, as two MoS journalists researched the aid worker, they were informed – and saw documentation confirming – that Meegan had been investigated in 1986 by Kenyan police concerning allegations of sexual misconduct by a young male in that country.

Homosexuality itself is a crime in Kenya, and this particular complainant was a staff subordinate who was completely reliant on Meegan for his income.

The High Court heard the allegations at the time were so serious that an ICROSS board member, Dr Vincent Kenny, travelled to Kenya to assess the situation and assist Meegan.

Dr Kenny, a former chief of research in the department of international health and tropical medicine at the Royal College of Surgeons, claimed in a sworn affidavit that in 1986 he spoke by phone to Meegan, who denied the allegations were true but feared for his personal safety.

Dr Kenny also stated he spoke to Patrick Shaw, a CID officer in Nairobi. According to Dr Kenny’s affidavit, Mr Shaw said the allegations were extremely serious and, if proven, would result in a jail sentence for Meegan.

Mr Shaw allegedly expressed concern about the impact the allegations would have on the NGO community, the court heard.

Dr Kenny claimed in his affidavit that when he arrived in Kenya, he advised Meegan to return to Ireland and promised he would endeavour to get him into a masters course at Trinity College, which he did.

Meegan agreed and – before doing so – left a note at Dr Kenny’s hotel. A copy of that note, signed by Meegan, was provided to the High Court.

Meegan wrote that ‘true or false there is a sufficiency of information on the files to case a prosecution’ and ‘whatever the outcome the damage would be done if a prosecution took place.’ It continued: ‘While it may be technically a winnable situation, the effects would be detrimental to us all.’ He wrote he had been advised to leave.

The court heard that in January 1987, Dr Kenny presented a report to the ICROSS board stating that the operations and future of the charity’s projects in Kenya were in jeopardy as a result of the allegations about the personal conduct of Meegan.

He told the board Meegan had agreed to leave Africa in the interests of ICROSS, according to his affidavit. Just weeks later, however, Dr Kenny found out Meegan had returned to Kenya and was ‘interfering with the work of the charity’, his affidavit stated.

‘I raised concerns with the board of ICROSS Ireland about this and the fact that we had not put proper structures in place in the Kenyan project. I was worried that there were no statistics and no credible accounts coming from Icross Kenya.’ Dr Kenny resigned. Meegan, however, stayed in his job.

But Judge Nicholas Kearns was told in evidence that in February these claims – and a series of others – came to the attention of The Irish Mail on Sunday’s investigative reporter Michael O’Farrell. He, and African-based foreign correspondent Barbara Jones, looked into them.

Affidavits provided by the newspaper stated Miss Jones, in Kenya, recorded signed statements from six men employed by ICROSS who claimed to have been subjected, with varying degrees of consent, to sexual acts by Meegan – including sexual assault and caning on the bare buttocks.

All of them expressed considerable discomfort with Meegan’s actions because of their subordinate position relative to Meegan, financially and in terms of power and influence, the court heard.

Affidavits from the newspaper stated that none of the men received financial compensation for their statements and put themselves in relative jeopardy as all homosexual activity can be prosecuted in Kenya – and is condemned socially and traditionally.

One of the men, Meriape Ole Sangaire, 42, was the first employee to go to authorities about Meegan’s assaults, the court heard. He was the same staff member who made the allegations about Meegan in 1986, according to court evidence.

Meegan strenuously denies Meriape Ole Sangaire’s allegations in their entirety and accuses the Kenyan of fabricating the claims in the hope of gaining financially.

When speaking to Miss Jones on March 1, 2010, he said he first met Meegan in 1983, when he was 15. The court heard that Meegan, who borrowed land from the family to build an Icross compound, asked Meriape to come work for him and promised him an education. But after a few years, the abuse began.

‘I became Meegan’s first victim, I realise now,’ Meriape stated in documents-seen by the court. Meriape said he succumbed to a drinking problem and ended up in rehab. So did another man who gave statements to Miss Jones, Nixon S. Jacob, according to court evidence.

The court heard Nixon said he met Meegan when he was 13. He said he was also paid to be caned by Meegan, who became aroused by this. Nixon also said Meegan paid for men he contacted on gay websites to come to Kenya, according to court documents.

The High Court heard that another Kenyan man, James Njenga, made similar comments in his statement, claiming that young men were flown from Europe at the charity’s expense to sleep with Meegan. Meegan strongly denies all of the claims.

He was like a dictator: Volunteer Brion Ó Loinsigh claimed Mr Meegan used cash from the charity to fund his lifestyle

Another former ICROSS employee, Haron K Wambiri, described how he would create ghost projects for ICROSS, according to affidavits from the newspaper.

The court heard that, for example, donor money would be raised for a women’s empowerment group, and Haron would get his family to gather women together and give them a donation of Ksh100,000 or so for a project already up and running.

Haron would get an ICROSS signpost put up, take photographs to satisfy donors, then ICROSS would pocket the rest of the money, the court heard. Haron also said his job was to hand out cash to young men who agreed to be caned, according to documents seen by the court.

They would receive Ksh50 for being caned with trousers on, Ksh100 for being caned on bare buttocks, the court heard.

The financial irregularities within ICROSS prompted a PriceWaterhouseCoopers audit commissioned by U.S.AID, according to emails shown to the judge.

This audit, seen by the MoS, contained a alarming allegations. The court heard that, according to PWC, the charity did not maintain a cheque dispatch register for project payments or a fixed assets register.

The audit claimed the charity did not have an accounting policies and procedures manual and lacked reviews of bank reconciliations.

Some expenses were questioned as being ineligible or unsupported, and no supporting documents existed for various project work. Other documents appeared altered – including some related to travelling costs – and the charity had no centralised payroll processing package.

Financial concerns within ICROSS were also outlined in a letter sent by the charity’s board to Meegan in 2006 and outlined in court. Among the persistent allegations mentioned was the skimming off of ICROSS funds for personal use and to support Meegan’s lifestyle.

Another was that Meegan used his position to induce employees to engage in false reporting about the use of donated funds and the effectiveness of projects. The court heard that the board said the allegations must be investigated and proven false if ICROSS were to continue receiving any funding from donors.

The board requested Meegan agree to such an investigation, according to emails put before the judge.

But the board’s concerns about his behaviour did not stop there, evidence showed. The letter outlined in court addressed a ‘constant stream of allegations’ regarding Meegan’s affinity for the company of young men, including alleged inappropriate sexual relations with young men in the Ngong Hills ICROSS compound.

It pointed out that some of the allegations deal with Meegan’s abuse of power and authority to extract sexual favours. The letter outlined in court also addressed allegations by several different people that Meegan regularly visited chatrooms which would not be considered appropriate for someone in his position.

It stated journalists asked the charity about Meegan’s apparent membership of gaydar.com.

The MoS viewed and provided the court with a copy of a profile of Meegan on the gay website, which features a seductively posed photograph. Meegan insisted in an affidavit that the site was a fake.

Throughout the MoS investigation, the newspaper repeatedly contacted Meegan for responses to the various allegations according to affidavits from the newspaper. He repeatedly declined to comment and sought a High Court injunction to stop the MoS from publishing anything about his personal life – claimin statements would defame him and infringe on his rights.

His legal team even furnished two affidavits in which Meriape allegedly rescinded the allegations he made about Meegan. But according to evidence seen in court, Meriape’s name is spelled wrong. And in another affidavit, he vehemently denies he was involved in making those statements and denounced them as forgeries.

Court evidence also revealed, however, that just weeks ago Meegan tried to get Kenyan police to make criminal complaints against witnesses who had given statements to the MoS – and to have the men arrested and charged.

The judge raised the issue in court, asking whether Meegan unleashed police on the men because of the paper’s enquiries. But the court was told the men who spoke to the MoS remain anxious to see Meegan brought to justice.

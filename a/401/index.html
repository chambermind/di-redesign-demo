Dialogue Ireland draws attention to NAPCP’s failure to make report available

Therapy Groups

Therapy Groups – Roebuck House

NAPCP: Dialogue Ireland is publishing this letter in protest at the lack of transparency by NAPCP in refusing to allow its published report being made available.

Dialogue Ireland is receiving reports that though the name Roebuck House is no longer up on the Rathgar Road premises the same practices revealed in the Irish Times, Liveline and in the NAPCP Report are continuing with people reporting practices that we hoped were no longer occurring. http://www.locallink.ie/view.php?i=304454

We intend to picket the premises to draw attention to these developments.

WE STRESS THAT THE MESSIANIC BELIEF THAT REGISTRATION WILL

SOLVE ALL THE PROBLEMS IS NOT BORNE OUT BY EXPERIENCE. CERTIFICATION WILL NOT STOP MALPRACTICE.

——– Original Message ——–

Subject:

Refusal to allow access to published document

Date:

Fri, 13 Feb 2009 01:02:41 +0000

From:

Dialogue Ireland <info@dialogueireland.org>

Reply-To:

info@dialogueireland.org

Organisation:

Dialogue Ireland

To:

John Farrelly <johnfarrelly@accord.ie>

Dear John,

Further to my call today it is clear that you are allowing your fear to takeover from your duty of care to current victims of Roebuck House. Also I believe your best interest as an organisation is in publishing your report so that those who were victims can take comfort. You must expect terrible threats from people who reject what you found in your report. However, they should not dictate your policy. In June 2008 this you folks said, “In a letter, the NAPCP has praised the ‘courage and determination’ of complainants and congratulated them on “the firm stand you took on behalf of all of us involved in counselling and psychotherapy”. You then proceeded to not allow them the opportunity of receiving the report, a most bizarre response having commended

them for their courageous stand.

Most contradictory was this statement, “John Hughes of the NAPCP, which was founded in 1963 as a certifying agency for counsellors and psychotherapists, said the board would now be circulating its findings on the Roebuck centre. “We have no power to stop them so we are taking all steps to alert relevant authorities including the gardaí and psychotherapy and counselling bodies,” he said.”

In fact you are doing the opposite, you are refusing to help those left in the field of caring for past, present and future victims of this dysfunctional centre.

I requested this document last year and was stonewalled, I requested it again last week, and was directed to speak to you and now you are too busy to engage. It is my intention to publish this mail on my web site if you do not furnish us with a copy of your report after giving a period to reflect on your position. It is not a question of you needing to go to the board again. You decided last year to publish the report but

immediately withdrew it so that its findings can’t be used. Like Irish banking, counsellings’ problems will not be solved by registration and certification, rather it will require courageous people to police the area. Certification will not stop malpractice.

Regards 

MIKE GARDE 

DIRECTOR

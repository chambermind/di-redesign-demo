The Irish Times – Friday, April 30, 2010

Complaints about advertising upheld

ALISON HEALY

COMPLAINTS ABOUT weight-loss claims, mobile phone offers and car adverts were among those upheld by the Advertising Standards Authority in its latest bulletin. Its complaints committee upheld 15 of the 20 cases it examined. Three of the 20 cases came from the authority’s monitoring programme. One such case involved press advertising for Tony Quinn slimming products. One advertisement claimed a woman had lost 31 lb (14kg) with Mr Quinn’s weight loss system. Another claimed a product could shrink the user’s waist.

The authority wrote to Mr Quinn seeking substantiation for the claims made. The company said all testimonials featured genuine customer statements and said the products were not required to undergo individual testing to bear claims as they were food products. The complaint was upheld.

Complaint

Bulletin             10/2

Batch No.        192

Ref      12749

Product            Health & Beauty

Advertiser        Tony Quinn

Agency

Medium            Press

Complaint

Advertisement:

Press advertising for Tony Quinn states Weight Loss imagine 31 pounds of butter. Thats how much body fat Ashika Shah lost with our weight loss system. Ms. Shah in her testimonial featured in the advertisement said I was fed up with not being able to wear the clothes I wanted. I changed my diet, introduced supplements and took up Tony’s system of weight loss training. There were two other similar testimonials quoted in the advertisement. The Weight Loss Pack referred to was Amino-Or & Amino Energise. 1 months supply 65 and 3 months 175. The second item advertised was Profemme. The advertisement stated Streamline your silhouette with Profemme. 49 1 Kg. Shrink your waist. Help balance hormones. Great for skin and teeth. The footnote of the advertisement also referred to the following Supplements also available for arthritis, eczema, psoriasis, stress, sleep, sports.

Complaint:

The advertising came to the attention of the Secretariat through its general monitoring programme. The Secretariat contacted the advertisers directly and asked them for substantiation for the slimming claims made in their advertising and also drew their attention to the fact that marketing communications should not offer any product or treatment for serious or prolonged ailments or for conditions requiring the attention of a registered medical or qualified practitioner.

2006 Code

8.5 9.1

2002 Code

Response

The advertisers in their response stated all testimonials featured in their advertising were genuine customer statements and that these were kept on file. In relation to the footnote at the bottom of the advertisement naming certain conditions, the advertisers said that the products mentioned on the page did not relate to these conditions. They said that the statement did not refer to them offering products to prevent cure or treat such conditions. In relation to the request for substantiation in respect of the Weight Loss Pack and Profemme the advertisers forwarded a lengthy document in relation to the constituent parts of the products.

Further Information:

The Secretariat asked the advertisers for information/substantiation on product testing for the actual products advertised as opposed to the constituent parts.

The advertisers said that the products in question were food products and that food products are not required to undergo individual testing to bear claims. They said that under EU food law claims attach to the ingredients and to nutrients of which the food is a source and that food claims must in fact be on the basis of such ingredients or nutrients. Furthermore they stated that to be a source of the ingredient or nutrient in question is sufficient for a food to bear a claim. The Secretariat wrote to the advertisers once again referring to Section 9.1 of the Code and asked them if there was evidence that the products advertised had achieved the results referred to and to please forward the relevant substantiation. The advertisers did not respond to this request.

Conclusion

Complaint Upheld.

The Complaints Committee considered the detail of the complaint and the advertisers response. The Committee noted the contents of the document forwarded by the advertisers in relation to the constituent parts of the products advertised. However, they noted that the advertisers had not forwarded substantiation in relation to the claims made in their advertising on the actual product itself.

The Committee accepted that the advertisers were not offering to prevent or cure the ailments mentioned in the footnote of the advertising but reminded them that marketing communications should not offer any product or treatment for serious or prolonged ailments or for conditions requiring the attention of a registered medical doctor.

Action Required:

The advertisement must not run in its current format again.

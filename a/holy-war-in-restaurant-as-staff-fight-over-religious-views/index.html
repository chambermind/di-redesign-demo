Holy war in restaurant as staff fight over religious views

AN ALLEGED row over religion between two staff members at a Tuam restaurant ended up with one man grabbing the other in a headlock.

Matt Morris, Dennistown, Belclare is charged with assaulting James Rushe on May 2 at Waterslade House Restaurant, Shop Street, Tuam.

Garda Hanley told Tuam Court that on the day he received a complaint from an employee in the restaurant. It appeared there had been a row over religion which ended in Mr. Rushe claiming he was assaulted by Mr. Morris.  When Garda Hanley interviewed Morris he admitted the offence. He had caught Rushe in a headlock in the kitchen and they fell to the floor as a result. There were no long term injuries, the Garda said.  Addressing Judge Mary Fahy, Rushe said Morris was a Born Again Christian and he had been preaching at him. He claimed he had been sacked soon after for not answering his phone having only worked there a few months.

Mr. Morris told the Judge that he did grab Rushe in a headlock but that it wasn’t viscious. “You shouldn’t have done that,” said Judge Fahy. Morris said it was just a discussion. He claimed Rushe had been provoking him by saying “f-ing Jesus.”

Judge Fahy told Morris he was being paid to work and not to preach or discuss religion. Morris denied he was preaching and said he was in an evangelical group.

“If you disagree on something you walk away. You don’t assault someone,” said the Judge.

Morris’s solicitor said his client wouldn’t be in a position to pay compensation and thought that a probation report would be helpful. Judge Fahy adjourned the case until December 17 to allow for the preparation of such a report.

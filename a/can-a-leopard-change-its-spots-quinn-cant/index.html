Can a leopard change its spots? ~ Quinn can’t

Tony Quinn

Can a leopard change its spots; Quinn can’t? ~Early Quinn HQ Artane.

This is the house in Artane which was the site of the first Tony Quinn commune in 1973.  It was owned by a follower of his at the time.   His organisation was then called “Yoga,” which was his start before its next incarnations of Blueprint, Educo, Good Life etc.  This follower came under Quinn’s influence and “gifted” the house to him.

This was one of the first times when a follower was influenced to make a big cash or material donation to Quinn.  But the follower’s family took the matter to Court and Quinn had to rent the house instead.  This was an early example of action against him because of his cult activities and using his influence over his followers to get them to give him major assets.  This has been repeated in the Educo years where families have sought to protect assets from Quinn’s influence over his followers, and also recently where he has tried to run an oil company in which he had made no investment as a cult-like organisation through his influence over some of its directors.  This has resulted in three separate legal actions against him, one of which was settled after a default judgment against him, another of which resulted in the damning judgment of Judge Bannister which said Quinn had been running the company as though it was his own private property with no regard for shareholders’ rights, and the other of which is due to come to Court in January 2013.

Also as we reported in November it looks like settlement is the only game in town. It is all done and dusted and people are now hiding behind confidentiality agreements. No more see you in court stuff, rather see you in the virtual bank.

In Artane in 1973, Quinn packed young followers of his into the house, having them sleep on the floor as there weren’t enough beds, sending them out to drop leaflets all over the city for hours on end, often returning only late at night.  From reports we have heard from ex-followers who lived there, conditions were overcrowded, chaotic and unhygienic.  They still had to pay rent for this as though they were living in a normal place.

Quinn didn’t live there.  He was still living at home with his Mammy.  Although he was 27 years old at the time, he had never lived away from home, but much younger people in the Yoga classes were pressured to move to the crowded Artane commune and work for him without pay – they had to find the money for food, rent and other necessities themselves.  Many of them worked and then had to leaflet for most or all of their spare time.

The next commune was Templeogue, a much bigger house where Quinn eventually left his Mammy and moved in.  Here he had a huge bedroom to himself (we are told it was the former chapel in what had been a religious house) and his own separate facilities such as his own kitchen.  In this commune the followers selected for his own personal service could tend to him, while the “runners” (low level followers) did the “grunt” work of leafleting etc.  We are told, however, that even the “elect” had to leaflet from time to time, possibly to pacify the runners, possibly to remind them that only Quinn was truly special and of superior rank.  Quinn never did any of this work.  This has been the pattern of his life ever since.

Sometimes we hear from people who want to make excuses for Quinn – say he started out meaning well, but got flattered, corrupted, etc along the way.  The history of the communes in Artane and Templeogue is proof that only two years after he started in 1971 he was eager to exploit his followers in every way he could, and the only thing that controlled him was determined opposition by people who were not under his influence.

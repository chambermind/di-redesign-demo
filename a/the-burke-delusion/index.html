Enoch Burke ‘clearly has something to hide’ about his income, judge says.

Sacked schoolteacher ‘lies in every social media post’ about why he is in prison

Shane Phelan

December 09 2025

A judge has said it is clear from Enoch Burke’s refusal to disclose information about his income and expenditure that he has “something to hide”.

Mr Justice Brian Cregan also speculated that the sacked schoolteacher’s refusal to abide by court orders could be motivated by his family’s wish to make money from his campaign.

The judge made the comments in a blistering ruling rejecting an application in which Mr Burke sought the deletion of several paragraphs from a recent judgment jailing him for the fourth time for contempt of court.

Mr Justice Cregan stood over his comments, in which he described Mr Burke as a “a baleful and malign presence”, “roaming” the corridors of Wilson’s Hospital School and “stalking the school, its teachers and its pupils”.

Mr Burke had claimed the judge assassinated his character, but Mr Justice Cregan rejected this, saying he had chosen his words carefully and was satisfied there was no error of fact.

“Mr Burke has assassinated his own character by his deeds and words. I have simply described what the evidence has shown,” the judge said.

Later in the ruling he described the case as “exceptional” and referred to Mr Burke’s repeated refusal to disclose information about his earnings.

“Why is it exceptional? Because Mr Burke has made a calculation that it brings him notoriety and his 15 minutes of fame,” the judge said.

“It could also be because he and his family wish to make money from this campaign.

“I note in this regard that Mr Burke has refused to comply with a number of court orders directing him to furnish an affidavit of income and expenditure. It is clear that he has something to hide in this regard.”

It is the second time in recent weeks that Mr Justice Cregan has raised the issue.

Last month, he said Mr Burke’s refusal to disclose such information raised questions as to whether the teacher had other sources of income.

Mr Burke attended the hearing via video link from Mountjoy Prison, where he was returned on November 25.

He has now spent more than 520 days behind bars for contempt of orders requiring him to stay away from the Co Westmeath school, where he was a teacher of history and German.

Mr Burke, a fundamentalist Christian, was suspended from work in August 2022 after publicly clashing with management over a request from the then principal that a transgender pupil be called by a new name and they/them pronouns.

He was sacked in January 2023 for gross misconduct and is appealing his dismissal.

Despite the suspension and sacking, he repeatedly turned up “for work”, even when the school obtained court orders barring him from doing so.

As well as being imprisoned four times for contempt, he has been fined €225,000 by the High Court, fines he refused to pay.

He has repeatedly wrongly claimed to have been imprisoned and fined for his opposition to “transgenderism”, with several judges making clear the sanctions were for breaching court orders and not due to his religious views.

Before delivering today’s ruling, Mr Justice Cregan asked Mr Burke’s mother Martina and siblings Isaac and Ammi to leave the courtroom, saying they were “consistent disruptors of court business”. They refused to leave and had to be removed by gardaí.

Mr Burke objected to their removal but the judge directed that his microphone be muted and he was not given the opportunity to address the court further.

Today’s ruling related to Mr Burke’s objection to the wording of a judgment handed down by Mr Justice Cregan on November 18. He complained there were “several factual errors”.

In one paragraphs cited by Mr Burke, the judge said the teacher had been “roaming” around the school’s corridors, and was “a baleful and malign presence, an intruder, stalking the school, its teachers and its pupils”.

In another, the judge said Mr Burke’s “level of verbal aggression, unregulated anger and lack of self-control, combined with his deliberate strategy of confrontation, make him a potential danger to pupils and teachers of the school – as they have indicated in their affidavits”.

Mr Burke complained the judge’s comments were “baseless”. He said the word baleful meant “threatening harm” and “menacing”, and the word malign meant “evil in nature or effect”.

The teacher claimed the comments were contradicted by an affidavit in which the current principal, Noel Cunningham, said he had never seen Mr Burke touch or handle any person in his attempts to gain entry to the school.

Mr Burke also said no teacher or pupil had ever sworn an affidavit regarding him or his conduct.

But the judge said Mr Burke had obtained word meanings from “some sort of Google search”, which only gave one meaning, and an extract from the ‘Little Oxford English Dictionary’, which also only gave one meaning.

Mr Justice Cregan said that among the definitions of the word “baleful” in the Oxford English Dictionary were “full of malign, deadly or noxious influence; pernicious, destructive, noxious, injurious, mischievous, malignant”.

He said the meanings of “malign” in the dictionary included “characterised by ill will”, “malignant” and “malevolent.”

“Most, if not all, of these meanings of the words ‘baleful’ and ‘malign’ apply to Mr Burke’s ongoing trespassing and unlawful presence at the school. I chose these words carefully. They seem to me to be entirely apposite. There is no error of fact. I will not be removing these words,” Mr Justice Cregan said.

The judge went on to say “roaming” was entirely accurate and that he has used the word “stalking” figuratively, rather than referring to the criminal offence of stalking.

Mr Justice Cregan also stood over his comment that the teacher was “a potential danger to pupils and teachers”, citing an affidavit sworn by Christopher Woods, chair of the school’s board of management, which expressed concern the school could be held liable should Mr Burke cause a health and safety risk.

The judge rejected Mr Burke’s claim that the comments amounted to “character assassination”.

It was Mr Burke’s own conduct that had assassinated his character, the judge said.

“Character is revealed by both actions and words,” said Mr Justice Cregan.

“This then, is Mr Burke from a review of his actions and words.

“A teacher who verbally berates a bishop during a religious service. A teacher who berates the principal after the school service, in front of pupils, teachers, and parents.

“A teacher who has been dismissed for gross misconduct. A man who trespasses upon school property when the school does not want him there.

“A man who breaches court orders directing him not to trespass on school property. A teacher who would rather that the school pay money for security guards to keep him out rather than spend €1,000 a week on the needs of its pupils.

“A man who is prepared to sacrifice the pupils of the school for his cause.

“A man who prefers a strategy of confrontation rather than one of dialogue and compromise. A man who exhibits an abnormal degree of verbal aggression and disrespect.

“A man who lacks insight into the inappropriateness of his behaviour.

“A man who verbally abuses all judges before whom he appears. A man who lies on affidavit, a man who lies to security guards, and a man who lies on every social media post which he puts up online about the reasons why he is prison.”

The judge said one of the most disturbing aspects of the case was “the amount of lies being told by Mr Burke about this matter”.

“It is not open to Mr Burke to state falsely why he is in prison and to pretend that he is in prison for a different reason,” the judge said.

“Yet in statement after statement to this court, and elsewhere, Mr Burke says that he is in prison because of his opposition to transgender rights. He knows these statements are lies. Yet he continues to make them.

“Mr Burke seems to be trying to inhabit a reality of his own, with his own ‘Alice in Wonderland’ language, where words mean what he says they mean, where his lies are the truth, and everything everyone else says are lies.

“This court is concerned with what are, objectively speaking, the facts and truth of the situation.”

UNHAPPY 40th ANNIVERSARY FOR QUINN IN HIS CULTIST ACTIVITIES

By one of our Tony Quinn Experts

Magical Yoga Medicine Man:

It’s now 40 years since Quinn, the former butcher and bodybuilder started his cult activities in July 1971 when he transformed an existing class run by a sincere woman into his own version of a Yoga class. Quinn incorporated his own version of occult teachings with messianic claims and prophecies fronted by yoga exercises and healthy eating to attractive the general public . Behind the scenes Quinn ran, with a brutal regime of fear and dogma that was not to be challenged, a cult of mostly young people who worked incessantly for him for little or no money. Many of those who broke away told the stories of his vindictive nature, wholesale exploitation sexually, mentally and also financially by using their unpaid labour for himself while they lost their youth and career opportunities and in some cases their minds and health. Though many were dissatisfied by aspects of Quinn’s activities they often stayed in his communes around Dublin supporting each other in the hope that the group would evolve into a positive spiritual force in the world and the accumulating financial assets would be distributed fairly in the group. Needless to say this never happened and by the early 90s many of the original group had left leaving those with deep financial slavery or extremely emotionally tied up / manipulated by Quinn hanging on to mingle with the new followers they were recruiting.

Blueprint for Living:

The failure of Quinn’s claims to be the Messiah, his failed promises for great spiritual achievements for those who would follow him, the failure of his group to become a major spiritual force in the world and to transform the world spiritually was all too obvious. Despite his followers’ tremendous efforts to promote Quinn including ensuring most of Ireland got the guru’s message and advertisements for his products delivered in his Blueprint newspaper into the letter boxes around the country, Quinn failed to make his promised breakthrough. His products, teachings, lack of real professional training /qualifications to justify his activities and general failure to deliver on his promises were frequently criticized by professional authorities and knowledgeable experts. Faced with the failures of the 20 years Quinn decided to blame his followers for all the failures and announced he was now more or less going it alone unless those who wished to stay with him accepted his new version of the old cult.

Educo Seminars:

This new version which evolved into the Educo seminars contained a far greater emphasis on worldly success but also contained the old occultist and messianic pretensions but they were kept further in the background to avoid frightening off the public. To get out to a new generation of “punters” it was necessary to have a serious rebranding and relaunching of the old guru and occultist as he had received a lot of negative publicity in the Irish media over the years. This involved his manager Colette Millea having the task of attempting to whitewash the old guru and his sordid activities especially his bizarre sexual antics. This should not read as a prurient interest in sex, but rather as a dimension which has been brought to our attention by many women over the past 20 years. There is also referenced on our web site to these issues going back to the ‘70’s. https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2010/02/16/summary-of-media-coverage/

This was the equivalent of trying to whitewash a dung-heap and was inevitably going to fail. The bad smell kept leaking out and eventually matters blew up more seriously for the guru. In the beginning of the new millennium unfortunately for Quinn a major TV documentary blew open the Educo organisation and its cultist nature, the exploitation and the bizarre goings-on in the background in Quinn’s movement. His sidekicks’ and manager’s attempts to rebrand Quinn and sell him as a “success” coach and business guru, as Ireland’s answer to Anthony Robbins, was now in tatters. However by a pyramid selling-style operation, the cultist manipulation and pressure exerted on selected seminar attendees, the organization continued to draw in reasonable numbers attracted by the quasi-spiritual elements of seminars or the promises of “get rich quick by mental power” techniques the guru proposed. Unfortunately for Quinn as the decade progressed dissatisfied ex-Educoists and older voices from the past shaking off their fear began to reveal more and more about the dark side of the cult. The false claims and shady and corrupt financial and other problems were highlighted. The reports of Quinn’s widespread use of his “kundalini” sex and occult sex magic followed by the sexual beatings and domination including the violation felt where surgical enhancements and alterations had been endured to pander to Quinn’s tastes, while under the mind manipulation but later greatly regretted, were extremely disturbing. Despite Quinn’s old strategy of hiding behind flunkies sometimes frontmen but mostly frontwomen he was experiencing his past catching up with him especially in relation to the dire treatment of young female members of his cult. Then dramatically later in the last decade Quinn was the subject of a stream of highly critical media reports that really publicly exposed the guru, his inner teaching, the extreme messianic pretensions at the core of his philosophy and the bizarre sexual perversions and many other undesirable aspects to an already suspicious public. By the end of the last decade and after the media criticism which still continues in the present the “guru” now in pension age had lost a great deal of his following.

The issue of oil:

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2009/01/18/the-tony-quinn-story-%E2%80%9Cfrom-butcher-to-oil-slick%E2%80%9D/

His diehard followers, including the handful embroiled with him since the 70s, continue to want to believe in Quinn and recruit anyone they can while the cynical ageing guru has transferred much of his remaining amorous drives to an oil company. Quinn initially was an ardent critic of the oil venture in Belize but now benefits greatly from the financial and legal protection and power of the oil company by having joined the board of the company. Initially Quinn’s relationship with the oil project was to vehemently reject it in public pronouncements and he predicted it to fail but when it succeeded, through Educo supporters on the board of the company he was brought into the company. The oil connection has been an advantage and disadvantage for Quinn. The ongoing conflict within the company in the years since Quinn became involved and much of this conflict questions Quinn’s influence and role, has led to many reports on the company’s problems all of which keeps bringing Quinn’s name up again and again. The public can draw their own conclusions about Quinn’s about this and about Quinn’s track record. The great decline in attendance at Educo seminars despite a raft of strategies and pressures to get them to listen to the “guru” is proof of the public’s rejection of Quinn. His regular legal threats and attempts to intimidate critics and ex-members has failed to silence their voices.

The 40 years anniversary of the Quinn cult provides a good opportunity to reflect and evaluate further on the mental and financial damage experienced by many at the hands of this man and his accomplices. Forty years and a sense that whenever he sends a writ all should cave in. Many media outlets are unwilling to take their public remit seriously.

Sun goes down on Icross: Colin Coyle and Tom Lyons The Sunday Times March 7, 2010

Icross Charity

From The Sunday Times

March 7, 2010

Sun goes down on Icross

Colin Coyle and Tom Lyons

http://www.timesonline.co.uk/tol/news/world/ireland/article7052670.ece

At its annual charity balls in Dublin, sketches by Britney Spears, Bono, Sarah Jessica Parker, Cate Blanchett and Roy Keane were auctioned, while the model Rosanna Davison and members of The Corrs hosted €2,000 tables. Despite being behind bars, Jeffrey Archer contributed a drawing of a lighthouse, “a beacon of hope”.

Icross Ireland also had the support of Garret FitzGerald, the former taoiseach, and Liz O’Donnell, a former minister in charge of overseas development. Its biggest donors included NCB Stockbrokers, which gave it €137,000 between 2006 and 2007, and Sir Anthony O’Reilly’s Ireland Funds, which donated €21,000 in that period. Irish Aid, the government’s overseas development agency, was also a supporter, giving the charity €450,000 between 2000 and 2005 to be spent on projects for Aids victims and orphans in Kenya.  But now Icross Ireland, which was founded by Mike Meegan, a former Rehab/ESB International Person of the Year, is to close following allegations of financial impropriety. After an inspection trip to Kenya in 2007 by Irish Aid auditors, questions emerged about the whereabouts of almost €100,000 of taxpayers’ money. Subsequent audits and field reports commissioned by Icross Ireland into its Kenyan division found evidence of financial mismanagement. The Irish and Kenyan branches of the charity are at odds. Although separate legal entities, the organisations are intertwined, with Icross Ireland raising funds for Icross Kenya’s local operations.  Icross Ireland has returned €97,000 to Irish Aid and is seeking to redirect another estimated €200,000 in private donations to other “worthy causes”. A spokesman said that an estimated €300,000 in bequests to the organisation would not be accepted.

Icross Ireland confirmed last week it had received several “personal” allegations in recent weeks about Meegan — understood to be claims of sexual abuse, which Meegan denies — and said that it was taking them seriously.

Meegan has been regarded as one of Ireland’s most respected charity campaigners. In 2005 he was awarded an honorary degree by NUI Galway on the same day as the actor Martin Sheen. But his reputation as a selfless campaigner has been questioned in recent years. In 2006 it was discovered that he had overstated his qualifications in an attempt to win grants, claiming he had a doctorate even though his qualification came from Knightsbridge University, an unaccredited “mail order” college. It later emerged that he had represented himself as a priest in the 1980s and received a donation from FitzGerald addressed to “Fr Meegan”. When questions about this title were raised, he said that he intended to return to his studies and eventually become a priest. In subsequent interviews, he was referred to as “Brother Michael Meegan”.  The Sunday Times also revealed that the Elton John Aids Foundation and US Aid, the American government’s overseas development wing, had stopped funding the organisation after carrying out audits of its books. Brion Ó Loinsigh, a former volunteer, voiced concerns to Irish Aid in 2007 that money and medicine sent from Ireland weren’t reaching their intended recipients in Kenya. Growing concerns about Meegan’s operations in Kenya led Meteor to “postpone” a prestigious €100,000 humanitarian award it intended to give him in 2007. Previous recipients include Bono, Elton John and Adi Roche.

Vincent Kenny, a former Icross director, said last week that Meegan had been the subject of “regular allegations on an almost annual basis” since the 1980s, some “serious”.

Kenny said he travelled to Kenya on behalf of the charity to investigate the claims but “by the time I met [the accuser], he had withdrawn the allegations”. The former director claimed it was “astounding” that, despite concerns over Meegan’s work in Kenya, numerous institutions continued to endorse him, including the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland.

Yesterday a solicitor for Meegan warned The Sunday Times that any abuse allegation made against Meegan would trigger High Court proceedings. “Michael Meegan never sexually abused the individual in question, or any other individual,” said PB Cunningham & Co, a Dublin legal firm.  “Our firm is in possession of copies of two sworn statutory declarations of [a named person] which make clear that any suggestion that he was abused by Michael Meegan is ‘completely false’.”

Icross Kenya said yesterday that allegations of financial impropriety on its part were false but Icross Ireland was being run in an “unsatisfactory” manner. It alleged there has been a failure within Icross in Dublin.

It also defended its own financial management, saying: “As with any multi-centre programme in rural areas there are always areas to improve and develop. Over the last two years significant restructuring and restaffing has taken place.”

Ever since we heard about Liam Egan we have been trying to get a handle on the influences which led him and wife to convert to Islam. Now we have a greater understanding of his journey we can understand the black and white thinking and extremist rhetoric he views represent. He was a member of the International Church of Christ.* This was a group that were very active in Ireland until their eclipse in the midst of the last decade. They believed that they were the only Christian Church and unless you were baptised by them you were going to hell.

http://www.dialogueireland.org/dicontent/a2z/dubliccoc/index.html

A former leader from the group wrote to me in these terms about 5 years ago:

“I suppose it was hyped up by the black and white attitude we had to everything and also your sometimes belligerent approach.  However, we should have listened, we didn’t and that, as far as I can see, was wrong.

I see now the damage of not taking instruction, going it on our own, being elitist.  It shipwrecked many peoples faith, and I feel awful about that.  If only with the wisdom of hindsight we could turn the clock back.”

We can see where the extreme views which Liam brought with into Islam have their origins in this movement. It is clear then that he jumped from one form of extremism into another. I wish to repeat this has nothing to do with Islam or for that matter Christianity.

What is interesting is that a Syrian Muslim converted to the International Church of Christ in London. He was Ayman Akshar. He was the founder of TOLC and a dedicated opponent of the Church in London. http://www.tolc.org/

He helped so many people who got involved with International Church of Christ in London, but unfortunately 5 March 2002: Ayman Akshar, the founder of TOLC, died this morning at 7:10 GMT at the age of 42.

“Ayman touched so many lives with his good humour, energy, kindness and belief in what was right. Life won’t be the same without him.”

He was a speaker at a conference in Dublin in 1999. Many of those who left the group in Ireland were helped by the Church of Christ in Ranelagh, who pastor is Tony Coffey. Considering the topic it is interesting that Ayman told me when at our Conference that his life was at risk for converting to Christianity if he returned to Syria.

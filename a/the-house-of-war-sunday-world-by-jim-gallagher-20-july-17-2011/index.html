THE HOUSE OF WAR SUNDAY WORLD BY JIM GALLAGHER 20 July 17, 2011

House of Prayer

THE HOUSE OF WAR AS PREVIOUSLY LOYAL AIDES QUIT

SUNDAY WORLD BY JIM GALLAGHER

July 17, 2011

EXCLUSIVE Woe for Christina

THE Visionary’s’

empire starts

to crumble as

lieutenants

abandon her

 House of War1

House of War2

SELF-PROCLAIMED VISIONARY CHRISTINA GALLAGHER’S HOUSE OF Prayer Empire is at war following the shock resignation of some of her inner circle of diehard supporters.

 

As just a few dozen of her last remaining believers met in Achill Island yesterday for an anniversary celebration, her worldwide organisation was imploding. American businessman John Rooney, Gallagher’s one-time chief lieutenant in the US and main money man, has finally seen the light and had a bitter split with the woman he once revered. All mention of Rooney and the House of Prayer he built for her in Steubenville, Ohio, has been wiped from Gallagher’s official website. The Sunday World can also reveal today that the House of Prayer’s chief fundraiser in Ireland, Majella Meade, has also quit the organisation.

Fanatical

The former fanatical devotee is now being blasted from the pulpit of the House of Prayer on Achill Island for treachery. Even the Virgin Mary has deserted Gallagher. Christina claimed Our Lady told her she will no longer be appearing to her because of all the media attention. From now on, Gallagher must take her guidance from Jesus alone. Last night, it was some of that media attention that led to an official complaint to the gardai after a Sunday World photographer was almost run down by an angry member of the congregation. The snapper was standing across the road from the House of Prayer discreetly taking snaps of the crowd when a car was driven straight at him. “I managed to dodge out of the way but I think this guy was trying to clip me with the mirror,” the shocked snapper said last night. “He then stopped the car, reversed and jumped out screaming at me that I wasn’t to take his picture. I hadn’t, as I had my camera over my shoulder when he came at me.

“Suddenly he hit me a belt to the side of the head and I reached out to stop him from hitting me again. Then he landed another thump and got back in his car and sped off.” The photographer added: “I had been there all day discreetly taking pictures and nobody was taking any notice of me. “But when Christina herself came out to address those gathered outside and ranted about the media intrusion, I felt the atmosphere soured. I had no choice but to go and report the assault to the Gardai in Westport.” Today’s revelations come just three weeks after the Sunday World revealed how the entire board of the House of Prayer resigned after they grew disillusioned with 58-year-old Gallagher, who lives a millionaire’s lifestyle.

This newspaper can also reveal that the Mayo “visionary” has finally admitted that a secret £2m mansion uncovered by the Sunday World in Shropshire, England, in March is her home from home. Her official website admitted she had “procured”

the mansion for herself and got her gullible supporters to pay for it. Devotees had to sign contracts saying they knew their donations were for Gallagher’s “personal use” and that their “absolute” gifts could never be reclaimed by themselves or their families “at any time, under any circumstances”. Gallagher has told her followers that Jesus guided her to the mansion, which has its own lake, seven bedrooms, seven bathrooms, indoor swimming pool, gym, snooker room, bar, hot-tub, steam room and lift. Her website said Our Lady had chosen her previous home, a magnificent €4m palace in Malahide, Dublin, where she still spends most of her time. The secret estate was no longer “a secure place” after the Sunday World uncovered it in 2008 and so, according to her website, she needed the new one in Shropshire. The departure of John Rooney will be a massive blow to the self-proclaimed visionary.

The 67-year-old grandfather, who is listed in the land registry as the owner of Gallagher’s Malahide mansion, helped to launch her American empire. He met Gallagher while on a trip to Ireland in 1998 and he and his whole family became obsessed with her.

Mystic

The Mayo mystic, who claims she gets regular messages from the Virgin Mary and Christ about the imminent end of the world, was even godmother to one of his grandchildren.

Rooney ran the Confraternity of Our Lady Queen of Peace which was behind Gallagher’s five houses of prayer in Ohio, Texas, Minnesota, Florida and Kansas. There is also one in Mexico. Two years ago the Confraternity, of which he was president and secretary, had assets of $6.7m and income of nearly $3m. Now Rooney has finally cut his ties with Gallagher. It was unclear last night what will happen to the Ohio House of Prayer, which Rooney and his wife Joanne were instrumental in building. One family source thought he might hand it over to the local Catholic

diocese. The Mayo woman and her obsessed priest Fr Gerard McGinnity were at its launch in December 2006, when she claimed Jesus, the Virgin Mary and St Bernadette all appeared to her. Gallagher claimed anyone who donated to the house would go to Paradise on Judgement Day. The Sunday World left messages on John Rooney’s phone but he did not respond. The departure of Majella Meade is another huge blow to the millionaire mystic, as she was the chief fundraiser for the Achill House of Prayer. Meade took the Sunday World to the Press Council in 2009 over our exclusive exposés of Gallagher’s astonishing lifestyle. But despite  making over 100 complaints, she lost them all.

Cancer

The 32-year-old, who has been battling cancer, did not want to comment last night except to say: “I am happy to be out of the House of Prayer. I have been struggling with illness and just want to get on with my life.” The three directors who quit last month – Annette Casey, Eithne Lavery and Bridie Conneally – also declined to comment but said we should ask the House of Prayer for their letter of resignation which would explain everything. “You should ask the five members who choose the directors,” they said. The five “members” are Christina Gallagher, her estranged husband Paddy, daughter Mary, Fr McGinnity and devoted ex-nun Treasa Doyle. Gallagher, who was born into poverty in Knockmore, Co Mayo, has led a life of luxury since opening the House of Prayer in July 1993.

Proposals made by a research Scientist in regard to improving the Educo Seminars

Tony Quinn

Proposals made by a research Scientist in regard to improving the Educo Seminars

Date: 17 January 2003

Dear XXXXXX

You have asked me to put some suggestions in writing. These ideas have emerged as a result of my observations of a number of people I know very well xxxxxx who have attended TQ seminars. They were stimulated in particular by experiences that xxxxx and I had during the 6 weeks after xxxx returned from Tony’ Palm Springs seminar (TQ32). I should say at the outset that xxxxx and I are now extremely content with the results of the seminar.

These suggestions are nothing more than suggestions, and I would like you to appreciate that I make these at your request. I have neither attended a seminar myself nor am I an expert in hypnotherapy, energy medicine or other related disciplines. I have, however, been a research scientist (biology) for many years and have worked for the past 10 or so years with Imperial College London.

These suggestions are offered as a possible means of further improving the profile of the TQ organisation, and should therefore be seen as contributing to a solution rather than a problem. Importantly, they also anticipate difficulties which might emerge in the future, given reactions by certain elements in the community.

The suggestions I have developed can be divided into 2 main areas:

  1. Building an effective support system. By working with individuals and families who might have responded negatively (possibly through lack of understanding of the TQ principles), a negative feedback system (common to all healthy and dynamic biological systems) is created. It effectively turns a negative in to a positive, rather than ignoring the negative.
  1. Making appropriate and legitimate scientific claims about research conducted to-date. This is necessary to prevent or reduce potential future criticism of the TQ approach.

These will be dealt with in-turn:

1.         Building an effective support system

1.1              Dialogue Ireland. I have spoken at considerable length with Dialogue Ireland (DI) on several occasions over the last four or so weeks in order to understand their perspective on TQ. I made contact with DI after there was no response to a ‘call for help’ email sent to a seminar facilitator by Ellie. It appeared from these conversations that DI do not have sufficient information on which to make full judgements as to the content and purpose of the TQ seminars, and there is evidence that they have made various attempts to contact the TQ. They told me that the TQ organisation had not responded to their communications. It is important to appreciate that DI see themselves as offering ‘pastoral support’ for people who have returned from TQ seminars and are feeling disorientated or are in some form of distress – and more importantly for family members and friends of seminar participants who are having difficulty coping with behavioural and other changes in their loved ones. DI deal with a wide range of quasi-religious organisations and have told me they are unable to devote adequate time to the TQ issue. Their total budget is claimed as €12k pa so they find it frustrating that a large organisation like that of TQ does not in their view support properly the small proportion of people and their families who have suffered some adverse reactions to TQ seminars. DI claim that the number of people contacting their organisation for support has been increasing quite dramatically over the last two years or so, although they have not provided an actual breakdown of figures. It is strongly suggested that communication channels between the TQ organisation and DI are opened, and it is possible that I could facilitate this process. One approach might be to invite key personnel in DI to a meeting, show the new video, and then move to open discussion.

1.2              Screening of participants. The TQ organisation should screen candidates for seminars much more stringently. No-one should be allowed to board a plane to the destination of the seminar unless all criteria in questionnaires are met. The questionnaires should seek to determine any past history of serious psychological or emotional disturbance.

1.3              Monday meetings. I am aware of attendees to these meetings who have said that information espoused at meetings is sometimes erroneous and in conflict with the TQ material, this situation arising particularly when persons less familiar with the material are asked to facilitate meetings because the usual facilitator has been unable to attend. Some form of accreditation to verify a person’s familiarity with the material, if this does not already exist, may be useful, so that it can be ensured that only TQ-accredited persons facilitate meetings. If such a person is not available for a given meeting, the meeting should be cancelled. Meetings should also provide a forum that helps people to achieve their goals.

1.4              Grounding of seminar participants. Several people, some of which are specialists in psychology or neuroscience, have indicated to me that they have noticed that significant numbers of people returning from TQ seminars remain in a ‘zombie-like’ state for several days or weeks following their return. This clearly creates some difficulties for these persons and probably even more so for their friends and families. Some specialists have suggested that it might be possible to provide a programme at the end of the seminar which helps to ground people better so that they are in turn better able to integrate into their lives following their return.

 

1.5              Dedicated  support system for seminar participants. A contact support person(s) should be clearly nominated at each seminar so that TQ seminar participants (and nominated relatives, partners etc.) are able to gain prompt support by telephone and e-mail. This support should be provided within 12 h of telephone or e-mail contact being made. Such a person may need to be qualified in psychology, psychotherapy or a related discipline. A specific password-protected area of the TQ website could also be set up for dedicated use by seminar participants; this could have a Q & A section, testimonials as well as a facility for email support.

1.6              Controls for TQ e-groups. Conditions should be applied to regulate the nature of communications on the TQ email groups. Communications should be kept to matters pertaining to the material and related subjects. This encourages the use of the site specifically for support/growth and provides an environment of real ‘safety’, shown to be important in other forms of group therapy e.g. re-evaluation counselling (after Harvey Jackins).

 

2.         Making appropriate and legitimate scientific claims about research conducted to-date

2.1              The TQ literature (handed out at seminars and for promotion). This should be updated to more accurately reflect the published science on the subject (this is not to say that methods, approaches or processes that are not [yet] in the published literature are invalid, my point is simply that false claims must not be made). References to published research should be given where appropriate. If a something is said to be scientifically validated it must have been subject to peer review and therefore findings supporting it must be published in a reputable peer-reviewed journal. The simplest way of ensuring this is carried out correctly is to involve personnel in the School of Psychology at the University of East London (UEL) in the editing and compilation of materials.

2.2              Verbal communications by TQ agents. It is important that presentations and other forms of promotion and verbal communication made by TQ agents are accurate scientifically. It is suggested that all agents are provided with written briefings indicating the status of validated scientific knowledge on the subject so that clear distinctions can be made over what is proven and not yet proven from a scientific standpoint.

2.3              Video. It is strongly recommended that UEL are involved in assessing the scientific validity of statements made in the new video and that any adjustments are made prior to its release to ensure maximum credibility of the ideas and approaches expressed. It may be necessary to distinguish those processes that are presently validated by science, and those that are not (yet), stressing that because something is not validated scientifically does not imply it does not exist or is unimportant.

 

2.4              Catalepsy and other demonstrations. Codes of conduct relating to the public demonstration of catalepsy are published by some major, internationally recognised professional bodies (e.g., International Society for Professional Hypnosis) and these should be heeded as far as possible. Should catalepsy be demonstrated, an accurate explanation of the current knowledge on mechanisms causing it should be given. If relevant, it may be necessary to provide information on possible side effects.

I hope these suggestions, which are my own opinions, might be of some use to you and the organisation. Should you have any queries, please do not hesitate to get back to me.

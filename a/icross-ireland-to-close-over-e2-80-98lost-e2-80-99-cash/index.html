Icross Ireland to close over ‘lost’ cash, Colin Coyle

http://www.timesonline.co.uk/tol/news/world/ireland/article7052632.ece

Icross, a charity whose supporters have included former taoiseach Garret FitzGerald, is to be shut down after failing to account for the expenditure of €97,000 of government funds on projects in Kenya aimed at helping orphans and Aids victims.

A spokesman said it is being wound up because of “concerns about financial impropriety and allegations of a ‘personal nature’ against Mike Meegan”, its Kenya-based founder. Its funds — estimated at €200,000 — are to be given to other charities, while it will not accept bequests amounting to €300,000.

An Irish Aid audit of Icross Kenya, the African wing of the charity which is fronted by Meegan, found explanations about the expenditure of taxpayers’ money to be “unsatisfactory”. Documents released under the Freedom of Information Act show that the state gave the charity €137,988 in 2004 and €225,964 in 2005. Almost €100,000 of this could not be “traced” when a team of Irish Aid officials visited Africa to inspect Icross Kenya’s books in 2007.

Icross Ireland is a separate legal entity to Icross Kenya but the organisations are intertwined. The Irish wing is involved in fundraising, while Icross Kenya works “on the ground” in Africa. A spokesman for Icross Ireland said it had “moved to shut down Icross Kenya” but this was complicated as it was a separate legal entity.

The Irish Aid audit of Icross Kenya found that €20,000 intended for a community- based orphan care project was “unaccounted for”. Irish Aid was also unable to trace €11,000 intended for a project titled Strengthening of Reproductive Health Services. Auditors were told by Icross Kenya that “it was not possible for [them] to have access to the account from which the funds were expended”. The costs for constructing and equipping a care centre ran over-budget by €11,000.

Icross Ireland agreed to investigate the findings of the audit, including evidence of “fraud issues” relating to training expenditure. A subsequent audit by Ernst & Young of Icross Kenya, commissioned in 2008 by Icross Ireland, found “a wide range of internal control weaknesses”. Although Icross Kenya again agreed to put in place “rigorous financial systems and controls”, a field report by an consultant hired by Icross Ireland concluded that the recommendations had been ignored.

Icross Ireland has now refunded €97,000 to Irish Aid. Icross Kenya has rejected allegations of financial impropriety and said Icross Ireland is being run in an “unsatisfactory” manner.

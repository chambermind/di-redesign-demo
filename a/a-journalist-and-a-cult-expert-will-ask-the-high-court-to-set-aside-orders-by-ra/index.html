A JOURNALIST and a “cult expert” will ask the High Court to set aside orders by Ray Managh

Tony Quinn

A JOURNALIST and a “cult expert” will ask the High Court next week to set aside orders directing them to give evidence in a multimillion-dollar US lawsuit. The lifestyle guru and businessman Tony Quinn is one of a number of defendants in the lawsuit.

 

‘Sunday World’ journalist Nicola Tallant and Mike Garde, director of a charity that helps victims of cults, have written extensively about Mr Quinn. They were considered by defendants in the case in Denver, Colorado, to have relevant

evidence. Mr Justice Gerard Hogan last month directed they should give evidence in the case but, since the application had been made in their absence, he allowed them to apply to have his orders set aside. The evidence was to be recorded in Ireland in writing and on video. Counsel for Mr Garde, Seamus Tuathail, said his client held himself out to be an expert on cultism and could not be forced to give evidence as an expert witness.

Ronan Lupton, for Ms Tallant, said she would be claiming journalistic privilege and opposing the court orders. The US case arises from the sale of shares in International Natural Energy (INE), an oil company in which Mr Quinn is a director.Former director Jean Cornec is suing for $15m (€12m) for shares he claims he sold to a fellow director, Susan Morrice, and was never paid. A number of parties are suing and counter-suing each other in the US case. Counsel for {Mr Cornec,}correction this should read  Susan Morrice Jim O’Callaghan, told the court he required an adjournment to facilitate detailed briefing on Colorado law with regard to journalistic privilege. Mr Justice Hogan said that he would consider the evidence and hear legal argument on September 7 as to whether he should set aside his orders directing Ms Tallant and Mr Garde to put evidence on deposition.

Catch Trustees
A Court Report
11th June 2013

Dialogue Ireland Report
https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2013/06/13/hades-or-heaven-friday-june-14-the-four-courts/

A Judge has directed the Gardai to locate and bring before the Court the trustees of a-non denomination church who have continued to obstruct bank appointed receivers from taking possession of three of their properties
Brendan Hade, his wife Shelia and Gerry Byrne, who are the trustees of the Victory Christian Fellowship, are wanted arising out of what the High Court heard is an organised campaign prevent the recievers from taking possession of premises at Kilmacud House, Kilmacud Road, Upper Stillorgan, Co Dublin; Westland Row and Firhouse Road, Tallaght.

Yesterday at the High Court Mr Justice Paul Gilligan was informed the trustee’s current whereabouts are unknown to the Gardai, who made a series of dawn raids at the trustees homes on Wednesday.

The receivers, chartered accountants Paul McCann and Patrick Dillon, were appointed last month over the churches properties by Bank of Scotland after it failed to satisfy a demand for €18m in respect of unpaid loans.  However since they they have been prevented from gaining access to the buildings.

Last month they obtained temporary injunctions against the trustee, and two of the Hade’s sons Niall and Brian Hade,  requiring them hand over possession of the premises to the receivers and restraining interference with the receivers carrying out their duties.

Brian Hade

The receivers claim the court’s orders have been ignored. The trustees along with several unnamed others have continued to blockade and prevent them from gaining possession of the properties. Last week the Court granted orders that the defendants be brought before the court for their alleged contempt of court orders.

Yesterday Mr Justice Gilligan heard Gardai were unable to locate either Brendan and Shelia Hade of Fountain Head, Rockbrook, Rathfarnham Dublin or Mr Byrne, of Woodstown, Knocklyon, Dublin.

They were neither at their homes nor at any of the three premises at the centre of the case.

In order to enforce the attachment orders made by the High Court last week Gardai had to force entry into both the Hades and Mr Byrne’s homes shortly before 6am on Wednesday morning.

Mr Justice Gilligan directed the Gardai to use “every reasonable resource at their disposal” to locate the trustees who he said are “at large” and bring them before the High Court. The matter was adjourned to Friday morning next.

Two of the Hade’s sons Mr Niall Hade and Mr Brian Hade, who the receivers also alleged had obstructed the receivers, were brought before the court by the Gardai. Niall Hade was detained at his home at Highfield, Woodtown Way, Stocking Lane Rathfarnham, while his brother was detained at his parent’s home.

Mr Brian Hade, who said he has a lease to run a cafe at the Westland Row premises, was released from custody after he agreed to comply with the Court orders. Mr Hade told the court he had “broken no laws” and was a “law abiding citizen.”

However after being informed by the Judge about the serious consequences of non-compliance with the orders Mr Brian Hade gave an undertaking he would obey the orders.

His brother, represented by Mark Lynam Bl said the issue with the church and the receiver’s actions had nothing to do with him. He had nothing to do with any blockade, and was happy to comply with any court order. He was not a trustee of the church. Any matter to do with it was his parent’s business, he added.

He was on a family holiday in France, and was unaware of efforts made to inform him of the court proceedings against him. He returned on Monday. The Judge directed Mr Niall Hade be released after lawyers for the receivers said they were no longer seeking orders against him.

Yesterday (Wednesday), James Doherty Bl, for the receivers said members of a security firm hired by the receivers had visited the premises in recent days, and again remained unable to gain access to the properties.

Person conducting the blockade, counsel said, had acted in a threatening manner towards the security staff. They had refused to identify themselves or say on whose authority the acted. At one of the premises one person involved in the blockade would only identify themselves as “Mother Theresa”.

Counsel also said some of those conducting the blockade had walkie talkies, and an Alsatian dog.

Counsel asked if the court was minded to extend the attachment orders to any others involved in the blockade. However Mr Justice Gilligan said he was only prepared to deal with the proceedings “step by step,” and was no prepared to extend the orders at this point in time.

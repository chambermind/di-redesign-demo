23 December 1978

The Left-Hand-Path Of Tantra… And The Ramakrishna Mission

Religion

By Willem Van Hatten

From one of our subscribers, paster Willem C. van Hattem, Parana, Brazil, we have received the following letter, which is a commentary to “Salvation and Death” by Johannes Aargaard, UP-data Vol. 2. No. 2.

Dear Prof. Aargaard:

As a good Lutheran pastor I cannot else than concord with you that your ‘guru’ must be opposed not because his practice and preaching do not fit, but precisely because they fit together so wel;. I also concord that he teaches a tantric way of salvation. But I’m asking myself if you are doing justice to the guru when you write “sex at the same time is given both too little and too great importance” (p35).

Doesn’t our “guru” teach here the Ramakrishna view of sex?

On page 157 of the book Swami Ramakrishna (Hinduism its meaning for the liberation of the spirit) we read: “For a certain type of aspirant, called ‘heroic’, the actual drinking of wine and practice of sexual union are prescribed, and the teacher carefully points out that the joy and stimulation arising from these are to be utilised for the uplift of the mind and the physical plane” and on the same page: “Even on the physical plane, a couple becomes united in the sexual act, but the unity of Siva-Sakti and the bliss derived from it are experienced only by liberated souls”.

This is a part of the Vamachara or Left-Hand-path prescribed by Tantra for the awakening of Kundalini by heroic characters (vira).

On page 159 we encounter the three groups of aspirants according to thier mental disposition as: animal heroic and divine. From the animal-type is said: “He is not allowed even to touch the gfive ingredients of the left-hand-ritual”. Are these animal-types perhaps what your guru calls “ordinary persons”? I.E: does your (not-so) good friend perhaps substitute some words? And does he understand under the “ordinary persons” those who not yet raised themselves above the common round of convention, nor have cut the three knots of hate, fear and shame?

My question is: Does the guru not simply convey to his disciples the left-hand-path of Tantrism, obscured by his “translation” of some words? You wrote: … a man who can see no difference between himself and God (P35) Bit: “Finally the Kundalini riseds to the lotus at the cerebrum and becomes united with Siva, or the Absolute, and the spirant realises…his union with Siva-Sakti” (Nikhilanda p 152). And: “The disciple does not look upon his guru as a physical being, but as the embodiment of God” (p 153). And: “The sadhaka of divine (divya) … good in every way, he is regarded as the embodiment of Siva” (p 158). Now the ” aspirant learns from the teacher how to use the aforesaid five ingredients for his spiritual awakening” (p 157)

Now I can fully accept the fact that there are some hysterical disciples who like to have the Shakti stuck in the second chakra, just as we in the west had nuns who liked to confess sins only for being stripped and whipped by their confessor, who – I think – derived some pleasure from this too, as your guru as a human being certainly will do too, or…is he earnestly teaching how to use the ingredients? You write that the man was “unable to discover a natural, relaxed relationship.” My question is: is there anything like this on the left-hand-path?

The too little and the too great importance of sec you attribute to the teachings of the guru, I think we find also in the official missionary literature of the Ramakrishna Mission. Writes Nikhilanda:

( too little )

A ) Sexual union is something sacred, whose purpose is the creation of new life, and it should therefore not be resorted to in an irresponsible manner.

( too much )

B ) a couple in the sexual act..the unity of Siva-Sakti.

C ) a hero ( vira ). He has the inner strength to ” play with fire ” and to burn his worldly bonds with it.

D ) The sadhaka of divine disposition…can do no evil.

At the other hand it seems to me that the second chakra not only is the fatal point where power can be lost through sexual activity. I think this is the fate for “ordinary man” or the animal-type, for the ” heroic-type ” however  and your guru appears (or seems) to belong to them – it is also the point where power may be gained. As the guru has such an outspoken preference for the strong ones or he­roic types, we must conclude, that he as the embodiment of the godhead ( Siva ), teaches how to use the ingredients and how to play with fire to gain strength.

Now I come to my point and principal question:

Also you write: “it is the same with most gurus” ( p 34 ) thus breaching the boundaries of the gurus sect, did you not too much see the guru as an isolated phenomenon “a third class guru with first class disciples”, or at least depict your guru too much as an isolated phenomenon? As I already stated at the beginning of my letter I concern fully with your conclusions, but I think these conclusions not only concern Swami Narayanananda, but the Ramakrishna Mission as well.

May be, that the swami broke away from the Ramakrishna Mission, but – I think – his teachings are basically the same. So I think you owe your readers one more article on this sub­ject, placing the guru against the Ramakrishna-mission back­ground and inserting in your conclusions two words only after the first word of the ultimate paragraph: But, as Christians, we cannot allow our sympathy etc

Objectively, as scientists of religion, I think we cannot resolve which of the systems is really sick, but thanks to God in Christ, we may resolve this by faith. It is this faith that speaks out of your conclusions, and I think, that even as scientists of religion we cannot leave this faith at home. So, dear brother, thanks for the conclusions of your article, but your guru does not stand alone, we find his colleagues with the same Ramakrishna background and the same missionary outlook in:
England (Ramakrishna Vedanta Centre)
South Africa (Ramakrishna Centre of S.A., Johannesburg)
Australia (Ramakrishna Vedanta Society)
U.S.A. (Ramakrishna-Vivekananda Centre, New York, and 7 other centres:)
South America (Ramakrishna Ashrama, Buenos Aires, Argentina) Fiji islands (Ramakrishna Mission)
France'(Centre Vedantique Ramakrishna, Gretz)
Japan (Nippon Vedanta Kyokai, Tokyo)
Mauritius (Ramakrishna Mission Vacoas, Mauritius)
– and the many free-lance gurus’ operating yoga-centres and schools.

Might you not like to write another article, then you may  also adapt this letter for publication, provided that you concord with me. It is my opinion that the swami fits beauti­fully not only in the tantric system but in the left­hand-path also as exposed by Swami Nikhilannada in an of­ficial edition of the Ramakrishna-Mission.

Please forgive me my observations on your article, but wor­king at the moment on a booklet (Hinduism and its modern ramifications: Yoga, TM, Ramakrishna, Harekrishna and iso­lated gurus) I discovered so much identity, that I think it necessary that your “Danish Guru” is seen in line with the others. Especially Ramakrishna, as many people – according to the adds and rubrics in certain esoterical and spiritual reviews here in Brazil – have a keen interest in Kundalini­Yoga. I think, however, that the main interest is sex:

Sex played on a spiritual level, or which should be a spiritual level. Interesting that most of the adds (+ 90%) of persons interested in Kundalini-Yoga, come from women, which leads inevitably to the question: is Kundalini a new word for upperclass prostitution? Who knows??? Anyway it sounds more sophisticated to say: I practised Kundalini-Yoga, then: I slept with a whore: The matter, however, is the same, and here we are back with the activities of the “guru”, with one more question: is it not possible that the guru is honest, but that simply the eastern “methods” – which I regard with you as abhorrent – attract some westeners (e­specially women) as a “new” way to satisfy their lusts?

But when women are attracted, the men follow, something ever happening after Genesis 3:6b. Anyway Kundalini-yoga is part of a mission program and this in a world which is over­sexed and looking for new stimulus. Isn’t it wonderful when this new stimulus has a spiritual basis too?

Isn’t this what many look for: satisfying their lusts and doing this in an – assumed – spiritual way? -cfr.. Children of God!!

I could continue in this way, asking questions, but already I took too much of your time, so this must be the end of .this letter.

Thank you for your fine article on your “Danish” guru, but please see the worldwide implications.

Yours in Christ

Willem C. van Hattem

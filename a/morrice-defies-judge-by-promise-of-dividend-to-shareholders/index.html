MORRICE DEFIES JUDGE BY PROMISE OF DIVIDEND TO SHAREHOLDERS

Tony Quinn

Judge Bannister of the BVI Commercial Court says that there is a “serious question” that the 64,000 Class A shares “gifted” to Tony Quinn for no payment to the company could be legal under the terms of the INE’s true Operation Agreement of 2002.

http://www.eccourts.org/judgments/decisions/2012/HC/stkitts/SMLifeVenturesvSusanMorriceetal.pdf

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/07/30/judge-bannisters-judgement-read-it-for-yourself/

“It seems to me that there is a serious question (I put it no higher) about the purported allotment to Mr Quinn. Allotments of shares to Additional Members under the 2002 Operating Agreement required to be supported by the written consent of members holding at least two thirds of INE’s outstanding voting units.  Assuming that the signatories constituted a majority on 10 October 2006, the members were required when allotting shares under the 2002 Operating Agreement to stipulate for payment of a Capital Contribution in consideration of the allotment.  The written members’ resolution makes no reference to the provision by Mr Quinn of any Capital Contribution in exchange for the issue of the shares.    Although, as I have said, Capital Contributions are widely defined, they are not so widely defined as to cover an allotment in consideration of no Capital Contribution at all.  While there is evidence that Mr Quinn involved himself in the affairs of INE to some extent, there can be no suggestion that such activity was in satisfaction of a promise made in exchange for an allotment of Class A shares:. On the contrary, all appearances point to the shares as having been intended as a gift from. Mr Quinn’s admirers, Ms Morrice and Ms McCaffrey.”  [Clause 24, page 7]

So, the 2002 Operating Agreement needs not just a two-thirds voting majority of the shareholders to vote to issue new shares, but ALSO needs a Capital Contribution, that is money or services equal to the value of the shares, to be made to the Company.  Judge Bannister has already given his opinion on this – there is no proof whatsoever that the second condition, a Capital Contribution, has been met.  Tony Quinn’s services to the company, such as they were, were not made as a Capital Contribution in exchange for shares.  Therefore there is a “serious question” about the validity of the shareholding.  The “gifters”, or at least Morrice, appear to have realised this problem and tried to strengthen Quinn’s grip on his “free” shares by concocting a letter justifying the gift “in gushing terms” and stating its value as $16 million.  Judge Bannister gives his opinion about this letter

 “I have no doubt that this letter, which sits uncomfortably with the other part of Ms Morrice’s case, that Mr Quinn was universally treated as a Class A member of INE from the outset, is a clumsy forgery. By this I mean to say that while Ms Morrice and Mr Cornec may have signed it (although that appears improbable) Ms McCaffrey did not and, even if she did, the document is plainly an after the event concoction written in an unsuccessful attempt to legitimize the allotment.” [Clause 27, page 8].

Who gets hurt by this?  The Class B shareholders – issuing more shares means that each share already existing is worth a smaller piece of the company, because the pie is divided into more slices.  The 2002 Operating Agreement, which formed the contract by which Class B shareholders made their capital contributions i.e. their money, did not allow this to happen unless the shares were issued in exchange for a contribution which made the pie bigger.  This cannot be changed by a vote of any number of shareholders.

So if even one Class B shareholder wishes to present a petition to Judge Bannister to rule that the terms of the 2002 Operating Agreement were not met and the shares issued to Quinn are invalid, it seems that Judge Bannister is waiting to hear from them.

“Whatever the true position about the purported allotment of 10 October 2006 there can be no doubt that Ms McCaffrey, having signed the resolution, joined in the ceremony of 10 October 2007 and held Mr Quinn out as a Class A member,, can have no personal standing to complain of It, SM Life can be in no better position. There remains, however, a serious question whether INE itself or members of INE who did not participate in this purported allotment are bound by it.”  [Clause 28, page 8]

That is, Sheila McCaffrey (who took the Court action in the name of her company, SM Life) was actually one of the people who gave the shares in the first place, so she cannot go to Court about this because she cannot bring an action against herself.  Judge Bannister, under the rules of the Court, cannot issue a Judgment on something that no-one has asked him to judge.  No-one has asked him to judge on the validity of the shares yet.  Only shareholders who did not participate in the gift, the Class B shareholders, are in a position to do this, because they are the ones who were hurt by it and so have a right to take a petition to the Court.

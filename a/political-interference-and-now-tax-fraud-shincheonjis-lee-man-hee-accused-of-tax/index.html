Political Interference and Now Tax Fraud—Shincheonji’s Lee Man-hee Accused of Tax Evasion Worth an Estimated 7.5 Billion Korean Won.

ChristianShincheonji

In Seoul, South Korea, the joint police-prosecution investigation headquarters investigating Shincheonji for ‘political interference’ has added suspected tax evasion of 7.5 billion won (approximately 4.5 million Euros) to the list of charges against leader Lee Man-hee.

The legal problems are mounting for Shincheonji (SCJ) and its 95-year-old leader Lee Man-hee, who—amongst Shincheonji adherents anyway—is considered the immortal messiah, the ‘promised pastor‘ who will lead the sealed 144,000 of the Twelve Tribes through the end times and divine judgement described in the Book of Revelation.

However, it was a frail, elderly man with a walking cane who still required another’s arm for support as he struggled into the Seoul Central District Court on the 24th of June, 2026, to answer to a detention warrant hearing for alleged political interference.

As of last week, Korean prosecutors have added tax evasion to the list of charges against Lee Man-hee and other members of SCJ. They are accused of corporate tax and VAT evasion between 2016–2020 for earnings from SCJ’s ‘snack bars’ that were registered as churches. According to ChosunBiz:

The task force believes they disguised snack bars operated by local churches nationwide as sole proprietorships under believers’ names to conceal revenue-generating businesses. It concluded they evaded taxes by keeping double books for the stores and hiding cash sales.

Earlier, in 2020, the National Tax Service conducted a tax audit of Lee and 12 Shincheonji tribes and reported them to prosecutors on suspicion of tax evasion. The Suwon District Prosecutors Office decided not to indict in 2021, but the task force decided in February this year to reopen the investigation.

Lee Man-hee’s initial June arrest came as part of a police investigation into impeached former president and former leader of the People Power Party, Yoon Suk Yeol, who declared martial law in December 2024, citing that the rival Democratic Party (DPK) was colluding with North Korean communists and engaging in ‘anti-state activities‘.

As of 2026, Yoon has been jailed for life for his failed insurrection, but the charges are still mounting, with him receiving additional sentences for illegally sending drones to North Korea and a suspended sentence for lying during his 2022 presidential campaign.

Yoon is not the only one in deep legal trouble.

The team of prosecutors and police investigating found connections between politicians and the Unification Church (AKA the Moonies), and Yoon’s wife and former first lady, Kim Keon Hee, recently was found guilty of accepting bribes in the form of luxury bags from Hak Ja Han, the widow of another “second coming/messiah” from South Korea—and the focus a few older Dialogue Ireland posts—the late Sun Myung Moon.

More on the Unification Church in the Dialogue Ireland archives:

The legal dragnet didn’t stop with just one problematic Christian group, as the joint team investigating the former president believes it has evidence that Lee Man-hee pressured over 50,000 of his followers to join the PPP in a suspected attempt to influence the 2022 election and secure political support for SCJ.

Lee has been suspected of using the church’s regional branches to pressure more than 50,000 followers to join the People Power Party, or PPP, from 2021 to 2024 in hopes of influencing the party’s presidential and legislative primaries. Investigators suspect the campaign, which allegedly included efforts to support Yoon’s presidential bid, was aimed at winning favorable treatment for the church, including permits to expand its facilities. (Source: AP News)

The front and pretext for this bold, brazen, and politically motivated move was…a Pilates club.

On the 21st, a Shincheonji official, Mr. Lee, who was investigated by the joint police-prosecutors task force (headed by Kim Tae-hoon), stated, “At the time, Pilates was popular among young people, so we named the operation ‘Pilates Operation’ to make it sound like an exercise club.” (Source: The Chosun Daily)

For a religious group that claims it is ‘the truth,’ SCJ has a real issue with lying, especially where its recruiting of new members is concerned.

SCJ recruiters (known as ‘harvesters’ in SCJ lingo) will seldom divulge their real identity or intentions, and will use a front organisation or alias to avoid any connection to SCJ. Just a few of the names used (You can learn more about the various names and faces of SCJ here):

  • New Heaven and New Earth
  • Seven Thunders 
  • Mannam Volunteer Association
  • Mannam International Youth Coalition (MIYC)
  • International Peace Youth Group (IPYG)
  • Heavenly Culture, World Peace, Restoration of Light (HWPL)

SCJ released an official statement on their YouTube channel two days after Lee Man-hee’s arrest.

We deeply regret the Prosecution-Police Joint Investigation Headquarters’ application for an arrest warrant against the Church’s Chairman, Mr. Lee Man-hee.

Chairman Lee has acted responsibly by complying with every request from the authorities and has demonstrated good faith by cooperating fully throughout the investigation. Therefore, it is our deepest regret that a request for an arrest warrant has been filed against Mr. Lee, despite there being no risk of flight or evidence tampering.

In particular, despite his advanced age of 95, Mr. Lee has consistently responded to the investigation with sincerity. This clear history of compliance points to the lack of necessity and the disproportionality of pretrial detention.

Shincheonji Church of Jesus intends to present a thorough account of the relevant facts at the upcoming warrant review hearing, and we trust that the Court will render a wise judgment grounded solely in objective facts and legal merits.

Only today, the 28th of July, the Korea Times reported that police investigators back in March had overlooked SCJ membership in the political opposition, the DPK.

This story thus far beggars belief, but it feels like we’re in for more twists and turns and political-religious scandals down the line.

With their leader in jail, sources report that SCJ internationally is responding to the arrest by ramping up its recruitment activities. Recruiters can usually be found hanging around university areas during term-time, targeting undergraduate students who might be away from home for the first time and, therefore, receptive to a friendly stranger’s advances and an invitation to a group activity.

Still, since the news of Lee Man-hee’s summertime arrest is outside of their normal recruiting period, they’ve had to swap university campuses for city centres, but are still—according to sources—as present as ever on MeetUp and dating app Bumble.

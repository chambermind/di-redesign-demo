CULT CHARACTERISTICS AND THEIR RELATION TO THE CHURCH WITH NO NAME, 2X2s…..

Two by Twos

These characteristics have been proposed by Lynn Cooper in New Zealand. They are firmly theological, sociological and psychological in tone.

We have noted that “a Worker” is trying to answer people and I find the responses seem more to express the attempts of those opposed to him to catch him out like the Pharisees at the time of Jesus. Rather than allowing him to reply there is tendency to bait him and to get him to trip himself up.

People are allowed to disagree, and because they do does not make it a cult. The same cultist tendencies and attitudes can be found in all the groups from mainline to independent churches. The 2X2s may be heretical from the perspective of Orthodox Christianity, but also their anti intellectualism which we alluded to earlier may lead to bad practice and lack of theological reflection and renewal, however, this does not constitute cultism. Also “a Worker” has been willing to come on here and answer his critics. He can’t be blamed if you do not believe him or that you accuse him of lying and hiding the truth. He is a busy person and allowing for different time zones please try to give him space to reply. I know in my private correspondence with him we totally disagree but out of the respect of difference we are gradually beginning to understand each other.

“a Worker” on the other hand is saying in his replies here on the blog that he is sorry if some people experienced his movement in a negative way. He has suggested that in those circumstances they are right to join other Christian movements.

However, from the perspective of DI he does not take any ownership for his movement’s behaviour in other jurisdictions and the litany of complaints which are outlined below and in earlier posts by former members and those still in the group. These are not not just makey uppy comments made by those affected by the  2X2s but arise out of pain and from growing up within the group and having experienced it in a very negative way. He is beginning to understand that we are not just dealing with some group of apostates, but real people who are hurting. Again he is going to give some thought to that.

The danger is that “a Worker” is becoming the great deflector in saying this does not happen where he is. What DI is saying is that he can’t be expected to answer people who may be in Canada, the USA or Britain and Ireland when he can only respond to issues where he is. He has pointed out that the group has no structure to address concerns, no central authority only a loose regional structure. I was raising the need for the academic study of theology, biblical studies and Church history. They have no seminary or theological college. Most of their leaders may have third level education but not in theological disciplines.

If he was a Baptist he could only answer for his local congregation and could not address the position of Baptists in say Kentucky? The point I am making is that many of you come back at him and expect him to answer as if he was a bishop of a diocese and when he gives his experience he is shot down as someone who is hiding things. He has informed me that he is on a learning curve and we had a long Skype conversation which we will both reflect on. Because he reports what is going on in his area does not mean he is being untruthful but he can’t be expected to answer for what is happening in say Luxembourg. He has clearly agreed that if the abuses which are reported are true he opposes them.

We do not control the comments that are made on this site, but we are able to observe and give some feedback:

* Cults keep secrets have a persecution complex

* believe they are the only ones who have the ‘truth’.

* Produce ‘clones’ – think and dress the same.

1.  A cult believes their group is the only way to Heaven, salvation or enlightenment.   Often the group claims a special revelation.

2.  The group is elitist, claiming it is the only one that has the truth. Members are the ‘chosen’ people and are spiritually superior to non-members. A ‘we alone are right’ mentality exists. The group refuses to consider that it might be wrong.

3.  No outside church, especially traditional churches are accepted as valid.

4.  The group has a polarised us-versus-them mentality concerning the rest of society.

5.  The groups unwritten rules and traditions take precedence over the Bible.

6.  Members who faithfully follow the group’s beliefs and traditions are considered to be special, chosen, hearty ‘saints.’

7.  Members who fail to remain faithful and leave the group have failed God, will suffer for their decision and will lose any hope of eternal salvation.

8.  Former members are treated with suspicion. Ridicule with patronising sympathy and in many cases shunned.

Leadership Characteristics

9.  The group focuses upon a leadership that members appear to be zealous, unquestioning commitment towards.

10. Often the members allegiance is based upon the belief the leadership is, or represents or posses a special anointing form God.

11. The group leadership does not make itself accountable to any authorities.

12. Indiscretions and improper conduct by the leadership are often ignored or covered up.

13. Significant and sometimes total decision making authority rests with the leadership.

14. There are no means by which a member can appeal a decision by the leadership. Followers are not perceived as having any rights. Dissidents are excommunicated.

15. The leadership will impose pressure, or openly dictate, how members should think, act, feel, dress.

16. The leadership is often authoritarian and enforces obedience to the leadership and the group’s standard.

17. The leadership may avoid or brush off questions.

How cults control their members

18. The group’s recruitment techniques are often deceptive.

19. New members are often lavished with love and attention. Later, further love and attention is dependent upon the member’s performance (love is conditional, not unconditional).

20. The leadership induces & manipulates feelings of fear & guilt as a method of control.

21. The members are instilled with a fear of ‘losing out’ or suffering a lost eternity if they ever leave.

22. Members often experience feelings alternating between happiness at being chosen to know the truth and the crushing feelings of guilt, fear shame, poor self confidence and self esteem for not measuring up to the groups expectations.

23. Emotional/mental problems and depression are common.

24. Independent thought, rational examination of the group, asking questions, expressing doubt is discouraged or even punished.

25. Members are not to discuss any negative feelings or opinions they might have about the group.

26. Little, if any room is allowed for individual interpretation or deviation from the group’s beliefs.

27. The group’s doctrines are often vague and have inconsistencies.

28. Flaws are said to be in the person rather than in the group.

29. Members are expected to reduce contact with people outside the group as well as outside activities, cutting them off from outside influences and making them more dependent on the group.

30. Members are expected to devote significant time to the group.

31. Individuality is seen as bad and conformity to the group is good.

32. Any attack which occurs against the group is used to verify and reinforce the group’s importance to members.

33. The group often makes used of jargon or words that are common to the group in order to confuse outsider’s examination of the group.

34. Secrecy of the group’s activities is maintained behind a wall of silence.

35. Members are often kept in the dark concerning aspects of the group.

36. The financial activities of the group are often shrouded in secrecy.

37. The leadership maintains some form of its surveillance over the members. Informers or gossips are used by the leadership as a method of surveillance and control.

Questions to ask yourself

Do you really believe that ‘the Truth’ (2×2 church) was “from the beginning”?

Are you more thankful for the day the Workers came into your life or for the day when Jesus came into your life?

Have you ever asked the workers questions and got satisfactory answers, or were you made to feel that you ‘lacked faith’, ‘needed to submit more’, etc. i.e did you come away feeling you had the problem?

Have you ever asked the workers to help you understand the Scriptures. If so what did they tell you?

Do you look down on outsiders, or see others as ‘lost’, especially other Christians?

Do you uphold the way, yet find some things ‘hard to believe’?

Do you believe you must conform to certain rules, such as, wear your hair up, not wear trousers etc. if you are a woman, to please God?

Do you feel like you must attend every meeting or have to give a reason if you don’t?

Do you believe the meetings are the only way – or do you believe that Christ died for all?

Do you believe that the workers are ‘continuing’ the work of Christ?

Are you held in ‘the Truth’ out of fear of losing out, of losing friends and family etc?

Do you notice that the workers take some Scriptures literally and some figuratively?

If you are disturbed by any of the above, you are not alone. You are entitled to ask questions and receive honest answers. If you do not receive satisfactory answers then go to some one who will give you the answers you are seeking. There are many people who are willing to talk to you and help you find the answers you are seeking.

HOLY WAR House of prayer directors quit as they lose faith in ‘visionary’ Gallagher.

HOLY WAR

House of prayer directors quit as they

lose faith in ‘visionary’ Gallagher

Sunday World Sunday 26, June, 2011

By JIM GALLAGHER

ROW: Discord has broken out in Gallagher’s organisation

Directors quit

pdf above:

CHRISTINA Gallagher’s House of Prayer has become a House of War after three directors quit. Self-styled “visionary” Christina Gallagher’s moneymaking operation has been dealt a critical blow following the internal revolt. The three outgoing directors are believed to have grown disillusioned with the self-proclaimed holy woman, who runs the prayer centre with a rod of iron. At the same time Gallagher took legal action to stop TV3 broadcasting a news programme about her wealth. The Midweek programme had travelled to England to film a £2 million luxury mansion belonging to the Co. Mayo grandmother which was uncovered by the Sunday World in March.

Blow

This reporter was due to take part in the Wednesday night show. But that morning Gallagher’s lawyers sent a warning letter threatening legal action. The programme will now be shown at a later date. The resignation of the three directors, which was formally notified to the Companies Registration Office this week, will be a huge blow to 58- year-old Gallagher. The three women had all been loyal supporters of the House of Prayer leader. Annette Casey, of Castlebar, Co. Mayo, Eithne Lavery, of Stepaside, Co. Dublin, and Bridie Conneally, of Co Roscommon, took over as directors three years ago after a previous board quit. Gallagher had handpicked them specially as loyal followers.

The three are believed to have grown sceptical of some of Gallagher’s outlandish claims. Her messages regularly predict that the end of the world is imminent and that the only ones who will be saved on Judgement Day are those who attend one of her seven houses of prayer or those who support her financially. One group of followers had recently fallen out of favour with the dictatorial leader.

They had formed the Blue Ivory Trust several years ago to buy Gallagher’s luxury home outside Newport, Co.Mayo, after she claimed she could not afford it. Hundreds of thousands of euro were collected from followers. But the Sunday World discovered three years ago the house still belonged to Gallagher despite her taking in all the money raised. Now Gallagher has fallen out with the group. Their plaque has been taken off the house – one of six fabulous homes linked to the money-loving religious guru – and a new sign erected with the name Fruitful Branch Foundation.

Gallagher has also transferred the house from her own name to this new organisation – a limited company – on the official land register. But a Sunday World investigation has revealed Fruitful Branch is linked to Christina Gallagher. Companies Registration Office records show its secretary Eileen Blake, also a director, is the volunteers’ supervisor at the prayer centre and a Gallagher loyalist. Battle Insiders have revealed there has been a battle for dominance in the House between members of the Blue Ivory and the Fruitful Branch.

The three women have been replaced by little known followers: Karla O’Kane, of Killea, Co. Donegal, Patrick Coleman, of Kilmeady, Co. Limerick, Josephine Butler, of Bansha, Co.Tipperary, Bernice Marsh, of Dublin and Marjorie Cunniam.

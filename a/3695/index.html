CHRISTINA’S STATESIDE EXPLOSION

Greedy guru warns of Armageddon

and seeks dollars for bomb shelters

MONEY-mad ‘visionary’ Christina Gallagher is demanding cash for a CHAIN of bomb shelters across America.

And the self-proclaimed holy woman, who runs the controversial House of Prayer on Achill Island, is trying to terrify her supporters into following her orders. The Sunday World revealed last month how the cult leader slammed her ageing congregation for not donating enough to build one underground refuge which “God desired”. But now Gallagher (57) has begun fundraising in the US for a whole series of shelters across North America for what she says is the coming Armageddon.

Desire

In her latest message Christ supposedly told her: “My daughter, I desire that you make known to My people in the Americas that I, Jesus, desire they participate in

bringing about a number of underground shelters for the safety of those who are to be the remnant of salvation. “Soon, very soon, the world will be plunged into the black apostasy that will engulf the world. “There will be war and through the availability and use of nuclear power, the annihilation of many parts of the world and its inhabitants will be brought about. “God does not desire you to fear. He desires your response and your swift action to bring about this work.”

The ‘message’ went on: “My daughter, I desire you make this known throughout the world. “The world’s powers are in the grasp of a great unrest and the preparation of war is now a reality in union with antichrist. “He is about to create a great abomination. The world as you know it will become changed and disfigured.

“Food will be limited. Hunger and death will be everywhere. Great calamities and disasters will befall the world. The waters will be poisoned. Make preparation now.Time has been set for the world’s destruction.” Gallagher, who the Sunday World exposed as a fraudster two and a half years ago, has been preaching

her death and destruction messages for more than 20 years. Opponents claim the Mayo grandmother is now concentrating on the US, where she has five houses of prayer, because her fundraising in Ireland has largely dried up. She is believed to have

recently returned from the States after a money-raising tour. Thousands of Irish pilgrims used to flock to Gallagher’s Achill Island centre every year until a series of Sunday World exposés. We revealed how she had turned from a “humble housewife” to a designer-clad cult leader living in a €4 million mansion on a luxury private estate.

The bomb shelters are the latest in a long line of scams. She is currently also selling a picture of the Virgin Mary in the House of Prayer shop for a whopping €250, which she claims will “save” lives on Judgement Day.Followers are told that only the

€250 version will work – not smaller cheaper copies. After our revelations three former followers took legal action against Gallagher and had their €250,000 returned.

We began our investigation in 2007 after learning that Gallagher was telling her American donors that she wanted “cash only” from now on and no cheques. And the money was to be sent direct to her home. She also launched other dubious appeals such as the “Blue Ivory Trust” which was intended to buy her own luxury home in Newport,

Co Mayo, as a shrine to herself. She kept the money and the house. Brainwashed

She also tricked millions of euro out of pensioners around Ireland, claiming the money was desperately needed for a “secret mission to save millions of souls.”

Some of those who handed over their life savings later admitted they had been brainwashed. Gallagher also tells her supporters that only those who attend her

Houses of Prayer or support them financially will get salvation. As she was raising all this cash, Gallagher was snapping up fabulous properties for herself and her family

even though she did not work and had no formal income. The Sunday World also revealed that Gallagher’s right hand man in America, John Rooney, was a convicted

conman who spent time in jail in Texas. Gallagher’s astonishing millionaire

lifestyle was highlighted in the book Immaculate Deception by this reporter, published last year.

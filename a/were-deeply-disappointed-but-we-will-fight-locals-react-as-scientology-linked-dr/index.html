‘We’re deeply disappointed but we will fight’ – locals react as Scientology-linked drug treatment centre gets court approval

Scientology

November 19, 2021

By Conor Feehan

Tomas West psychotherapist poses this question to political representatives and health officials.

“The correct way to tackle this is on health and safety. Request to have qualified psychological oversight. Ask the government, in light of their proposals to regulate psychotherapy why they would allow an unregulated and oppositional medical ideology to be practised in the country.”

Residents in a Co Meath town have vowed to continue to oppose a controversial drug treatment facility that has been given the green light after a lengthy legal battle.

he Narconon Trust, which is linked to the Church of Scientology, bought a former national school building in Ballivor in 2016 after a move to convert it into a nursing home for the elderly by a previous developer failed.

It then set about turning it into a drug rehabilitation centre, a move that was opposed by locals.

Some argued the conversion to a drug treatment centre needed a planning application that they could make submissions on.

Others were not happy that it was to become a drug rehab centre, and others said they would be happy with a rehab centre but only if it was run by or came under the inspection regime of the HSE.

Those opposing the facility say private drug treatment centres are not regulated and anyone can set one up.

Claire O’Mara, who helped establish the Ballivor Says No group when Narconon Trust’s plans first became known, said the group is “not giving up” despite the Court of Appeal this week giving the residential drug rehabilitation centre the go-ahead.

“I could set up a drug treatment clinic in my sitting room, charge people €25,000 and tell them to take five Smarties and two Maltesers,” she said.

“There is no legislation covering this here. We’re all deeply disappointed. And we’ve constantly told them that they’re not welcome.

“We will keep fighting. The solicitors are not giving up just yet. We have another one or two things that we can run with so hopefully we will be able to keep fighting them in court and will take them to court for many years to come.”

Another local said she was unhappy with the rehab centre being located so close to amenities for children.

“The GAA pitch is right beside it, and the children’s playground is right across the road from it,” she said.

“I’m not saying that people that have problems with drugs don’t have to be looked after. They do, but in the right place in the right setting.”

Fine Gael councillor Noel French said: “We’ve fought this. It was a hard fight. The main bugbear I always had about this was that the people of Ballivor did not get a say in whether this came to their village or not.

“Another problem that I have is that anybody can open a rehabilitation centre in Ireland and they do not have to meet any standards.

“If they are HSE funded, they have to meet the HSE standards. But if they are a private company not looking for state support, then my understanding is that they do not have to meet any standards.

“The sandwich you buy in a petrol station have to adhere to more regulations than these drug treatment centres.”

Meath West TD and Aontú leader Peadar Tóibin said the State has a duty of care to patients in need of drug rehabilitation and the community of Ballivor, and it has “refused to provide that duty of care”.

“In response to a parliamentary question that I tabled to the Department of Health, the department stated that there is no evidence to suggest that the drug treatment programme, provided by the Church of Scientology, would meet any of the criteria set out by the HSE for agencies that provide addiction services on its behalf,” he said.

“There is no evidence of effectiveness, value or usefulness in the treatment programme provided to patients who attend their facilities.

“I have learned from questioning the HSE that there are no minimum standards at all for residential treatment or rehabilitation centres specialising in addiction.”

The Church of Scientology was approached for comment.

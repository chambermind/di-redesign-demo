Open letter no. 2 to Eileen Barker of Inform concerning a debate with Dr John Butler

Dear Eileen,

Now I was not sure whether you would reply to my last letter, so after reflection have decided to write again. As you are on a number of my email lists I get messages regularly suggesting you are away at some conference or other. In fact, I am curious to know what your actual relationship is now to Inform?  Have you actually retired or are you active in your Emeritus role?  Has someone else been appointed or how does it work?

Below you will see my first mail to you and my concerns:

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2009/11/02/dr-eileen-barker-correspondence-1/

I have also met you at a number of ICSA conferences. You know how they work. At any given time there could be 4 separate lectures or presentations going on so that a debate about your methodology and approach just does not take place. Again when you hold a Seminar at Inform you normally have a theme and a number of people engaging, but is not a debate about the issue of Cultism:

http://www.inform.ac/

I am attending our Fecris Conference in London on April 16-18.

I note you are holding one a week later:

So on reflection and see our Conference here in Dublin in 1998

where we were hoping that you would debate what you talked about here in Ireland last year

Namely: Monday, 2nd November, 1830 (Q303): Professor Eileen Barker (London School of Economics)- Evil Cults and/or New Religious Movements? An up-to-date appraisal by an objective, value-free LSE sociologist

However, Johannes Aagaard from Aarhus where we first met was affected by the condition which robbed him of his ability to debate because of a loss of balance. Unfortunately, he died in 2007 as this condition intensified.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2009/11/02/eileen-barker-at-dcu/

However, Dialogue Ireland would like debate with you about the views you lectured on in Dublin. We would be suggesting that a value free sociological method is totally inadequate to deal with the complexity of cultism. We would propose Dr. John Butler a member of our board and resident in London to debate this motion with you. We would be grateful if you could facilitate this at the LSE  and  after discussion agree on a neutral Chair like Dr Michael Langone or anyone we could all agree on?

JOHN_BUTLER_BRIEF_BIOGRAPHY_M_GARDE

So I look forward to hearing from you Eileen and could discuss this further.

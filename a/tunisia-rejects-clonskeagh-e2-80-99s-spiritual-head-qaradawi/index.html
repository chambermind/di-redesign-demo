Tunisia rejects Clonskeagh’s spiritual head Qaradawi

Islamism

From one of our special correspondents:
It is astonishing the degree to which the Islamists and in particular the Muslim Brotherhood have been completely wrong-footed by the events of the Tunisian Revolution. Their battle cry “Islam is the answer” in which they mean the form of fundamentalist Islamism that they advocate rings hollow as it has not lead to a single instance of regime change in the Arab world in its modern history, instead their machinations have only delegitimized the opposition and entrenched dictators who have used them as a pretext to tighten their grip on power. The driving force behind the first ever popular overthrow of an Arab dictator has instead been grassroots democratic movements, trade unions, student unions and various non-religious civil society groups. They in turn have not called for Sharia law to be implemented as the Islamists would have it but instead for greater freedom, the end of the police state and for truly democratic and transparent general elections. Each of these demands are an anathema to the Islamists and the Muslim brotherhood: in their dystopian state  freedoms are to be curtailed, the people are to be monitored and controlled even closer; in fact down to the minutiae of their personal behaviour and religious convictions, and democracy and elections are considered an un-Islamic and diabolical “Western innovation”. What the Tunisian people have proven is that “Freedom and Democracy is the Answer.”
This nevertheless has not stopped the Muslim Brotherhood from trying to jump onto the bandwagon but the response of the majority of the Tunisian people has been near-unanimous: “We did not need you to gain our freedom and we do not want you now only so that you can take it way!” This has been best illustrated by the response to a statement made by the Muslim Brotherhood’s spiritual leader Yusuf al-Qaradawi regarding the Tunisian Revolution. The vast majority of the comments have denounced his interference, and labelled him nothing more than an opportunist.

For the first time in its modern history the Arab world has an example of an Arab and Muslim majority country overthrowing a dictatorial regime through peaceful protest and general strikes led by civil society groups rooted in democracy and pluralism which even in the face of riot police using live ammunition did not waver. The immediate prospects in Tunisia are of free democratic elections within the next two months guaranteed by the fact that the people will not hesitate to march again. This has been a complete paradigm shift which has altered the very way Arab populations view their oppressive governments and the ability of those regimes to cling to power through brute force. A viable alternative has been presented not of an “Islamic Caliphate” but of an open, free and truly democratic state which functions under the rule of law and is guide by the will of the people. From Casablanca to Amman, from Cairo to Riyadh and from Damascus to Aden the cry of the Arab people is now “Today Tunisia, Tomorrow Us!.”

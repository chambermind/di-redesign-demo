‘Cultish’ group has to cancel forum

LARISSA NOLAN

A RELIGIOUS group, branded a cult, has been forced to call off a recruitment drive in the wake of media attention over its methods.  The King of Kings Fellowship (KoK) in Tuam, Co Galway, had planned a major conference in Dublin last week, but this was cancelled due to lack of interest after the Sunday Independent revealed the fellowship was being investigated for “cultist tendencies”.  Furthermore, a number of those who had bought a €35 daily tickets for the two-day event have still not been refunded by the Pentecostal group.  Cult-buster Mike Garde, of religious watchdog Dialogue Ireland, has been monitoring the suspected cult since former members told of how it tries to control the minds of its mostly female members, and puts huge financial pressure on them.  Mr Garde said: “I believe that recent stories in the media dampened interest in the conference. Those who would have gone were put off when they read about the group’s methods.”  Sources in Tuam say that the group could not sell enough tickets for the women-only event. At least four KoK members left after it was highlighted in the media.  KoK leader Mary Sheridan had hoped that her King’s Daughters of Ireland conference in the plush Berkeley Court Hotel would be a good recruitment vehicle for the movement.  Bobbie Houston, leader of Hillsong, Australia’s biggest new religious movement, had been drafted in to give a talk at the two-day conference.  However, ticket sales were poor and the event was cancelled a week before the scheduled date.  According to the Berkeley Court, a number of ticket-holders have called the hotel complaining that they had not been refunded as yet.  A spokesperson in the conference section of the hotel said: “We have had a few people calling to say that they have been finding it difficult to contact the offices of the group to get a refund.”  Recently, the Sunday Independent revealed that the King of Kings’ influence was leading to break-ups of marriages and families.  The mostly female members are encouraged to put the church first at all times and to leave their partner if they refuse to join up.  Local priest Stephen Farragher said that members are afraid to do anything without consulting Mary Sheridan first. “The group has all the hallmarks of a sect and is using mind control and psychological intimidation,” he said.  It is heavily financially driven, with members handing over 10 per cent of their earnings to the group, as well as donating money, giving up cash from business profits, house sales, inheritance and other windfalls.  The movement champions the “prosperity gospel”, which states that the more members give to the church financially, the more they will receive from God. According to its former book-keeper John Lespoir, the group has received more than €300,000 in donations since it was established in 1994. Its status as a registered charity means it can accept donations tax free.

Balanced discussion

2010/09/20 at 10:55 am

Please, is it time for things to calm down a little? Everybody is getting so upset with the way the posts are going. It seems that it is getting harder to know if people are making up stories (a witch-hunt) or telling the truth about Tabor. In order to be fair to Tabor and get to the truth of the matter, I think it is possible to work in conjunction with what Jeanette Rigney and her students are saying themselves. This way each viewer will be able to see how things are with their own eyes, thus keeping things real, and that way they can make up their own minds.

The original post asked the question Is the Tabor Psychotherapy Course going the way of Roebuck? In 2007 there was a lot of publicity concerning Roebuck House centre in Rathgar, Dublin, (a similar set-up to the Tabor Centres) which ended with charges of financial, psychological and emotional abuse brought against the therapists there. On inspection however, it does look to me that Tabor seems to be displaying many of the same cultist traits as those exposed at Roebuck, but let each reader make up their own mind about this.

Regarding Roebuck: One of the complaints against Roebuck was that it got its staff and students to do things with the property (repairs, improvements, donating etc), things that added value to the property and business. This showed the control Bernie Purcell had over them without them even knowing that they were being manipulated. This is also evident at Tabor, as shown in the examples below. (All of these examples you can see either on Jeanette Rigneys Facebook Newsletters, or in the communication between the students on the Tabor Facebook Students Page.

Jeanette Rigney wrote:-
“Some will have heard that we had a bit of a disaster at the centre in Mullingar, a couple of day’s before the end of the year burst pipes in the Attic caused considerable damage to a’s room, the Store room, and the Hallway. My Comment: It is clear from what she says that she rang a student at 1 o’clock in the morning for help, but also left it to him to carry out the repair work while they (Jeanette Rigney & John Sherwin) went on holiday. It was then left to another (past student) to do the clean up. Jeanette’s use of language is interesting; she uses the word “community” emotively, not so much as in a group of people with a commonality, but more as family there to help. The words “family and community” are words bandied around a lot in the communication between all members of Tabor (that can easily be seen if you are willing to read the Tabor students Facebook pages).

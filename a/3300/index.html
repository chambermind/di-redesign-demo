Disgraced Meegan is still raising cash and planning a move to Cambodia

By Michael O’Farrell INVESTIGATIVE CORRESPONDENT

MeeganMay 2 Mail on Sunday

THE Kenya-based charity boss who failed in his efforts to gag an Irish Mail on Sunday investigation into his activities is continuing to fund raise through a newly registered British charity and two other charitable ventures.ICROSS founder Michael Meegan is also understood to be planning a temporary move from his base in Kenya to a new location in Cambodia. He is facing a string of allegations from a number of former staff, volunteers and colleagues. The High Court heard claims last week, vehemently denied by Meegan, that he made sexual advances towards male staff, caned his employees for sexual gratification and used charity donations to fund his lifestyle. It was also revealed that Meegan’s charity ICROSS had sought to have the Police set on those testifying against him. However, as a result of those actions involving the police, some of the staff members who allege they were abused have now, through their legal representative, sought the protection of the Kenyan Human Rights Commission. Concerns over Meegan’s behaviour has already caused support for ICROSS to collapse in Britain. In Ireland, its board has frozen all accounts and returned almost €100,000 in Government grants. Since allegations about his activities at ICROSS first began to surface in recent years, Meegan has stopped filing ICROSS accounts to the British Charity Commission and registered a new charity called New World International (NWI). He is one of the three registered trustees of NWI which, in addition to its own fundraising campaign, has sent a touring exhibition called Africa Awakes around the world collecting donations for ICROSS and NWI. Another charitable money spinner, publishing house Create Africa, is also still operating. A British Charity Commission spokesman said last night: ‘Concerns have been raised with us relating to Michael Meegan and his involvement with NWI. We are currently assessing the matter to determine what action is required.’ NGO leader Dr Vincent Kenny, whose sworn statement helped lift the lid on the Meegan scandal, said last night: ‘There are lots of people in high places who are aware of these allegations and yet they continue to support him and in doing so I feel they are validating his work.’ Dr Ronan Conroy of the Royal College of Surgeons, one of Meegan’s most dedicated followers and closest friends, said: ‘I’m a piano player and I’m struck by something said by a rather waspish music critic Charles Rosen. He said you should judge a pianist by the best they have ever done. Sometimes we want to judge people on the worst they have ever done. ‘As a scientist I can have my own personal opinions but we have to limit my judgment to what the evidence is. At the moment I don’t know what would happen if empirical evidence were to emerge.’

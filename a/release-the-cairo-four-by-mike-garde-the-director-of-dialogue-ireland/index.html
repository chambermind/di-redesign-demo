Release the Cairo Four by Mike Garde, the Director of Dialogue Ireland.

Human RightsIslamismReligion

What is happening in Clonskeagh?

There has been very little analysis about what is going on with Irish Islam since the beginning of the Arab Spring. Mary Fitzgerald wrote an article about the Clonskeagh mosque and its affiliations.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2011/03/09/the-future-of-islamic-ireland-by-mary-fitzgerald/

She noted, Many Muslims in Ireland speak of the ICCI as the “Ikhwani” mosque, an Arabic-language reference to the Muslim Brotherhood, which, since its foundation in Egypt, in 1928, has grown to become the world’s most influential transnational Islamist movement. However, since writing that in early 2011 the situation in Clonskeagh seems to have radically changed since the coup or democratic rising at the end of June. We referenced this at the time.

 It was clear that the Maktoums were losing interest in supporting Clonskeagh and the MB as this link below suggests.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2013/07/04/connecting-the-dots-the-muslim-vatican-in-clonskeagh-is-outdotted/

Ali Selim the spokesperson for Clonskeagh had been at a conference at Trinity College on Education in February and seemed very confident at the time.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2013/02/19/all-faiths-and-none-an-interfaith-forum/

 However, that was likely because Morsi had now taken over where the Maktoums had been before. That all came to an end, and so Sheikh Hallawa had to give his own press conferences when his children were in trouble in Egypt. Where is Ali Selim?

  https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2010/08/23/ali-selim%E2%80%99s-mask-begins-to-slip-by-our-irish-islamist-expert/

We also reported at that time that Qatar had stripped Qaradawi of his citizenship and he went to Egypt as the current spiritual leader of the Muslim Brotherhood. The action of  the  Qataris is strange as they now seem to be the only Gulf state supporting the Muslim Brotherhood?

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2013/07/04/qatar-strips-qaradawi-of-citizenship-muslim-irb-reaches-its-end-game/

 Yesterday the New York Times reported the problems Hamas is having and this puts into firm relief how the Clonskeagh Mosque is no longer under patronage.

Pressure Rises on Hamas as Patrons’ Support Fades

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2013/08/24/pressure-rises-on-hamas-as-patrons-support-fades/

Ian O’Doherty was obvious very confused about the lack of real reporting by the Irish media in relation to events surrounding the Halawa siblings in Cairo.

http://www.independent.ie/opinion/columnists/ian-odoherty/an-irish-passport-cannot-be-used-as-a-get-out-of-jail-card-29521732.html

Basically reading between the lines he was saying that nothing anyone in Ireland could do would assist them in Egypt as they themselves contradicted themselves as what they were doing on live radio and are we to believe that the Egyptian authorities are unaware of their real intentions or that their Embassy which was subject to intense lobbying do not know that Sheikh Hallawa is a part of the Muslim Brotherhood?

Also , in the words of their sister Nosayba Halawa, “What you would do for your children if they were in this situation, just please do the same for my brother and sisters.” However, she did not just stick to this humanitarian appeal , but on all the the broadcasts and interviews was making strong political points about the coup. Surely, this was very unwise and the Irish media played along with this game. This actually put the siblings at further risk.

It is clear that the Halawas went to Egypt to participate in peaceful protest to bring back President Morsi to power. They were naïve in the extreme and had not adjusted their rhetoric to the situation which changed from popular protest and counter protest to an ugly massacre of civilians and violent attacks on the security forces.

I totally disagree with Ian O’Doherty though in his flippant use of the Halawa siblings story. In order to bring the greatest diplomatic pressure one has to recognise that these young people were idealists protecting the MB election from a coup. It should now be the case that Eamon Gilmore should be using every means possible to have these Irish Citizens freed. There is no if or buts about it, their father obtained citizenship and through him his children.

Regardless of differences of views or belief the human rights of these young people should be protected. They should not be subject to torture, nor should there be any aspersion that hey were involved with violent acts against the military! It is clear from their lovely friends who stood for them outside the Egyptian Embassy they are the future of this Republic. However, much we may disagree with the MB, we must protect their Human rights as Irish citizens.

Why was the fact that the European HQ of the Muslim Brotherhood was located in Dublin and Sheikh al-Qaradawi (86) is head of the European Council of Fatwa and Research (ECFR), a private Islamic foundation whose headquarters is in the Islamic Cultural Centre of Ireland (ICCI) in Clonskeagh, Dublin not mentioned? Hussein Halawa claimed he had no connection to the MB while there are documented records showing him to be the head of the Muslim Brotherhood in Ireland.

We can understand that when lives were at risk it is important for those that are at risk are protected, but was that the reason they suppressed the background to the MB or was it just that our journalists do not know the facts of the matter?

The dominant story from the Middle East in the Irish media continued to be that of the four Halawa siblings, all children of Sheikh Hussein Halawa, the Imam of the Clonskeagh Mosque.

Last Monday 19 Aug, Mary Fitzgerald’s report in The Irish Times showed that the involvement of the Halawas in recent Cairo events was of innocent visitors caught up in the pro- and anti-Morsi demonstrations.  She wrote that they had been ‘riveted by the wave of revolutions and uprisings’ that swept the Middle East in 2011.  They had joined the (pro-Morsi) protests at Ramses Square last Friday organized by the MB, but significantly, much earlier, had joined the weeks-long sit-in at Rabaa Square encampment (which was broken up by the army last week 14-15 Aug) and two weeks ago, had addressed a crowd at Rabaa against a banner reading ‘Egyptians Abroad for Democracy.’ Since Monday, however, Mary Fitzgerald has made no further reference to the latter information and, for three days in a row, has repeated the same formula: ‘The siblings were on their annual family holiday when they decided to take part in protests against the military overthrow last month of Mohammed Morsi…’

On 21 Aug, the Irish Examiner  elaborated on the information given by The Irish Times on Monday. Its report said that the siblings had not only addressed a huge crowd of ‘hundreds of thousands’ but ‘led the massive crowd in chants’. 

It showed photographs of them on the   platform at Rabaa Square and of one of them addressing the crowd. Thursday 22 August , however, there is no mention of this information in the Examiner’s report.  Instead, it says that the siblings ‘were on holiday in Egypt and sought refuge in the al-Fath mosque in Cairo last Friday afternoon amid raging violence on the streets.’ 

So today I published a two year article from Shane Phelan’s of the Irish Independent under the title, “Sheikh Hallawa deeply connected to Muslim Brotherhood.” The reason was to make us grasp what type of organisation our governments have granted a special position to in Irish society to represent Muslim concerns to the rest of Irish society.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2013/08/24/sheikh-hallawa-deeply-connected-to-muslim-brotherhood/

Again the news cycle has moved away from Egypt to the bloody events of Syria, more violence and more people who need our support. It is endless and relentless and as a result we must not forget the Cairo Four.

Is the Tabor Psychotherapy Course going the way of Roebuck?

Tabor Institute

Is the Tabor Psychotherapy Course going the way of Roebuck?

In January 2009, after the enquiry on the Roebuck House in Rathgar, Dublin, it was stated by the largest professional body that there was an urgent need to regulate psychotherapy and counseling in Ireland.   Almost two years later, there still seems to be problems out there and there is even a greater need for those regulations to be put in place.

DI has been approached by a number of people who are very afraid to go public, but are concerned about the two Tabor Psychotherapy Centres, (one is in Tallaght, Dublin  & the other in Ballinafid, Mullingar), both of which are run by Jeanette Rigney and John Sherwin, a husband and wife team.  They are particularly concerned about the Psychotherapy Institute at Mullingar, which advertise a 4 year Humanistic, Integrative and Transpersonal Pyschothreaphy Course.  They allege that both Jeanette Rigney and John Sherwin have violated their code of ethics as laid down by the Accreditation Boards of Ireland.

They claim the course offered what it advertised (a Psychotherapy Course), but as time passed the course has corrupted its practice, and to-day its tendencies and attitudes are, what could only be termed, cultist.  Concerns about Tabor have been voiced to the Boards by several counselors and supervisors over the past couple of years.  These people were personally involved with the Tabor students; however the professional bodies’ response so far has been that unless a person makes a complaint their hands are tied.  This results in catch 22 situation the reason being, that the complainants are afraid to identify themselves for fear of reprisals from Jeanette Rigney, and so this results in the Boards been unable to pursue the complaints.  So the situation continues.

These two centres are owned by Jeanette Rigney, who is a fully accredited psychotherapist, however her husband John Sherwin it is alleged has misrepresented his qualifications according to those who have contacted Dialogue Ireland.  He is not accredited with the IAHIP as was stated on their website. It is further claimed that he failed to graduate from the Tivoli Institute where he did his diploma, yet he works in the Tallaght Centre as a qualified Psychotherapist, and charges as such.  He also has been involved in the Psychotherapy Course as Group Process Facilitator, which breaks the conditions of the Course Accreditation Bodies, who require all facilitators to be accredited in order to meet the professional standards.  This raises serious issues about this couple.

What the Students on the Tabor Course over some years, report about the Course Director, Jeanette Rigney and her husband John Sherwin is truly frightening.  It raises issue of control and loss of autonomy the very things an education is supposed to help you overcome.

We will obviously give Jeanette Rigney and John Sherwin the right of reply to these allegations, and our blog allows people to leave anonymous comments which might open up a discussion on what we are covering. We have these testimonies in our possession but people are not yet willing to go public.

Jeanette Rigney is the charismatic leader of Tabor who seems to have a Pied piper type influence over her students. Over the years most of her recruiting for her Professional Psychotherapy Course was done through two Maynooth Counselling Courses that she runs as a tutor, (one in Tallaght and the other in Ballyfermot).  It is on those courses that she primes students, telling them that her course is the best in Europe (one-of-a-kind), and that all other courses in Ireland are inferior.  She suggest that many of the students need counselling if they are to pass the diploma for Maynooth (even though this is not a requirement of that course), and many do comply because of fear of failing the course.  Naturally, she recommends the Tabor Centre in Tallaght, telling them that she employs only the very best therapists, and that they should go there for her to be sure they are getting quality therapy.  These students who attend Tabor for therapy get rewarded by being treated “special”, and a grooming relationship starts.   By the end of the course she gets a high return of student to enrol for her course in Mullingar.  She further recruits from her client base as a therapist & Supervisor in Tabor.  Very few students would not have had a relationship with her prior to joining the Professional Course.  Although not stated on her website, when Jeanette is talking about her course she tells potential students that it is the first Transpersonal Psychotherapy Course in Ireland..  However, there are no guidelines for this kind of training course set in place by any of the Irish Accreditation Boards.  For those who are not familiar with the term Transpersonal Therapy, Tabor uses altered states of consciousness to access Spirit Guides.  Jeanette reveals that her guides are Jesus Christ, The Angel Gabriel and Michael, and that hundreds of angels protect Tabor from the outside world.

On Starting the Course: From the very beginning of the Tabor Psychotherapy Course, the conversion tactics begins each year.   The 1st week-end of the course is held in The Irish Centre for Shamanic Studies, (which is what it says, a Centre for Neo-Shamanic Training).  However, they call it “The Transpersonal Institute” in their course schedule.  This 1st week-end introduces the students to a lot of hype after the summer break, where the senses are excited by the clever use of a variety of effects that are applied, i.e. special lighting, smells (incense, sage sticks etc), colour, ritual and powerful sound effects that bring the students into altered states of conscious (live drumming and trance dance music) etc.  This “beginning” is designed to create an air of expectation of the year ahead. From there Jeanette uses “reward and punishment” in order to gain more control over the students, generating a great deal of fear and tension within them.  This tension is relieved by a great deal of trance work, which is a heavy part of the course content.  Other methods of soothing the soul are through Shamanic journeying, healing ceremonies and ritual work. The actual psychotherapy training comes second to the transpersonal work, and as a result, in the past the students had to do extra “skills training workshops” (external to the course) at extra expense to themselves, which is put on in the Tabor Centre by another tutor (Rachel Somers).  Rachael was also the outside marker/examiner.

The whole set-up is very incestuous.  Most students see therapists at the Tabor Centres, supervision is held in the school, the outside examiner gives the skills training.  And they are encouraged to buy into other workshops that Jeanette provides outside of the course for “growth purposes” (i.e The transformation Game, The Wilderness Experience, etc.).

Tabor is a money making machine, and Jeanette gets her cut from all of the above areas.  For example, Therapists & supervisors and Tutors pay for using the rooms used with the students.  The workshops run by Jeanette and John are filled almost fully by the students.  It would seem that quite a few students are also having to do repeat years (an extra €4000 plus) per student.  It would be interesting to know why this is happening? Is she taking unsuitable student on to her course to fullfill her need for numbers, or could she be punished some of the students for not doing extra workshop with her?

Tabor is an environment in which students experience being intimidated, controlled, and confused, while at the same time bombarded with loving gestures (provided with spiritual practices, loving talk,  not to mention lots of biscuits bars to comfort them).  They go through love/hate relationships with Tabor, and especially Jeanette.  When they try to talk to her about any problems they are experiencing with her, she quickly tells them “it’s your transference, take it to your therapist”, then dismisses them, refusing to talk. When they try to talk to John Sherwin (Jeanette’s husband and Business Administrator), he tells them he can’t interfere with what Jeanette says.  So there is “no win”.  He acts as her buffer, leaving them no place for a grievance process for the students.

One student put it this way. “The students in therapy with me often spoke about feeling like “captors”.  The course cost each student  an investment in the region of €30,000 over the 4 years.  Many borrow the money from Credit Unions, having put themselves in such debt they don’t feel that they can leave.   It is not so bad if they are one of Jeanette’s “flavour of the month student” and feeling loved and special, but this changes like the Easterly wind coming off the Irish Sea…..bringing quite a chill of rejection and confusion with it.”

“A lot of the time I recognized the mind control used by Jeanette Rigney was quite subtle and sophisticated, ways the students were unaware of.  For example:-

  • ways she uses them for attracting new students &  clients  (links on their websites, word of mouth selling, enlist personal friends, sell the course at the Open Day etc.)
  • making changes and improvements to the Centre (for example, building the Ciarn  and the Labyrinth on the grounds, and donating all the trimmings for same.  Being called to help clean up when they had a flood at Christmas.  Donate books so that Tabor can have a library etc).
  • getting them to recruit more business for her (i.e. two taxi men students drove their taxi with  magnetic signs for Tabor on their doors while on the course, others provided administration and office assistance etc.
  • control of information (i.e. prohibiting communication between student and anybody who has left or expelled from the course (both peers and teachers who suddenly vanished).  Briefing students about what they can/cannot tell their therapists (when there is a problem at Tabor) etc.
  • the use of language that set the students up to seeing themselves as having a “special identity and cognition” ….such as  “the Community of Tabor”
  • the extensive fear and guilt imposed that encourages their conformity (they all were instructed to wear white and red for their graduation day, (a second graduation was held for families with cap and gown).  I do not know of any other course that has 2 graduation days.  It shows that Jeanette is aware that something has to be hidden from family and friends that would not be acceptable).  In effect there is a “secret” aspect to the course.
  • by pitting them again each other and causing suspicion of each other, “divide and conquer” tactics.
  • The love bombing of friendship that goes on when Jeanette is happy, to be followed with severe punishment when she is unhappy, causing great insecurity in the students.
  • Time Control she puts on them, keeping them so busy over the week-end that they hardly get time to think. (i.e.  Within one hour break they must do their peer group/study group/skills training, and prepare and eat their lunch).  Not always giving the lecture on a topic before the date the essay has to be handed in.  Telling them that several have been failed by the outside examiner on the essays, but leaving them all until the next months before telling the names of those who actually failed.  This brings up enormous anxiety in all the students.
  • Lack of boundaries: There are none of the clearly defined boundaries that are usually in place on Psychotherapy courses.  The students are affected by Jeanette’s moods and bullying.  Then there are times when she is the over loving mother, lying on the floor cuddling them in “healing” embraces. Students are verbally abused when she goes into rages (often). At times the students feel very unsafe, but there are no adequate complaints procedures in place.  While students are in therapy with the Tabor therapists, she will offer some of those student/clients placement within the centre.  When therapist complained about these boundaries being broken she refuses to talk to them, bullies them with silence, and brought in the students anyway.  Today she has about 6 students working in Tabor as therapists, which means that the therapists who were complaining were bullied into leaving (after giving years of service to her and Tabor). She brings students to her home, spoiling them, and makes them think that they are her friends.”

To sum up one our informants writes:

“Many of the students are not aware of the extent to which they are manipulated, exploited, or abused.  Some even show unquestioning commitment to Jeanette Rigney as their leader.  To those who try to question things, they are soon discouraged, some are even punished.  Many of the mind-altering techniques (i.e. chanting, drumming, ritual, incense etc) are used to suppress the anxiety of the group.  Jeanette as leader, dictates her wishes, which must be obeyed.  This keeps the students very dependent on her.  She tell them constantly that she is special, on a mission, and with her psychic powers she knows what they are thinking.  She tells the students that people envy her, and therefore they must be careful what they say about Tabor, because not everybody understands how Spirit works.  She controls the students through inducing feelings of guilt or shame.  They must cut ties with those students or teachers that leave the course, some have even been encouraged to leave their families.  They are also encouraged to bring in new members, and use every opportunity to spread the word about Tabor.  They are expected to devote time and energy to improving the “family or community of Tabor”.  Past members are encouraged to return, and help the students with skills, attend further workshops and skills training etc.  For those students that become disciples of Jeanette Rigney (the ones most likely to be offered to come and work as Therapists in Tabor), they believe there is no life outside of Tabor.”

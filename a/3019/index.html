Women-only religion has ‘cultist tendencies’

Sunday July 11 2004

LARISSA NOLAN

A MOVEMENT that specifically targets women is being monitored by Ireland’s leading cult expert, who claims its influence is breaking up marriages and families. Mike Garde from religious watchdog Dialogue Ireland believes a religion called the King of Kings Fellowship (KoK) is dangerous and has “cultist tendencies”. Mr Garde has spoken to former members of the group based in Tuam, Co Galway and says it may be controlling the minds of its mostly female followers and putting pressure on them to give money to the group, which is also a registered charity.  He said that involvement in the KoK has led to the break-up of some followers’ marriages and has destroyed families.  “The KoK is focused on women – men are completely sidelined or pushed out,” said Mr Garde.  “Members are isolated from their families. If a woman’s husband is not interested in the group, she will be encouraged to leave him – the religion itself must come first at all times.” The KoK is a Pentecostal group founded in 1994 by middle-aged mother-of-five Mary Sheridan, who uses the Bible to preach to her flock.  It is a registered charity, which means that all donations to the group are tax-free.  Tuam parish priest Fr Stephen Farraher has counselled former members and said the KoK has “all the hallmarks of a sect.  “Members are afraid to do anything without consulting Mary Sheridan first. From what I have heard, I believe the group is using mind control and psychological intimidation.”  Mike Garde said the KoK is heavily financially driven, with followers handing over 10 per cent of their earnings to the group, as well as donating money into baskets and giving up cash from business profits, house sales, inheritance or other windfalls.  Ms Sheridan and her husband Ozzie used to live in a small, rented house in Tuam five years ago, but have recently built their own large bungalow on the outskirts of the town. The group meets in a prefab situated beside the family’s bungalow.  At meetings held five times a week, Ms Sheridan speaks mostly of the “Prosperity Gospel” and tells group members that if they give to the church, God will send wealth and prosperity their way. She speaks for hours at a time, often building up to a shriek or “talking in tongues” and frequently carries out exorcisms of members she believes have a “demonic spirit” in them.  Ms Sheridan describes herself as a prophet of God and has told followers she plans to raise up 12 women around her as Jesus did with the disciples.  It used to have about 30 members, but numbers are dwindling and the KoK is currently trying to attract more women to the group.  It is holding an event in Dublin in a fortnight called the King’s Daughters of Ireland International Women’s Conference, which will bring attention to their movement. The two-day event on July 28 and 29 costs €35 per day.  Said Mr Garde: “This conference is definitely a recruitment drive.” He has been speaking to former members of the group who he says are very reluctant to speak out about the KoK but tell tales of its controlling nature.  “They want to let people know that followers of the religion are trapped by it, they want to effect change.”  One ex-member said: “I was in the group for a while and saw evidence of manipulation, hypocrisy and false teachings. They meddled in peoples’ personal lives and caused great anxiety and hardship. When I told the group of my concerns they said I had to submit to the leadership, so I left.”  Mike Garde tried to arrange a meeting with Mary Sheridan and other heads of the group to sit down and resolve any issues that were causing him concern. But she refused to meet him and accused him of calling the religion a cult.

When the Sunday Independent called to Mary Sheridan for a comment, she shut the door and insisted we contact the KoK office in Tuam.  A spokesperson for her said: “We are holding an international conference for women only next month and if you want to find out about it, you are welcome to come.”

Will the real Susan Morrice please stand up?

Tony Quinn

Susan Morrice here shows the person she might have been if she had not met Quinn. Quinn believes that charity is for bums but it is clear that if Susan was really herself she would be a person that really supported the issues she claims to support here. Not only does she want people to think outside the box, but she encourages them to question the box itself. The box is Educo and the key to the box is Quinn. This is what Justice Bannister thinks of her charitable works! Questioning the box itself means questioning Quinn.

 [66] I reject the story about the attempted investment. …. These payments appear to have been simple withdrawals for private purposes which should never have been made and which demonstrate a failure to distinguish between company and personal property….

[70] No serious attempt has been made by Ms Morrice to account for these figures or to explain what of INE’s needs required her to travel to this extent on its account. Figures of this sort clearly cry out for some sort of justification. The difficulty is that I do not have sufficient information to be able to say what, if any, of these amounts represent unratifiable drawings on the part of Ms Morrice. The most that can be said is that a significant proportion of these expenses must have been personal.

[71] INE paid Mr Quinn or his companies US$544K in 2008 and US$201K in 2010. The first of these figures are said to represent reimbursement by INE to Ms Morrice of seminar fees for BNE personnel which had been paid in the first instance by Ms Morrice. They obviously cannot be ratifiable as a legitimate expense of INE but what appears to have happened is that INE has paid instead of BNE. It seems to me that it would be unjust in those circumstances to treat these payments as having damaged INE, since had BNE paid directly there would have been a corresponding effect on INE’s balance sheet. The latter of the two figures represented the fees to permit Ms Morrice, Mr Stewart and an associate of Ms Morrice’s called Mary Ann Malone to attend a 13 day seminar in the Bahamas. The invoice was signed off by Ms Morrice. The figures are inherently preposterous and no reason has been given why the business of INE required, or justified, expenditure of this sort. There was no suggestion that this was in reality an expense of BNE which would have indirectly flowed through to INE. It seems to have been a jaunt for which Ms Morrice should have paid out of her own pocket. A further sum of US$62,309 paid by INE to Ms Morrice turns out to have been the amount of an Educo gym franchise fee due from her to Mr Quinn, but which she has failed to pass on. The evidence showed, however, that in the ordinary way she would have been reimbursed for this sum by BNE, so that on a look through basis INE has lost nothing in the result……..

[91] In this case the company’s true Operating Agreement has been flouted since August 2006 and has been completely disregarded since December 2007. The present board shows no appetite for doing anything other than continuing to ignore the rights of the Class B membership under the 2002 Operating Agreement and its track record offers no grounds for believing that its behaviour will change. There are serious uncertainties as to the identity of the company’s Class A membership. Those uncertainties must inhibit any proper continuing management of the company as an investment fund, since distributions are impossible in a company whose share register is affected by such uncertainty. The participation of Mr Quinn, to the tune of US$1.6 million, in the LRP illustrates the significance of the problem. The company has held one (possibly two – the witnesses were uncertain) Annual General Meeting in the nine and a half years of its existence, in contravention of clause 9.01 of its Operating Agreement. No dividends have been paid. Company funds have been distributed in unlawful disregard of the principles of pari passu distribution, so that uncertainty affects the allocation (if it has ever been attempted) of profits under clause 12.02 of the 2002 Operating Agreement. Under the 2007 Operating Agreement Class B shares may be freely transferred only to persons who have attended one of Mr Quinn’s seminars and other transfers are the subject of severe restrictions and, in certain circumstances, expropriation. Mr Quinn, who has played a part in the affairs of the company since August 2007 (the LRP, for example, was his idea), turns out never to have been properly appointed to the board. Although the practice appears to have been at any rate temporarily halted, a section of the membership has felt free in the past to treat company funds much as if they were their personal property. The company’s assets purportedly stand charged with satisfaction of an obligation to buy Mr Quinn’s Class A shares (assuming that he has any) under his purported option agreement in circumstances where there is no one at board level willing to challenge that position…..

Finally, there is evidence that INE money is being used to fund litigation by Ms Morrice and Mr Quinn in a manner not authorised by the 2002 Operating Agreement.

Susan Morrice is an Entrepreneur, Visionary and Philanthropist whose purpose and vision is to make a difference in her community, seeing the potential in all of us and empowering individuals she encounters to do the same.

Susan Morrice was born in Belfast, Northern Ireland in 1952. She studied at Trinity College, Dublin and holds a Bachelor of Art in Geology. She makes her home in Denver, Colorado and has two children, Hannah and Clare.

From 1976 Susan worked all over the world. Places such as China, the Philippines, Algeria, Morocco, Turkey, Italy, Spain, United Kingdom, Ireland and the USA. In 1981, she set up Morrice and Associates to carry out Geological consulting on a worldwide basis. She worked for different oil companies, including ARCO with Dr. Tommy Thompson in Morocco, Quintana, in Idaho, USA and Spain, Ulster Natural Resources in Ireland and Rankin Oil in Belize. In 1984 she traveled to Belize and acquired an onshore license very close to Spanish Lookout. Susan put together a joint venture and shot BZ $10 million worth of seismic in the Gallon Jug area in Northern Belize.

Unfortunately at that time because of the unstable political atmosphere in Central America, she could not raise the necessary funds for drilling a well. Then with Mike Usher, she started Belize Natural Resources and acquired a large offshore block near Gladden Islan, off the coast of Belize.

Susan put together a joint venture with major oil companies, Petro Fino (Belgian national oil), Deminex (the German oil company), Magellan (Australian oil company) and Dover (USA oil company). Sadly, they spent over  $12 million but failed to find any oil. In 1994, Susan started the International Pavilion bringing in 52 countries, their Ministers of Energy and teams to exhibit their oil and gas potential at the International American Association of Petroleum Geologists (AAPG) conference. This International Pavilion has continued to grow each year and has lead to billions of barrels being discovered in many countries. She was awarded the Distinguished Service Award by the AAPG for making a difference in the world.

Throughout Susan’s career, she has always been concerned with the social impact of her business on the local economy, population and government. She instills in her company the holistic approach to business that not only focuses on growth, efficiency and revenue, but also addressing other key concerns that affect the countries she does business in.

In the case of Belize Natural Energy (BNE), Susan has spearheaded social initiatives to get involved in local schools by volunteering her time, providing mentor ships and distributing books. These programs get Susan, BNE and her colleagues involved in the community where they do business while giving back to those people. “We really think it is quite important to get involved with our local community where we do business. Not only to explain our business and why we do what we do, but to help improve the lives of the residents,” Susan says. “We want to encourage and empower people to strive for their dreams and know that success can be achieved through hard work and passion.”

Susan also has a program of sponsorships where she identifies low-income Belizean children and subsidizes their higher education. One such student, Karena Gill, currently studies at the Colorado School of Mines in Golden, CO. She is working toward a degree that is near and dear to Susan’s heart, Geology. “Ms. Morrice’s generosity and kindness is like no other and I can honestly say that I would not be here right now if it were not for her encouragement and support,” says Karena. “She encourages people, pushes them to succeed and discover the “oil in you”, simply because she genuinely wants the best for everyone. She always expects you to give it 110%, because that is exactly what she does. Her work ethic is admirable, she truly loves life and what she does and she always makes time for the little things.”

Susan sincerely wants people to use the energy and spirit inside them to set and achieve their goals. Not only does she want people to think outside the box, but she encourages them to question the box itself.

Coming from an Irish background, Susan already had a global mindset instilled in her from an early age. The Irish are known for their pioneer spirit and Susan was no different. She has traveled the world working in the geology field while being exposed to many different cultures. This has given her a perspective that most people can only dream of.

This global perspective has served her well during the course of her career. This perspective promotes new ideas and concepts and urges her to reach out to local governments. This way she gets involved with not only their social programs but it creates a dialogue on what BNE and herself can do to help with other government initiatives as well. The best example of this is Susan’s relationship with the First Lady of Belize, Mrs, Kim Simplis-Barrow. Since Susan’s primary business is located in Belize it made sense to start there.

Susan finds great value in education and thinks it is such a tragedy if children do not have access to it. She encourages students to pursue the more creative arts such as art and music while trying to unlock their potential by encouragement, involvement and sponsorship. The Panerrifix Steel Band is one such project she is involved with that has members that range from 11-20 years old. They play at a wide variety of events in Belize that include church gatherings, graduations, parties, parades and tourist resorts. Susan was instrumental in bringing the band to Denver for a cultural exchange. “Music is an outlet that encompasses what I encourage in others which is using that energy that is inside all of us to create something,” Susan says. “I hope people can open up and embrace that type of energy to chase those things that inspire and fulfill them.”

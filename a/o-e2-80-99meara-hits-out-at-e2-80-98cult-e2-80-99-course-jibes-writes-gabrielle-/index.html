O’Meara hits out at ‘cult’ course jibes writes Gabrielle Monaghan, Sunday Times June 5, 2011

Landmark

KATHLEEN O’MEARA, the former senator who is seeking a presidential nomination from Labour, has emailed members of its parliamentary party to defend her involvement with an American provider of self improvement courses that have been dubbed “cult-like”. O’Meara, who announced her intention to seek the Labour nomination last Monday, included a section on Landmark Education in a question- and-answer-style email in which she stated a few issues had come up “which I thought would be useful to answer”.

“A few years ago, you were in the media about something called Landmark. What was that about and what’s your involvement now?” she asks. O’Meara then defends the company, which has been criticised for its intense courses and accused of being cult-like, an accusation Landmark states is
unfair and untrue. In the email, O’Meara refers to a Sunday Tribune article in 2005 in which she spoke about how Landmark had changed her life. She told the newspaper she had taken four Landmark courses, was leading introductions to the organisation around Ireland as a volunteer, and that her children had participated in seminars. “In 2005 I was asked by a Sunday Tribune reporter to give an interview about a course called the Landmark Forum and which I had gained personally from. I was shocked when the newspaper created a sensational story out of it,” she states. The former senator says: “At the time my elderly father was very ill and it was very upsetting.” She adds that Landmark Education, the company which delivers the Landmark Forum, “is a global training and personal-development company and their education is cutting edge. Six thousand people in Ireland have done the Landmark Forum and got considerable benefit from it”. T h e  S u n d a y  T r i b u n e reported that Landmark has been referred to as a “possible cult or cult-like” organisation in France, and quoted a company spokeswoman rejecting this description. O’Meara’s  explanation of how the course benefited her was also reported. Last week, Landmark, which has its headquarters in San Francisco and generated $75m (¤51.3m) in revenue last year, was criticised by callers to the Liveline show on RTE Radio. A number of people complained about expensive weekend courses, including 14-hour days in which participants were encouraged to discuss past regrets. They said they were asked to recruit family members and friends to courses. Mike Garde, director of Dialogue Ireland, which has monitored Landmark for 11 years, claimed former participants had found the organisation “cult-like”, and others had said family members had become estranged after taking Landmark courses. Garde said O’Meara invited him to Leinster House in 2004 to explain the organisation’s benefits. O’Meara declined to comment this weekend. Irish “graduates” of Landmark have set up their own internet forum detailing when courses take place and the achievements of participants. A “self-expression and leadership programme” was held on M a y 1 4 , c o s t i n g  € 2 0 0, according to the website. Deborah Beroset, the company’s director of public relations, said “the facts are clear that Landmark Education is not a cult or cult-like”. Beroset argued that Liveline depicted the company “in a false and defamatory light”. “All of Landmark Education’s programmes are conducted in a business conference setting, in either a hotel conference room, a convention centre, or in our own business facilities,” Beroset said. “It is ridiculous to suggest people are ‘locked up’ for long days without a break. “Landmark Forum tuition in Ireland is €400 for the entire three-and-a-half days, and included in the tuition is a 10-week follow-up seminar series. For anyone to represent that our programmes cost large sums of money is absolutely untrue.” When announcing her presidential bid last week, O’Meara said she was “hopeful” of securing support from Labour party colleagues. A current head of advocacy and communications at the Irish Cancer Society, she will compete for the Labour nomination against Michael D Higgins, a former minister, and Fergus Finlay, a former adviser to the Labour party leadership and now chief executive of Barnardos, the children’s charity. A decision is due to be made on June 19. Asked whether O’Meara’s involvement with Landmark would affect her chances of getting a nomination, a Labour spokesman said: “An individual’s involvement in any
organisation outside the Labour party is entirely a matter for themselves.”

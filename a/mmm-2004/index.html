TITLE: Stench has residents make plea to council

DATE:             November 17, 2004

SOURCE:        Gatton, Laidley and Brisbane Valley Star

AUTHOR:

`YES it stinks… stop the stench.’

So proclaims a petition circulating in the Balsam Hill-Spring Creek area in objection to the dumping of animal effluent and offal one property on Gatton-Esk Road.

The petition to Laidley Shire councilors requests the council to, “pursue the ire of pollution on and emanating from the property bordered by Balsam Hill Road and Gatton-Esk Road, requiring the owners and tenants to cease and desist from importing, pro­cessing, storing and dispersing noxious and offen­sive animal waste products and effluent in the quar­ry on that site.

“We also ask that council enforces clean up and restoration of the site and prevents reoccurrence of these activities which are detrimental to the health and well-being of the residents who are being affected by offensive odours and contaminated dust leav­ing the site and reaching people, property and crops in the surrounding-areas in Laidley, Gatton and Esk shires”

One of the affected residents, Geoff Proudfoot; drew on his agricultural, social service and health qualifications when he spoke to Laidley councillors at, the ordinary meeting last Wednesday about the stench.

“All the way to Lockyer Waters, into the Esk Shire and along the Gatton-Esk Road towards Gatton, peo­ple had-experiences of foul smells, especially dur­ing the night or at times of breeze from the direction of the site concerned.

“Expresions of disgust, anger, frustration, disap­pointment, dismay and anxiety were accompanied and balanced by expressions of relief that council was on-the job and determined to stand up to those who would damage the lifestyle of our chosen home, location,” Mr Proudfoot said.

He explained that the smell was so bad at times residents in the district–more than 100 families – were often forced to close up their house to escape the stench.

“The problem has been increasing over the last month, and with truck loads (of effluent and offal) arriving all the time becomes worse night by night.

“As summer temperatures increase and rainfall occurs the degree of pollution will become even more severe:

“Farms local to the site of air pollution, many of whom are using food safety programs are receiving a dusting (from the site), so if the dust is contaminat­ed… so are their products;” Mr Proudfoot said.

“Poultry; cattle, tomatoes, olives, fruit and even turf will soon become unwelcome exports from our area if such polluting events as we have recently experienced are permitted to continue.

“Any attempt to pass-off a fertiliser factory or plant as a legitimate rural activity, while at the same time rendering neighboring farming and dwelling unviable, loses legitimacy to the point of being recognizable as an act of bastardry,” he said

EPA

Senior environmental health officer with Laidley Shire Council, Chris Wold, met with the operators of the Balaam Hill property and Environmental Protec­tion Agency (EPA) representatives last Thursday

“The operators have been bringing in a number of  materials, such as pig effluent, paunch, and vegetable matter, to make a soil conditioner to spread over crops.

We had an in-depth discussion on site about the problem of dumping these products, and the  operators have agreed to undertake certain practices.

“They, are currently undertaking action to sta­bilise odours by adding cypress pine sawdust to the effluent at a 1:1 ratio, and the EPA requires tie to now add the sawdust at a 4:1ratio.

“The EPA also requested an environmental man­agement plan from the operators, which will involve community consultation involving neighbouring properties. ,

“There was good co-operation from the operators, and I expect the concerns of the people to be resolved with these actions-in place to minimize odours,” Mr Wold said.

Laidley councilor, Geoff– Lester, who was approached by concerned residents after the effluent was first dumped in late July; said he wanted to see the problem removed completely.

“I feel it is imperative that the stench be removed, I don’t want to see them bring it in at all.

“I feel for the locals, the smell is intolerable… it should be cleaned up at once,” he said. .

At the ordinary meeting of council last week, Lai­dley mayor Shirley Pitt said she was still receiving letters from residents regarding the issue.

“It is certainly a matter of grave concern,” Cr Pitt said.

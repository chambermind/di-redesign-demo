Katie Taylor avoided Tony Quinn for her preparations…..Steve Collins did not.

Tony Quinn

It was wonderful to see Katie Taylor after her unique Olympian feat. There was the professionalism of her father and Billy Walsh, the Head Coach of the Irish boxing team mixed the human pride at her success.

This makes one reflect on Steve Collins who in 1995 as he himself admits on this video,*

http://www.educoworld.com/steve-collins-boxing-tony-quinn-ireland/

when at a pretty low stage of his life saw the Late, Late Show where Quinn really hood winked Gay Byrne with the idea that he was a Dr of Hypnotherapy and performed operations which really were dependent on the expertise of Dr Jack Gibson. Jack was airbrushed out of the script and Quinn used that to hook Steve with a left that led him to advise him in his fight against Chris Eubank. The evidence we reveal shows that Quinn is involved in finding a way to bring about a situation where he uses other people, other people’s money to gain influence for himself.

Tony Quinn is still using material from when he advised Collins to put lives at risk and what was in fact fraud to gain advantage over another boxer Chris Eubank.

Years after Collins realised he was both helped and conned, he told me in a restaurant in Phibsborough, Dublin that he now realised he had been a victim of misrepresentation. He has had not had an easy life since then. Has Quinn offered him any support from the millions he has made out of him?

Here are some extracts…..

He hit the canvas twice. Rather than finish Steve Collins off, Eubank retreated. He stuck up his plumage and strutted a little, he made faces and taunted Collins, but he didn’t do what was required of a professional boxer in such circumstances. He didn’t step in and deliver the concussive blows which would lay Collins out on the canvas.  In the ring, Eubank put Collins’ life before his career. He had good reason for his caution, Collins’ antics in the preamble to that fight were shameful. … It was another thing entirely for Collins to pretend that night to be fighting under the influence of hypnosis. It will be remembered, by those willing to do so, that the wheeze of the week, the hoot du jour, in Millstreet was stirred up by Collins in cahoots with Tony Quinn. Collins, with typically-subtle, pre- fight antics spelled out in great neon letters that he would be hypnotised for the fight and would be feeling no pain…… Tossing in the possibility of  boxers continuing to absorb punishment beyond then natural threshold because some hokey medicine man in a rented tuxedo has conditioned him to feel no pain makes boxing more obscene than ever.

In his autobiography, Collins quotes from Eubank’s first introduction to Tony Quinn as follows … Quinn: “Well my name is Tony Quinn and I am a doctor of clinical hypnosis and I’ve been working with Steve for the past month … when Eubank hits him, I’ve ensured Steve will not feel any pain …”  Eubank: “This is not right. How can I fight a man who has been hypnotised? He will not feel pain. This is dangerous. It is wrong.” Collins: “On the way back to the safe house, we rolled around with laughter. ‘We’ve fooled him,’ I told Tony. ‘We’ve fooled the poser’.”

Here are some extracts…..

Collins announced on the eve of the fight in Millstreet that he had employed” a hypnotist, Tony Quinn, to help him to prepare. He said he would be under hypnosis during the fight and that he would feel no pain and would not bleed. Eubank fell for what was, in retrospect, a mischievous ruse and threatened to pull out. He was persuaded to go on with the fight , however, but he had lost a psychological battle and although he put Collins on the floor midway through the fight , he lost the title on a points decision.

Boxer Steve Collins agreed in the High Court yesterday that he had paid £360,000 for the services of health adviser Mr Tony Quinn, following his two world title fights. Mr Quinn was mentor to Collins for his contests.

Quinn walked away in 1995 with £360,000 for basically a con job, a bit of blarney. He was not a sports scientist, but a sport’s racketeer.

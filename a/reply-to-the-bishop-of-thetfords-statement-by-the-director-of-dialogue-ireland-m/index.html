Reply to the Bishop of Thetford’s Statement by the Director of Dialogue Ireland Mike Garde

Abuse by Church and Irish institutions

The Rt Rev Alan Winton, Bishop of Thetford, released a statement last night saying: “Rev William Stewart has decided to resign from his current post as Rector of the Chet Valley benefice.”

You will note that I was in touch with the bishop nearly 18 months ago.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2011/07/12/statement-of-the-rt-revd-alan-winton-bishop-of-thetford-on-ministry-of-willi-stewart/

Before addressing his claims let us ask a very simple question. When Willi Stewart applied to take up the post as Rector of the Chet Valley benefice, did Alan Winton inform those in authority of not only his sexual assault but also of earlier failures in the 90’s?

By reading the correspondence you can see that as a bishop the paradigm for addressing issues of a moral nature is either civil or criminal law not the gospel at the heart of his ministry. There is no reference to spiritual qualities or whether he has addressed the victims of his actions, their families etc applying the principles of Christian discipleship found for example in Mat 18.

“In recent months he has been the subject of a campaign focused on a matter from his past. A number of parishioners have been supportive of him and his family but he has increasingly felt that under the present circumstances he is no longer able to continue his ministry.”

Let us explain the reasons for placing in a very low key way the factual information we have on the internet.* In 2004 when these issues became public I was approached for support by the family of the victim of sexual assault. Let us be very clear this was not a case of a minor and therefore child abuse, but rather an example of grooming at the boundary of adulthood where Willi was engaged in a mentoring relationship. This is an example of a very intelligent young man who was because of the power relationship a vulnerable adult.  At my advice the family sought assistance and a meeting with the former Archbishop of Dublin John Neill in the presence of the former Archdeacon Gordon Linney. Nothing concrete resulted and when I contacted the Archbishop subsequently about further evidence showing that Willi had had an affair with a young man who was also being mentored the Archbishop asked me to never contact him again.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/05/31/is-sex-between-a-spiritual-teacher-and-students-harmful/

If this material has been up since June of last year and the local newspaper was in touch with me, but decided to not follow up the story, to categorise a duty of care in a very low key way as a ‘campaign’ is certainly a strange way of describing what was inevitable from the responses of both the Church of Ireland and the Church of England.

In 2004 Willi and his whole family went to live in the USA following his vacating his ministry in Dublin.

Following the wall of silence from the Archbishop I made the point that if Willi was allowed back into ministry and had not addressed the victim I would be forced to publish the material not because I was being vindictive but as a professional I had a duty to report on this. This highlighted sentence has led to some confusion. What is meant here was if the Church Of Ireland, Core, or Willi Stewart had engaged with the victim and the family in a process of healing there would have not been a need for this to be published . All that happened was that there were some pastoral contacts but in general nothing happened. No process of repentance or engagement with the victim and family has taken place. Also the Judge in January 2005 clearly understood that this was the end of Willi’s ministry. Also the Probation Act was predicated on the full glare of publicity being shone on Willi Stewart’s actions. This having not happened the real story never came out.

Dialogue Ireland no longer represents this particular victim’s interest, but has a general duty of care to vulnerable adults.

In Jan 2005 I received a request from the victim to attend the court case at short notice to assist his mother as he was doing exams that day. He thought it would just be mentioned for a future trial date. When we entered into the Bridewell court room we were informed that this courtroom was the wrong place. We then moved to another courtroom and we discovered that the summons was not in order. This would normally lead to a delay of the hearing but it was agreed overcome these problems and the case went ahead to facilitate Willi’s return to the USA. Willi  pleaded guilty and the victim had no desire that Willi would receive a custodial sentence. The mother was asked to make give a victim impact statement. She had no legal advice or help thinking that this would be an interim and did not realise her son would have no opportunity to give an victim impact statement at a later date. The purpose of the exercise was that this plea would be broadcast in the media as a warning to others and as confirmation of the heinous crime committed. The judge also heard the testimony of the Archdeacon who claimed that Willi’s transgression was a, ‘once off act of madness’ which then was the prism the judge used to give Willi the benefit of the Probation Act.+

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2011/07/18/archdeacon-gordon-linney-claims-willis-crime-was-“once-off-moment-of-madness-”/

You have to remember that Willi wished to live in the USA and if he had a criminal record he could not have returned there. So the import of the Judge giving him probation was the following:

The Act allows judges wide latitude to dismiss a charge tried summarily against a defendant even when the court thinks it is proved, or to conditionally discharge a defendant (whether the charge is tried summarily or on indictment).

 The fact that the media in Ireland totally  missed this story and Willi had the Probation act granted to him had two unintended consequences:

  1. No one knew anything had happened.
  2. Also the impression developed that it was just a minor indiscretion.

Willi went to the States but a catch 22 emerged as the Archbishop revoked his licence and he could not then remain there. He came back to Ireland and moved to Kilkenny in another diocese under Bishop Michael Burrows who began a process of reinstating his licence and this formed the basis for the comments below by Alan Winton. In other words we have done a check and we have found that he is not subject to any legal impediments. Did any of these Christian ministers ask whether he had attempted to be reconciled with the victim and his family? Had he done anything to make it possible for us to not publish the material we had on our site. No he acted in a most brazen manner and like an ostrich just pretended this would go away by moving to a new location.

We sought information as to why Willi was re licensed. We had been told by the Archbishop that the licence would not be reinstated. The move to Loddon was the trigger for publishing the material.

 “In appointing William Stewart the Diocese followed the normal procedure of seeking assurance from the Church of Ireland that he was a priest in good standing, and a CRB check was made and returned clear. On this basis he was offered the post of Rector of the Chet Valley Benefice in good faith.”

Alan Winton was given the full background and claimed he was satisfied there were no issues when I was on the phone to him in June 2011, but now he tells us he only consulted with the Norfolk police once the information was published on the internet.

He knew I intended to publish the material that month. He informed me he had no concerns. So is he referring to recent events or last year?  Notice the concern for children. There has never been a concern about Willi and children. However, the issue of vulnerable adults shows some confusion.

We note you have clear guidelines concerning this issue in the diocese:

http://www.norwich.anglican.org/children/safeguarding

The document is totally relevant to Willi’s situation. Alan Winton knew the case involved a vulnerable adult according to the material supplied to him by Dialogue Ireland. Why did he seek the advice of the Norfolk Police? He obviously did not have a criminal record as he was graciously only given the Probation Act. So they ask the wrong question knowing the answer before asking it and then use procedures inadequate to address the actual concerns.

 “When information about William was posted on the internet, the Diocese consulted Norfolk Police. The advice they gave was that the offence was not against a child or vulnerable adult, and he did not have a criminal record, and they therefore regarded the matter as a diocesan responsibility to manage in accordance with its normal procedures.”

He obviously made a false start as at the heart of the gospel he preaches is asking people to turn from a life based on self and making amends to those they have offended. Every time they approach communion they raise the issue of not going to the altar till they have addressed those the have offended. Did he inform the congregation of this unresolved aspect of his life? I assume that google did its work and the real world began to impact his reality. As a result of his failure to address his past he had to resign. The strain on the victim is hard to quantify and his life one can only assume is as a hell hole as this aspect of his life is left unresolved in my opinion. The family are out on a limb treated as not worthy of comfort and support. That is the reality and please do not shoot the messenger.

The Probation of Offenders Act 1907 (7 Edw. 7 c. 17) is an Act of the United Kingdom Parliament, commonly referred to as just the Probation Act.

Enactment

The Act was passed on 21 August 1907 and originally extended throughout the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. It remains in force in the Republic of Ireland.[1]

Summary

The Act allows judges wide latitude to dismiss a charge tried summarily against a defendant even when the court thinks it is proved, or to conditionally discharge a defendant (whether the charge is tried summarily or on indictment). The power may be invoked when the court is of the opinion that

“ having regard to the character, antecedents, age, health, or mental condition of the person charged, or to the trivial nature of the offence, or to the extenuating circumstances under which the offence was committed, it is inexpedient to inflict any punishment or any other than a nominal punishment, or that it is expedient to release the offender on probation[.] ”

In practice, cases may be dismissed under the Probation Act for a defendant on condition that he pays a contribution to charity,[2][3][4] or repays an amount stolen,[5] or pays the costs arising from his actions.[3] They may also be dismissed where the offence is technical or trivial.[6] The application of the Act has occasionally caused controversy where victims or persons affected by the crime feel that the dismissal is inappropriate.[7]

Despite the name, a dismissal under the Probation Act does not put the offender on probation in the sense of having to report to and engage with a probation officer, unless it is expressed to do so. Indeed, if a defendant is “given the Probation Act”, it does not count as a criminal conviction,[8] although it has been known to negatively affect travel abroad.[9]

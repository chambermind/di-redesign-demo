Review of TV3: Ireland’s Secret Cults Programme by the Director of Dialogue Ireland Trust

CultismHouse of PrayerPalmarian ChurchScientology

Looking at the programme in the round this is likely the best TV documentary done on cultism in Ireland ever.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2011/09/27/tv3-irelands-secret-cults/

The best candidate before this was the Late Late Show from February 1995 which looked at Scientology and the experience of Mary Johnston. http://www.dialogueireland.org/dicontent/resources/video/latelate.html

The late Gerry Ryan fancied this programme as one of the highlights of Irish television. https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2010/02/23/maeve-sheehan-meets-mary-johnston-mary-won-a-settlement-from-scientology-in-2003/

At first the choice of movements to look at seemed a bit strange, namely the Palmarians, Scientology and the House of House of Prayer. They did not seem to hang together, and the fact that the three groups were mixed together seemed incongruous. That was my initial reaction, but with reflection and talking to others about it I think it actually was a good idea in that with the wide difference in the groups there was a common thread of non communication, manipulation and undue influence. It illustrated the attitudes and tendencies that make up the cultist agenda we call cultism, a word also referenced in the programme. So at the end of the programme one had a feeling that the groups had a connection. I believe this has implications for a variety of players. The Catholic Church is itself becoming closer to becoming a player in the cultist drama when it fails to act in regard to the cries of those who have been abused by the House of Prayer and by a priest from the Diocese of Armagh. The House of Prayer claims to be Catholic when it is not, and the presence of McGinnity compromises AB Neary and the failure of the Cardinal to discipline his priest for not functioning as a proper PP and by making himself unwelcome in another diocese raises questions about his role as protector of the integrity of the role of Primate. Also lending support to a heretical group confuses the faithful and the fact that it has been allowed to go on for so long is a disgrace. I believe she has excommunicated herself, but it is now time for a formal process of excommunication to be put in place. Also there is a need for the parish of Knockbridge to claim their space and call McGinnity before the Parish Pastoral Council to give an account of his ministry. One wonders if the council does not = McGinnity?

The House of Prayer presents itself as within the Catholic family and tries to deflect any questioning of this, whereas the Palmarians claim to be the Catholic Church and the Pope in Rome is a fraud. Both make apocalyptic claims and use fear as a tool of control.                                                       This documentary then challenges the single minded strategy of Anonymous which views Scientology as the only issue and group to be addressed.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2011/09/27/additional-scientology-material-for-the-tv3-piece/

Obviously those in that movement have a right to that opinion but what this programme shows is that these issues are the same in all groups. People can’t involve themselves with every group, but I believe there would be a lot that could be done in bringing the enthusiasm, expertise, and shear commitment found in Anonymous to a variety of situations. As a cult professional I have found their arrival has given me a new lease of life and it informs my way of working with a number of groups like Abundant Life, Tony Quinn, Landmark and others.

Finally the term religion was used of these groups. In Ireland we have a problem of categorisation. The Catholic Church, Church of Ireland etc are all part of the Christian Religion. They are not religions in their own right, but denominations or confessions within the Christian Faith. We also have this problem in the way the Census asks these questions in that it also calls all the denominations religions!

Also Scientology claims to be a Religion when it is not, but the most important lesson to learn about Cultism is that it is not about religion. That is diversionary tactic to get you wrapped up in the theological fog. Jim Gallagher from the Sunday world has a great way with words and keeping it simple. He keeps away from the theology and follows the money trail.

In the end it was a good programme and it will likely travel well like a good wine.

Who is Michael Barger?

Scientology

He is a Catholic activist with an intense interest in awakening the Catholic world to the human rights issues connected to Scientology.

Here recounts how the article by John Coleman, S.J. came to see the light of day.

John Coleman, S.J. has written a stunner of an article on Scientology with up to the minute reference to the Murder of Tom Ciancio in Denver, the lawsuit threat against the Daughters of St. Paul.  In appears in America Magazine, the premier Jesuit magazine in the US.

John Coleman is one of the leading Catholic intellectuals of our day. He is a sociologist, theologian, and social ethicist; and has done much work in the area of freedom of conscience and Catholic social thought. When he was professor of Social Values at Loyola Marymount in Los Angeles, he lectured at Thomas More College in Perth  and is a friend of Fr. Kevin Long there.  He now lives in San Francisco and is on the staff of St.Ignatius Church on the campus of the Jesuit University of San Francisco.

This is an important article. It is the first by a Jesuit intellectual and appears in the premier Jesuit magazine in the US, with a long and honored pedigree. Up to now Randy Sly at Catholic Online has been almost the only US Catholic journalist writing in depth on the cult. I hope other Jesuits will follow. . The article is masterful, and John Coleman analyzes the cult with the deft hand of a practiced sociologist. Here is his conclusion:

What to make of all of these allegations? Scientology tends to defend against its detractors (especially defectors) by reminding the public of the sour grapes of disgruntled former employees and devotees. There is some truth to that rejoinder but simply too many allegations, from a multiple number of former Scientology members (many of whom held high posts in the organization), recounting similar stories of forced abortions for female Sea Org members, doctoring or destroying of internal documents etc. Clarity should be maintained between genuine religious freedom to believe what one wants and allegations of criminal or legally unacceptable behaviors. For me, religious liberty implies complete freedom of exit from religion. Scientology makes it difficult for disgruntled former members to leave, except on its own long-drawn out terms involving confessions that the member is harmful to the church and promises not to sue Scientology. Just leaving on one’s own is punished by being hounded by private investigators. I suspect with so much smoke, somewhere there must be a real fire. While the organization hates the term, it is a totalitarian “cult.” It just may also be criminal.

– Michael Barger

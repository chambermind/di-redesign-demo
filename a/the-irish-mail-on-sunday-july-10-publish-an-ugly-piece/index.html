The Irish Mail On Sunday (July 10,) publish an ugly piece

House of Prayer

Dialogue Ireland has noticed that the House of Prayer has now decided to use the internet to fight its corner. We thought the public should know about it, as they are now responding with a weekly report. It is called digging and the more you dig the bigger the hole gets. But true to form DI always allows both sides to have their say. We are beginning to develop a strategy to protest outside Fr McGinnity’s Church in Co. Louth. If that does not produce results we will have to go to Armagh as that is where the this problem will find resolution.

Fr McGinnity goes as a recognised priest of the Armagh Diocese into the Tuam archdiocese, he messes in an unauthorised joint. He gives it an aura of respectability.  He should have his permission to operate outside his Diocese stopped. How many delegations does Cardinal Brady need to receive before he acts? We are increasingly getting sick of the prevarication and refusal to deal with issues in this country. If you think a Eucharistic congress will change anything while the current show goes on forget it. Please contact us and we can consider  how to advance this issue.

Statement: Further Attacks

The Irish Mail On Sunday (July 10,) publish an ugly piece, the content vindictive and false, a further attempt to smear and defame the good name of Christina Gallagher

http://www.christinagallagher.org/en/

Our Lady Queen of Peace House of Prayer is, and has always been, an autonomous entity (a company with its own directors and staff), totally separate from Christina. It is fully open to the public, and the prayers are those of the mainstream Catholic Church. It is false to refer to this work as “a cult”.

The House depends for its upkeep entirely on donations freely given by the public who frequent it. Mrs. Gallagher neither owns nor is she a beneficiary of any activity at the Achill House of Prayer, nor has she ever requested fundraising or monies for her personal use. The article also wrongly says that the House of Prayer lost its charitable status because of a totally separate purchase of a property in Dublin. This is also false.

Regarding the reported bequest by Mr Eric O’Prey, a letter of notification had been received by office administration at the House of Prayer, but had not yet come before the directors of the House. The article infers that the newspaper tried to contact the House of Prayer, but this is also untrue. The article makes reckless and unsubstantiated comments, calling on people to reclaim donations, and ridiculously referred to Christina Gallagher as a “multi-millionaire”. Through these and other such wild claims, she is made the victim of further threats, hate mail, gross invasion of privacy and stalking. (On Garda advice, records have been kept of vehicles stalking her home.)

On June 26, two separate newspaper articles, both seeking to create sensation and negativity, were published in the Sunday World and Mail on Sunday. As one would expect, the tabloid media coverage sought only to create more negativity, sensation and further smear upon the visionary, and incite hatred. One consequence has been more intensive harrassment.

As House of Prayer directors come and go, they make their own personal decisions in freedom if and when they leave. References to internal conflict at the House of Prayer are untrue, there is no division whatsoever among those within the House of Prayer. Christina would repeat that the house in Newport belongs to the Fraternity, and is held and maintained by the ‘Fruitful Branch’, who are Fraternity representatives. Some Fraternity members and House of Prayer staff hold little prayer meetings there from time to time.

On June 26, the Irish MoS article had partial extracts from previous tabloid attacks spattered throughout, and was another attempt to create sensation, e.g. “amassing a vast fortune and several multi-million homes.” The real facts are available to anyone seeking the truth on http://www.christinagallagher.org and the linked site.

Why would any reputable newspaper print such lies and fiction about their “undercover reporter” attending services at the House of Prayer and hearing “Miss (sic) Gallagher and her spiritual director”, Fr McGinnity seeking donations and warning “that the anti-Christ is coming”? It is doubly repugnant to suggest that this occurred during a prayer service. All sincere visitors to the House of Prayer can verify that the weekly programme of prayers is one of joy and hope, a fact substantiated by many testimonies of spiritual uplift and healing.

The article also portrays a partial and distorted data point from the accounts, which has been taken out of context. There are no issues. The only truth in the entire Mail article was Treasa Doyle’s statement: “….after what the media have put Christina through during the past five years, the miracle is that she is still alive.” The deterioration in Christina’s health (directly related to the severe stresses of her mission and the treatment she has received) including a heart attack, a mini stroke and other serious conditions, bears this out. The fact is incontrovertible. That a tabloid journalist who went so far as to produce a book, and then admitted in an RTE interview that he had never even spoken to Christina, and that his book was based on heresay is beyond belief. Is there any law to protect a person against such wild and relentless onslaughts?

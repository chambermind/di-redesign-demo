23 December 1978

International Association for Mission Studies (IAMS): The Maryknoll Congress 1978

Conferences & Commentaries

Workshop-Report From Workshop III (“New Religious Movements”)

Because of time limitations, the workshop did not deal with revival or splinter groups within Islam, Buddhism, Hinduism, or Christianity in general. Since this conference is convened in the USA, appraisals have been limited to those religious movements especially which have received publicity and notoriety in the West.

We have used some time for extended personal introductions, for a survey of movements in various areas of the world,• and an audiovisual presentation of representative groups in Europe and North America. The area surveys pointed to the following groups:

SOUTH ASIA — Ramakrishna Math and Mission; Arya Samaf,; Divine Life Society, Shivananda;. Hare Krishna; Bhakti; Sai Baba; Sathia Sai Baba; Shree Rajneesh; Muktananda.

EAST ASIA — Soka Gakkai, Komeito; Zen Buddh; Unification Church, Tongil Family; Chundo Gyo; Chundo Kwan (olive tree); Kye Yeong San complex.

NORTH AMERICA — Transcendental Meditation; Zen/Tibetan groups; Scientology; Spiritualism; Unification Church; Hare Krishna; Eckankar; mixed Hindu Groups; 3HO (happy, healthy, holy) Organization, Yogi Bhajan; Black Muslims.

EUROPE — Hare Krishna; Divine Light Mission; TM; Theosophy, a form of spiritualism; Ramakrishna/Vedanta Society; Kriya Yoga – Yogananda, Satyananda; the Babas; Siddha Yoga; other Tantric e.g. Ananda Murti; Western occult groups – Golden Dawn, Scientology, Ordo Temnli Orientis (Crowley); The Fourth Way; Christian offshoots – Church of Armstrong, Children of God, Unification Church.

Two case studies were considered: Transcendental Meditation and Tantra and Hatha-Yoga (see texts prepared as study materials for Workshop III).

Transcendental Meditation

  1. Contrary to official contention that the movement is a neutral technique, TM is a religion to be rejected because of inhuman anthropology; it is a manipulative system headed by a guru who finds it difficult to distinguish between truth and untruth.
  2. One cannot be a part of the TM system unless initiated, cannot be initiated without participating in the invocation ‘ritual, and cannot participate in the ritual without worshipping the guru.
  3. At least one million Americans have been initiated at payment of $100 to receive a mantra which could be read from a book in any public library.
  4. Although TM comes out of the Hindu tradition, it does not have integrity within that tradition; the Maharishi may be winning the world, but losing his soul.
  5. Maharishi is like a revivalist without a conversion experience, for he cannot point to his own moment of achieving the seventh and most intense level of consciousness.
  6. Mantra (the name of God) is religious and a consequence of the ritual. No guru in the Hindu tradition would give a mantra at the end of two introductory lectures.
  7. There is no place in the religion of TM for the weak; the process is one of moving from spiritual weakness to power. The fit will lead with no place for the unfit, the ignorant, says the Maharishi.
  8. The movement is elitist, semi-fascist, ignoring the world’s social and economic problems; it is a politically dangerous movement.

Tantra And HathaYoga

  1. Yoga is essentially a ritual of sex.
  2. Yoga can only be understood as the entire yoga syndrome is understood.
    1. Pranayama to train breathing by developing the big muscle to prolong interval between inhaling and exhaling toward the end of immobilization.
    2. Asana to alter consciousness through posture.
    3. Mantra to chant or hammer the special word into the mind.
    4. Raja to create a milieu so that the imagination is freed.
    5. Sex to transfigure sexuality into spirituality; toward immobilization of the semen, retained for inner purposes.
  3. The combination of the yoga syndrome creates an electric shock in the body providing a potency never before experien­ced — an orgasm with tremendous power rocketed out of the circuit of life.

Conclusion

  1. An important consideration is to place the Eastern move­ments within the larger setting out of which they arose originally.
  2. The essential question we must ask is, in what ways are these manifestations an indictment of ourselves? What do these religions have to offer which the church has not offered?

Toward A Statement Of Response

  1. We recognize that the popularity of these new religious manifestations stems from the fact that they respond to the deeply felt spiritual needs of human kind. In the face of this fact we admit our own shortcomings. We often speak of God’s gift of the Holy Spirit in obscurantist terms which people may not understand. Our worship is often meaningless, and is couched in rituals and liturgies which do not seem to communicate, and therefore do not adequately serve as meeting points of the holy and the human.
  2. We recognize that many of the new religious manifestations are derivative of the ancient religions of India, whereas others are derivative of Christianity and a variety of Asian religions. These new religions employ spiritual insights and techniques drawn from these religious traditions, but often employ them in a perverse manner. While we recognize this fact, we also affirm that the God who has spoken to us in Jesus Christ has not left himself without witness in other religious traditions. We recognize this fact also.
  3. We recognize that many of the new religious movements, while claiming to answer the deeply felt spiritual needs of human kind are essentially manifestations of the demonic. These religious manifestations exploit the spiritual needs of humankind with their manipulative techniques and often enslave them to the power of individual leaders. In the face of this fact we admit our responsibility to resist these religious manifestations by exposing their hidden mo­tives and the evil which they perpetuate.

Recommendations

  1. That the Christian study centres in Asia be requested to make special study of religious splinter movements in Asia, especially the guru and tantric movements, so that we may have a clearer understanding of their roots, the causes which give rise to these movements, and the roles they play in Asia and the West. (It will be good if members of other faiths may also be included in the research).
  2. That centres for the study of religion and society throughout the world arrange seminars and study groups on the new religious movements, investigating descriptively the movements and the social conditions from which they spring, and reporting findings through literature and audio­visuals by the channel of the IAMS.
  3. That the IAMS Executive seek funding from international agencies such as WCC and WLF to support such studies.
  4. That the ASM organize and support a task force to study the new religious movements and report their findings in a special issue of Missiology.
  5. That the churches commission pastors and youth workers, preferably as a joint undertaking, to provide a ministry for former members of the new religious movements and disenchanted members seeking to get out. Such a ministry should not be one of a deprogramming by force and counterbrainwashing but through love and patience within a caring support community.

August 24, 1978
Maryknoll NY USA

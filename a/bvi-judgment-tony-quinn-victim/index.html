BVI JUDGMENT – TONY QUINN, VICTIM?

Tony Quinn

In Judge Bannister’s Judgment, Clause 19 says

It appears that there is a body of opinion in the Republic of Ireland which is passionately opposed to Mr Quinn and to whatever it may be that he stands for or promotes, to the point where campaigns have been run against him in the media and where he is, or has been, subjected to serious intimidation.

http://www.eccourts.org/judgments/decisions/2012/HC/stkitts/SMLifeVenturesvSusanMorriceetal.pdf

This could sound like sympathy from the Judge for Quinn, but we need to note that Judges can only make judgments on the evidence that people present to the Court, if evidence is not presented they cannot rule on it.

So it is in this light that Judge Bannister’s statements about opposition to Quinn and intimidation of him need to be read – the Judge is summarizing the evidence he has heard, which is clearly not much. The Judge hasn’t heard enough about “whatever it is [Quinn] stands for or promotes” to form an opinion. He has been told, and believes, that campaigns have been run against Quinn in the media, but of course, those campaigns could very well be entirely justified – why otherwise would the media bother campaigning against someone who runs a few courses and a few shops, when there are bigger scandals, in politics, finance, etc to campaign about?

And what about the intimidation? When someone is acting in such a way that even though, as in Quinn’s case, he is not of any great national importance, nevertheless campaigns are run against him in the media that are critical of his claims and actions. Given the level of damage and hurt that he has done to some people it is very possible that they might rant at him, issue vague threats etc. Here at Dialogue Ireland we do not condone such behavior – such intimidation is never the way. We know how unpleasant it is – we have been on the receiving end of severe intimidation based on nothing other than the malice of some cult leaders. Some individuals experience intimidation because of their own actions, for example, criminals often feel intimidated by the actions of the police so this kind of intimidation is good for society.

Nothing actually happened to Quinn, obviously. He has not been injured in any way. In fact, in court he said he feared a “tiger kidnapping”, a common crime which is nothing to do either with the Educo or the oil business, but something likely to happen to anyone who appeared wealthy, and/or knew people who could be blackmailed to commit a crime in exchange for the release of the person who had been kidnapped.

Nevertheless, these so-called “threats” and fears for his personal safety from a common crime (tiger kidnapping) which threatened many people, were a useful “cover” for Quinn to spent US$1.8 million of shareholders’ money on “security”. The Judge had a very firm opinion about that [Clause 74]

It is clearly excessive. Whatever may have been the need for some personal security, the expenditure was made in very large part for the purposes of Mr Quinn, who appears to have hired what amounted to a private army at the sole expense of INE. It seems to me that there is a prima facie case for supposing that a substantial amount of this expenditure is to be treated as if it was a gift to Mr Quinn [i.e. he should repay this to the company].

And here again we run into the question of evidence, that it seems that wrongdoing has certainly been done, but the Judge needs evidence which no-one has presented to him [Clause 74].

However, before making a finding that any of this expense amounted to an unratifiable abuse of INE’s funds the Court would need to be in a position to establish what of it was justifiable and what was not. That is not possible on the evidence before the Court.

The reason that this evidence could not be presented to the Court is that Quinn and Morrice control the company’s records so no-one can get hold of the detailed accounting information that would be needed for the Judge to make a ruling on how much money they should pay back to the company – this needs to be an exact figure.

Susan Morrice also complained of intimidation and the Judge reports in Clause 19

These campaigns [against Quinn] seem to have extended to persons from time to time associated with him and Ms Morrice, who remains a supporter of and who is supported by Mr Quinn, told me of intimidatory behaviour to which she has been subject, especially in Denver, Colorado, where she lives. I have no reason to doubt this evidence.

There may, of course, be other things going on in anyone’s life, disputes which could lead to threats, bins being turned over (one of the things Morrice complained of) etc. Again, apart from the bins being turned over, nothing actually happened to Susan Morrice, or her dogs (who she claimed were threatened). Nevertheless, Morrice seems to have jumped to the conclusion that it was all down to her association with Quinn, and this justified her in spending huge amounts of shareholders’ money on “security”. Note the Judge did not doubt that she felt threatened, but did not consider this was down to the company to pay for whatever excessive amounts she felt entitled to spend to protect herself and Quinn. Note that unspecified amounts of this sum were spent, not on defending dogs, bins and persons from tiger kidnapping etc, but

for defending the company against non physical attack from its enemies by, e.g., surveillance operations [Clause 74]

i.e. spying and hacking, as Susan Morrice stated in court, to find out who was spreading these “negative” reports, by which she seems to mean that some people did not like Tony Quinn and felt they had a right to say so, which does not actually affect the oil company. Quinn has been spending huge sums of OPM (Other Peoples’ Money – this time the oil company’s money) on legal cases. Quinn, through Morrice, has been constructing a false conspiracy theory as part of a legal ploy to defend his actions and attack Dialogue Ireland and others so it’s not surprising he keeps up the propaganda about conspiracies in true cult leader fashion. One only has to ask the question, in whose interest is it for Quinn and Morrice to be frightened for their lives, as they claim so emotionally, and such evidence as exists for their fears who could be behind it? The intelligent readers can use their own minds to think through what is the reality separated from the fiction and who is involved and what are the motives.

There are many occasions in the Judgment where the Judge says he does not have enough evidence, for instance, to decide exactly how much of Susan Morrice’s travel expenditure is unjustifiable as company expenses. She spent US$603,000 of the company’s (i.e. the shareholders’) money on travel and accommodation between 2008 and 2011, US$376,000 of this was in 2009 alone). In Clause 70 he says

Figures of this sort clearly cry out for some sort of justification. The difficulty is that I do not have sufficient information to be able to say what, if any, of these amounts represent unratifiable drawings on the part of Ms Morrice. The most that can be said is that a significant proportion of these expenses must have been personal.

This is the case for many of the huge expenses that Quinn and Morrice ran up, causing the Judge to note that they “felt free to treat company funds as though they were their personal property” [Clause 91]. The problem with this is that only the people in control of INE, Quinn and Morrice, have access to the information on the accounts which would be needed to give this kind of evidence to the Court. So, Sheila McCaffrey could not get this kind of evidence to make the case, but the Judge states very clearly that he believes there is a case to answer, for Quinn and Morrice. One person who could get this information would be a Liquidator appointed in response to a Court order to wind up the company. A Liquidator would be able to demand that all expenses paid should be justified, or the money repaid to the company for the benefit of the shareholders. In Clause 91 the Judge says that he would be prepared to consider ordering the company to be wound up because

The present board [controlled by Quinn and Morrice] shows no appetite for doing anything other than continuing to ignore the rights of the Class B membership under the 2002 Operating Agreement and its track record offers no grounds for believing that its behaviour will change.

It seems the Judge believes that it is very unlikely that the truth about the expenses will ever come out while they are in charge. All misspent money is coming out of the pockets of the shareholders. In Judge Bannister’s opinion, the Class B shareholders are the victims in the case.

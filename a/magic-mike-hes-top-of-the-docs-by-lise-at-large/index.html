Magic Mike: he’s top of the docs

By Lise at Large

Tuesday March 14 2006

When you see the words ‘handsome doctor’, ‘saving lives’ and ‘loads of awards’ placed together, various lab coat-wearing cuties from TV soaps such as ER and Scrubs spring to mind.

When you see the words ‘handsome doctor’, ‘saving lives’ and ‘loads of awards’ placed together, various lab coat-wearing cuties from TV soaps such as ER and Scrubs spring to mind.

But back in the real world, we have a home-grown hero scooping gongs left, right and centre, for his tireless efforts to combat poverty, disease and famine in Africa. An RTE documentary on Dublin-born doctor, Mike Meegan, has just picked up its third American award, a Silver Angel, presented by the California-based Excellence in Media Foundation.

The Foundation described the documentary on Mike’s work with aid agency Icross, as “a wonderful and inspired piece of television”.

The documentary, When You Say 4,000 Goodbyes, has already won awards at film festivals in New York and Chicago, and Mike himself picked up the International Person of the Year Award two years ago in Dublin.

Speaking from his base in Kenya yesterday, Mike was thrilled at the news of the third award.

“It’s amazing,” he told me. “The effect of the programme has been incredible; we’ve raised almost $1.5m since it was first shown on RTE last May,” he said. Icross run clinics providing medical aid for over 200,000 people across Kenya and Tanzania.

And the honours continue to roll in. Next month, Mike flies to Dublin to be presented with a medical doctorate by the Chancellor of the National University of Ireland, Dr Garrett FitzGerald on April 7.

Also collecting honorary doctorates at a ceremony in the Royal Hospital Kilmainham are Apocalypse Now and Badlands star Martin Sheen, fashion designer Philip Treacy and UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, Louise Arbour.

“Of course I’m deeply honoured by the award, and it also gives me a chance to come home and thank all the people who give so much of their time and money to helping Icross,” said Mike.

And maybe if he had a spare moment, our superdoc could try to sort out the near-anarchy in our allegedly First World A&E departments, because sure as hell no-one here seems able to.

– Lise at Large

Charity boss loses bid to gag media over sex claims

By Paul Caffrey
Irish Mail
Last updated at 9:05 AM on 20th April 2010

http://www.dailymail.co.uk/news/article-1267387/Charity-boss-loses-bid-gag-media-sex-claims.html

An Irish charity boss yesterday lost a High Court battle to try to suppress an investigation into a series of devastating claims about his activities in Kenya.The High Court heard claims Michael Meegan who was the subject of a glowing RTÉ documentary in 2005, sexually assaulted a number of young men over a 20-year period. According to evidence presented yesterday, Meegan also allegedly paid his workers for letting him cane them for his own sexual pleasure. High Court battle: Irish charity boss Michael Keegan has been accused of sexually assaulting young men while working in Kenya. Affidavits shown to the High Court further alleged that Meegan, whose charity fundraisers have been supported by celebrities from Chris De Burgh to Britney Spears, skimmed donor accounts to support his lifestyle and flew gay partners from around the world to visit him using the money given to his ICROSS organisation.

Meegan strenuously denied all the claims uncovered by the Irish Mail on Sunday and insisted he was the victim of a conspiracy by ‘rival’ Kenyan aid workers. He alleged that his accusers were making up false stories in the hope of being able to sue for compensation or that they had been bribed to make their statements. However, Meegan’s legal team admitted that three weeks ago ICROSS Kenya had tried to get Kenyan police to arrest his accusers – and said they wanted the investigation by The Irish Mail on Sunday gagged until those concerned had been put on trial and were convicted or cleared. The High Court also heard claims that affidavits from Kenya, lodged on Meegan’s behalf and purporting to cast doubt on the integrity of one of the alleged victims, were forgeries. Evidence shown to the court said claims against Meegan had first been made to police in Kenya in 1986 and similar allegations had been made since then by a variety of Kenyan men. Independent evidence from luminaries of the charity world including a leading Irish professor, a British peer and a charity worker now in Chad with Concern, detailed Meegan’s overtly sexual behaviour towards the young men in public. Yesterday, the president of the High Court, Mr Justice Nicholas Kearns, threw out Meegan’s application to have The Irish Mail on Sunday gagged. In a landmark ruling, Judge Kearns said such a ‘draconian’ measure would only be appropriate if the paper had no evidence to back up its case. He said he was satisfied there was a reasonable basis for the contention the newspaper may succeed at the trial of any libel action. He also ruled that the question of Meegan’s sexuality was simply a by-product of the sexual assault claims.  Earlier, the hearing was presented with statements by five Kenyan men who claimed to have been sexually assaulted by Meegan.

One, Meriape Ole Sangaire, said the abuse began in 1986 and his parents made him report Meegan to the police.  According a sworn affidavit from charity guru Dr Vincent Kenny, then a member of the ICROSS board, the claims were investigated by Kenyan CID – but a deal was done under which Meegan escaped prosecution if he came back to Ireland and didn’t return to Kenya.  But, Dr Kenny said in his affidavit that a few months later Meegan returned to Nairobi.  When the board of ICROSS did nothing about it and refused to order him home, said Dr Kenny, he resigned.  Mr Justice Kearns also lambasted Meegan’s lawyers for making ‘untrue’ claims in an effort to prevent the case being heard last week. On Friday, Liz Walsh BL, for the Mail, warned the case needed to be heard urgently – as Meegan had set Kenyan police on the paper’s star witnesses. Miss Walsh told the judge: ‘They have set the police on our witnesses. This has realised our worst fears. The overriding concern here is the safety and well-being of those five men. They are at the mercy of the Kenyan police.’  She said their best guarantee of safety was for the story to be published so the eyes of the world would be on the Kenyan authorities.  But Meegan’s lawyer, Morgan Shelley BL, contended they would need up to two days to wade through the written evidence involved, which they suggested included affidavits of up to 60 pages.

Judge Kearns agreed the case couldn’t be heard on Friday and deferred the hearing until Monday. But yesterday he rebuked Mr Shelley, who he said had made a ‘completely false’ statement about the affidavits’ length.  Judge Kearns said there was an obligation on Meegan’s side to ‘make full and honest disclosure to the court’ in their application for an injunction against the newspaper group but ruled:  ‘This has not happened in this case. ‘I am particularly angered by the fact that on Friday, when this matter brought before the court at 12.45, I was assured by counsel for the applicant [Meegan] that this was a matter that would take two days. This was a completely false representation.’  The judge refused Meegan’s claim that he should get an injunction against The Irish Mail on Sunday. He awarded costs against Meegan on the grounds that he had been misled and as Meegan had failed to win his case.  Leading senior counsel Michael Cush, for Associated Newspapers Ireland, said yesterday: ‘In this case we have the simplest [defence] of all. Truth. We have a very significant body of evidence to substantiate the allegations we propose to make.’  Even after the ruling, counsel for Meegan tried to secure a stay of execution on the order that would extend the gagging of the media – on the basis of a possible Supreme Court appeal. But Judge Kearns refused to place a stay on his order. Eventually Meegan and his legal team decided not to appeal the ruling. Meegan and his organisation have received millions from donors including the Irish and the U.S. governments. The MoS began investigating these allegations in February. A month ago it revealed the Irish board of ICROSS had returned almost e100,000 to the Irish Government and frozen its accounts following an audit.

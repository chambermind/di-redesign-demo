The Simon Wiesenthal Center: On a Cruise to Nowhere?

Scientology

The Simon Wiesenthal Center is, per their website, “a global Jewish human rights organization that confronts anti-Semitism, hate and terrorism, promotes human rights and dignity, stands with Israel, defends the safety of Jews worldwide, and teaches the lessons of the Holocaust for future generations. With a constituency of over 400,000 households in the United States, it is accredited as an NGO at international organizations including the United Nations, UNESCO, OSCE, Organization of American States (OAS), the Latin American Parliament (PARLATINO) and the Council of Europe.”

They are known for their ‘Museum of Tolerance’ which, again according to their website, is “The Center’s educational arm, founded in 1993 challenges visitors to confront bigotry and racism, and to understand the Holocaust in both historic and contemporary contexts. The Museum has served over 5 million visitors with 350,000 visiting annually including 150,000 students. Over 1.5 million children and youth have participated in the Museum experience and its programs. Over 200,000 adults have been trained in the Museum’s customized, professional development programs which include Tools for Tolerance®, Teaching Steps to Tolerance, Task Force Against Hate, National Institute Against Hate Crimes, Tools for Tolerance for Teens and Bridging the Gap.”

So why is The Simon Wiesenthal Center planning this May 5th to honor Tom Cruise with their 2011 Humanitarian Award? Such a decision may lead them on a Cruise to nowhere writes Stephen R. Wiseman MD.


-L. Ron Hubbard in Science of Survival

Hubbard wrote his long, rambling Science of Survival in 1951, and to this day it is referred to by his brain-child, Scientology, as “the most useful book you will ever own”. The basis of this book, which presents many of today’s most fundamental aspects of Scientology belief and practice, is that all human beings can be assigned a numerical place on a “Tone Scale” invented by Hubbard. The utility of such a placement, according to Scientology, is extremely high, for by knowing only “one or two characteristics of a person,” the Tone Scale can produce “an accurate index of their entire personality, conduct and character.”

A chapter in Science of Survival, entitled “Actual Worth to Society Compared to Apparent Worth”, suggests what  Scientologists are to do with those they deem low on the Tone Scale. To this end, the emphasis is not on an individual’s “worth” to him or herself, but rather his deemed worth to greater society. I could go on. But let’s review: a core, fundamental piece of current Scientology belief and practice is learning about and utilizing Hubbard’s Tone Scale, whereby an individual’s apparent worth to society is determined by a self-appointed Scientologist-judge. The judge only needs to know one or two simple characteristics of the individual to reach a blanket conclusion about his or her entire makeup and value; those deemed lacking, in the best of all possible worlds, will be denied basic rights or simply made to disappear.

To say the least, this makes me pretty confused why the Simon Wiesenthal Center will be honoring Hollywood actor Tom Cruise with its 2011 Humanitarian Award on May 5.

As most of us know, Cruise has been a devoted Scientologist for many years. Thanks to his public support of the organization, his friendship with group leader David Miscavige, and his periodic outbursts against psychiatry, Cruise’s image is invariably intertwined with that of Scientology. When he succeeds, or in honored in any way, it is reflected back on the organization to which he belongs and to which he has given so much credit already.

Unlike Hubbard, I believe that an individual is more than the company he or she keeps, and there is undoubtedly more to Cruise than Scientology. However, this is not an organization one dabbles in at the higher levels; remember, this is a group that demands its elite Sea Org members sign a “billion year contract” for service, and disciplines its adherents in a ritualized, highly abusive fashion according to legions of escapees. Cruise himself, in his infamous, leaked, black-sweatered Scientology video clip explains to other followers “You’re on board or you’re not on board…okay, if you’re on board, you’re on board like the rest of us—period!”

For this reason, it is utterly inconceivable that Tom Cruise has not read, studied, and been drilled on the contents of Science of Survival. It is inconceivable that as a high-ranking Scientologist, he could choose to forgo or ignore key pieces of Hubbard’s teaching and scripture.  To my knowledge, he has never publically even hinted at criticism of the organization, or Hubbard’s writings which are all carefully preserved word-for-word by Scientology and as per the group’s doctrine, cannot ever be changed. Whether he or his agents like it or not, as a vastly wealthy, vastly famous public figure, Tom Cruise must and openly does stand by the Tone Scale and Science of Survival.

My interest in Scientology comes from being a Low Tone individual myself, namely a psychiatrist. Hubbard attacked psychiatry in the mid-1950’s after the humiliating failure of his new “science” of mental health and the spectacular onset of populist psychopharmacology, and over the years this has become incorporated into Scientology doctrine as an ever-lasting “war” between the forces of good (Scientology) and evil (psychiatry). With the creation of the Citizens Commission on Human Rights in 1969, Scientology has even had its own front group dedicated to the destruction of my profession, and of my individual colleagues for no other reason than the specialty training we chose to pursue after medical school. In modern Scientology-speak, all psychiatrists are by definition criminals and murderers, and any destructive efforts against us are inherently justified and justifiable.

If this sounds like the embodiment of Science of Survival, well, yes, this is absolutely correct. I am not just talking about some words on paper in the abstract, but like many things in Scientology, a way of seeing, believing, and by necessity acting. By erupting against psychiatry and Brooke Shields on The Today Show on June 24, 2005, Tom Cruise showed his organization, and the world, that he is firmly “on board” with the war. This is a man who already deems me a criminal, and wants to destroy my career, my family’s livelihood, and my patients’ access to evidence-based mental health treatment solely on account of his knowing what medical specialty I practice. This is precisely the Tone Scale in action, and if such action is not a full 180—degrees opposite to what the Simon Wiesenthal center tells us it’s about, I don’t know what is.

Perhaps the vilest hypocrisy regarding the Wiesenthal decision comes from Scientology’s ongoing portrayal of psychiatrists as “the men behind Hitler”. On a consistent basis, the group has used references and images of the Holocaust to further their efforts to destroy my profession by linking it and it alone to the Nazi atrocities of World War II. To so grotesquely both use and trivialize the Holocaust through simplification and deliberate misinformation—in an effort to eliminate a group of doctors solely on account of our belonging to our particular profession—is something Scientology must answer for right now.

My belief until now is that the Simon Wiesenthal Center would demand such answers from the Scientology hate-group and others. But in honoring Tom Cruise and thereby Scientology, this opportunity has disappeared—and been replaced, to be honest, with a truly foul stench.  If it is too late to cancel Cruise’s award, it remains my hope that the SWC can rehabilitate itself in front of thousands of psychiatrists and millions of public, would-be supporters by investigating the hateful conduct towards our profession by Scientology, along with the sickening and cynical Holocaust manipulation accompanying it.

Dr. Stephen Wiseman is a Clinical Associate Professor in the Department of Psychiatry at the University of British Columbia, and Consultant Psychiatrist at St. Paul’s Hospital in Vancouver, BC.

Other panel members consist of sociologist of religion Professor Stephen Kent; psychiatrist Lynn Partridge; and former Scientologist Nancy Many, author of My Billion Year Contract.

Meet the Neighbours before they have moved in. What kind of Muslims are these?

Religion

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/05/16/e40m-islamic-centre-for-clongriffin-by-jack-fagan-irish-times-may-16-2012/

Six months later we have no idea who these individuals are and what charitable organisations they represent? When the Builder Gerry Gannon was approached there was no reply either.

This was what we were informed about the people involved in this project before we saw the building taking shape:

http://www.gannonhomes.ie/

The group is anxious to make it known that the proposed amenities will be available not only to the Muslim population but also to families of other faiths – or no faith – with the intention of promoting harmonious relations. Funding for the development has been promised by 10 wealthy individuals as well as charitable organisations in the Middle East.

Most of the Dublin-based group promoting the Islamic Cultural Centre are professionals working in medicine, research and the universities. Once the centre has been developed, it will be capable of accommodating up to 5,000 people at any one time. The new campus will also include 147 houses and apartments.

It is time that we got to know who are going to inhabit this space what they represent and how they are going to go about what they call promoting harmonious relations with families of other faiths – or no faith? Otherwise it looks like a contrived plan with a developer producing a fake faith community out of the thin air. It is now a bit late for answers as the buildings are going ahead? You will note

The Irish Times – Saturday, October 20, 2012

Mosque proposed for Clongriffin

TIM O’BRIEN

http://www.irishtimes.com/newspaper/ireland/2012/1020/1224325506784.html

DUBLIN CITY planners are to meet public representatives on proposals for a €65 million, mixed-use development based around a new Islamic cultural centre on the city’s northern fringe.

The council is currently considering a planning application which includes a large mosque, a 34 classroom school, conference centre, assembly hall, playground and swimming pool on the site in Clongriffin.

The development also includes a small number of apartments and retail units. It is backed by Gerry Gannon, whose Gannon Developments has funded the nearby Dart station and a range of public facilities in Clongriffin.

These include an internal street network, a 460-space park-and-ride facility, retail outlets, apartments and houses.

Local Labour Party TD Seán Kenny told The Irish Times he was not opposing the development but felt there were a number of issues which needed to be settled.

He said the inclusion in the plans for “only 192 car parking spaces is entirely inadequate for a 600 people capacity conference centre.”

He also said he failed to see why the application included retail units, including a crèche, bookshop and library, “when there is a vast amount of empty retail units nearby on Main Street.”

Mr Kenny said a meeting would be held on Tuesday week between public representatives and planners.

There was a large mosque in Clonskeagh which blended in well with the surroundings there. The new project could also blend in if it was integrated “with the existing retail, residential, and access footprint in Clongriffin.”

21 December 1978

US Government Charges Against Scientology

Scientology

By Jakob Andersen

One of the people who most fearlessly has been fighting Scientology is the Danish reporter Jakob Andersen. He has now been in contact with the US-authorities concerning this case against Scientology.

His report is to be read in connection with the parallel report on the French case in Paris. By one estimate, 3 million Americans espouse the teachings of 3,000 religious and non-religious cults. Some of them are considered harmless, others are labelled as weird and a few have proved, that they can be outright dangerous. From time to time the attention of the public is focused on one after another of these groups, mostly because of some exposed practice which has created widespread criticism. Just now the whole world of cults has been brought to trial in the media -in the wake of the tragic mass deaths in Guyana. The very fact that the Peoples Temple led so many of its followers to their destruction has aroused renewed anxiety: What are other cults capable of?

In the office of the U.S. Attorney in Washington D.C. it is possible to get the answer to that question, at least in regard to one of the most controversial cults, the Church of Scientology.

Eleven top-scientologists have been indicted and the government charges, that the scientologists;
1)  infiltrated various agencies and departments of the United States.
2)  placed agents in these agencies.
3)  used bogus credentials to gain access to various agencies.
4)  utilized electronic devices, stole documents, burglarized government offices, a.s.o

Most of the evidence outlined against Scientology is based on the cult’s own internal documents, which were seized by the FBI in a raid last year. 134 FBI agents burst into Scientology headquarters in Washington and Los Angeles and carted away thousands of documents, a lock-picking kit, eavesdropping equipment, two pistols and a lot more.

Among the indicted is Mary Sue Hubbard, the wife of L. Ron Hubbard, who founded the Church of Scientology some 25 years ago. Also indicted is Jane Kember, who is next in the line of command and who holds the title of Guardian World Wide.

The FBI and the U.S. Attorney have been able to prove, that it is not only some irresponsible members of the cult, who have acted on their own, but that the topmost leader of Scientology planned and executed a complex conspiracy against the law and the government. The case is so strong, that it could become a blow which Scientology will not be able to overcome in a near future. I have investigated the matter in the United States, and I have examined the huge piles of related documents in the archives of the U.S. Courthouse in Washington. Therefore – despite the fact that it is impossible to list the devastating evidence in this article – I dare say, that I seldom have seen such a well documented criminal case. Scientology has been caught red-handed, and if the crimes of their leaders do not start processes of analitical thinking in the minds of the followers, it will be a new sad example of the fanatical mental blindness, which so often has been registered in the past.

To many people it is not very surprising, that Scientology applied criminal behaviour to its struggle for survival and expansion. During its tumultuous existence it has often been proved, that Scientology is based on cynical aggression rather than love, tolerance and understanding. Its founder, L. Ron Hubbard, relentlessly has directed his followers to execute violent attacks on those who dared critizise the practice of the cult. Governmental hearings in several countries have documented the hateful attitude by which Hubbard and his organization have tried to overcome criticism. Instead of positive reactions to the impulses from the surrounding world Scientology has developed a barren stubbornness, which is unequaled. “Dont give in to anybody or anything. Fight.”

Fight is the codeword for Hubbard, and in a so-called Policy Letter to his followers, he has told them how to do it. The Policy Letter deals at length with the measures which should be taken against those, who critizise the organization, and a short quotation will do as an example. Hubbard wrote:

“This is correct procedure:
1) Spot who is attacking us.
2) Start investigating them for FELONIES or worse using own professionals, not outside agencies.
3) Don’t curve our reply by saying we welcome an investigation of them.
4) Start feeding lurid, blood sex crime actual evidence on the attackers to the press.
Don’t ever tamely submit to an investigation of us. Make it rough, rough on attackers all the way.”

The story of Scientology is also a story of harassment of its critics. Government officials, journalists, theologians and others in many countries have been subject to intense investigation and harassment if they dared speak up against Hubbard and his flock. The pending case in Washington proved most lately, that Scientology has been waging an extensive, sophisticated campaign to attack and discredit its “enemies”, including investigators from the Internal Revenue Service, the Justice Department and the press. In the court assistant U.S. attorney Raymond Banoun also charged that Scientology has been conducting “a campaign of vilification” against prosecutors and a federal judge involved in Scientology cases in court.

Every time Scientology is being exposed in the midst of a campaign of harassment, its spokesmen always tell the public that the movement is being discriminated in violation of the religious liberty. The self-designed role of martyrs has become a familiar part of the cult’s defences, and most people never get a fair chance to get to the bottom of a Scientology case, because the organization is highly complex both in structure, teachings and public appearance. By cleverly devised tactics it is often possible for Scientology to sidestep serious accusations, and it requires obstinate investigators to penetrate the mist of misleading explanations, which flows in an endless stream from the headquarters of the organization.

The allegation that Scientology is the target for evil conspiracies is repeated over and over again and so is the claim, that it is recognized as a “bona fide” religion in many places. Scientology rides high on the fact that western countries have freedom of conscience as essential parts of their constitutions. Every country has its laws which protect minorities against persecution, and especially religious movements enjoy rightly this protection in a very wide sense. It is this fundamental tradition which has made it a most extraordinary exception that a religious organization is being outlawed as such – but that is exactly what has happened to Scientology.

The country is Denmark, which cannot be accused of intolerance in matters concerning the rights of minorities. Section 140 and section 266b of the Danish penal code is the safeguard against persecution, blasphemy and other violations of ethnic and religious groups, and every religion, every cult – no matter how weird – is protected by these sections – except Scientology.

The story is as follows:
Some years ago Scientology sued a newspaper for libel because it had described how the practice of Scientology had caused mental breakdowns, and how the cult had claimed healing abilities which had no foundation in reality. During the trial the newspaper produced devastating evidence on a wide range of practices of Scientology, and the judgment concluded that Scientology should NOT enjoy the protection of the law in re­gard to the abovementioned section of the penal code. As grounds for this conclusion, which was unprecedented in Denmark, the judgment listed the following:

Scientology is calculated to arouse sceptism and distrustfulness because of
a)  the content of its theories, for example demonstration of experiences in earlier lives and spiritual healing.
b)  its propagandistic activities.
c) its commercial organization including demands for payment of very considerable amounts of money for granting various kinds of cure of souls.
d) its collecting personal informations and its internal admini­stration of justice, and
e) its markedly manifested intolerance towards psychiatrists, psychologists and other people who think differently.

The judgment continued:

The movement therefore must come to terms with scrutinizing criticism of its theories and its activities, and the court finds, that it cannot plead the principles of the provisions of section 140 and section 266b of the penal code.

This judgment was passed on the 19th of June 1974 in the lower court of Copenhagen (Københavns Byret) and was upheld the 18th of November 1977 in the High Court (Ostre Landsret). (Case nr. 330/1974).

In short this judgment meant that the traditionally liberal Denmark for the first time in ages denied a religious movement the protection against discrimination, and that fact indicates how Scientology has behaved in this country. The veil was removed, and an ugly face appeared behind it.

So far Scientology has suffered terrible defeats in several trials in Denmark, and I shall mention only a few of the accusations against the cult, which the courts have found had been properly set forth:

  • “(Scientology is) mental quackery.”
  • “(Scientology is` religious swindle.”
  • “(Scientology practices) brainwashing.”
  • “Scientology is evil.”

The list contains scores of charges, which it has been impossible for Scientology to get declared improper. I think it is important that Scientology in Denmark do not any more enjoy the traditional protection of the penal code, because the practices of the cult are so earthly, that it requires earthly methods to deal with them. It would be a caricature of the common sense of society, if Scientology – behind an otherwise sound and precious shield – should be allowed to do what common citizens never could get away with.

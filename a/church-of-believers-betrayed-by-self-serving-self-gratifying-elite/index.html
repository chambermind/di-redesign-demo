Church of believers betrayed by self-serving, self-gratifying elite

Monday, December 07, 2009

THE issue of clerical abuse in the Irish Catholic Church has unearthed depths of depravity and corruption that those of us who soldier in the ranks of the lay faithful can scarcely fathom.

It is not that the lay faithful who have served on committees and ad hoc groups within the church were not entirely unaware that something was a bit whiffy about the structures and modus operandi of the organisation. Where power is given to individuals with little or no checks, accountability or limits of tenure, it is a reasonable deduction that this power will be abused in some way by a significant number of those individuals.

Until such time as the structures of organisation and decision-making are honestly and humbly looked at there will be no fundamental change. While it can be safely said at this point that the welfare of children is no longer a concern where the Catholic clergy is concerned, if for no other reason than the extreme wariness and caution of parents and guardians in this regard, that is not to say that any fundamental lessons have been learned by the hierarchy or that power will not continue to be abused in other ways.

Human beings have many foibles, many bad tendencies, many addictions – many routes to sinfulness as followers of the gospel would express it.

Putting human beings in situations where they can exercise as much self-will as they like without let or hindrance is at the very least dangerous. The church, in its justifiable concern for orthodoxy of religious practice and belief, has allowed a structure of governance to evolve where every detail of organisation, expenditure and pastoral activity within a parish is firmly in the control of one man.

If and where this is changing it is because of force of circumstances and not because of any new thinking or enlightenment.

It is a sad fact that few if any of us, whether we be lay person or clergy, feel we will abuse power entrusted to us. It is a universal blind spot. We can always justify and excuse the ways we exercise power even if it finally comes down to the defence that our personal stewardship, whatever its failings, is steadier and more reliable than the alternative of consultative and shared decision-making. The fact is it is not. Recent disclosures, if nothing else, should finish off that argument once and for all.

The main reason for the denials and cover-ups regarding the heinous activity now fully and finally unmasked is that this structure of power and privilege came before fidelity to the gospel.

The institution and its ministers were more important than their raison d’etre. The “church” was always in the right. When it clearly was not, there was and still is an inability to admit wrong, to apologise without equivocation and to resign from office where trust was betrayed or lost.

The real church, which is the body of believers and followers of Christ, is not the church that is now being arraigned. It is not this Catholic community of saints and sinners which continues to endure and indeed flourish in the world at large. It is not the sincere people of God who include numerous priests and religious as well as laity. What has fallen spectacularly from grace is the structure of power and privilege that has attached itself to it like a vice over the centuries.

A structure that is ridden with gradations of rank and privilege that would be more appropriate to some arcane royal household than the followers of a man who was at odds with every system he encountered, whether religious or secular.

The church model, in its most extreme excesses at least, looks more and more to be an Irish phenomenon and one we have exported to the US and Australia.
Most likely this is to do with the role of the Irish church among a downtrodden and impoverished people who looked to it for protection and direction against a hostile, alien government. Unfortunately, however, many people will fail to make that distinction between the church of the gospel and the church of the self-serving, self-gratifying elite who have betrayed it.

Margaret Hickey
Castleowen
Blarney
Co Cork

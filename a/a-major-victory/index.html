21 December 1978

A major victory?

Scientology

By Peter Jensen, Church Of Scientology, Denmark

American Scientologists have scored a major victory in their case against U.S. government agencies with the recent decision by a U.S. court to seal all Church documents seized by the FBI in July 1977 raids. The decision, which has nullified present efforts by the U.S. Justice Department to prosecute the reform-minded Scientologists, is being viewed as a sign of the growing impact that the Church is having in presenting its case of constitutional abuse by American government agencies to the courts and tothe public.

The win for the Scientologists in the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals, has rocked the beleaguered Justice Department who have been hard pressed to avoid public disfavour for their handling of several key issues during the past year.

The Constitution of the United States is famous for its protection of individual citizens and groups from violations by government agencies of the freedoms which first inspired the founding of the nation. Freedom of religion, freedom of speech and freedom from invasion of privacy and property by illegal search and seizure, are some of the safeguards embodied in the document. The Constitution further protects the right of political organizations to gather and practice freely.

However, numerous Congressional investigations and media exposes have revealed that, since Watergate, constitutional violations have continued to be a routine activity of U.S. federal agencies. Not a week has gone by without new allegations of wrongdoing.

When a former FBI Director, Clarence Kelley, testified before a Senate Committee investigating FBI illegalities that J. Edgar Hoover’s infamous COINTELPRO (Counter Intelligence Program) had stopped in 1966, he was not telling the truth. Breaking into private homes and offices without a search warrant, covert penetration and harassment of dissident groups and illegal wire-tapping are some of the practices which have continued well past the date.1)

Another former FBI Director, L. Patrick Gray III, and two other high ex-FBI officials have now been indicted for ordering surreptitious break-ins. At least 67 other FBI agents and officials have been implicated.2)

In one of the many court actions brought against the bureau the Socialist Workers Party has sued the FBI for $40 Million. The left wing group has charged the federal agency with carrying on a campaign of harassment and intimidation against them, including the deployment of 1300 paid informants over a 40 year period.

Federal Judge, Thomas P. Griesa, presiding, expressed shock that “What (these) informants provided the FBI with was a consistent recital of peaceful lawful political activities, peaceful, lawful personal activities and a total absence of any criminal activities or plans of any nature whatever”. 3)

When Attorney-General, Griffen B. Bell, refused to comply with a court order to surrender government files related to the case, Judge Griesa charged him with contempt.4)

In a related matter current FBI Director, William Webster, has proposed a 10-year moratorium on disclosure of material under the Freedom of Information Act from the agency’s investigative files. Webster has defended his proposal by stating, International Herald Tribune 10 Nov 1978, “(that it) would be to minimize the danger to informants whose identities might inadvertently be revealed”. 5) Yet an earlier story which appeared on a national wire service reported that “Justice Department and FBI officials… are considering how to dispose of many of the 6.5 million files the bureau has collected on citizens and groups.” 6)

More recently, in a more serious matter, Gary Rowe Jr., the FBI’s top informer inside the Ku Klux Klan in the 1960s, has been indicted for the 1965 killing of civil-rights worker Viola Liuzzo. Rowe has also been implicated in an Alabama church bombing which killed four black girls attending Sunday school.7)

The Scientologists have claimed, perhaps with some justification, that the massive attack launched against them by the Justice Department and FBI has been a smokescreen intended to draw attention away from the constitutional abuses brought to light by Church researches. The 28 year battle between the Church and the Government, which culminated in a raid by 150 FBI agents on Scientology churches in Los Angeles and Washington in July 1977 brought these comments from noted American syndicated columnist, James J. Kilpatrick: “What troubles me in this affair is the sheer, crushing power that our government can bring to this bear when it chooses… the pattern of harassment is clear… They descended with crowbars, battering raids, sledge hammers and power saws. They invaded living quarters, where married members of the Church staff were still in bed… they ransacked church files, including files that deal with the Church’s pending lawsuits against the government.”.

Kilpatrick continued, “Even if the Scientologists prevail in the end, they will have been put to stunning legal expenses. Their normal operations will have been disrupted for months. And all for what? Is the FBI’s purpose prosecution or persecution?”8)

In fact it has been estimated by Washington lawyers, that American taxpayers have had to pay $100 million to support the dozen different agencies involved in the 28 year attack.

The story began at least as early as 1951, when the book “Science of Survival” by Church Founder, L. Ron Hubbard, attacked the use of psychiatric mind control techniques.

Walter Bowart, in his best selling book “Operation Mind Control” has credited Hubbard, who served in the U.S. Navy during the war, with “uncovering the tin of a vast iceberg of mind-control research using drugs as an aid to hypnotic induction”. Bowart’s book reveals that Hubbard had accidently run across and then attempted to expose the Mind Control Program undertaken by Americal intelligence agencies. 9)

It was the U.S. Navy who had been authorized to carry out most of the Mind control projects that involved drugs. A CIA report obtained under the Freedom of Information Act described the Navy Drug project as one which sought to “isolate and synthesize pure drugs for use in effecting psychological entry and control of the individual.”

In relating Hubbard’s early findings, Bowart wrote, “In 1951… he described “a secret of certain military and intelligence organizations. He called it “Pain-Drug-Hypnosis” and said it “is a vicious war weapon and may be of considerably more use in conquering a society than the atom bomb. This is no exaggeration. The extensiveness of the use of this form of hypnotism in espionage work is now so widespread that it is long past the time when people would have become alarmed about it… Pain-Drug-Hypnosis is a wicked extension of narcosynthesis, the drug hypnosis used in America only during and since the last war.. ”

Interestingly enough the FBI’s interest in Scientology also began in 1951.10)

Other agencies quickly joined in and started their own files on the new group. The Food and Drug Administration for example, was fed by irate psychiatrists and American Medical Association officials who were angered at Hubbard’s criticisms of the use of unproven treatments such as electro-shock, lobotomy, and excessive drugging. 27 file cabinets worth of FDA dossiers obtained by the Scientologists under the FOI Act revealed an obsessive interest by the agency in the Church. The Scientologists have pointed to the self-help approach expounded by Hubbard as one reason for the concern by the medical monopolists.

U.S. Air Force files hardly showed less restraint in describing the membership of the group as being composed of “homosexuals, communists or both…”

The first inkling the church had that it was on someone’s meanie list was in 1958 when then Vice President Nixon ordered action against it, reported Washington Star Religious Editor, William Willoughby, in his column of 21 December 1974. “The Founding Church of Scientology here in Washington made the mistake of quoting him on mental health. This was printed in the church’s publication in California. Within hours the Secret Service was at the publication office, warning that “Nixon’s name was not to be printed in the church’s publication”. 11)

Omar V. Garrison, author of “The Hidden Story of Scientology” reports on the next installment:
“On January 4, 1963 in Washington, a group of U.S. Marshals and deputized longshoremen, acting for an agency of the federal government (FDA), desecrated, looted and terrorized a religious center.”
“That is now a historical fact”. 12)

The Church has documented many other instances of abuse which they are now using as evidence in a $ 750 million damage suit accusing the CIA, FBI, IRS, Justice Department and various other government agencies.13)

As a result of the Church’s experiences with government abuse it has taken up the cause of social reform, not only in matters relating to itself but issues which range from human rights on the one hand to, on the other hand, rehabilitation of drug addicts, and criminals and better care for mental patients and the elderly. 14)

That is the other side of the story.

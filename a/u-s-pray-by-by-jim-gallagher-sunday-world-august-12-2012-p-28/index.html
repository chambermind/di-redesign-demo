Christina begs pilgrims to cover the costs of her luxury trip to the States

WEALTHY House of Prayer boss Christina Gallagher is getting her humble followers to pay for a luxury trip to the USA. The money-mad guru plans to travel to America

for several weeks in September – but wants her disciples to pay for it. The self-proclaimed visionary, who lives like a pop star in a fabulous €4 million mansion in plush Malahide, Co. Dublin, and who has a portfolio of other stunning properties, is sking her American followers to cough up the fares for herself and spiritual director Fr Gerard McGinnity.

US trip

Obsessed

A begging email was sent out earlier this month to followers in the US, entitled: “Awesome News from the House of Prayer!” Then in giant capital letters it said: “GREAT NEWS FOR PILGRIMS.”

The email said Gallagher (59), and her obsessed right-hand man would be visiting her Houses of Prayer in America. It read: “This will be a great gift of grace for the Mission and the Houses. Please join us in prayer for funds for their travel and flight.”

Then, more blatantly, it said: “If the funds are made available, Father and Christina hope to be here in September.” The letter, from local House of Prayer official Francie Lloyd, makes it sound as if the visit is a rare privilege for followers, when in fact Gallagher travels to the US every year. And she always flies first or business class.

In the past, when Gallagher visited the US, each of her houses of prayer were told to raise $15,000 each to cover all costs. Gallagher and McGinnity usually spend a few days in New York at the beginning and end of each trip, where she loves to go shopping.

An insider told the Sunday World that Gallagher plans to spend a week in each of three of her houses of prayer – in Florida, Texas and Minnesota. There was no mention of Kansas or Mexico, where she also has centres. The Sunday World has uncovered a long list of scams run by the notorious Gallagher to fund her astonishing lifestyle. These included demanding cash for bomb shelters across America which had been “ordered by God”; the sale of €250 pictures with “miraculous powers” and beads

for an “endless rosary” at €50. Gullible Massive amounts have been donated by gullible followers who believed their money was going to “Our Lady’s work”. Instead, the money was used to buy a string of fabulous homes for herself and her family, seven of which the Sunday World has so far discovered.

Landmark: Anonymous Comm2Post A scary lesson in influence and using the ejector exit!

Landmark

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/category/landmark/

Submitted on 2013/06/24 at 11:49 pm

I went to an introductory meeting recently. Being completely honest, I had no clue about the organisation or what it did prior to this. I didn’t even know the name. Someone had told me of an information evening nearby that may be of benefit to my college studies, without disclosing the name of the organisation, and so I decided to go along…..

Within the first 10 minutes of the meeting I remember saying to myself ‘Wow, this is almost like a cult,’ jokingly. But that idea seemed more like reality as the night went on. The concept of improving one’s life and so forth appeals to me, but the way they went about it did not.

From the very start we were highly pressured into signing up. So much emphasis was on the sales side of things, which instantly turned many of us off. I was paired with a graduate when discussing the 7 benefits you’re supposed to obtain from the course, and found him completely false, unrelatable and again, extremely pushy. The organiser of the evening was even more unappealing

Coming towards the end of the night he asked me if I thought I would join up. I admitted straight away that I didn’t think so. I emphasized the financial side of things, one concern for me along with the fact we were highly pressurized, as well as thinking the whole thing just common sense disguised as some great enlightenment you had to pay 400 euro to achieve. He tried to explain that 400 euro was nothing compared to a lifetime of happiness. I again tried to explain to him that I was a student who was working every weekend just to make enough money to get through my degree, yet he still persisted. After saying ‘yeah, I suppose could save for it’, just so he would possibly stop pressuring me, he tried to put me off this idea and encouraged me to sign up spontaneously on the spot, like he did, or so he said. I said I would look into it some more when I got home, online specifically, to which he responded with more pressure to sign up on the spot. I now realise that this was probably to prevent me from discovering all the controversy around the organisation, as I had made him aware that I had never heard of it before.

 The meeting was 3 hours long, and I left after 2. I was uninterested, to start with, but also freaked out, uneasy and quite annoyed to be honest.

I was later informed, after I left, that when others who attended the meeting sought some more advice from other graduates, the organiser of the event saw these attempts and interfered, reminding them they were just about to sign up and didn’t need any more information.

 When I myself went to leave, I approached the graduate I had been speaking to all evening, who was also taking registration. I explained to him that I had to leave suddenly. He wasn’t convinced, and to say he was unimpressed would be an understatement.

After researching and reading ‘reviews’ like these it’s nice to know that it wasn’t just me who felt unsettled by this organisation, and I truly hope nobody else is roped in to it under false pretences. I could definitely see the ‘brainwashing effect’ on the graduates that many reviews discuss, which was quite frankly frightening.

Like I said, I knew literally nothing about this organisation before I so happened to show up at one of their meetings, and so I hope that this post can give others an idea about just how much an effect this organisation can have on a fresh mind after just 2 hours.

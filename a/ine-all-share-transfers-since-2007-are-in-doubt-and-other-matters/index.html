INE: ALL SHARE TRANSFERS SINCE 2007 ARE IN DOUBT – AND OTHER MATTERS

Tony Quinn

Prologue:

DI has been receiving reports for all of last week of continued attempts to abuse B shareholders. We obviously have no interest in the oil issue as such but rather we are concerned for the Ex Educoists and those still under Quinn’s influence. We understand that Sheila is being put under extreme pressure and that the dividends have still not been received. DI is evaluating whether it will engage in a protest outside the AGM. We will take advise from those who can brief us on the right course of action. In this case even one person being there is enough to express the principle of concern for the victims of Quinn.

We take very seriously the judgement of Justice Hogan:

5. Although Mr. Quinn is not a party to the litigation (and, hence, not represented before me), it is fair to say that his career has engendered some controversy. While it would be inappropriate to dwell on these matters in circumstances where he was not represented before me, the evidence before me nonetheless suggests that he professes what many might regard as rather unorthodox religious views. His supporters appear to subscribe passionately to these views and often participate in what are described as “Educo” seminars run by Mr. Quinn and his close associates. In this regard and to anticipate somewhat, it may be observed that Ms. Tallant, an investigative reporter with the Sunday World newspaper has penned several articles in that newspaper in which she brands Mr. Quinn as a sham who exploits the religious sensibilities of the vulnerable for financial gain, often using hypnosis and other techniques subverting the will and reason. Mr. Cornec (and others) maintain that Ms. Morrice herself has come under what they see as the baneful influence of Mr. Quinn and that she herself has effectively gifted large amounts of stock to him, while also permitting him to use the assets of the company in a wholly unorthodox fashion. Thus, for example, Bannister J. found that some US$1.8m of INE money had been spent in the last few years on security and surveillance personnel, thus allowing Mr. Quinn to hire what the judge described as virtually his own “private army”.

Judge Bannister ruled that the 2007 Operating Agreement which was “sneaked in the back door” of INE – transforming it from a normal limited company owned by its shareholders to something that shareholders had put their money into, but which was run on very different lines from a normal company – was illegal. In the Original Agreement of 2002, shareholders were entitled among other rights to

* open information on the company’s accounts and management including some director appointments, including the right to question the Board at an Annual General Meeting

* the right to vote on the issue of new shares

* the right to fair and proportionate dividends

* the right to have their share of the company’s profits allocated to them each year

* the right to sell their shares to whomever they wished at the best price they could achieve, provided neither the Board nor any of the other members wanted to buy them – if the Board or other members did want to buy them, then they would have to match the price that the shareholder could obtain outside.

Because the new Agreement was “sneaked in the back door”, not openly put to the shareholders and voted on by them, it was ruled illegal by Judge Bannister and so EVERYTHING that happened under it is now in question. This includes all sales or transfers of shares that occurred during the period that the company was being run by this illegal agreement. Why?

BECAUSE SHAREHOLDERS, INCLUDING ANY CLASS B SHAREHOLDERS WHO MAY HAVE EXCHANGED THEIR SHARES FOR EDUCO SEMINARS, DID NOT DO SO UNDER THE FAIR AND OPEN RULES OF THE 2002 AGREEMENT [see footnote 15 on page 16 of Judge Bannister’s Judgment which states that Tony Quinn acquired some Class B shares in this way]

THEY DID SO UNDER THE VERY RESTRICTED RULES OF THE 2007 AGREEMENT – WHICH WERE THAT NO-ONE COULD SELL OR TRANSFER SHARES WITHOUT THE PERMISSION OF THE BOARD UNLESS TO AN EXISTING SHAREHOLDER WHO HAD BEEN TO EDUCO

THESE RESTRICTED RULES HAVE NOW BEEN JUDGED ILLEGAL AND THEREFORE HAVE NO FORCE IN COURT. SO ANYONE SELLING OR TRANSFERRING SHARES UNDER THE CONDITIONS OF THIS ILLEGAL AGREEMENT MAY HAVE DONE SO UNDER DURESS (= HARDSHIP, PRESSURE) – THEY WERE ILLEGALLY AND WRONGLY INFORMED THAT THEY HAD VERY LIMITED OPTIONS FOR SELLING THEIR SHARES AND HAD TO TAKE ONE OF THE OPTIONS OPEN TO THEM, ONE ALLOWED BY THE BOARD. AND IN MOST JURISDICTIONS, IF YOU DO SOMETHING UNDER DURESS, THEN IT DOES NOT STAND UP IN LAW – YOU CAN HAVE THE DEAL SET ASIDE.

We can see how savagely these restrictions were applied in the case of Jean Cornec – the only way that he could get out of the trap of being unable to sell his shares was to agree to sell his shares to Susan Morrice, who imposed a condition that he could not make any criticisms (“negative remarks”) about INE or any of its shareholders or directors. Susan Morrice then claimed he had made negative remarks, and did not pay him most of the money, but gave the shares to her nephew, Josh Stewart, leaving Mr. Cornec with no shares and without the majority of his money. No wonder Judge Bannister referred to the 2007 restrictions as “savage”! This is now being fought out in Court in Denver, USA.

Apparently Tony Quinn, the “Director who never was”, will not be at the AGM later this month. So Susan Morrice will be representing the Board. There are some interesting conversations that the shareholders can have with her, for instance, they could demand

* all share transfers since 2007 are to be cancelled and the shares restored to their original owners, who are free to find buyers for them under the terms of the 2002 agreement and to claim the dividends they are due and their allocation of profit during the years they held the shares, unless the original owners are still willing to let the transaction stand

* a detailed listing of the expenses incurred by the company in security, travel, Educo seminars, legal expenses and other items to be provided to the shareholders together with copies of invoices and an explanation of why these expenses should be paid for by shareholders – if the shareholders don’t agree, these expenses are to be personally repaid to the company by the people who benefitted from them, who are according to Judge Bannister, mostly Tony Quinn and Susan Morrice

* the legal cases currently being pursued or paid for by the company against various people are to be explained, together with estimated costs including costs that will have to be paid to the other side if they win, and if the shareholders don’t agree that they want to pay for these costs, these legal cases are to be dropped and the costs already run up to be repaid to the company

* she is to explain how evidenced and fact-based comments, whether “negative” in her opinion or not, by the media and other sources about Tony Quinn or any other individual cause any loss to an oil company that does not even sell to the retail market (that is, there are no BNE or INE petrol stations that customers could boycott, its oil is sold to the commercial market that bought oil from Saddam Hussein and Colonel Gaddafi, so does not seem to be concerned whether oil companies are run by nice people or not).

* she is to explain how she claims that someone from the Middle East was apparently going to invest $100 million in the company – a company that struck oil at its first drilling, has a huge income flow from apparently such a high-quality product that expensive refining is not required, and had credit available from banks – what was this investor going to get in exchange for such an enormous investment, given that the company was successfully launched with an investment of $6 million? Judge Bannister said he found no evidence that this $100 million investment was a real possibility – Clause 66 of his Judgement says “I reject the story about the attempted investment. Not a single document was produced to support it and Mr. Stewart [Susan Morrice’s nephew to whom she gave Mr. Cornec’s shares] did not give evidence. These payments [for fees for negotiating this supposed investment] appear to have been simple withdrawals for private purposes which should never have been made and represent a failure to distinguish between company and personal property”. These fees were eventually repaid to the company, but in Judge Bannister’s opinion there was never a real possibility of investment in the first place. Since the fees were repaid, why does this matter for INE shareholders? Because Ms Morrice claims it is because of “negative remarks” etc spread by a conspiracy including Jean Cornec, Sheila McCaffrey and assorted others including Dialogue Ireland that this potential investor ‘broke off negotiations” {Clause 65] and she is spending and planning to spend large sums of shareholders’ money on legal cases for damages for this, even though a Judge has already stated he finds there was no potential investment in the first place.

* even if there was a potential investor, who broke off negotiations because of unfavourable information received about the company, she is to explain how it is that any negative reputation the company had acquired was due to comments in Ireland, far from Nevis and Belize where the company was based, about Tony Quinn, who was not legally a Director and very probably not legally a Class A shareholder, rather than due to

– the company had not filed company records properly as found in the Marriott case

– the company had not had an AGM annually as required

– Tony Quinn, who had no experience in the oil industry and with a then 35-year history (now 40-year history) of being reported in the press and elsewhere for running a cult, most of this extremely unfavourable [see DI’s many archives for these reports], had been appointed to the Board and issued a huge shareholding for no consideration, and a condition had been applied to share ownership that share transfers would only be automatically approved if the transferee was an Educo member, otherwise the Board could refuse to allow the shareholder to sell or transfer at all – Justice Hogan in the Dublin High Court judgement last week said of this restriction “This is a classic example of oppression of minority shareholders, since it is impossible to see what legitimate reason there might be for such an eccentric – and there is, I fear, no other word for it – requirement.” [Clause 6, Hogan Judgment]

– three former Directors were taking legal action against the company, mostly because their ability to sell their shares was being “savagely” restricted and they were consequently in a poverty trap, owning shares which were technically a valuable asset but which they could not freely sell

What investors in their right mind would invest in a company where that was going on? These are not normal, transparent, commercial dealings – they are eccentric and oppressive.

SUSAN MORRICE CANNOT COMPLAIN THAT COMMENTS ABOUT TONY QUINN ARE AFFECTING THE OIL COMPANY, BECAUSE

* A COMMERCIAL OIL COMPANY’S REPUTATION DEPENDS MOSTLY ON ITS OIL RESERVES, THE ONLY OTHER THING WHICH MIGHT SERIOUSLY AFFECT IT IS IF THERE IS SOMETHING PECULIAR AND UNCOMMERCIAL ABOUT HOW IT IS BEING RUN

– there has been, but this is her responsibility and that of her fellow Board members, not anyone else

* TONY QUINN’S NEGATIVE REPUTATION WAS A PRE-EXISTING CONDITION WHEN SHE AGREED TO ISSUE SHARES TO HIM AND APPOINT HIM TO THE BOARD (WHICH TURNED OUT TO BE ILLEGAL) – SHE THEREFORE EXPOSED THE COMPANY KNOWINGLY TO WHATEVER DAMAGE THIS ASSOCIATION WITH TONY QUINN CAUSED – if he never chose over decades to defend his reputation in Court with his own money, there is surely no justification now for defending it with shareholders’ money

As for the idea that the fact he holds some Class B shares (transferred under the illegal 2007 Agreement) gives him a connection in reputation with the company – this is ridiculous. A minority Class B shareholder’s reputation has no effect on a company.

IF THE SHAREHOLDERS AT THE AGM DON’T LIKE THE ANSWERS THEY GET FROM SUSAN MORRICE ON THE ABOVE – THEY CAN ALWAYS GO TO JUDGE BANNISTER

THEY CAN STILL DO THAT EVEN IF THEY GET A DIVIDEND – IT DOES NOT AFFECT THEIR LEGAL RIGHTS AS THEY ARE ENTITLED TO A DIVIDEND BASED ON THEIR ALLOCATED SHARE OF THE PROFITS FROM THE TIME THEIR SHARES WERE ISSUED

We are putting out this post because it is part of Dialogue Ireland’s job description as a cult information consumer assistance organisation to make available comments and points of view that may not be readily accessible to people who have questions and are no longer satisfied with the answers they have been getting from those who have an interest in influencing their opinions. Similarly, cult information consumer assistance organisations find themselves explaining to people who signed a billion-year contract with Scientology when they were 17 that it is not actually legally binding. We are not in a position to give professional legal advice, obviously, but we aim to help people reach a position where they are making an informed choice, which is the basis of a free and democratic society.

DIALOGUE IRELAND’S WORK HAS BEEN RECOGNISED AS A VALUABLE PUBLIC FUNCTION IN THE RECENT HIGH COURT JUDGMENT IN DUBLIN BY JUDGE HOGAN – HE REFUSED TO ALLOW DIALOGUE IRELAND DIRECTOR MIKE GARDE TO BE FORCED TO REVEAL THE IDENTITIES OF PEOPLE WHO SOUGHT HIS HELP IN RELATION TO CULTIST ABUSE, WHICH HAD BEEN SOUGHT BY SUSAN MORRICE. AS HE STATED IN HIS JUDGEMENT,

“If [as is claimed] Mr. Quinn holds unorthodox religious views and is effectively the leader of a religious cult which has used psychological techniques as a means of controlling gullible adherents, then – to use the language of Article 40.6.1 [of the Irish constitution] – the media are clearly entitled to educate public opinion in this regard. This is perhaps especially true if Mr. Quinn were to have access to enormous funds via what appears to be the largesse of some of the founding members of INE.” [Clause 58, Hogan Judgment]

Cultist leaders work hard to keep their followers misinformed – and it’s painful to realise that someone you admired and believed was only interested in taking as much money and work from you as they could. We’ve been told by many people what Tony Quinn calls his “dear” followers behind their backs – “foolies”, as he laughs all the way to the bank in Kenya, Jersey etc with their money (despite apparently knowing nothing about “financials”). We can see from his treatment of Sheila McCaffrey, surely the follower who gave him more than any other by a long way, what matters most to him – money and control. We understand that people often find it easier to cling to their illusions – but when the cost becomes too great, we’re here with the information they need.

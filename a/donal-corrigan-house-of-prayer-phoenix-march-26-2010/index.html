Donal Corrigan V The Law Society

LEGAL EAGLE and former House of Prayer devotee Donal Corrigan has recently felt the wrath of Ken Murphy’s Law society, which hauled Corrigan up before the High Court over complaints related to conveyancing matters. Corrigan – who the Law Society has claimed may be operating without practising certificates or insurance – has been less than helpful with the Law Society in its investigation.

Significantly, Corrigan wasn’t called to appear before the Solicitors Disciplinary Tribunal (SDT), as would be the norm in these cases. This is because he failed to attend meetings and failed to reply to correspondence from the Law Society, so it opted to by-pass the SDT altogether and bring proceedings against the bould Corrigan straight to the High Court. Corrigan had already failed to turn up a High Court hearing earlier this month, and could face possible imprisonment should be fail to turn up to the nest hearing.

Such behaviour is rather surprising from a man who was once dedicated to spreading the good word of the Lord, through the “visionary” Christina Gallagher and her Achill-based House of Prayer (HoP). Corrigan was a HoP director from 1998-2008 and also acted as the company’s solicitor, at one point threatening to sue the Sunday World over a stoyry about Gallagher’s supposedly Lavish lifestyle. Recently filed accounts show that the HoP had accumulated profits of €1.7m at the end of March 2009, having dropped almost €200,000 that year. The accounts also note that during the year the Revenue withdrew the HoP’s charitable staus, a decision that the directors are hoping to overturn; otherwise they could be landed with a tax bill in the region of €125,000, in interest plus penalties.

Corrigan had also found himself caught up in another controversy, this one over disgraced legal eagle Thomas Byrne. The two were named as defendants in a case taken by HB Band over a €1m Dublin property, where Corrigan’s practice was located. That dispute was settled in the Commercial Court early last year.

Fri, 5 Mar 2004 – Irish Independent artice on Landmark

Landmark

Irish Independent

Ben Quinn

A controversial US organisation, which claims to enhance the quality of people’s lives, is holding an intensive three-day coaching seminar in Dublin this weekend, costing €415 per participant.

It denies accusations of `brainwashing’ but critics claim that it employs group pressure techniques to influence seminar participants, and that they turned into `robots’ during intensive all-day sessions.

The San Franciso-based organisation, known as Landmark Education, denies that it is a cult, instead describing itself as a `global education enterprise.’

It has hired facilities in Grafton Street‘s plush Westbury hotel for the sessions, from today (Friday) until Sunday.

But Mike Garde of Dialogue Ireland, which campaigns to highlight new religious movements and groups accused of being `cults’, has already expressed concerns about the event.

“This is the kind of thing that can be very dangerous to people’s

psychological well being,” added Mr Garde.

He also expressed concerns about the amount of control that he said could

be exercised over participants in lengthy sessions using psychological

techniques.

Disaffected participants have claimed that they were brainwashed by

Landmark, and relatives have complained that loved ones have been so altered by experiences with the group that their personalities all but vanished.

According to a spokesperson for Landmark, the organisation “does not use coercive pressure or deception to get people to join. There is nothing in

Landmark Education for people to join.”

“The facts are clear that the Landmark Forum and Landmark Education’s

other programs do not involve the use of brainwashing or anything that

remotely resembles such techniques.”

Among those who have taken part in Landmark courses is a Co Longford

priest, Father Eamonn O’Connor, who said that the facts were that Landmark

Education was a profit-making company, doing “marvellous work.”

Despite initially being sceptic, he now says: “Having taken some of their

Courses I found them to be well worth the effort.”

But he admits that others in the Catholic Church would be uncomfortable

about Landmark Education. And although he has recommended that his Bishop take some of the organisation’s courses the bishop is still sceptical.

“His line is `is there nothing about God in it?’’’ said Father O’Connor.

Landmark, which has 58 offices worldwide, claims that 125,000 participants take part in its programmes each year.

Participants in the organisation’s `Landmark Forum’ sessions, which begin

at 9am and end sometime between 10pm and midnight, are encouraged to wear comfortable clothing along with 75-250 others.

The sessions are described as “a powerful, accelerated learning

experience” and “a guided dialogue between the instructor and

participants.”Participants are encouraged to contact relatives and friends and tell them about the courses.

The organisation hit the headlines in the UK four years ago when it was

reported that a young woman who supervised Prince William during his gap-year expedition to South America was involved in Landmark.

BNE and GOB at loggerheads over $5 million trust by Adele Ramos

Tony Quinn

This article from Belize points to a fundamental principle at the centre of Quinn’s philosophy – Use other peoples’ money. We have had glowing reports about how the oil company is a real charitable institution.  Adele Ramos goes behind the rhetoric.

BNE and GOB at loggerheads over $5 million trust by Author: Adele Ramos – adelescribe@gmail.com

http://www.amandala.com.bz/index.php?id=10407

Chairman of the Belize Natural Energy Charitable Trust, Orlando “Landy” Burns, confirmed to Amandala  today that he has received a letter from three directors of the trust, all appointees of the petroleum producing company, Belize Natural Energy (BNE), requesting that he resign from the post.

Burns, who is also a hardware businessman in Orange Walk and a politician up north, told Amandala today that the only one that can remove him is the government — not BNE. “Government has no say and they are 50-50 partner,” complained Burns.

“From day 1, I have been telling them that we need to change the name [of the trust],” said Burns, who proposes that the name should rightfully be the GOB-BNE Trust Fund.

He said that he has been on the board of the trust since its launch, two years ago, and while he was able to have his say, he has never been able to have his way, because BNE had always run things on the trust.

Burns pointed out that the logo for the trust and the logo for the company are nearly identical, and BNE uses the trust fund as a PR fund for the company — citing a $14,000 bill for September for two media houses. There was a prepayment of $11,000 to one media house that the board had not approved, said Burns.

As we reported in the mid-week edition of Amandala, the Government and BNE are at loggerheads over the recent stance that the Government has taken that it will not agree to automatically renew the contract of Deborah Sewell, general manager of the trust until the end of September 2010.

Kevin Herrera, a BNE trustee Amandala spoke with this evening, said that the BNE directors want a chairman who can build a consensus. He told our newspaper that Burns has said numerous times since he has become chairman that he has a casting vote he can use.

That casting vote, said Herrera, has not ever been used at any meeting of the trust, because they try to work by consensus.

Herrera said that their request to Burns to resign is nothing personal, but they just need “a guy that can bring things together.”

Burns said that Herrera and the two other BNE trustees, Daniel Gutierrez and Melanie Escalante, had walked out of the September 15, 2010, meeting when they brought up the issue of Sewell’s contract. He said that while the BNE trust had spent thousands for advertising in September, the BNE directors have not supported a project from some Belizean Americans abroad who want to get three dump trucks and 500 garbage bins for municipalities — he thinks because “it will make the government look good.” The BNE trust was asked for US$5,000 to co-finance the project, said the chairman.

Burns claimed that while he was willing to compromise to have Sewell hired on a 3-month extension until the post is advertised and all applicants are given a chance to bid for the job, the BNE directors refused to support the municipal garbage project, initiated by Belizeans abroad.

When we asked Herrera about this, he said that the matter was never formally tabled at the board. He also said that there are guidelines on what gets funding, and they have to ensure that the funds go to charity, though he did concede that the trustees do exercise discretion in giving grants from the funds.

Burns told Amandala, however, that the BNE trust has loaned funds to “friends.” He cites the case of a businessman who had been given a BNE contract, but also loaned funds from the trust “at credit union rate” to purchase a truck.

In an interview with Channel 7 News this week, Sewell alleged that Burns, a United Democratic Party hopeful for Orange Walk East, had been using the trust fund to garner political mileage.

Sewell also alleged that Burns had failed to give account for the monies spent to provide donated chairs for schools in Orange Walk. The cost is usually $3 per chair, but Burns paid $5 for getting chairs delivered and distributed to Orange Walk and did not give account, she said.

For his part, Burns claims that the costs were for transporting and offloading, and that the business community there chipped in to help defray costs, because there were schools that could not afford to pay for the chairs. He also said that out of 20 containers that came to Belize, there was only 1 sent to the north.

With regards to Sewell’s contract, Burns alleges that Sewell had asked for a huge salary increase – from $55,000 a year to $93,000, with “a lot of perks.” There was a board counterproposal, he said, for a lower salary increase, with an increased telephone allowance, as well as more vacation time, a plane ticket, and a vacation grant, among other incentives.

The BNE trust, he said, now has three staff remaining, and the accountant, Abner Valladarez, is now in charge.

The BNE side has claimed that the Government has tried to abuse the fund for political purposes, and took issue with a proposal for a $250,000 discretionary fund. “The word is emergency fund,” said Burns. He said that when this came up, as a part of the proposal to help St. Peter Claver School get safer buildings to replace the asbestos roof buildings, the trustees claimed, “it is not in the budget.”

Burns said that there is pressure from the BNE side to change the constitution of the trust, “so they can have their way.”

The Opposition People’s United Party has demanded an investigation into the BNE allegations.

“The PUP supports the position of the outgoing general manager of the trust that her contract was not renewed because she would not bow to pressure from the chairman, Landy Burns, who is a political aspirant in the Orange Walk East division,” says the Opposition via a press release.

BNE chief executive officer, Dr. Gilbert Canton, told Amandala Monday that the company wants to resolve the dispute with the government.

Herrera and Canton both told us that this is the first time there has been dissent among the trustees.

As an aside, we note that petroleum revenues have been in excess of $600 million since BNE pioneered in Belize five years ago, and the money that goes into the fund represents less than 1% of the proceeds. Government’s take of petroleum proceeds has been estimated at around 20 cents on the dollar — the rest going to BNE, which has claimed that most of the funds has gone into paying expenses.

Of note is that the BNE Trust and BNE operate out of the same offices at Mountain View Blvd, Belmopan.

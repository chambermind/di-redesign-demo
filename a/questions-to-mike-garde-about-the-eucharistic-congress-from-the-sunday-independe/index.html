Questions to Mike Garde about The Eucharistic Congress from the Sunday Independent and his answers.

Abuse by Church and Irish institutionsConferences & CommentariesHouse of PrayerReligion

It is obvious when a journalist is writing an article they can’t always use all the material you give them.

http://www.independent.ie/national-news/underfire-church-to-break-from-tradition-3105751.html?start=1

In Ireland though where there is extreme sensitivity around Religion and following the recent  Mission to Prey Prime Time Investigates documentary it is necessary to make sure the context of the interview is clear to the public. The reader of the article would not be aware of the questions that were posed to us, as the shape of the article did not make clear the question and answer format used by the journalist Joanna Kiernan. As an addition to the article then I am providing some commentary and will be posting further articles from matters arising.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/05/13/under-fire-church-to-break-from-tradition-by-joanna-kiernansunday-independent-sunday-may-13-2012/

Parts left out in the article will be highlighted to give a better understanding of the context of my interview.

Mike Garde is a Mennonite who is member of Grosvenor Baptist Church, and was the first non Roman Catholic to study for the Bachelor of Divinity at St Patrick’s College, Maynooth in 1975. He obtained his MA in Theology from the Milltown Institute.

 Q – How do you feel about the Eucharistic Congress taking place in Ireland next month?

http://www.iec2012.ie/

http://www.iec2012.ie/index.jsp?a=2125&n=4209&p=101

 A – It really does not impact me at all. It strikes me as an attempt to do what was done a few years ago when relics were brought round the country. I believe some Catholics view it as a strategy to renew the Catholic Church in Ireland. My evaluation is that it will be an abject failure in that regard.

 Q – Do you feel that the Eucharistic Congress in 2012 will have changed from that of the Congress last held here in 1932?

 Yes the position of the Catholic Church in 1932 was that of a Church which represented the people and was close to them. However, they had become a dominant political force, having taken over when the British left. It was Brits out, Rome in. We must remember that the Catholic Church does not only rely on spiritual authority but as a vestige of its history has linked its identity to the Vatican State. Hence for the spiritual welfare of Catholicism we should not have diplomatic relations with the Vatican. I believe that for it to really flourish as a minority faith it must relinquish its connection to secular power. Catholics as a cultural Irish identity are a majority, but the Catholic Faith is a threatened species.

Q -Do you feel that people of different beliefs are being included more so in this Congress? If not, how could they be included more?

The author categorises my response as arguing,

Mr Garde is not impressed with the level of non-Catholic inclusion in the 2012 Congress.

What I actually said on that issue was the following: Members of other Churches have been invited to both speak at the Theological Conference which precedes the Congress and at the Congress itself. I see no problem with the Catholic Church controlling the agenda of its own event.

http://www.iec2012.ie/media/TheologySymposiumMay20121.pdf

I was addressing the fact that the Roman Catholic Church in its official teachings regards itself in the Premier league and other Christian Churches are in the second division, not whether it was inclusive in its Congress which I had no opinion on.

DECLARATION
“DOMINUS IESUS”
ON THE UNICITY AND SALVIFIC UNIVERSALITY
OF JESUS CHRIST AND THE CHURCH

17.  Therefore, there exists a single Church of Christ, which subsists in the Catholic Church, governed by the Successor of Peter and by the Bishops in communion with him.58 The Churches which, while not existing in perfect communion with the Catholic Church, remain united to her by means of the closest bonds, that is, by apostolic succession and a valid Eucharist, are true particular Churches.59 Therefore, the Church of Christ is present and operative also in these Churches, even though they lack full communion with the Catholic Church, since they do not accept the Catholic doctrine of the Primacy, which, according to the will of God, the Bishop of Rome objectively has and exercises over the entire Church.60

On the other hand, the ecclesial communities which have not preserved the valid Episcopate and the genuine and integral substance of the Eucharistic mystery,61 are not Churches in the proper sense; however, those who are baptized in these communities are, by Baptism, incorporated in Christ and thus are in a certain communion, albeit imperfect, with the Church.62 Baptism in fact tends per se toward the full development of life in Christ, through the integral profession of faith, the Eucharist, and full communion in the Church.63

http://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000806_dominus-iesus_en.html

 A – In Ireland we tend to look at persons from different denominations or Confessions as different Religions. It is totally based on the view that Catholicism sees it self as Christianity and other Christian Churches are recognised but in the second division.

Members of other Churches have been invited to both speak at the Theological Conference which precedes the Congress and at the Congress itself. I see no problem with the Catholic Church controlling the agenda of its own event. However, if you wished to address the sectarian context of Irish history then choosing the Eucharist as its theme is the most divisive doctrine you could choose. It is the central sacrament of the Catholic faith and that which divides it from all other Christians on this island.

This was left out,

A Congress that would have invited other Christians to focus on Jesus Christ the centre of our faith would have been a sign of the New Ireland not just a ‘Catholic’ event. It would have done in the spiritual sphere what the Queen, Ian Paisley, Bertie Ahern, Tony Blair, Martin McGuinness and Peter Robinson have embarked upon in the political sphere. It was life changing and was what Jesus was about. This is just the Council of Trent on Facebook.  

 Q –Is the Eucharistic Congress in your view, an opportunity for healing and Change in the Catholic Church?

This again was my most important comment, but it arose from the question asked. It is the one I feel strongest about,

A – It could have been but a I have been of the opinion already since last year that unless Cardinal Brady not just apologised but resigned for his role in the sex abuse scandal this exercise would be doomed. Most people are aware of his role in regard to children, less well known is how he has allowed elder abuse fester and has closed his ears to the cries of the victims of Christina Gallagher of the House of Prayer in Achill. As a Doctor of Canon Law he has allowed this abuse to continue by allowing a Marian scam to continue by not discipling a priest from his Archdiocese who flagrantly endorses this project by entering the Archdiocese of Tuam. The very clear canonical disciplines he claims to have followed in regard to children he seems totally unwilling to follow in regard to the House of Prayer.

Priests who express minority views at the edge of Catholic doctrine are silenced and sent to monasteries, whereas, clear theological and moral failure is ignored or condoned.

http://www.rte.ie/podcasts/2012/pc/pod-v-28041243m16smfsbriandarcy-pid0-2596368_audio.mp3

Without repentance there can be no forgiveness, and without forgiveness their can be no reconciliation. The Eucharist can’t be the central mystery of Catholicism without good authority being exercised. It will therefore be an empty jamboree. I hope I am proved wrong. I am waiting for my ticket in the post!

Sex claim Icross boss moves to grab Irish cash by Colin Coyle 25 April 2010

Icross Charity

Sex claim Icross boss moves to grab Irish cash by Colin Coyle 25 April 2010, The Sunday Times

© 2010 Times Newspapers Ltd. All rights reserved

ICROSS KENYA, the charity organisation fronted by Mike Meegan, an Irish aid worker accused of sexual assault and financial impropriety, is trying to seize control of funds held by its sister organisation in Ireland.  Last month Icross Ireland, which has €266,000 in its accounts and the same again promised in bequests, said it was winding up its operations and would return all funds to the Commissioners of Charitable Donations and Bequests to be redistributed to worthy causes.  But Icross Kenya, Meegan’s organisation, has sent a series of legal letters to Icross Ireland seeking the release of all funds. It claims that money donated to the Irish wing was intended for use “on the ground” in Kenya and that Icross Ireland has no legal right to return the money.  The Irish wing of the charity decided to cease operations after The Sunday Times published an Irish Aid audit report, which showed that almost ¤100,000 of taxpayers’ money is unaccounted for by Icross Kenya. The Irish wing of the charity has also become increasingly concerned in recent years about ongoing allegations of a sexual nature against Meegan, first revealed in this newspaper last month.  Further claims emerged last week when Meegan failed in an attempt to stop the Mail on Sunday from publishing interviews with a series of alleged victims in Kenya. Five Kenyan men claim to have been sexually assaulted by Meegan, with the first documented case dating back to 1986.

In an interview yesterday with the Irish Times, Meegan denied all the sex charges. He claimed they are the result of a “campaign of raw vindictiveness” by former aid colleagues with whom he has fallen out. Meegan said he never had sex with any staff, or with a minor, and claims that all his sexual partners have been Caucasian. He said that he has shared beds with other men while in the bush, but this is common in Kenya.

Meegan claims that his “victims” were offered money by the Mail to make statements – the newspaper denies this – and claimed that one alleged victim, Meriape Ole Sangaire, retracted his original allegation.

Icross Kenya has made a criminal complaint against three Kenyan men who have spoken out about Meegan, accusing them of lying under oath. The organisation claims that Sangaire, one of the three, has sworn conflicting affidavits.

In the interview yesterday, the charity worker admitted that his organisation’s failure to meet Irish Aid’s standards was a “huge f***-up”. “We didn’t meet their criteria for [accounting for] expenditure. They didn’t know where money went, but that didn’t mean it was stolen or swiped.”

Meegan and Icross Kenya did not respond to further queries from The Sunday Times last week. He has refused a series of interview requests with this newspaper.  Questions about Meegan were first raised by the press in 2006 when it was discovered he had overstated his qualifications and was claiming to be a doctor. During the 1980s, the charity worker frequently represented himself as a priest.  He said yesterday he had called himself a doctor “in good faith” and didn’t realise that the “mail-order” college that awarded him his qualifications was not accredited. He intends staying in Kenya, despite having no “personal income”.

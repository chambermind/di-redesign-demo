‘Jihadists brainwashed our girl’ The Sunday Times March 14, 2010 By Mark Tighe

Islamism

From The Sunday Times March 14, 2010 By Mark Tighe

‘Jihadists brainwashed our girl’

Ms Paulin Ramirez

The family of an American woman, who was arrested in Waterford on suspicion of being involved in a plot to kill a Swedish cartoonist, say they are worried about the safety of her six-year-old son who is in the care of the Health Service Executive.

Gardai have confirmed that Jamie Paulin-Ramirez, 31, a medical assistant born in Kansas city, was one of seven Muslims arrested last week. Paulin-Ramirez is the second American woman to be linked with the plot to kill Lars Vilks, who depicted Muhammad as a stray dog. Last October the FBI arrested Colleen LaRose, who used the online pseudonym “Jihad Jane”, over her involvement in the alleged plot. Paulin-Ramirez is the girlfriend of Sharif Damache, an Algerian who has Irish citizenship and whom gardai suspect of being the leader of the Irish group. Gardai say Damache is separated from his Irish wife. Christine Holcomb-Mott, Paulin-Ramirez’s mother, told The Wall Street Journal that the six-year-old’s father was a Mexican who had not seen his son in five years. Gardai claim Paulin-Ramirez had up to four previous marriages and told her family she had converted to Islam only last Easter. The family said the boy’s name had recently been changed to Wahid. “This is about my baby,” said Paulin-Ramirez’s mother. “We need some help to get this baby back. I’m concerned about my daughter but I’m concerned about our baby boy because he shouldn’t be caught in the middle of this.” Holcomb-Mott said her daughter began wearing headscarves and later a hijab last year before she left for Ireland. “It came out of left field,” she said. “I knew she was talking to these people online . . . what caused her to turn her back on her country, on her family and become this person? I don’t know how or why. “All I know is she was in contact with this Jihad Jane. The only thing I could think of is that they brainwashed her.” George Mott, Pauline-Ramirez’s step-father, also compared her with LaRose, 46, the suburban Philadelphia woman now in custody.

“Jamie took off just like Jihad Jane took off on her boyfriend,” he said. “We have been trying since September of last year to get her back here and get that baby back here.” Mott said that a few months before Paulin-Ramirez disappeared, he confronted her about her increasing devotion to Islam. “What are you going to do, strap a bomb on and blow up something?” he said he asked his step-daughter. She is said to have replied: “If necessary, yes.” Last year Paulin-Ramirez told her aunt she wanted to study to become a doctor and signed up for nurse practitioner courses. She took out student loans of about $3,000 (€2,180) last year, according to her mother and stepfather. Her mother now believes she used that money to get to Ireland. She had told the family she was going away for a weekend but failed to return. The family informed police and later the FBI came to take her computer from the family home. The FBI informed the family last week that Paulin-Ramirez had been arrested in Ireland. Michael Holcomb, her older brother, told The Wall Street Journal that he was “actually relieved” his sister was arrested because it might help the family get her son returned to America. “Other than that, I don’t care what happens to her,” he said.

Holcomb thought his sister’s interest in Islam was not serious. “I thought this was just a phase she was going through, that she was trying to find herself,” he said.

“Now I’m angry And disappointed. She had a good job and she gave it all up. It’s beyond me.”

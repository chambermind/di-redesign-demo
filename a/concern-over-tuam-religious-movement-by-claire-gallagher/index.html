Concern over Tuam religious movement By Claire Gallagher

TUAM HERALD WED 14/7/04

Concern over Tuam religious movement

By Claire Gallagher

A RELIGIOUS movement based in Tuam and described by one of Ireland’s leading religious watchdogs as having cultist tendencies is causing concern for a number of townspeople. The leadership of the controversial group The King of Kings Fellowship (KoK)  has also been called into question by the Church that originally gave it a licence, former members, and leaders of other Churches. Once known as the Tuam Christian Fellowship, the movement came to Dialogue Ireland’s (the religious watchdog).  Mike Garde’s attention most recently when he was contacted by Harcourt Square Garda Station, Dublin. The Gardai had received an anonymous fax which suggested that Mr Garde was investigating an event to be held by the Tuam group in a Dublin hotel at the end of the month. Until then Mr Garde had no knowledge of the two day event entitled King’s Daughters of Ireland International Women’s Conference which costs €35 per day at a hotel that charges €4,370 per day for its conference room. The KoK is an Evangelical Pentecostal group founded in 1994 by mother-of-five Mary Sheridan. It is a registered charity which means all donations to the group are tax free.  The group now meet in a prefab situated beside the Sheridan family home, close to the town of Tuam.

Between 1999 and 2000, this Tuam movement received a temporary, pastoral licence (renewable annually) from a Christian Fellowship known as Foursquare International. By June 19, 2001 the leadership of the Foursquare Church was writing to the Sheridans (Mary and her husband Ozzie) declaring a parting of the ways in terms of denominational affiliation. The Church is therefore not covered, licensed or ordained by the Foursquare denomination.

One-time members of the KoK have already claimed that the movement specifically targets women, that it has been responsible for breaking up marriages and puts pressure on followers to give money to the group. Over the past few years the activities of the religious grouping have come to the attention of Tuam Parish Administrator Fr Stephen Farragher and his colleagues. Families and individuals that were involved, directly or indirectly, were concerned about the workings of the group and in particular the style of leadership that was exercised within the group, he said. In an effort to provide pastoral care for people who had been hurt by this grouping the help of Mike Garde from Dialogue Ireland, who has considerable experience of dealing with such issues, was enlisted. When he, Mike Garde, tried to enter into dialogue with the leadership of this religious grouping, in order to express the concerns of ex-members and the individuals and families affected by this grouping, all his efforts met with failure. The leadership simply refused to meet with him, Fr Farragher continued. Having inquired further into the workings of this grouping it was learned that when it was previously known as Tuam Christian Fellowship, it was part of a larger grouping of Christian Fellowships, known as Foursquare International. However this  grouping eventually dissociated itself from the activities of the Tuam group. When asked why it dissociated itself from its activities the Executive Council of the Fellowship of European Foursquare Churches, together with the Foursquare Missions International Supervisor/Co-ordinator for Europe issued a statement in which it outlined its concerns. Of particular concern was the extreme control of members that was exerted by the leadership and the inability of the leadership to accept criticism, he said. The statement issued by Foursquare International said, Many disagreements, which could have been simple and calm discussions, turned into manifestations of high emotions and accusations of spiritual-blinded-ness … on the part of Foursquare leaders attempting to bring a balance.

Fr Farragher explained that the evidence of those who have spoken to him, Mr Garde and his colleagues seems to totally back up the findings of Foursquare International.  These findings have been the focus of the pastoral care that we have tried to provide for ex-members and the families and individuals that have been hurt by the activities of the group. It is a source of regret that all attempts to address these issues with the leadership in a courteous and amicable manner were met with a refusal to co operate on their part. The Tuam Herald made several attempts to call Ms Sheridan at her home and place of business, but were unsuccessful in contacting her.

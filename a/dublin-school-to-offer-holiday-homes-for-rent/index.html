Dublin school to offer holiday homes for rent

JOHN SCOTTUS SCHOOL NORTHUMBERLAND ROAD.

Gabrielle Monaghan and Jill Nesbitt

The parents of pupils at a school in Dublin plan to offer their second properties for rent in an effort to raise funds for its activities.

The board of governors of John Scottus junior school in Ballsbridge and the fee-paying senior school in Donnybrook have set up a website, http://www.jssholidayhomes.com/index.html, to organise the rentals. The parents will retain ownership of their homes but the school will keep the income.

Eithne Dorman, one of the governors, says on the site that the schools are now gathering “exciting properties” donated by people associated with John Scottus or the philosophy school to generate “much-needed revenue”.

Michael Telford, headmaster of the senior school, declined to comment on how the scheme would work or why the schools would need more funds, given that it generated €2m from fundraising, fees and voluntary contributions in the year to July 2008.

Michael Delaney, another governor, dismissed the holiday-home rental scheme as just “low-level fundraising that would be lucky to raise €10,000”. Other members of the board would not talk about the school’s finances.

The Revenue Commissioners held talks in 2005 with the senior school after it abolished fees in favour of voluntary contributions of up to €5,100 a year that parents could, in turn, write off against tax. The school now charges fees of a similar amount, but its national school relies on voluntary contributions.

Delaney sent a letter to parents in September 2008 seeking €3,725 for each junior-school pupil towards “supporting small class sizes, additional classes in science, art and philosophy, the provision of a hot meal each day as well as your child’s books and learning materials”.

The Department of Education said the primary school has only had provisional recognition from the state since 1999, though it does contribute to its rent and teacher salaries. It paid €1,043,280 to the John Scottus national school between September 2008 and August 2009 in teachers’ salaries and €891,629 in rental, capitation and other grants.

The school has also received funds from an associated organisation — the School of Economic Science. Known in Ireland as the School of Philosophy and Economic Science, it frequently advertises 12-week “practical philosophy” classes in The Irish Times. The School of Economic Science valued the property it rents out to John Scottus and the School of Philosophy and Economic Science at €4.6m last year.

Earlier this year, Clara Salaman, a British television actress, published a novel based on her experiences at the St James (Independent) School in London, which is also run by the School of Economic Science and of which her parents were members. Salaman said that she and her siblings felt that the organisation “had stolen our parents from us”. Trousers were banned for women, as was television, pop music, radio, most books, meat and cooked food.

She described the organisation as “a self-styled, extremely strict, truth-seeking, spiritual society that demanded an extraordinary amount from its members.” Salaman said the then leader “ruled with fear. If questions were asked, the challenger was shunned”.

Telford, whose wife, Mary, runs the John Scottus junior school, said he was not familiar with the book and “not interested” in it.

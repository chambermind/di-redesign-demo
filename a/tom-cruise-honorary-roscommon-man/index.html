Tom Cruise honorary Roscommon man?

Scientology

Is Tom Cruise about to be made an honorary

Roscommon man?

By Conor Heneghan

Forget Chris O’Dowd or Shane ‘Cake’ Curran, the most famous man from Roscommon may soon be probably the most famous man in the world itself.

http://www.joe.ie/news-politics/current-affairs/is-tom-cruise-about-to-be-made-an-honorary-roscommon-man-0035991-1

It is getting suspiciously close to April Fools’ Day so we treated this story with a little caution when we first stumbled across it but according to RSVP magazine, Tom Cruise, or Tom O’Cruise as he shall henceforth be known, will be made an honorary Irishman at a (not so secret now) secret gathering in celebration of his Roscommon roots next week.

Cruise is due in Ireland for the premiere of Oblivion in Dublin next Wednesday and while he’s around, he is expected to head west for the celebration which is being organised as part of the Gathering and which certain Government officials had been aware of, but had been warned to keep schtum about.

According to RSVP, Tom’s great-grandfather on his mother’s side is believed to have emigrated to America from a farm in Kilteevan, Co. Roscommon.

As a child, Tom visited Roscommon with his father and is rumoured to have paid a secret visit to relatives there while filming Far and Away, where he played a character with arguably the worst Irish accent ever heard on the big screen… and that’s saying something.

RSVP also add that he allegedly expressed an interest to buy the land that his ancestors would have worked when it went on sale last year for €300,000 but it ended up being bought by a nearby farmer.

They also quote the Mayor of Roscommon Tom Crosby, who said: “It always struck me that he had very much of an Irish look about him, and west of Ireland in particular. This is a great opportunity for Roscommon. We can even run a brief course on the accent and teach him the typical Roscommon word – ‘How’reya’.”

Any chance of the gathering remaining secret seems to have gone, however, as famous Roscommon man Chris O’Dowd tweeted a link to another version of the story on the Irish Central website and as we’re assuming that every Roscommon person on Twitter follows the Boyle native, if they didn’t already know about it, they do now.

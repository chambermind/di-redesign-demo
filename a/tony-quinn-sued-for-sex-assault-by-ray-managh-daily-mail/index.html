Tony Quinn sued for ‘sex assault’
By: Ray Managh

TONY Quinn is being sued over allegations that he sexually assaulted and beat a woman.

Marie Lalor, from Ballycarnane Woods, Tramore, Co. Waterford, was yesterday given the go-ahead to serve the summons on the mind guru by mail as her previous attempts had been thwarted.

Mr Quinn, who is estimated to have earned more than E50million through his Educo Mind Power system – which he claims unlocks a person’s hidden potential – lives mainly in the Bahamas but is in Dublin this weekend to address a health seminar at the RDS.

In the High Court yesterday, Barrister David Conlan Smyth told Mr Justice Patrick McCarthy that Brian Comiskey had been unable to serve a plenary summons on Mr Quinn or any of his representatives because of high walls and gates surrounding his seaside Martello Tower home in Malahide, north Dublin. And although the businessman would be in Dublin for a few days, he was frequently surrounded by security guards, making it impossible to contact him personally. Miss Lalor is claiming damages for sexual assault, as well as alleging that Mr Quinn procured sexual relations against her will, the court heard. She also alleges assault and battery. It is also claimed that Mr Quinn obtained money by false pretences.

He also faces allegations of fraudulent misrepresentation, intentional or careless infliction of mental suffering and subornation by undue influence. Miss Lalor has also issued the summons against Human Resource Potential Seminars Ltd, the company Mr Quinn runs from Jersey. He was not represented in court yesterday.  In bringing a civil claim for sexual assault, Miss Lalor has waived her anonymity.

Mr Quinn is a controversial character, who charged upwards of E18,000 for a two-week course which allows people to unlock their dreams. And in January last year, some 600 people attended his seminar at the RDS.

The son of a taxi driver from Stoneybatter, Dublin, Mr Quinn grew up as a fan of Charles Atlas, the iconic ‘scrawny weakling’ who became a muscleman. Being an average child, the youngster believed he too was destined for great things.

A one-time apprentice butcher, he developed an interest in fitness and body-building and in the 1970s began running yoga courses. He developed a spiritual lifestyle and set up houses in Kilbarrack and Howth, also in Dublin, where people could live in a yogic state of being. Joined by followers prepared to work relentlessly on his behalf, he was an enigmatic character.

His big break came on The Late Late Show and he went on to help boxer Steve Collins prepare for his championship fight against Chris Eubank in 1995.

Mike Garde, director of Dialogue Ireland, an organisation that monitors cults, says that he has worked with 300 people who have been left dissatisfied by their experiences of Tony Quinn. When Liveline ran a segment on the lifestyle and fitness entrepreneur, presenter Joe Duffy was inundated with calls from those who were disgruntled after attending Mr Quinn’s seminars.

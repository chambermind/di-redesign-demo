Response concerning Anne a lay apostle by Kevin J. Symonds

Direction for our TimesHouse of Prayer

By coincidence DI used the comparison of the House of Prayer and the Organisation Direction For Our Times to make make a slight tongue in cheek comment that such was the poor level of cooperation between the House of Prayer and the Archbishop of Tuam that what was happening in Kilmore looked like perfection in comparison. Little did we know we were in fact about to add another visionary group to our list of categories. We noted a comment left by Kevin J. Symonds raising concerns about Anne a lay apostle.

This was our view:

Dialogue Ireland has formed the impression that Bishop Leo has not got the theological expertise necessary to do this job, and that he is a bit of a lone ranger, and he does not have great support among his priests, but compared to the situation in Tuam it appears on the surface to be the way things should be. However, DI has been approached by Kevin Symonds with evidence that suggests that Kilmore has a different problem namely that the Bishop has not discerned the situation correctly and there is a major problem with Direction for our Times. While on Skype to Kevin he informed me that he had received a mail from Ireland indicating that this topic was shared on Live Line and might be on again next Monday. This is what appeared on Liveline’s web site today.

Dr Marc Miravalie a professor of theology based in Ohio tells Joe why he supports Anne the Lay Apostle who is based in Cavan.

We hope to have that material and if it appears on Monday on our site. In the meantime we are happy that Kevin has written a piece for us linking extensive articles about this movement. We thank him for taking the time.

Yesterday, I had the occasion to come across an article about Direction For Our Times (DFOT) on Dialogue Ireland.[i]  The article compares DFOT with Christina Gallagher (CG) and her Houses of Prayer in an attempt to demonstrate that CG should take lessons from DFOT on how to work with the Church’s hierarchy.

I wrote a comment at the end of the entry on Dialogue Ireland that invited the author of the piece to read my articles on Catholic Lane concerning DFOT.  I have subsequently been invited to write a response on Dialogue Ireland by Michael Garde and I am pleased to do so.

Before continuing, the reader is encouraged to read my articles on Catholic Lane so as to receive a better understanding of the background from which the following remarks are made.  You will find them here:  Article One,[ii] Article Two,[iii] Article Three,[iv] Article Four.[v]

My primary objection to the Dialogue Ireland article is its claim that CG should follow the example of DFOT with respect to obeying the lawful authority of the Catholic Church.  The article portrayed DFOT in a positive light of obedience when this is, in fact, very questionable.

In the theology of the Catholic Church, anyone who claims to be receiving messages from heaven, or seeing visions is said to be experiencing “private revelation.”  Any such person, according to said theology, is to report their experiences to the local authority (one’s parish priest, or, if the phenomenon requires, the local Ordinary such as a Bishop).  One can read more about this process by reading an important document from the Vatican on this subject.[vi]

In the case of DFOT, the claimant of the alleged private revelation is a woman named Kathryn Ann Clarke who goes by the pen name “Anne a lay apostle.”  She has submitted all of her writings to her Bishop, Leo O’Reilly of the Diocese of Kilmore.  According to the teachings of the Catholic Church, this submission is a good and commendable thing.  It is a step in the right direction, a fact that is reiterated ad nauseam in DFOT promotional literature in an attempt to ingratiate the organization with fair-minded Catholics who take fidelity to the Church very seriously.

There is an intimate part of the process, however, that is not focused upon by most authors in their writings on DFOT.  Once a person presents him or herself to the authority of the Church for scrutiny, it is presumed that the local Ordinary will perform a rigorous investigation into the claims and the life/character of the claimant.  Any such investigation is usually before allowing any writings to be published and disseminated and is done for the preservation of faith and good morals.

In the case of DFOT and/or Kathryn Clarke, it is questionable whether or not Bishop O’Reilly fulfilled the obligations that fell to him.  Again, for a fuller discussion of the matter, the reader is encouraged to visit the above-mentioned articles on Catholic Lane.  It suffices to say for our purposes here that when one looks at the timeline, Bishop O’Reilly granted permission for Kathryn to publish and disseminate her alleged revelations only a few months after she put pen to paper.  This raises serious doubts as to the integrity and reliability of the permission he has given to Kathryn.

Furthermore, private investigations have raised serious questions and objections about Kathryn Clarke and/or her writings.  Many of these things will be revealed over the course of the next several weeks on Catholic Lane as well as my blog, Desiderium.  From these investigations, it can be stated that had Bishop O’Reilly performed a thorough investigation prior to granting his permission, we would not now be talking about Kathryn and her alleged messages.

Given all the above, I respectfully state that Dialogue Ireland’s point about CG needing to take a page from DFOT about submitting to the local Ordinary is ultimately moot.  If the original submission to Bishop O’Reilly was not followed-up with a rigorous investigation, then the enterprise that is DFOT is fundamentally and fatally flawed from the beginning.  To use DFOT’s submission as an example or model to others, then, does not take the above into account and leaves one wide open to reasonable criticism.

-Kevin J. Symonds

Report of the High Court case in regard International Natural Energy (INE), Sept 7th 2012

Tony Quinn

Mr Justice Gerard Hogan* began hearing submissions in regard case in regard International Natural Energy (INE.) Former director Jean Cornec is suing for $15m (€12m) for shares he claims he sold to a fellow director, Susan Morrice, and was never paid. A number of parties are suing and counter-suing each other in the US case. It arises from the sale of shares in International Natural Energy (INE), an oil company to which Mr Quinn was appointed a director. Former director Jean Cornec has brought the action against the company, claiming he wasn’t paid for $15 million in shares he sold to a fellow director, Susan Morrice. The company claims he broke the contract of sale by engaging in a campaign of disparagement of the directors, including Mr Quinn. Today was a continuation of the adjourned case from Tuesday August 28.

https://dialogueireland.wordpress.com/2012/08/29/a-journalist-and-a-cult-expert-will-ask-the-high-court-to-set-aside-orders-by-ray-managh/

As part of the proceedings, the defendants in the US claim Mr Garde and Ms Tallant have information in relation to the alleged campaign of disparagement. The case is being taken under a little-used provision in the 1856 Foreign Tribunals (Evidence) Act which allows for Irish witnesses in overseas cases to give evidence here before an appointed examiner. At the deposition, the witnesses could be examined and cross-examined by lawyers representing the parties in the US action.

Mr Justice Gerard Hogan began the day by stating that it would be impossible to complete this case in one day and this was agreed by all. It was agreed later to come back to the court on Monday 10th and it was pretty clear that judgement would be reserved until October.

Counsel for Susan Morrice Jim O’Callaghan+, began by suggesting he would need 2 hours to complete his evidence. He had the difficult job of taking the two cases of Mr Garde and Ms Tallant together. He actually was on from 11.20 AM till 15.50 a total of 3 and a half hours. He apologised a number of times but he had a vast area to cover. His basic argument was that the court in Denver needed Mr Garde and Ms Tallant to fill in the cracks of evidence in relation to disparagement in case  between Jean Cornec and Susan Morrice. He believed these two persons had the evidence or at least they would be asked if they had the evidence to assist the court. The main evidence was that of an email sent out to a list of people which included some people affected by Tony Quinn, and as DI is a source of information to a list of journalists. What was noticeable was the attempt to conflate the person of Tony Quinn with the INE. So as we have continuously stated on our blog.

In the mind of Morrice anyone who discusses Quinn is in fact discussing the INE. If one is critical of Quinn one is disparaging an oil company. It was interesting that Jim O’Callaghan had obtained an email which suggested I had a contract with Katrina Skinner to be an expert witness. I had met with her in Nov 2008 and when nothing had resulted I contacted her about 15 months later to request clarification. In other words while I was supposed to be part of an active conspiracy to defame I was not in touch for over a year. What appeared to be a very strong argument will be easily addressed by our counsel Seamus Tuathail* when he shows the reply. It was surprising that a barrister of such experience would not give the full correspondence and depend on this one sided argument to develop his case.

Before Brian O’Moore began his reply Seamus Tuathail sought clarification as to whether the request to the High Court for the depositions had been properly delivered from the court in Denver.

At 15.50 counsel for Ms Tallant, Brian O’Moore SC began a pretty devastating evaluation of the concept of journalistic privilege and looking at the rules governing depositions in Denver contained in the statement by Jim O’Callaghan. We understand these can go on for up to 10 hours asking the questions fifty ways until one wilts. Understandably Canada is not too keen to let its citizens loose in that jurisdiction which seems more like police interrogation than answering questions.

As stated above Mr Justice Gerard Hogan had so much material to consider that if the case goes on much longer he will need a pick up truck to take away the documents. As a result he will need a lot of time to address the amount of material before forming his judgement. As Ireland has not updated the 1856 Foreign Tribunals (Evidence) Act, unlike the UK and Australia, there are many areas to be evaluated before the learned judge can reach his conclusions.
